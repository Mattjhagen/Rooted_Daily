Nehemiah 1

The Broken Wall

“Those who survived the exile and are back in the province are in great trouble and disgrace. The wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates have been burned with fire.” 1
“When I heard these things, I sat down and wept. For some days I mourned and fasted and prayed before the God of heaven.” 2

The Prayer of Confession

“I confess the sins we Israelites, including myself and my father’s family, have committed against you. We have acted very wickedly toward you.” 3
“But if you return to me and obey my commands... I will gather you from the ends of the earth and bring you to the place I have chosen.” 4
“Give your servant success today by granting him favor in the presence of this man.”
(I was cupbearer to the king.) 5

Translation Notes

1 Great Trouble and Disgrace (Ra’ah Gedolah we-Cherpah): In the scrolls, this is the "Exilic Trauma" continuing in the land. Without walls, the Temple (built in Ezra) was vulnerable, and the people were "Disgraced" in the eyes of their neighbors. The "Roots" were there, but the "Protection" was missing.

2 Sat down and Wept (Yashabti u-Bakhiti): Literally in the scrolls, "I sat down and I wept." This is the "Birth of a Burden." Nehemiah was a "High-ranking Official" in Persia, but his "Heart" was "Rooted" in the rubble of Jerusalem. His comfort in Susa could not mask his grief for Zion.

3 I Confess... Including Myself: The scrolls highlight Nehemiah’s "Corporate Responsibility." He didn't blame "Them"; he said "We." He was "Rooting" his prayer in the "Solidarity of Sin." He realized that the "Walls" would never be built until the "Heart" was confessed.

4 I will Gather You (Aqabetz-em): This is the "Remnant Promise" in the scrolls. Nehemiah was quoting Deuteronomy 30. He was "Reminding God" of His own "Contract." It signifies that "Prayer" is most powerful when it is "Rooted in the Word."

5 Cupbearer (Mashqeh): In the scrolls, this was a "Position of Extreme Trust." Nehemiah was the king’s "Last Line of Defense" against poison. He had the king’s ear. He was "Placed by Providence" in a "Persian Palace" to serve a "Jerusalem Purpose." The chapter ends with the "Request for Favor" before "This Man"—referring to the most powerful ruler on earth as just a "Man" before God.

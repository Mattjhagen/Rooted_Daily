Nehemiah 10

“All these now join their fellow Israelites... and bind themselves with a curse and an oath to follow the Law of God given through Moses.” 1

The Holy Time

“When the neighboring peoples bring merchandise on the Sabbath, we will not buy from them.” 2

The Firstfruits

“We also assume responsibility for bringing to the house of the LORD each year the firstfruits of our crops.” 3

The Maintenance of Presence

“We assume the responsibility for each person to give a third of a shekel each year for the service of the house of our God.” 4
“We will not neglect the house of our God.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Bind themselves with a Curse (Alah): In the scrolls, this is "High-Stakes Covenanting." They were essentially saying, "If we break this Word, may the judgment of God fall upon us." It signifies that the "Remnant" knew that half-heartedness had led to the first Exile. They were "Rooting" their obedience in "Total Consecration."

2 Not Buy from Them: The scrolls highlight the "Economic Sacrifice" of the Sabbath. By refusing to trade on the holy day, they were "Uprooting" the profit motive in favor of "Prophetic Rest." It was a "Statement of Sovereignty" to their pagan neighbors.

3 Firstfruits (Bikkurim): Literally in the scrolls, "The Early Ripe ones." Giving the first of the harvest was an act of "Radical Trust." It acknowledged that the "Land" belonged to God. They were "Planting" their gratitude before they even enjoyed the "Fullness" of the crop.

4 Third of a Shekel: This is a "Specific Rooted Adaptation" in the scrolls. The original Law required a half-shekel (Exodus 30:13), but because of the "Poverty" of the exiles (*Chapter 5*), they adjusted the tax to ensure everyone could participate. It signifies that "Holy Service" should be "Accessible to all."

5 We will not Neglect (Lo-Na’azob): Literally in the scrolls, "We will not abandon/leave." This is the "Climax of the Covenant." They were promising that the "House of God" would never again be "Uprooted" from their priorities. It was the "Anchor" of the new Jerusalem.

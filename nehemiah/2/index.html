Nehemiah 2

The Moment of Courage

The king said to me, “What is it you want?” Then I prayed to the God of heaven... 1
“Send me to the city in Judah where my ancestors are buried so that I can rebuild it.”

The Midnight Inspection

“By night I went out through the Valley Gate... examining the walls of Jerusalem, which had been broken down.” 2
“I went up the valley by night, examining the wall.”

The Call to Build

“Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, and we will no longer be in disgrace.” 3
“They replied, ‘Let us start rebuilding.’ So they began this good work.” 4

The Answer to Enemies

“The God of heaven will give us success. We his servants will start rebuilding.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Then I Prayed (Way-Ethpallel): This is the famous "Arrow Prayer" in the scrolls. Nehemiah didn't have time for a "Service of Worship"; he had a "Split Second" between the king’s question and his answer. It signifies that a "Rooted" life has a "Direct Line" to heaven that can be activated in the blink of an eye.

2 Valley Gate (Sha’ar ha-Gai): The scrolls record this "Stealth Inspection." Nehemiah didn't announce his arrival. He wanted to "Internalize the Rubble" before he "Mobilized the Reform." He was "Gaining a Perspective" that was "Unfiltered" by the reports of others.

3 No longer be in Disgrace (Cherpah): In the scrolls, this is the "Strategic Motivation." Rebuilding the wall wasn't just about "Civil Defense," but about "Spiritual Dignity." A city without a wall was a "Laughingstock." He was "Rooting" the project in the restoration of the "Honor of God."

4 So they Began (Way-Yithchazzqu): Literally in the scrolls, "They strengthened their hands." The people "Caught the Fire" of Nehemiah’s vision. It marks the "Re-rooting" of the national will. They moved from "Surviving the Ruins" to "Mastering the Masonry."

5 God of Heaven will Give Success (Elah Shamayya): The scrolls highlight Nehemiah’s "Spiritual Defiance." Sanballat mocked them as "Rebels," but Nehemiah identified them as "Servants." He was claiming a "Sanction" that was higher than the "King of Persia." His "Confidence" was the "Root" that could not be shaken by mockery.

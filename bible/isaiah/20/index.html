Isaiah 20

The Naked Prophet and the Stripped Captives.

Three Years Barefoot

“Take off the sackcloth from your body and the sandals from your feet.”
“Just as my servant Isaiah has gone naked and barefoot for three years.” 1

Stripped Captives

“So the king of Assyria will lead away stripped and barefoot the Egyptian captives.” 2

Dismayed Boasters

“Those who trusted in Cush and boasted in Egypt will be dismayed and put to shame.” 3

How Then Can We Escape?

“See what has happened to those we relied on... How then can we escape?” 4

Translation Notes

1 Naked and Barefoot (a-rom we-ya-cheph): In the scrolls, this is the "Exhibitory Root of Humiliation." Isaiah is "Uprooted" from "Decorum" to become a "Living Oracle." It signifies "The Vulnerability of the Prophet."

2 Stripped and Barefoot (cha-shu-phey sheth): Literally "With buttocks uncovered." The scrolls highlight the "Graphic Root of Defeat." The "Glory" of Egypt is "Uprooted" to show "Exposed Shame." It signify "The End of National Pride."

3 Dismayed and Put to Shame (we-chāt-tu wa-bō-shu): The scrolls highlight the "Psychological Root of Disillusionment." "Trust" (*Mab-bat*) in the "South" is "Uprooted." It signifies "The Collapse of Worldly Security."

4 How then can we Escape (we-ēkh nim-ma-lēt a-nach-nu): The scrolls conclude with this "Escape Root." The "Coastland" (*I*) realizes that "Reliance" (*Mis-an*) is "Uprooted." It marks the transition to "The Burden of the Desert" in *Isaiah 21*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Stripped Sign." Trust the King, not the Coast.

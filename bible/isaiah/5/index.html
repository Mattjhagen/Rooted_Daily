Isaiah 5

The Song of the Wild Grapes.

A Song for My Love

“I will sing for the one I love a song about his vineyard.”
“When I looked for good grapes, why did it yield only wild ones?” 1

Woe to the Greed

“Woe to those who add house to house and join field to field.” 2
“Woe to those who call evil good and good evil.” 3

The Distant Banner

“He lifts up a banner for the distant nations... they come with speed and alertness.” 4

The Lion’s Roar

“Their roar is like that of the lion... they seize their prey and carry it off.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Wild Grapes (be-u-shim): Literally "Stinking things." In the scrolls, this is the "Agricultural Root of Disappointment." The "Choicest Vines" (*So-req*) were "Rooted" in the "Best Soil," but produced "Rottenness" (*Beushim*). It signifies "The Failure of Character."

2 Add House to House (ma-gi-ey Ba-yith be-Ba-yith): The scrolls highlight the "Expansionist Root of Injustice." "Wealth" is "Uprooted" from "Community" to "Self-Isolation." It signify "The Monopoly of Greed."

3 Call Evil Good (o-me-rim la-ra Tob): The scrolls highlight the "Linguistic Root of Perversion." When "Definitions" are "Uprooted" from "Truth," the "Social Fabric" is "Trampled" (*Mi-re-mas*). It signifies "Ethical Relativism."

4 Distant Banner (Nes la-Goy-im me-ra-choq): The scrolls highlight the "Summoning Root of Judgment." God "Roots" the "Agent of Justice" in a "Distant Signal" (*Nes*). It signify "The Reach of Divine Sovereignty."

5 Seize their Prey (we-yap-phet we-en Matz-tzil): The scrolls conclude with this "Predatory Root." There is "No Deliverer" (*En Matzil*) once the "Lion" of judgment is "Rooted" in the "Seizure." It marks the transition to "Isaiah’s Call" in *Isaiah 6*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Sovereign Lion." Yield to the Master.

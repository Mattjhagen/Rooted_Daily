Micah 7

The Watcher in Hope and the Sea of Forgetfulness.

Misery is Mine

“What misery is mine! The godly have been swept from the land.” 1

I Watch in Hope

“But as for me, I watch in hope for the Lord, I wait for God my Savior.” 2
“Though I have fallen, I will rise.” 3

Who is a God like You?

“Who is a God like you, who pardons sin and forgives transgression?” 4

Depths of the Sea

“You will hurl all our iniquities into the depths of the sea.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Godly swept from the land (ā-bhādh chā-sīdh min-hā-ā-retz): The scrolls highlight the "Vanishing Root of the Faithful." "Righteousness" is "Uprooted." The "Hasidh" (*Godly*) is "Lost" (*Abhadh*). It signifies "The Aesthetic of the Moral Vacuum."

2 I Watch in Hope (atz-ap-peh): The scrolls highlight the "Vigilant Root of the Prophet." "Despair" is "Uprooted." Micah is "Rooted" in "Watching" (*Tzaphah*). It signify "The Persistence of Faith in the Dark."

3 Though I have Fallen (kī nā-phal-tī qām-tī): The scrolls highlight the "Resilient Root of the Rising." "Defeat" is "Uprooted." The "Fall" (*Naphal*) is "Rooted" in the "Rise" (*Qum*). It signifies "The Spiritual Elasticity of the Remnant."

4 Who is a God like you? (mī-ēl ka-mō-khā): A wordplay on Micah’s name (*Mī-khā-yāh* = Who is like Yah?). The scrolls highlight the "Unrivaled Root of the Mercy." "Accusation" is "Uprooted" by "Pardon" (*Nose*). It signify "The Aesthetic of Divine Forgiveness."

5 Depths of the Sea (bim-tzū-lōth yām): The scrolls conclude with this "Abyssal Root of the Forgetting." "Guilt" is "Uprooted" and "Buried" in the "Depths" (*Metzuloth*). It marks the end of *Micah* and the transition to *Nahum 1*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Sea." Your sins are gone.

Judges 5

1On that day Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang this song:
“When the princes in Israel take the lead,
when the people willingly offer themselves—
praise the LORD!”
“Hear this, you kings! Listen, you rulers!
I, even I, will sing to the LORD;
I will praise the LORD, the God of Israel, in song.”
“When you, LORD, went out from Seir,
when you marched from the land of Edom,
the earth shook, the heavens poured,
the clouds poured down water.” 1
“Villagers in Israel would not fight;
they held back until I, Deborah, arose,
until I arose, a mother in Israel.”
“Wake up, wake up, Deborah!
Wake up, wake up, break out in song!
Arise, Barak!
Take captive your captives, son of Abinoam.”
“In the districts of Reuben
there was much searching of heart.”
“Why did you stay among the sheepfolds
to hear the whistling for the flocks?” 2
“From the heavens the stars fought,
from their courses they fought against Sisera.” 3
“The river Kishon swept them away,
the age-old river, the river Kishon.
March on, my soul; be strong!”
“‘Cursed be Meroz,’ said the angel of the LORD.
‘Cursed be its people bitterly,
because they did not come to help the LORD,
to help the LORD against the mighty.’” 4
“Most blessed of women be Jael,
the wife of Heber the Kenite,
most blessed of tent-dwelling women.”
“Through the window peered Sisera’s mother;
behind the lattice she cried out,
‘Why is his chariot so long in coming?
Why is the clatter of his chariots delayed?’”
“So may all your enemies perish, LORD!
But may all who love you be like the sun
when it rises in its strength.”
Then the land had rest forty years.

Translation Notes

1 Earth Shook / Clouds Poured: In the scrolls, this poetic description explains the military victory. The iron chariots of Sisera were his greatest strength, but a sudden, divinely-ordered storm turned the Kishon valley into a swamp. The heavy iron chariots became stuck in the mud, turning a "Technological Advantage" into a "Fatal Liability." God used the weather to neutralize the enemy's hardware.

2 Searching of Heart / Sheepfolds: Deborah’s song is a "Political Critique" in the scrolls. She exposes the tribes that chose "Economic Stability" and "Comfort" over national duty. Reuben "searched their hearts" (debated the issue) but ultimately decided that their sheep were more important than their brothers' survival. It teaches that "Passive Deliberation" is often a cover for "Active Cowardice."

3 The Stars Fought: This is a powerful ancient Near Eastern image in the scrolls. It implies that the entire "Cosmic Order" was aligned against Sisera. He wasn't just fighting an army of 10,000 peasants; he was fighting the Creator of the Universe. When we are aligned with God's purposes, the "stars" are on our side.

4 Curse of Meroz: Meroz was a city that lay directly in the path of the retreating Sisera. They had the perfect opportunity to capture him, but they did nothing. In the scrolls, this is the only city explicitly cursed by the "Angel of the LORD." It teaches that "Neutrality" in the face of evil is a spiritual crime. To do nothing when you have the power to help is to be "Bitterly Cursed."

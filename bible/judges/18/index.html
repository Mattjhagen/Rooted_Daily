Judges 18

1In those days Israel had no king. And in those days the tribe of the Danites was seeking a place of their own to settle, because they had not yet come into an inheritance among the tribes of Israel. 1
2So the Danites sent five warriors from Zorah and Eshtaol to spy out the land and explore it.
3While they were near Micah’s house, they recognized the voice of the young Levite; they turned aside and asked him, “Who brought you here? What are you doing in this place?”
4He told them what Micah had done for him, and said, “He has hired me and I am his priest.”
14Then the five men who had spied out the land... said to their fellow Danites, “Do you know that one of these houses has an ephod, some household gods and an image overlaid with silver? Now you know what to do.”
17The five men who had spied out the land went inside and took the idol, the ephod and the household gods...
18The priest asked them, “What are you doing?”
19They answered him, “Be quiet! Don’t say a word. Come with us, and be our father and priest. Isn’t it better that you serve a tribe and a clan in Israel than just one man’s household?” 2
20The priest was very pleased. He took the ephod, the household gods and the idol and went along with the people.
24Micah replied, “You’ve taken the gods I made, and my priest, and gone away. What else do I have? How can you ask, ‘What’s the matter with you?’” 3
25The Danites answered, “Don’t shout at us, or some of our reckless men will attack you, and you and your family will lose your lives.”
26So the Danites went their way, and Micah, seeing that they were too strong for him, turned around and went back home.
27Then they took what Micah had made, and his priest, and went on to Laish, against a people at peace and secure. They attacked them with the sword and burned down their city.
30There the Danites set up for themselves the idol, and Jonathan son of Gershom, the son of Moses, and his sons were priests for the tribe of Dan until the time of the captivity of the land. 4

Translation Notes

1 Dan seeking a place: In the scrolls, this migration is a sign of "Spiritual Failure." Dan had been allotted land in the south (Joshua 19), but because they could not drive out the Philistines, they chose to look for an "Easier Target" in the north. They chose a peaceful, unsuspecting people (Laish) to conquer rather than the enemies God had assigned them.

2 Isn’t it better: This is the logic of "Pragmatic Religion" in the scrolls. The Levite was offered a "Promotion." He didn't care about the morality of the theft or the idolatry; he only cared about the "Size of the Congregation" and his "Career Path." When religious leaders prioritize "Scale" over "Truth," they become the servants of whatever group is strongest.

3 What else do I have: Micah’s lament in the scrolls is pathetic. He had invested all his spiritual hope in "Gods he made." When those objects were stolen, he was left with nothing. It is a powerful warning against "Man-Made Religion"—it offers no security and can be taken away by anyone with more physical force.

4 Jonathan son of Gershom, the son of Moses: This is a shocking revelation in the scrolls. (Some manuscripts try to hide this by changing "Moses" to "Manasseh"). The young Levite was the actual grandson of Moses! In just two generations, the family of the great Law-Giver had become the founders of an idolatrous tribal shrine. It shows how quickly "Legacy" can be lost if it is not personally owned.

Judges 21

1The men of Israel had taken an oath at Mizpah: “Not one of us will give his daughter in marriage to a Benjamite.”
2The people went to Bethel, where they sat before God until evening, raising their voices and weeping bitterly.
3“LORD, God of Israel,” they cried, “why has this happened to Israel? Why should one tribe be missing from Israel today?” 1

Solution 1: Jabesh Gilead

8They asked, “Which one of the tribes of Israel failed to assemble before the LORD at Mizpah?” They discovered that no one from Jabesh Gilead had come...
10So the assembly sent twelve thousand fighting men... “Go and put those living in Jabesh Gilead to the sword... save only the young virgins.”
12They found among the people living in Jabesh Gilead four hundred young women who had never slept with a man, and they took them to the camp at Shiloh.
14So the Benjamites returned at that time and were given the women of Jabesh Gilead. But there were not enough for all of them. 2

Solution 2: The Dancers of Shiloh

19But they said, “Look, there is the annual festival of the LORD in Shiloh...”
20So they instructed the Benjamites, “Go and hide in the vineyards”
21“and watch. When the young women of Shiloh come out to join in the dancing, rush out from the vineyards and each of you seize one of them to be your wife. Then go back to the land of Benjamin.” 3
23The Benjamites did as they were instructed. Each man carried off one of the women who were dancing. They returned to their inheritance and rebuilt the towns and settled in them.
24At that time the Israelites left that place and went home to their tribes and clans, each to his own inheritance.
In those days Israel had no king;
everyone did as they saw fit. 4

Translation Notes

1 Why has this happened: In the scrolls, this is a classic example of "Moral Blindness." The Israelites were weeping over the consequences of a war *they themselves* had waged with excessive violence. They were blaming God ("Why has this happened?") for the results of their own uncontrolled rage and foolish oaths.

2 The Jabesh Gilead Massacre: This is one of the most morally bankrupt acts in the scrolls. To solve the problem of a missing tribe, they committed *another* mass slaughter of their own people (the inhabitants of Jabesh Gilead) to "create" virgins for the Benjamites. It shows that in a world without objective Law, "Solutions" become as wicked as the "Problems" they solve.

3 Seize one of them: This "Abduction of the Dancers" in the scrolls is the final low point of the book. The elders of Israel actually *encouraged* kidnapping as a way to bypass their own foolish oath. They were treating women like "Commodities" to be distributed to keep the tribal system running. The book ends not with a victory, but with a "Social Patch" made of trauma and deceit.

4 The Final Refrain: This is the ultimate "Epitaph" for the period of the Judges in the scrolls. It explains *why* the book is so dark. Without a King (both a human leader and the recognition of God as King), society had dissolved into "Radical Subjectivity." When every man is his own God, the result is the chaos, violence, and tragedy witnessed in these final chapters. The book is a "Crying Need" for the King who was to come.

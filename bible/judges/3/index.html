Judges 3

1These are the nations the LORD left to test all those Israelites who had not experienced any of the wars in Canaan
2(he did this only to teach warfare to the descendants of the Israelites who had not had previous battle experience).
5The Israelites lived among the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.
6They took their daughters in marriage and gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods.

Othniel: The Spirit-Empowered

7The Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD... and served the Baals and the Asherahs.
8The anger of the LORD burned against Israel so that he sold them into the hands of Cushan-Rishathaim king of Aram Naharaim, to whom the Israelites were subject for eight years.
9But when they cried out to the LORD, he raised up for them a deliverer, Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, who saved them.
10The Spirit of the LORD came on him, so that he became Israel’s judge and went to war. The LORD gave Cushan-Rishathaim king of Aram into the hands of Othniel. 1

Ehud: The Left-Handed Deliverer

12Again the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD, and because they did this, the LORD gave Eglon king of Moab power over Israel.
14The Israelites were subject to Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.
15Again the Israelites cried out to the LORD, and he gave them a deliverer—Ehud, a left-handed man, the son of Gera the Benjamite. 2
16Now Ehud had made a double-edged sword about a cubit long, which he strapped to his right thigh under his clothing.
20Ehud then approached Eglon... and said, “I have a message from God for you.” As the king rose from his seat,
21Ehud reached with his left hand, drew the sword from his right thigh and plunged it into the king’s belly. 3
22Even the handle sank in after the blade, and its fat closed over it, for Ehud did not pull the sword out.
26While they waited, Ehud got away... and escaped to Seirah.
27When he arrived there, he blew a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went down with him from the hills, with him leading them.
30That day Moab was made subject to Israel, and the land had rest for eighty years.

Shamgar: The Humble Savior

31After Ehud came Shamgar son of Anath, who struck down six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad. He too saved Israel. 4

Translation Notes

1 Spirit of the LORD (Ruach YHWH): This is the first mention of the Spirit empowering a "Judge" in the scrolls. It marks the beginning of "Charismatic Leadership." Othniel did not save Israel through political ingenuity, but through a supernatural endowment of power. His victory lasted for forty years—a full generation of peace.

2 Left-handed (Ish Iter Yad Yemino): Literally in the scrolls, "a man restricted in his right hand." In the ancient world, being left-handed was often seen as a deformity or a disadvantage. Ehud used this "disadvantage" to his benefit; the king’s guards only searched the left thigh (where a right-handed man would keep a sword). Ehud’s story teaches that God uses our "uniqueness" or "differences" as strategic assets.

3 Plunged it into the belly: The scrolls do not shy away from the graphic nature of this assassination. It is presented as a "Judgment on Gluttony." Eglon (whose name is related to the word for a "fattened calf") had grown rich and fat off the tribute of oppressed Israelites. His death was a literal and symbolic "gutting" of the Moabite occupation.

4 Oxgoad: This was a simple agricultural tool—a long stick with a metal point used to drive oxen. Shamgar was likely a farmer, not a soldier. In the scrolls, his one-verse story emphasizes that God does not need "Military Grade" weapons to save His people. A single faithful man with a farm tool can defeat an army of 600. It is the power of "Available Instruments."

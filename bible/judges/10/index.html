Judges 10

1After the time of Abimelech, a man of Issachar named Tola son of Puah, the son of Dodo, rose to save Israel. He lived in Shamir, in the hill country of Ephraim.
2He led Israel twenty-three years; then he died and was buried in Shamir.
3He was followed by Jair of Gilead, who led Israel twenty-two years.
4He had thirty sons, who rode thirty donkeys. They controlled thirty towns in Gilead, which to this day are called Havvoth Jair. 1
6Again the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD. They served the Baals and the Ashtoreths, and the gods of Aram, Sidon, Moab, Ammon and the Philistines. Because the Israelites forsook the LORD and no longer served him,
7he became angry with them. He sold them into the hands of the Philistines and the Ammonites,
8who shattered and crushed them that year. For eighteen years they oppressed all the Israelites on the east side of the Jordan...
10Then the Israelites cried out to the LORD, “We have sinned against you, forsaking our God and serving the Baals.”
13“But you have forsaken me,” the LORD replied, “and served other gods, so I will no longer save you.”
14“Go and cry out to the gods you have chosen. Let them save you when you are in trouble!” 2
15But the Israelites said to the LORD, “We have sinned. Do with us whatever you think best, but please rescue us now.”
16Then they got rid of the foreign gods among them and served the LORD. And he could bear Israel’s misery no longer. 3
17When the Ammonites were called to arms and camped in Gilead, the Israelites assembled and camped at Mizpah.
18The leaders of the people of Gilead said to each other, “Whoever will take the lead in attacking the Ammonites will be head over all who live in Gilead.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Thirty Sons on Thirty Donkeys: In the scrolls, this is a picture of "Regional Stability" and "Wealth." Donkeys were the transport of the nobility. Jair had established a family dynasty across Gilead. It shows that between the major crises, there were periods of local prosperity that were not recorded in detail because they lacked the "Drama of Deliverance."

2 Cry out to the gods you have chosen: This is a powerful moment of "Divine Sarcasm" in the scrolls. God was forcing the people to realize the futility of their idols. If they wanted the "Culture of the Nations," they had to deal with the "Silence of the Nations' Gods" when the crisis came. It was a call to "Intellectual Honesty."

3 He could bear Israel’s misery no longer (Tiktzar Nafsho): Literally in the scrolls, "His soul became short." This is a profound anthropopathism (attributing human emotion to God). It shows that God’s "Compassion" eventually overrides His "Judgment" when He sees sincere repentance and actual suffering. Even though they deserved the oppression, God’s heart was moved by their pain.

4 The Search for a Head: The chapter ends with a "Crisis of Leadership." After the death of Jair, there was no one left to lead. The people were looking for a "Commander" (*Katzin*) who could fight. They were ready to trade "Political Sovereignty" for "Military Security." This sets the stage for the rise of Jephthah.

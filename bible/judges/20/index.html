Judges 20

1Then all the Israelites from Dan to Beersheba and from the land of Gilead came together as one man and assembled before the LORD in Mizpah. 1
2The leaders of all the people of the tribes of Israel took their places in the assembly... four hundred thousand sword-wielding infantry.
13“Now surrender those wicked men of Gibeah, so that we may put them to death and purge the evil from Israel.” But the Benjamites would not listen to their fellow Israelites. 2
14From their towns they came together to Gibeah to fight against the Israelites.
16Among all these soldiers there were seven hundred select troops who were left-handed, each of whom could sling a stone at a hair and not miss. 3

The Two Days of Defeat

Day 1: “The Benjamites came out of Gibeah and cut down twenty-two thousand Israelites on the battlefield that day.”
Day 2: “The Benjamites came out... and cut down another eighteen thousand Israelites, all of them armed with swords.”
26Then all the Israelites, the whole army, went up to Bethel, and there they sat weeping before the LORD. They fasted that day until evening and presented burnt offerings... 4
28“Shall we go up again to fight with our brother Benjamin, or shall we stop?” The LORD responded, “Go, for tomorrow I will give them into your hands.”

The Ambush and the Fall of Benjamin

29Then Israel set an ambush around Gibeah.
37The men in ambush rushed suddenly into Gibeah; they spread out and put the whole city to the sword.
38The Israelites had arranged a signal... a great cloud of smoke rising from the city.
44Eighteen thousand Benjamites fell, all of them valiant fighters.
46Twenty-five thousand Benjamite swordsmen fell that day...
47But six hundred men turned and fled into the wilderness to the rock of Rimmon, where they stayed four months. 5
48The men of Israel went back to Benjamin and put all the towns to the sword, including the animals and everything else they found. And they burned down every town they came to.

Translation Notes

1 As one man: In the scrolls, this is the only time in the entire book of Judges where the nation is truly unified. Ironically, they unify not to fight the Philistines or the Midianites, but to destroy one of their own tribes. It is a "Tragic Unity" born of moral outrage.

2 The Refusal to Surrender: The Benjamites chose "Tribal Loyalty" over "Covenant Justice." By protecting the "Wicked Men of Gibeah," they made themselves accomplices to the crime. In the scrolls, this is the ultimate breakdown of the social order; the law of the tribe had become more important than the Law of God.

3 Left-handed Slingers: Benjamin (which means "Son of my Right Hand") was famous in the scrolls for its left-handed warriors (like Ehud in Chapter 3). This was a specialized elite force. Their precision was legendary. It shows that Benjamin was a "Military Powerhouse," which explains why they were able to defeat the much larger army of the rest of Israel for two days.

4 Fasted and Wept: After the first two defeats, the Israelites finally realized that they could not win by "Outrage" alone. They had to seek God’s "Authorization." In the scrolls, the first two days were a judgment on the *entire* nation’s pride, while the third day was the judgment on *Benjamin’s* sin. Both sides had to be humbled.

5 Six Hundred Men: The chapter ends with the tribe of Benjamin reduced to just 600 survivors. In the scrolls, this is presented as a "National Catastrophe." Israel had nearly "amputated" one of its own limbs. The zeal for justice had turned into a "Total War" that threatened the very existence of the family of Jacob.

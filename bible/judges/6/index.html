Judges 6

1The Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD, and for seven years he gave them into the hands of the Midianites.
4They encamped on the land and ruined the crops... they left no spare living thing for Israel, neither sheep nor cattle nor donkeys.
5They came up like swarms of locusts... it was impossible to count them or their camels.
6Midian so impoverished the Israelites that they cried out to the LORD for help.
11The angel of the LORD came and sat down under the oak in Ophrah... where Gideon son of Joash was threshing wheat in a winepress to keep it from the Midianites. 1
12When the angel of the LORD appeared to Gideon, he said, “The LORD is with you, mighty warrior.”
13“Pardon me, my lord,” Gideon replied, “but if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all his wonders that our ancestors told us about...?” 2
14The LORD turned to him and said, “Go in the strength you have and save Israel out of Midian’s hand. Am I not sending you?”
15“Pardon me, my lord,” Gideon replied, “but how can I save Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my family.”
16The LORD answered, “I will be with you, and you will strike down all the Midianites, leaving none alive.”
25That same night the LORD said to him, “Take the second bull from your father’s herd... Tear down your father’s altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah pole beside it.” 3
27So Gideon took ten of his servants and did as the LORD told him. But because he was afraid of his family and the townspeople, he did it at night rather than in the daytime.
32So because Gideon broke down Baal’s altar, they gave him the name Jerub-Baal that day, saying, “Let Baal contend with him.”

The Signs of the Fleece

37“I will place a wool fleece on the threshing floor. If there is dew only on the fleece and all the ground is dry, then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand.”
38And that is what happened. Gideon rose early the next day; he squeezed the fleece and wrung out the dew—a bowlful of water.
39Then Gideon said, “Do not be angry with me. Let me make just one more request... let the fleece be dry and the ground be covered with dew.” 4
40That night God did so. Only the fleece was dry; all the ground was covered with dew.

Translation Notes

1 Wheat in a Winepress: This detail in the scrolls highlights the "Extreme Poverty" and "Fear" of the nation. Wheat is normally threshed on a hill to catch the wind; a winepress is a hole in the ground. Gideon was trying to be "Invisible" to the enemy. It makes the greeting "Mighty Warrior" in the next verse a piece of "Prophetic Irony." God sees what Gideon *will be*, not just what he is.

2 If the LORD is with us: Gideon’s protest in the scrolls reflects the "Spiritual Cynicism" of his generation. They had heard the stories of the Exodus, but their current reality of starvation and hiding made those stories seem like myths. Gideon was demanding "Empirical Proof" for the Presence of God. It teaches that even great faith often begins with deep questions.

3 Tear down the Altar: Before Gideon could fight the Midianites (the external enemy), he had to deal with the "Idolatry in his own Home" (the internal enemy). In the scrolls, spiritual liberation always begins at the "Family Altar." Gideon had to choose between his father's approval and God's assignment.

4 The Fleece (Gizzat ha-Tsemer): In the scrolls, this is not presented as "Ideal Faith," but as "Patient Grace." Gideon was still wrestling with his insecurities. God met him in his weakness, providing the signs he asked for to stabilize his trembling heart. The signs represent "Sovereignty over Nature"—the same dew that God controls would soon be the same battle God would control.

Judges 15

1Later on, at the time of wheat harvest, Samson took a young goat and went to visit his wife.
2Her father said, “I was so sure you hated her that I gave her to your companion.”
4So Samson went out and caught three hundred foxes and tied them tail to tail in pairs. He then fastened a torch to every pair of tails,
5lit the torches and let the foxes loose in the standing grain of the Philistines. He burned up the shocks and standing grain, together with the vineyards and olive groves. 1
11Then three thousand men from Judah went down to the cave in the Rock of Etam and said to Samson, “Don’t you know that the Philistines are rulers over us? What have you done to us?” 2
12They said to him, “We’ve come to bind you and hand you over to the Philistines.” Samson said, “Swear to me that you won’t kill me yourselves.”
13“Agreed,” they answered. “We will only bind you and hand you over to them. We will not kill you.” So they bound him with two new ropes and led him up from the rock.
14As he approached Lehi, the Philistines came toward him shouting. The Spirit of the LORD came powerfully upon him. The ropes on his arms became like charred flax, and the bindings dropped from his hands.
15Finding a fresh jawbone of a donkey, he grabbed it and struck down a thousand men. 3
“With a donkey’s jawbone
I have made donkeys of them.
With a donkey’s jawbone
I have killed a thousand men.”
18Because he was very thirsty, he cried out to the LORD, “You have given your servant this great victory. Must I now die of thirst and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised?”
19Then God opened up the hollow place in Lehi, and water came out of it. When Samson drank, his strength returned and he revived. So the spring was called En Hakkore, which is still there in Lehi. 4
20Samson led Israel for twenty years in the days of the Philistines.

Translation Notes

1 The Foxes: In the scrolls, this is "Asymmetric Economic Warfare." By destroying the food supply (grain, grapes, olives), Samson was hitting the Philistines in their most vulnerable spot. It shows his creative, lone-wolf approach to deliverance. He didn't have an army, so he used the animal kingdom.

2 Betrayal of Judah: This is a low point in the scrolls. 3,000 men of Judah (Samson’s own kinsmen) were so cowed by the Philistines that they preferred to arrest their own savior rather than fight their oppressors. Their question, "Don't you know the Philistines are rulers over us?" shows a "Slave Mentality" that had completely forgotten the promises of God. Samson’s willingness to be bound by them shows his restraint and his focus on the real enemy.

3 Jawbone of a Donkey: The scrolls specify it was a "fresh" jawbone, which would be heavier and less brittle than a dry one. In the scrolls, this is another instance of Samson violating his Nazirite vow by touching a dead animal carcass. He was continually compromising his spiritual discipline to achieve his physical victories. However, the Spirit still empowered him to fulfill the purpose of delivering Israel.

4 En Hakkore (Spring of the Caller): This is one of the few times Samson actually prays in the scrolls. He realizes that despite his great strength, he is still dependent on God for a drink of water. God’s response—opening the rock—is a reminder of the miracles in the wilderness with Moses. It teaches that God provides for the "Individual Warrior" just as He did for the "Exodus Nation."

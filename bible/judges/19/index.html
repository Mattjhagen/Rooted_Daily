Judges 19

1In those days Israel had no king. Now a Levite who lived in a remote area in the hill country of Ephraim took a concubine from Bethlehem in Judah. 1
15They turned aside there to spend the night in Gibeah. They went and sat in the city square, but no one took them into their home for the night. 2
16That evening an old man from the hill country of Ephraim... came in from his work in the fields.
20“You are welcome at my house,” the old man said. “Let me take care of all your needs. Only don’t spend the night in the square.”
22While they were enjoying themselves, some of the wicked men of the city surrounded the house. Pounding on the door, they shouted to the old man, “Bring out the man who came to your house so we can have sex with him.” 3
25But the men would not listen to him. So the man took his concubine and sent her outside to them, and they raped her and abused her throughout the night, and at dawn they let her go.
26At daybreak the woman went back and collapsed at the entrance to the house... and there she lay until it was light.
27When her master got up in the morning and opened the door to the house... there lay his concubine, fallen in the doorway of the house, with her hands on the threshold.
28He said to her, “Get up; let’s go.” But there was no answer. Then the man put her on his donkey and set out for home.
29When he reached home, he took a knife and cut up his concubine, limb by limb, into twelve parts and sent them into all the areas of Israel. 4
30Everyone who saw it was saying, “Such a thing has never been seen or done, not since the day the Israelites came up out of Egypt. Just imagine! We must do something! So speak up!” 5

Translation Notes

1 The Concubine: In the scrolls, the relationship between the Levite and his concubine is strained from the start. She was "unfaithful" (or in some readings, simply "angry") and left him. The Levite’s pursuit of her and the father’s over-the-top hospitality serve as a "Domestic Prelude" to the national disaster. It shows a world where people are focused on "Eating and Drinking" while the social fabric is rotting.

2 No one took them in: This is a major "Red Flag" in the scrolls. Hospitality was the highest social virtue in the ancient Near East. The fact that a traveler and a Levite could sit in the town square of a "Brother Tribe" (Benjamin) and be ignored shows that the community had lost its "Covenant Identity." They were no longer "Their Brother's Keeper."

3 The Men of Gibeah: In the scrolls, this scene is a deliberate "Mirror Image" of the story of Sodom (Genesis 19). The point is clear: Israel had become what God had previously destroyed. The "Chosen People" were now acting exactly like the most wicked of the Canaanites. The "Gibeah Outrage" is the final evidence of the total failure of the period of the Judges.

4 Twelve Parts: This gruesome act in the scrolls was a "Shock Message." By sending a piece of the body to every tribe, the Levite was forcing the entire nation to witness the "Brokenness" of their society. It was a literal representation of the "Dismemberment of the Covenant." The body of the woman became a "Graphic Summons" to war.

5 Never seen or done: The reaction of the nation in the scrolls shows that despite their own failures, they still had a "Moral Compass" that could be shocked. This event marks the first time in the book where the "Entire Nation" (all twelve tribes) acts as a single unit. Tragically, it is to fight a civil war against one of their own.

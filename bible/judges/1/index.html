Judges 1

1After the death of Joshua, the Israelites asked the LORD, “Who of us is to go up first to fight against the Canaanites?”
2The LORD answered, “Judah shall go; I have given the land into their hands.” 1
3The men of Judah then said to the Simeonites their fellow Israelites, “Come up with us into the territory allotted to us, to fight against the Canaanites, and we in turn will go with you into yours.” So the Simeonites went with them.
4When Judah attacked, the LORD gave the Canaanites and Perizzites into their hands, and they struck down ten thousand men at Bezek.
8The men of Judah attacked Jerusalem also and took it. They put the city to the sword and set it on fire.
10They advanced against the Canaanites living in Hebron (formerly called Kiriath Arba) and defeated Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai.
12And Caleb said, “I will give my daughter Acsah in marriage to the man who attacks and captures Kiriath Sepher.”
13Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took it; so Caleb gave him his daughter Acsah in marriage.
19The LORD was with the men of Judah. They took possession of the hill country, but they were unable to drive the people from the plains, because they had chariots fitted with iron. 2

The Record of Incomplete Obedience

v.21: The Benjamites failed to dislodge the Jebusites from Jerusalem.

v.27: Manasseh did not drive out the people of Beth Shan, Taanach, or Megiddo.

v.29: Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites living in Gezer.

v.30: Zebulun did not drive out the Canaanites living in Kitron or Nahalol.

v.31: Asher did not drive out the people of Akko or Sidon.

v.33: Naphtali did not drive out those living in Beth Shemesh.

v.34: The Amorites confined the Danites to the hill country. 3

28When Israel became strong, they pressed the Canaanites into forced labor but never drove them out completely.

Translation Notes

1 Judah shall go: In the scrolls, the choice of Judah to lead the way marks their ascendancy among the tribes. This is the fulfillment of Jacob's blessing in Genesis 49, where he called Judah a "Lion's cub" and said the scepter would not depart from him. The book begins with leadership but quickly descends into chaos.

2 Chariots of Iron: This is a recurring theme in the scrolls. From a human perspective, the iron chariots were the superior military technology that Israel could not match. However, the scrolls elsewhere show that iron chariots were no match for God (Joshua 11, Judges 4). The failure of Judah in the plains was a "Failure of Nerve" and faith, not a lack of physical power.

3 The Incomplete Conquest: This long list of "Did Not" in the scrolls serves as the preface to the entire book. It explains why the following chapters are filled with trouble. By leaving the Canaanites in the land, the Israelites were choosing to live in a state of "Constant Compromise." They preferred the "Profit of Forced Labor" over the "Purity of the Inheritance."

Judges 7

1Early in the morning, Jerub-Baal (that is, Gideon) and all his men camped at the spring of Harod.
2The LORD said to Gideon, “You have too many men. I cannot deliver Midian into their hands, or Israel would boast against me, ‘My own strength has saved me.’” 1
3“Now announce to the army, ‘Anyone who trembles with fear may turn back and leave Mount Gilead.’” So twenty-two thousand men left, while ten thousand remained.
4But the LORD said to Gideon, “There are still too many men. Take them down to the water, and I will thin them out for you there.”
5So Gideon took the men down to the water. There the LORD told him, “Separate those who lap the water with their tongues as a dog laps from those who kneel down to drink.”
6Three hundred of them drank from cupped hands, lapping like dogs. All the rest got down on their knees to drink. 2
7The LORD said to Gideon, “With the three hundred men that lapped I will save you and give the Midianites into your hands. Let all the others go home.”
13Gideon arrived just as a man was telling a friend his dream. “I had a dream,” he was saying. “A round loaf of barley bread came tumbling into the Midianite camp. It struck the tent with such force that the tent overturned and collapsed.” 3
14His friend responded, “This can be nothing other than the sword of Gideon son of Joash... God has given the Midianites and the whole camp into his hands.”

The Strategy of Jars and Torches

16Dividing the three hundred men into three companies, he placed trumpets and empty jars in the hands of all of them, with torches inside.
19Gideon and the hundred men with him reached the edge of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch...
20The three companies blew the trumpets and smashed the jars. Grasping the torches in their left hands and holding in their right hands the trumpets they were to blow, they shouted, “A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!” 4
21While each man held his position around the camp, all the Midianites ran, crying out as they fled.
22When the three hundred trumpets sounded, the LORD caused the men throughout the camp to turn on each other with their swords.

Translation Notes

1 Boast against me: This is the central theological principle of the scrolls in this chapter. God was more concerned with the "Spiritual Pride" of Israel than the "Military Threat" of Midian. If they won with 32,000 men, they would believe in their own power. By reducing them to 300, God removed every possibility of self-congratulation. The battle was designed to be a "Demonstration of Divine Sufficiency."

2 Lapping like a Dog: In the scrolls, this test was about "Military Readiness." Those who knelt down and put their faces in the water were blind to their surroundings. Those who lapped from their hands kept their eyes up and their focus on the horizon. God chose the "Alert Minority" over the "Vulnerable Majority." It teaches that even in a miracle, God values "Watchfulness."

3 Loaf of Barley Bread: Barley was the "Poor Man's Grain." Gideon’s army of peasants was represented as a humble loaf of bread rolling against the sophisticated "Tents" of the nomadic Midianite invaders. It is a image of the "Weak Overcoming the Strong." Even the enemy’s own subconscious was being used by God to demoralize them.

4 Jars and Torches: This was "Psychological Warfare" at its finest. By smashing the jars simultaneously at night, Gideon created the illusion of a massive army of thousands, each leading a company. The Midianites, awakened from sleep by a deafening crash and a ring of fire, panicked. In the scrolls, "Light and Sound" are the weapons that expose the "Darkness and Confusion" of the enemy.

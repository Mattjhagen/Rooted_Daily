Judges 2

1The angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, “I brought you up out of Egypt and led you into the land I swore to give to your ancestors. I said, ‘I will never break my covenant with you,’” 1
2“‘and you shall not make a covenant with the people of this land, but you shall break down their altars.’ Yet you have disobeyed me. Why have you done this?”
3“‘Now therefore I tell you that I will not drive them out before you; they will become traps for you, and their gods will be a snare to you.’” 2
4When the angel of the LORD had spoken these things to all the Israelites, the people wept aloud.
5They called that place Bochim and offered sacrifices there to the LORD.
10After that whole generation had been gathered to their ancestors, another generation grew up who knew neither the LORD nor what he had done for Israel. 3
11Then the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD and served the Baals.
12They forsook the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who had brought them out of Egypt. They followed and worshiped various gods of the peoples around them. They aroused the LORD’s anger
13because they forsook him and served Baal and the Ashtoreths.

The Cycle of the Judges

1. Rebellion: “The LORD gave them into the hands of raiders who plundered them.”
2. Distress: “Whenever Israel went out to fight, the hand of the LORD was against them... they were in great distress.”
3. Deliverance: “Then the LORD raised up judges, who saved them out of the hands of these raiders.” 4
4. Relapse: “But when the judge died, the people returned to ways even more corrupt than those of their ancestors.”
20Therefore the LORD was very angry with Israel and said, “Because this nation has violated the covenant I ordained for their ancestors and has not listened to me,”
21“I will no longer drive out before them any of the nations Joshua left when he died.”
22“I will use them to test Israel and see whether they will keep the way of the LORD and walk in it as their ancestors did.”
23The LORD had allowed those nations to remain; he did not drive them out at once by giving them into the hands of Joshua. 5

Translation Notes

1 The Angel of the LORD: In the scrolls, this is a unique figure who speaks as God Himself. He moves from "Gilgal" (the site of the first covenant renewal in the land) to "Bochim." This movement symbolizes the shift from "Miraculous Success" to "Spiritual Sorrow." The Angel confronts them with their own broken promises.

2 Bochim (The Weepers): The people wept, but the scrolls suggest their repentance was shallow. They wept because of the *consequences* (the nations would not be driven out) rather than because of the *sin*. Bochim became a symbol of "Emotional Regret" that doesn't lead to "Behavioral Change."

3 The New Generation: This is a chilling note in the scrolls. In just one generation, the entire memory of God’s miracles was lost. It teaches that faith is not inherited; it must be taught and experienced by each new group. When the "Elders" who had seen the Jordan crossing died, the "Identity" of the nation collapsed.

4 Judges (Shoftim): In the scrolls, these were not "courtroom judges," but "Charismatic Deliverers." They were leaders empowered by the Spirit of God to provide military and social relief. However, their influence was usually temporary and local.

5 To Test Israel: The scrolls present a "Theology of Difficulty." God allowed the Canaanites to remain not just as a punishment, but as a "Testing Ground." He wanted to see if Israel would choose Him when the alternative was right next door. Every struggle was an opportunity to reaffirm the covenant.

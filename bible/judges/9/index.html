Judges 9

1Abimelech son of Jerub-Baal went to his mother’s brothers in Shechem...
4They gave him seventy shekels of silver from the temple of Baal-Berith, and Abimelech used it to hire reckless scoundrels, who became his followers.
5He went to his father’s home in Ophrah and on one stone murdered his seventy brothers, the sons of Jerub-Baal. But Jotham, the youngest son of Jerub-Baal, escaped by hiding. 1
6Then all the citizens of Shechem and Beth Millo gathered beside the great tree at the pillar in Shechem to crown Abimelech king.

Jotham’s Parable of the Trees

“One day the trees went out to anoint a king for themselves. They said to the olive tree, ‘Be our king.’”
“But the olive tree answered, ‘Should I give up my oil, by which both gods and humans are honored, to hold sway over the trees?’”
“Next, the trees said to the fig tree, ‘Come and be our king.’”
“But the fig tree replied, ‘Should I give up my fruit, so good and sweet, to hold sway over the trees?’”
“Finally all the trees said to the thornbush, ‘Come and be our king.’” 2
“The thornbush said to the trees, ‘If you really want to anoint me king over you, come and take refuge in my shade; but if not, let fire come out from the thornbush and consume the cedars of Lebanon!’”
23God stirred up animosity between Abimelech and the citizens of Shechem... 3
45All that day Abimelech pressed his attack against the city... he destroyed the city and scattered salt over it.
50Next Abimelech went to Thebez and besieged it and captured it.
53A woman dropped an upper millstone on his head and cracked his skull. 4
54Abimelech called urgently to his young armor-bearer, “Draw your sword and kill me, so that they won’t say, ‘A woman killed him.’” So his servant ran him through, and he died.
56Thus God repaid the wickedness that Abimelech had done to his father by murdering his seventy brothers. 5

Translation Notes

1 Murdered on one stone: In the scrolls, this was a "Ritual Execution" of his brothers. By using a single stone, Abimelech was trying to make his claim to the throne "Absolute" by removing every other possible heir in a single, cold-blooded act. It is the opposite of Gideon’s refusal of kingship.

2 The Thornbush King: Jotham’s parable in the scrolls is a classic of "Political Satire." The productive trees (olive, fig, vine) realize that they are too busy doing good in the world to waste time on the vanity of "Holding Sway." Only the "Thornbush" (Abimelech)—which has no fruit, no shade, and only causes pain—is arrogant enough to want to rule. It warns that when the "Best People" refuse to lead, the "Worst People" will fill the vacuum.

3 God stirred up animosity (Ruach Ra'ah): Literally in the scrolls, God sent an "Evil Spirit." This shows that God uses the internal corruption of wicked people to bring about their own destruction. The alliance between Abimelech and Shechem was based on blood and greed; therefore, it was destined to end in blood and greed.

4 The Millstone: An "Upper Millstone" was a heavy, circular stone used for grinding grain. In the scrolls, this is another instance of a "Domestic Tool" being used as a weapon of justice (like Jael’s tent peg). Abimelech, who had murdered his brothers on a "Stone," was himself killed by a "Stone." It is a poetic "lex talionis" (eye for an eye).

5 God Repaid: This is the summary of the scrolls for this chapter. The story of Abimelech is a "Parenthesis of Evil" in the book of Judges. He was not a judge raised up by God, but a tyrant raised up by his own ambition. His failure proved that Israel was not yet ready for a King, because they did not yet understand that a King must be a Servant of God.

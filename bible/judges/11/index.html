Judges 11

1Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty warrior. His father was Gilead; his mother was a prostitute. 1
2Gilead’s wife also bore him sons, and when they were grown up, they drove Jephthah away. “You are not going to get any inheritance in our family,” they said, “because you are the son of another woman.”
3So Jephthah fled from his brothers and settled in the land of Tob, where a gang of scoundrels gathered around him and followed him.
5When the Ammonites made war on Israel, the elders of Gilead went to get Jephthah from the land of Tob.
6“Come,” they said, “be our commander, so we can fight the Ammonites.”
9Jephthah answered, “Suppose you take me back to fight the Ammonites and the LORD gives them to me—will I really be your head?”
10The elders of Gilead replied, “The LORD is our witness; we will certainly do as you say.”
24“Will you not take what your god Chemosh gives you? Likewise, whatever the LORD our God has given us, we will possess.” 2
29Then the Spirit of the LORD came on Jephthah. He crossed Gilead and Manasseh... to attack the Ammonites.
30And Jephthah made a vow to the LORD: “If you give the Ammonites into my hands,”
31“whatever comes out of the door of my house to meet me when I return in triumph from the Ammonites will be the LORD’s, and I will sacrifice it as a burnt offering.” 3
34When Jephthah returned to his home in Mizpah, who should come out to meet him but his daughter, dancing to the sound of timbrels! She was an only child. Except for her he had neither son nor daughter. 4
35When he saw her, he tore his clothes and cried, “Oh no, my daughter! You have brought me down to misery and are among those who devastate me, for I have made a vow to the LORD that I cannot break.”
36“My father,” she replied, “you have given your word to the LORD. Do to me just as you promised...”
39After the two months, she returned to her father, and he did to her as he had vowed. And she was a virgin. 5

Translation Notes

1 Jephthah the Outcast: In the scrolls, Jephthah’s background as the son of a prostitute made him a social pariah. He was forced into the "Land of Tob" (which means "Good Land," perhaps ironically) where he became a leader of "Empty Men" (*Anashim Rekim*). His story teaches that God can take someone rejected by society and the family and turn them into a "Mighty Warrior" (*Gibbor Chayil*). His "Street Smarts" and toughness were exactly what the nation needed.

2 Jephthah’s Diplomacy: Unlike many of the other judges, Jephthah tried to avoid war through "Legal Argument." In the scrolls, he shows a deep knowledge of Israel’s history. He argued that Israel’s claim to the land was based on "Divine Grant" and "Defensive Victory." His argument to the king of Ammon is a classic piece of ancient international law.

3 The Rash Vow (Neder): This is one of the most debated passages in the scrolls. Jephthah, though empowered by the Spirit, allowed his "Anxiety" to drive him to make a bargain with God. He felt he had to "Buy" the victory with a sacrifice. It teaches that "Spirit-Empowered" people can still make "Human-Folly" decisions. God never asked for this vow; the Spirit was already on him for the victory.

4 Timbrels and Dancing: In the scrolls, it was traditional for the women to come out and welcome returning heroes (like Miriam in Exodus 15). Jephthah’s daughter was doing exactly what was expected of a faithful daughter. The "Irony" of her joy being the cause of her father’s grief is a hallmark of the Book of Judges’ tragic downward spiral.

5 He did as he had vowed: There is significant debate in the scrolls whether Jephthah literally sacrificed his daughter as a "Burnt Offering" (forbidden by the Law) or if he "Dedicated her to the Sanctuary" as a perpetual virgin (meaning his family line would end). The text’s emphasis on her "Virginity" and her "Weeping for her friends" rather than her life suggests the latter, but the "Burnt Offering" language remains a dark shadow over the story. Either way, Jephthah lost his only child because of his mouth.

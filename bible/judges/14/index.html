Judges 14

1Samson went down to Timnah and saw there a young Philistine woman.
2When he returned, he said to his father and mother, “I have seen a Philistine woman in Timnah; now get her for me as my wife.”
3His father and mother replied, “Isn’t there an acceptable woman among your relatives or among all our people? Must you go to the uncircumcised Philistines to get a wife?” But Samson said to his father, “Get her for me. She’s the right one for me.”
4(His parents did not know that this was from the LORD, who was seeking an occasion to confront the Philistines; for at that time the Philistines were ruling over Israel.) 1
5Samson went down to Timnah together with his father and mother. As they approached the vineyards of Timnah, suddenly a young lion came roaring toward him.
6The Spirit of the LORD came powerfully upon him so that he tore the lion apart with his bare hands as he might have torn a young goat. But he told neither his father nor his mother what he had done. 2
8Sometime later, when he went back to marry her, he turned aside to look at the lion’s carcass, and in it he saw a swarm of bees and some honey.
9He scooped out the honey with his hands and ate as he went along. 3

Samson’s Riddle

“Out of the eater, something to eat;
out of the strong, something sweet.” 4
15On the fourth day, they said to Samson’s wife, “Coax your husband into explaining the riddle for us, or we will burn you and your father’s household to death.”
17She cried the whole seven days of the feast. So on the seventh day he finally told her, because she continued to press him. She in turn explained the riddle to her people.
18Before sunset on the seventh day the men of the town said to him,
“What is sweeter than honey?
What is stronger than a lion?”
Samson said to them, “If you had not plowed with my heifer, you would not have solved my riddle.”
19Then the Spirit of the LORD came powerfully upon him. He went down to Ashkelon, struck down thirty of their men, stripped them of everything and gave their clothes to those who had explained the riddle.

Translation Notes

1 Seeking an occasion: In the scrolls, this is a crucial theological note. Samson’s personal impulses (his desire for a Philistine wife) were being "Overruled" by God to create a conflict. The Israelites were content with Philistine rule; God had to use Samson’s "Bad Decisions" to start a fight that would lead to their liberation. It shows God’s "Sovereignty over Human Folly."

2 Tore the Lion Apart: This is the first demonstration of Samson’s "Spirit-Given Strength." It was not his own physical muscle, but a supernatural surge of power. The lion represents the "Bestial Power" of the world that Samson was called to overcome. Interestingly, he kept this victory a secret, even from his parents.

3 Honey in the Carcass: In the scrolls, this was a "Violation of the Nazirite Vow." A Nazirite was forbidden to touch dead bodies. By eating the honey from the lion’s carcass, Samson was choosing his "Physical Appetite" over his "Spiritual Discipline." This is the beginning of his moral decline; he starts to believe his strength is his own to use for his own pleasure.

4 The Riddle: The riddle in the scrolls is more than a game; it is a "Diplomatic Weapon." By winning the bet, Samson would have gained wealth and status at the expense of the Philistines. By losing it through betrayal, it became the "Spark of War." The "Eater" was the lion, and the "Something to eat" was the honey. It is a metaphor for "Sweetness coming from Strength."

Judges 13

1Again the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD, so the LORD delivered them into the hands of the Philistines for forty years. 1
2A certain man of Zorah, named Manoah, from the clan of the Danites, had a wife who was childless, unable to give birth.
3The angel of the LORD appeared to her and said, “You are barren and childless, but you are going to become pregnant and have a son.”
4“Now see to it that you drink no wine or other fermented drink and that you do not eat anything unclean.”
5“You will become pregnant and have a son whose head is never to be touched by a razor because the boy is to be a Nazirite, dedicated to God from the womb. He will take the lead in delivering Israel from the Philistines.” 2
8Then Manoah prayed to the LORD: “Pardon me, my Lord. I beg you to let the man of God you sent to us come again to teach us how to bring up the boy who is to be born.”
17Then Manoah inquired of the angel of the LORD, “What is your name, so that we may honor you when your word comes true?”
18He replied, “Why do you ask my name? It is beyond understanding.” 3
19Then Manoah took a young goat, together with the grain offering, and sacrificed it on a rock to the LORD. And the LORD did an amazing thing while Manoah and his wife watched:
20As the flame blazed up from the altar toward heaven, the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame. Seeing this, Manoah and his wife fell with their faces to the ground. 4
22“We are doomed to die!” Manoah said to his wife. “We have seen God!”
23But his wife answered, “If the LORD had meant to kill us, he would not have accepted a burnt offering... or told us all these things.”
24The woman gave birth to a boy and named him Samson. He grew and the LORD blessed him,
25and the Spirit of the LORD began to stir him while he was in Mahaneh Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.

Translation Notes

1 Forty Years: In the scrolls, this is the longest period of oppression mentioned in the Book of Judges. It is significant because the people do not "Cry out to the LORD" in this chapter. They have become so accustomed to Philistine rule that they have stopped looking for a savior. God initiates the deliverance *without* being asked. It is a story of "Pure Grace."

2 Nazirite (Nazir): The word in the scrolls means "Set Apart" or "Consecrated." Normally, a Nazirite vow was temporary and voluntary (Numbers 6). Samson, however, was a "Lifelong Nazirite" by divine decree. His long hair was to be a visible "Banner" of his consecration. He was meant to be a living demonstration of what Israel as a nation was supposed to be: set apart from the world.

3 Beyond Understanding (Peli): This word in the scrolls is related to the word for "Miracle" or "Wonderful." The Angel’s name is "Wonderful"—the same title given to the Messiah in Isaiah 9:6. It shows that Manoah was speaking with a "Divine Manifestation." The Name was not hidden because it was a secret, but because it was too grand for human language to contain.

4 Ascended in the Flame: In the scrolls, this is a visual confirmation that the sacrifice was accepted. The Angel "hitches a ride" on the prayer/sacrifice of Manoah. It teaches that the way to the Presence of God is always through the "Altar"—through the shedding of blood and the fire of devotion.

Judges 8

1Now the Ephraimites asked Gideon, “Why have you treated us like this? Why didn’t you call us when you went to fight Midian?” And they challenged him vigorously.
2But he answered them, “What have I accomplished compared to you? Aren’t the gleanings of Ephraim’s grapes better than the full grape harvest of Abiezer?” 1
4Gideon and his three hundred men, exhausted yet pursuing, came to the Jordan and crossed it.
5He said to the men of Succoth, “Give my troops some bread; they are worn out, and I am still pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian.”
6But the officials of Succoth said, “Do you already have the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna in your possession? Why should we give bread to your army?” 2
12Zebah and Zalmunna, the two kings of Midian, fled, but he pursued them and captured them, routing their entire army.
22The Israelites said to Gideon, “Rule over us—you, your son and your grandson—because you have saved us from the hand of Midian.”
23But Gideon told them, “I will not rule over you, nor will my son rule over you. The LORD will rule over you.” 3
24And he said, “I do have one request, that each of you give me an earring from your share of the plunder.”
26The weight of the gold rings he asked for came to seventeen hundred shekels...
27Gideon made the gold into an ephod, which he placed in Ophrah, his town. All Israel prostituted themselves by worshiping it there, and it became a snare to Gideon and his family. 4
28Thus Midian was subdued before the Israelites and did not raise its head again. During Gideon’s lifetime, the land had rest forty years.
32Gideon son of Joash died at a good old age and was buried in the tomb of his father Joash in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.
33No sooner had Gideon died than the Israelites again prostituted themselves to the Baals. They set up Baal-Berith as their god. 5

Translation Notes

1 Gleanings of Ephraim: Gideon’s diplomatic answer in the scrolls is a masterpiece of "Soft Power." Instead of fighting with the jealous Ephraimites, he used a metaphor to show that their small contribution (capturing the bridgeheads) was more significant than his entire main battle. It is a lesson in "Humility as a Tool of Unity."

2 Succoth and Peniel: These towns represent "Cynical Neutrality" in the scrolls. They were Israelites, but they refused to help their brothers until they were certain of the victory. They were "Wait-and-See" believers. Gideon’s harsh punishment of them shows that in the biblical view, betraying your brothers in a time of crisis is a capital offense.

3 The Refusal of Kingship: This is one of Gideon’s finest moments in the scrolls. He understood that Israel was a "Theocracy"—a nation where God was the only King. By refusing the crown, he was honoring the unique identity of the nation. However, his subsequent behavior (collecting gold and having many wives) suggests that while he rejected the *title* of king, he began to enjoy the *lifestyle* of one.

4 The Golden Ephod: This is the great tragedy of Gideon’s legacy. An "Ephod" was a priestly garment used to inquire of God. Gideon likely intended it as a memorial to the victory, but it became a "Religious Distraction." The people began to treat the object as a source of power rather than God Himself. It teaches that even a "Memorial of Victory" can become an "Idol of Defeat" if it takes our eyes off the Creator.

5 Baal-Berith: Literally "Lord of the Covenant." The Israelites didn't just abandon religion; they "Syncretized" it. They took the language of the Covenant (Berith) and applied it to Baal. They were trying to have the benefits of the Law while living like the Canaanites. It was a spiritual "Hybrid" that God rejected.

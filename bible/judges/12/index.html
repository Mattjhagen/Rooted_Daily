Judges 12

1The Ephraimite forces were called out, and they crossed over to Zaphon. They said to Jephthah, “Why did you go to fight the Ammonites without calling us to go with you? We’re going to burn down your house over your head.”
2Jephthah answered, “I and my people were engaged in a great struggle with the Ammonites... Why should I have called you?” 1
4Jephthah then called together all the men of Gilead and fought Ephraim. The Gileadites struck them down...

The Shibboleth Test

5The Gileadites captured the fords of the Jordan leading to Ephraim, and whenever a survivor of Ephraim said, “Let me cross over,” the men of Gilead asked him, “Are you an Ephraimite?” If he replied, “No,”
6they said, “All right, say ‘Shibboleth.’” If he said, “Sibboleth,” because he could not pronounce the word correctly, they seized him and killed him at the fords of the Jordan. Forty-two thousand Ephraimites were killed at that time. 2

Ibzan of Bethlehem

v.8-10: Led for seven years. He had thirty sons and thirty daughters. He gave his daughters in marriage to those outside his clan and brought in thirty young women from outside for his sons.

Elon of Zebulun

v.11-12: Led for ten years. He was buried in Aijalon in the land of Zebulun.

Abdon son of Hillel

v.13-15: Led for eight years. He had forty sons and thirty grandsons, who rode on seventy donkeys. 3

Translation Notes

1 Jephthah vs. Ephraim: In the scrolls, this conflict mirrors the one in Judges 8. However, while Gideon used "Diplomatic Wisdom" to pacify Ephraim, Jephthah (the "Man of War") used "Military Force." The tone of the book is darkening; earlier, they fought common enemies, but now they are turning their swords on each other. Unity is dissolving into regionalism.

2 Shibboleth / Sibboleth: A *Shibboleth* in the scrolls means a "stream" or a "head of grain." The Ephraimites had a distinct dialect that lacked the "sh" (shin) sound. This linguistic slip became a "Death Sentence." In the scrolls, this is a tragic example of "Tribalism"—where brothers are killed because they sound different. 42,000 deaths over a single consonant shows the depth of the national tragedy.

3 The Donkey-Riding Legacy: Like Jair in Chapter 10, the mention of "Seventy Donkeys" for Abdon’s family in the scrolls is a symbol of "Aristocratic Peace." Between the violent deliverers (Jephthah and Samson), there were periods of quiet governance where family lines grew and prospered. It shows that God provides "Steady Administration" as well as "Miraculous Rescue."

Judges 4

1Again the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD, now that Ehud was dead.
2So the LORD sold them into the hands of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. Sisera, the commander of his army, was based in Harosheth Haggoyim.
3Because he had nine hundred chariots fitted with iron and had cruelly oppressed the Israelites for twenty years, they cried out to the LORD for help. 1
4Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was leading Israel at that time. 2
5She held court under the Palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went up to her to have their disputes settled.
6She sent for Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali and said to him, “The LORD, the God of Israel, commands you: ‘Go, take with you ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun and lead them up to Mount Tabor.’”
7“‘I will lure Sisera, the commander of Jabin’s army, with his chariots and his troops to the Kishon River and give him into your hands.’”
8Barak said to her, “If you go with me, I will go; but if you don’t go with me, I won’t go.”
9“Very well,” Deborah said, “I will go with you. But because of the course you are taking, the honor will not be yours, for the LORD will deliver Sisera into the hands of a woman.” 3
14Then Deborah said to Barak, “Go! This is the day the LORD has given Sisera into your hands. Has not the LORD gone ahead of you?” So Barak went down Mount Tabor, with ten thousand men following him.
15At Barak’s advance, the LORD routed Sisera and all his chariots and army by the sword, and Sisera got down from his chariot and fled on foot.

Jael: The Unexpected Hero

17Sisera, meanwhile, fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, because there was an alliance between Jabin king of Hazor and the family of Heber the Kenite.
18Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, “Come, my lord, come right in. Don’t be afraid.” So he entered her tent, and she covered him with a blanket.
19“I’m thirsty,” he said. “Please give me some water.” She opened a skin of milk, gave him a drink, and covered him up.
21But Jael, Heber’s wife, picked up a tent peg and a hammer and went quietly to him while he lay fast asleep, exhausted. She drove the peg through his temple into the ground, and he died. 4
23On that day God subdued Jabin king of Canaan before the Israelites.
24And the hand of the Israelites grew stronger and stronger against Jabin king of Canaan until they destroyed him.

Translation Notes

1 Iron Chariots (Rekhev Barzel): Hazor had been rebuilt and was once again the dominant power in the north. The nine hundred iron chariots were a symbol of "Overwhelming Force." In the scrolls, the fact that Israel was "Cruelly Oppressed" for twenty years shows that they had lost the courage to trust the promise of Joshua 17:18—that they *could* drive out iron chariots.

2 Deborah (Prophetess and Leader): Deborah is unique in the scrolls as the only woman among the Judges. Her title as "Prophetess" (*Neviah*) suggests she was primarily a "Speaker for God." Her leadership was "Civil and Spiritual" rather than primarily military. She provided the "Divine Perspective" that the men of her generation were lacking.

3 The Honor to a Woman: Deborah’s rebuke to Barak in the scrolls was a challenge to his "Spiritual Passive-Aggressiveness." Barak believed God’s promise, but only if Deborah came along to "Guarantee" it. Because he refused to lead based on the Word alone, God transferred the "Military Glory" to a non-soldier (Jael). It teaches that God will get His glory, even if He has to bypass the expected leaders.

4 The Tent Peg: Jael was not a soldier, but she used the "Tools of her Trade." In Kenite culture, the women were responsible for setting up and tearing down the tents. She used the very tool she used every day to build her home to destroy the enemy of God's home. It is a story of "Ordinary Domestic Tools" becoming "Instruments of Divine Justice."

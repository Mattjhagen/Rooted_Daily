Judges 17

1Now a man named Micah from the hill country of Ephraim
2said to his mother, “The eleven hundred shekels of silver that were taken from you and about which I heard you utter a curse—I have that silver with me; I took it.” His mother said, “The LORD bless you, my son!”
3When he returned the silver to his mother, she said, “I solemnly consecrate my silver to the LORD for my son to make an image overlaid with silver.” 1
4...Her son returned the silver, and she took two hundred shekels of silver and gave them to a silversmith, who used them to make the idol. And it was put in Micah’s house.
5Now this man Micah had a shrine, and he made an ephod and some household gods and installed one of his sons as his priest.
6In those days Israel had no king; everyone did as they saw fit. 2
7A young Levite from Bethlehem in Judah, who had been living within the clan of Judah, left that town in search of some other place to stay.
9Micah asked him, “Where are you from?” “I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah,” he said, “and I’m looking for a place to stay.”
10Then Micah said to him, “Live with me and be my father and priest, and I’ll give you ten shekels of silver a year, your clothes and your food.” 3
11The Levite agreed to live with him, and the young man became like one of his sons to him.
12Then Micah installed the Levite, and the young man became his priest and lived in his house.
13And Micah said, “Now I know that the LORD will be good to me, since this Levite has become my priest.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Consecrate to make an Image: The irony in the scrolls is thick here. Micah’s mother "consecrates" money to the LORD by violating the very first and second commandments (No other gods, no carved images). It shows that the people had completely lost the "Content" of their faith, keeping only the "Vocabulary" of the LORD (*YHWH*).

2 No King / Saw Fit (Yashar be-Einav): This is the "Thesis Statement" for the end of the Book of Judges in the scrolls. Literally, "Every man did what was upright in his own eyes." Without a central authority or a commitment to the objective Word of God, truth became "Subjective." Everyone became their own source of morality and religion.

3 Father and Priest: In the scrolls, a Levite was supposed to be a teacher of the whole nation, supported by the national tithe. Here, he becomes a "Religious Hireling" for a private individual. He sold his high calling for a suit of clothes and a small salary. It teaches that when the "Clergy" becomes a "Commodity," the faith is in danger.

4 Now I Know: Micah’s statement in the scrolls is a perfect example of "Superstitious Religion." He believed that having a Levite in his house was like a "Good Luck Charm." He ignored his idolatry and theft, thinking that a "Religious Professional" would force God to bless him. It is the definition of "Empty Ritualism."

Judges 16

1One day Samson went to Gaza, where he saw a prostitute. He went in to spend the night with her.
3But Samson lay there only until midnight. Then he got up and took hold of the doors of the city gate, together with the two posts, and tore them loose, bar and all. He lifted them to his shoulders and carried them to the top of the hill that faces Hebron. 1
4Sometime later, he fell in love with a woman in the Valley of Sorek whose name was Delilah.
5The rulers of the Philistines went to her and said, “See if you can lure him into showing you the secret of his great strength... each of us will give you eleven hundred shekels of silver.”
15Then she said to him, “How can you say, ‘I love you,’ when you won’t confide in me? This is the third time you have made a fool of me...”
16With such nagging she pushed him day after day until he was sick to death.
17So he told her everything. “No razor has ever been used on my head,” he said, “because I have been a Nazirite dedicated to God from my mother’s womb. If my head were shaved, my strength would leave me, and I would become as weak as any other man.” 2
19After lulling him to sleep on her lap, she called for someone to shave off the seven braids of his hair... and his strength left him.
20She called, “Samson, the Philistines are upon you!” He awoke from his sleep and thought, “I’ll go out as before and shake myself free.” But he did not know that the LORD had left him. 3
21Then the Philistines seized him, gouged out his eyes and took him down to Gaza. Binding him with bronze shackles, they set him to grinding grain in the prison.
22But the hair on his head began to grow again after it had been shaved. 4

Samson’s Final Strength

28Then Samson prayed to the LORD, “Sovereign LORD, remember me. Please, God, strengthen me just once more, and let me with one blow get revenge on the Philistines for my two eyes.”
29Then Samson reached toward the two central pillars on which the temple stood. Bracing himself against them...
30Samson said, “Let me die with the Philistines!” Then he pushed with all his might, and down came the temple on the rulers and all the people in it. Thus he killed many more when he died than while he lived. 5

Translation Notes

1 The Gates of Gaza: In the scrolls, Gaza was the premier Philistine stronghold. Carrying the city gates to a hill facing Hebron (the spiritual heart of Judah) was a massive insult to Philistine power. He was demonstrating that their "Security" was a joke to him. However, his visit to a prostitute shows that while he could conquer "City Gates," he could not conquer his own "Eye-Gate."

2 The Secret: Samson’s hair was not "Magical" in the scrolls. It was a "Sign of the Covenant." As long as he kept the vow, God kept the power. By allowing his hair to be cut, he was consciously "Shredding the Contract." He chose the pleasure of Delilah over the purpose of God. The tragedy was that he eventually forgot where his strength actually came from.

3 The LORD had left him: This is one of the most frightening verses in the scrolls. Samson was so used to having the Spirit’s power that he thought it was a "Natural Ability." He went out to fight expecting the usual surge, only to find he was just an ordinary man. It teaches that "Anointing" is a gift, not a right, and can be withdrawn if the vessel becomes defiled.

4 Hair Began to Grow: This detail in the scrolls is the "Glimmer of Hope." As his hair grew, his "Nazirite Consecration" was being restored in the silence of the prison. The Philistines thought they had defeated him, but they were ignoring the physical sign of his returning relationship with God. Blindness had finally brought him to a place of "Spiritual Sight."

5 Samson’s Death: Samson is the only Judge in the scrolls to die in the act of deliverance. His final prayer was for "Revenge," but God used it for "Liberation." By destroying the temple of Dagon and the Philistine leadership, he broke the back of the 40-year oppression. He was a "Flawed Hero" who achieved more in his failure and death than in his years of strength.

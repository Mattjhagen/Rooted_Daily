Ecclesiastes 5

Awe and the Vanity of Wealth.

Let Your Words be Few

“Guard your steps when you go to the house of God. Go near to listen.”
“God is in heaven and you are on earth, so let your words be few.” 1

Never Satisfied

“Whoever loves money never has enough; whoever loves wealth is never satisfied with their income.” 2
“The sleep of a laborer is sweet, whether they eat little or much.” 3

As they Come, so they Depart

“Everyone comes naked from their mother’s womb, and as everyone comes, so they depart.” 4

The Gift of Gladness

“To enjoy your work and accept your lot—this is a gift of God.”
“God keeps them occupied with gladness of heart.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Words be Few (yi-he-yu De-ba-re-kha me-at-tim): In the scrolls, this is the "Awe Root." "Fewness" (*Me-attim*) "Roots" the "Human" (*Adam*) in "Perspective" before the "Celestial" (*Elohim*). It signifies "The End of Self-Justification."

2 Never Satisfied (lo-Yis-ba Ke-seph): The scrolls highlight the "Insatiable Root of Materialism." "Money" (*Kesep*) "Uproots" "Satiety" (*Saba*). It signify "The Psychological Void of Accumulation."

3 Sleep... is Sweet (Me-tu-qah she-nath ha-O-bed): The scrolls highlight the "Physiological Root of Labor." The "Worker" (*Obed*) is "Rooted" in "Sweetness" (*Metuqah*), while the "Abundance" (*Saba*) of the "Rich" (*Ashir*) "Uproots" their "Rest." It signifies "The Paradox of Comfort."

4 As they Depart (ken-ye-lekh): Literally "So he shall go." The scrolls highlight the "Cyclical Root of Poverty." No "Profit" (*Amal*) can be "Rooted" in the "Afterlife." It signify "Material Impermanence."

5 Gladness of Heart (be-Sim-chath Lib-bo): The scrolls conclude with this "Occupational Root of Grace." God "Occupies" (*Ma-a-neh*) the heart with "Joy" (*Simchah*) so that the "Breath-like" (*Hevel*) nature of life is not a "Burden." It marks the transition to the "Vanity of Wealth" in *Ecclesiastes 6*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Joyful Lot." Accept it.

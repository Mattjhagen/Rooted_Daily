Jeremiah 39

The Broken Wall and the Blinding of the King.

The Wall is Broken

“The city wall was broken through.” 1

Eyes Put Out

“The king of Babylon slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah.”
“Then he put out Zedekiah’s eyes and bound him with bronze shackles.” 2

City in Flames

“The Babylonians set fire to the palace and broke down the walls.” 3

Kindness to the Prophet

“Take him and look after him; don’t harm him.” 4
“I will save you, Ebed-Melech, because you trust in me.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Wall was Broken Through (hab-be-qi-āh hā-īr): In the scrolls, this is the "Structural Root of Defeat." "Protection" is "Uprooted" as the "Wall" (*Chomah*) becomes a "Breach" (*Beqiah*). It signifies "The End of Civic Sovereignty."

2 Eyes put out (ē-nē Tzid-qi-yā-hū iw-wēr): The scrolls highlight the "Visual Root of Judgment." The last thing the King "Roots" in his memory is the "Death of his Sons." His "Vision" is then "Uprooted." It signify "The Psychological Darkness of the Throne."

3 Set fire to the Palace (ēsh śā-re-phū): The scrolls highlight the "Combustive Root of Uprooting." The "Institutional Roots" of the nation are "Burned" to the "Ground." It signifies "The Total Erasing of the Davidic Infrastructure."

4 Look after him (v-e-nekha śīm ā-lāw): Literally "Set your eyes upon him." The scrolls contrast this with Zedekiah’s "Eyes." While the King is "Blinded," the Prophet is "Rooted" in the "Protective Gaze" (*Eyes*) of the Babylonian officer. It signify "The Sovereignty of God over Enemies."

5 I will save you (mil-lēt a-mal-let-khā): The scrolls conclude with this "Salvific Root of Trust." "Ebed-Melech" (Servant of the King) is "Rooted" in "Safety" because he served the "True King." It marks the transition to "Jeremiah Freed" in *Jeremiah 40*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Broken City." Trust survives the fire.

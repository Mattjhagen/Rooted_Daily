Jeremiah 38

The Sinking Mire and the Rags of Mercy.

Sinking in the Mud

“There was no water in the cistern, only mud.”
“And Jeremiah sank down into the mud.” 1

The Rescue of Rags

“He took old rags and worn-out clothes.”
“‘Put these under your arms to pad the ropes.’ Jeremiah did so.” 2

The Last Choice

“If you surrender... your life will be spared.” 3
“But if you will not, this city will be burned with fire.” 4

Fear of the People

“I am afraid of the Jews who have gone over to the Babylonians.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Sank down into the Mud (way-yit-ba Yir-me-yā-hū bak-yā-wēn): In the scrolls, this is the "Viscous Root of Persecution." "Stability" is "Uprooted" and the prophet is "Rooted" in "Mire" (*Yawen*). It signifies "The Total Physical Immersion in Suffering."

2 Rescue of Rags (se-chā-bhōth be-lō-īm): Literally "Torn rags." The scrolls highlight the "Humble Root of Deliverance." "Prophetic Preservation" is "Rooted" in "Garbage" (*Rags*). It signifies "The Dignity of Small Mercies."

3 If you Surrender (im-yā-tzō thē-tzē): Literally "If you go out." The scrolls highlight the "Exodus Root of Survival." "Freedom" is "Rooted" in "Submission." It signify "The Divine Paradox of Safety."

4 Burned with Fire (bat-te-śā-rēph bā-ēsh): The scrolls highlight the "Pyrotechnic Root of Judgment." "Uprooting" takes the form of "Combustion." It signifies "The Total Destruction of the Material City."

5 I am Afraid (a-nī dhā-ghēgh): The scrolls conclude with this "Paralytic Root of Leadership." "Power" is "Uprooted" by "Peer Pressure" (*Fear of the Jews*). It marks the transition to "The Fall of Jerusalem" in *Jeremiah 39*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Pad of Mercy." Ebed-Melech shall be saved.

Jeremiah 48

The Wine on the Lees and the Shame of Chemosh.

Wine on the Lees

“Moab has been at rest from youth, like wine left on its lees, not poured from one jar to another.” 1

Moab’s Arrogance

“We have heard of Moab’s pride—how very proud he is—of his loftiness and his arrogance.” 2

The Cut Horn

“Moab’s horn is cut off; his arm is broken.” 3

Cursed is the Lax

“A curse on him who is lax in doing the Lord’s work!” 4

The Future of Moab

“Yet I will restore the fortunes of Moab in days to come.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Wine on the Lees (shō-qēt... el-she-mā-rāw): In the scrolls, this is the "Stagnant Root of Pride." "Refinement" is "Uprooted" because Moab has never been "Poured" (*Shaphakh*) from "Jar to Jar" (*Exile*). It signifies "The Dangers of Constant Comfort."

2 Moab’s Pride (ge-ōn Mō-ābh): The scrolls highlight the "Elevated Root of Arrogance." "Moab" (From Father) "Roots" his "Identity" in his own "Height." It signify "The Aesthetic of the High Places."

3 Moab’s Horn (qe-ren Mō-ābh): The scrolls highlight the "Symbolic Root of Strength." "Horns" were signs of "Power." "Authority" is "Uprooted" (*Gadha*) by "Amputation." It signifies "The Disarming of the Nation."

4 Cursed is the Lax (ā-rūr o-śeh mi-la-kheth Yah-weh re-miy-yāh): The scrolls highlight the "Diligent Root of Judgment." "Negligence" is "Uprooted." It signify "The Weight of the Divine Mandate."

5 Restore the Fortunes (we-shabh-tī she-bhūth): The scrolls conclude with this "Restorative Root." "Moab" is "Re-Rooted" in "Grace." It marks the transition to "Ammon and Edom" in *Jeremiah 49*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Restored Jar." Redemption remains possible.

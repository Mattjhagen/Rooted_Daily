Jeremiah 24

The Two Baskets and the Heart of the Exile.

Two Baskets

“One basket had very good figs.”
“The other basket had very bad figs.” 1

Planted for Good

“Like these good figs, I regard as good the exiles from Judah.” 2
“I will plant them and not uproot them.” 3

A Heart to Know Me

“I will give them a heart to know me, that I am the Lord.” 4

The Bad Figs

“So will I deal with Zedekiah king of Judah... and the survivors in this land.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Two Baskets (she-nē dū-dhā-yīm): In the scrolls, this is the "Sorting Root of Providence." "Individuality" is "Uprooted" as the "Nation" is "Sorted" into "Two Essential Qualities" (*Good/Bad*). It signifies "The Discernment of God."

2 Regard as Good (ak-kīr... le-tō-bhāh): Literally "I will recognize." The scrolls highlight the "Epistemological Root of Favor." "Judgment" is "Uprooted" for the "Exiles" (*Golah*) and "Grace" is "Rooted" in "Recognition" (*Akkir*). It signify "The Protective Gaze."

3 Plant and not Uproot (ū-ne-ta-tīm we-lō eth-tōsh): This is the central "Rooted Daily" motif. The scrolls highlight the "Permanent Root of the Remnant." "Instability" is "Uprooted" and "Endurance" is "Rooted" in the "Land." It signifies "The Future Restoration."

4 Heart to Know Me (lēbh la-dha-ath ō-thī): The scrolls highlight the "Relational Root of Newness." "Stoniness" is "Uprooted" and "Knowing" (*Da-ath*) is "Rooted" in the "Internal Man" (*Heart/Leb*). It signify "The Beginning of the New Covenant concept."

5 Zedekiah (Tzid-qi-yā-hū): The scrolls conclude with this "Ironic Root of Judgment." The king whose name means "Yahweh is Righteous" is "Uprooted" because of "Unrighteousness." It marks the transition to "The Seventy Years" in *Jeremiah 25*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Good Fig." Know Him.

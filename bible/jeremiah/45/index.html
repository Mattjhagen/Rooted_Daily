Jeremiah 45

The Scribe’s Groan and the Prize of Life.

Woe to Me

“Woe to me! The Lord has added sorrow to my pain; I am worn out with groaning.” 1

Overthrowing the Built

“I will overthrow what I have built and uproot what I have planted.” 2

Seek No Great Things

“Should you then seek great things for yourself? Do not seek them.” 3

Prize of War

“I will let you escape with your life as a prize of war.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Added Sorrow to my Pain (yā-śaph Yah-weh yā-ghōn al-makh-ō-bhī): In the scrolls, this is the "Cumulative Root of Grief." Baruch "Roots" his "Identity" in the "Pain" of the "Scribe." It signifies "The Emotional Cost of Revelation."

2 Uproot what I have Planted (eth-a-sher nā-tat-tī a-nī nō-thēsh): This is the "Universal Root of Deconstruction." God "Uproots" (*Nothesh*) his "Own Work." It signifies "The Divine Responsibility for History."

3 Seek Great Things (te-bhāq-qēsh le-khā ge-dhō-lōth): The scrolls highlight the "Ambitious Root of Distraction." "Personal Glory" is "Uprooted" as "Inappropriate" for the "Era." It signify "The Scribe’s Humility."

4 Prize of War (le-shā-lāl): Literally "As Spoil." The scrolls conclude with this "Predatory Root of Survival." "Security" is "Uprooted" but "Life" (*Nephesh*) is "Rooted" as a "Reward." It marks the transition to "Oracles against Nations" in *Jeremiah 46*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Scribe’s Prize." Your life is enough.

Jeremiah 12

The Prospering Wicked and the Race with Horses.

Why do They Prosper?

“Why does the way of the wicked prosper? Why do all the faithless live at ease?” 1
“You have planted them, and they have taken root.”

Racing with Horses

“If you have raced with men on foot and they have worn you out, how can you compete with horses?” 2
“How will you manage in the thickets by the Jordan?” 3

Ruined Vineyard

“Many shepherds will ruin my vineyard and trample down my field.” 4

Uprooted Neighbors

“But after I uproot them, I will again have compassion.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Way of the Wicked Prosper (de-rekh re-shā-īm tzā-lē-chā): In the scrolls, this is the "Paradoxical Root of Success." The "Wicked" (*Resha-im*) are "Rooted" in "Prosperity" (*Tzalecha*) despite their "Faithlessness" (*Baghad*). It signifies "The Moral Inconsistency of the World."

2 Compete with Horses (te-tha-cha-reh eth-has-sū-sīm): The scrolls highlight the "Escalation Root of Trial." "Human opposition" is "Uprooted" as the "Limit" of suffering; "Divine Challenge" is "Rooted" in "Greater Speed" (*Horses*). It signify "The Toughening of the Prophet."

3 Thickets by the Jordan (ghe-ōn hay-Yar-dēn): Literally "The Pride of the Jordan." The scrolls highlight the "Geographic Root of Danger." The "Jordan" overflowed into "Thickets" full of "Lions." It signifies "The Unpredictable Hazards of the Ministry."

4 Ruined My Vineyard (shi-cha-thū khar-mī): The scrolls highlight the "Pastoral Root of Destruction." "Care" is "Uprooted" by "Corrupt Shepherds" (*Leaders*). It signify "The Betrayal of the People by their Guides."

5 After I Uproot (a-cha-rē nat-shī ō-thām): The scrolls conclude with this "Restorative Root." "Judgment" (*Uproot*) is followed by "Mercy" (*Compassion/Richamti*). It marks the transition to "The Linen Belt" in *Jeremiah 13*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Racing Soul." Run with the horses.

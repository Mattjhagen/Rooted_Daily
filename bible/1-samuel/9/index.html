1 Samuel 9

1There was a Benjamite, a man of standing, whose name was Kish...
2Kish had a son named Saul, as handsome a young man as could be found anywhere in Israel, and he was a head taller than anyone else. 1
3Now the donkeys belonging to Saul’s father Kish were lost, and Kish said to his son Saul, “Take one of the servants with you and go and look for the donkeys.”
5When they reached the district of Zuph, Saul said to the servant who was with him, “Come, let’s go back, or my father will stop thinking about the donkeys and start worrying about us.”
6But the servant replied, “Look, in this town there is a man of God; he is highly respected, and everything he says comes true. Let’s go there now; perhaps he will tell us what way to take.”
9(Formerly in Israel, if someone went to inquire of God, they would say, “Come, let us go to the seer,” because the prophet of today used to be called a seer.) 2
15Now the day before Saul came, the LORD had revealed this to Samuel:
16“About this time tomorrow I will send you a man from the land of Benjamin. Anoint him ruler over my people Israel; he will deliver them from the hand of the Philistines.” 3
17When Samuel caught sight of Saul, the LORD said to him, “This is the man I spoke to you about; he will govern my people.”
19“I am the seer,” Samuel replied. “Go up ahead of me to the high place, for today you are to eat with me... And as for the donkeys you lost three days ago, do not worry about them; they have been found.”
“And to whom is all the desire of Israel turned, if not to you and your whole family line?”
21Saul answered, “But am I not a Benjamite, from the smallest tribe of Israel, and is not my clan the least of all the clans of the tribe of Benjamin? Why do you say such a thing to me?” 4
24So the cook took up the thigh with what was on it and set it before Saul. Samuel said, “Here is what has been kept for you. Eat, because it was set aside for you for this occasion...”
27...Samuel said to Saul, “Tell the servant to go on ahead of us—and the servant did so—but you stay here a while, so that I may give you a message from God.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Head Taller: In the scrolls, Saul’s physical appearance is the answer to the people’s demand for a king "like the nations." He looked exactly like what they thought a king should be: impressive, tall, and handsome. The scrolls emphasize his "Visibility." This sets up the later contrast with David, who is chosen for his "Invisibility" (the heart).

2 Seer (Ro'eh): The word in the scrolls implies "One who Sees" things that are hidden to others. It is the old term for a Prophet (*Navi*). Samuel was a Seer because he could see the "Future King" in a "Donkey Hunter." The scrolls include this parenthetical note to explain the change in vocabulary to later readers.

3 Reveal this to Samuel (Galah et-Ozen): Literally in the scrolls, "Uncovered Samuel’s ear." It is an intimate image of God whispering a secret to His friend. While Saul was busy looking for donkeys, God was busy setting up the throne. It teaches that "Providence" works through the most mundane errands of life.

4 Smallest Tribe: Saul’s response in the scrolls shows a genuine (initial) humility. The tribe of Benjamin had been nearly wiped out (Judges 20-21). For a man from this disgraced and tiny tribe to be called to the throne was shocking. In the scrolls, this is a pattern: God chooses the "Least" to show that the power is His.

5 A Message from God (Devar Elohim): The chapter ends on a cliffhanger. Samuel had honored Saul at the feast, but the "Official Message" was for his ears alone. This private meeting on the roof was the moment of "Selection" before the "Public Inauguration." It shows that leadership starts in a private encounter with the Word of God.

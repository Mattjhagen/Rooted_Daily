1 Samuel 15

1Samuel said to Saul, “I am the one the LORD sent to anoint you king over his people Israel; so listen now to the message from the LORD.”
3“Now go, attack the Amalekites and totally destroy all that belongs to them. Do not spare them...” 1
9But Saul and the army spared Agag and the best of the sheep and cattle, the fat calves and lambs—everything that was good. These they were unwilling to destroy completely... 2
13When Samuel reached him, Saul said, “The LORD bless you! I have carried out the LORD’s instructions.”
14But Samuel said, “What then is this bleating of sheep in my ears? What is this lowing of cattle that I hear?” 3
15Saul answered, “The soldiers brought them from the Amalekites; they spared the best of the sheep and cattle to sacrifice to the LORD your God, but we totally destroyed the rest.”
“Does the LORD delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices
as much as in obeying the LORD?
To obey is better than sacrifice,
and to heed is better than the fat of rams.” 4
“Because you have rejected the word of the LORD,
he has rejected you as king.”
27As Samuel turned to leave, Saul caught hold of the hem of his robe, and it tore.
28Samuel said to him, “The LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today and has given it to one of your neighbors—to one better than you.” 5
35Until the day Samuel died, he did not go to see Saul again, though Samuel mourned for him. And the LORD regretted that he had made Saul king over Israel.

Translation Notes

1 Totally Destroy (Charam): In the scrolls, this is the command of *Cherem* (Devotion). The Amalekites were to be completely given over to judgment for their generational cruelty and opposition to God’s plan. This was not a "Looting Expedition" but a "Judicial Execution." Saul’s failure to understand this distinction was his downfall.

2 Unwilling to Destroy (Lo-Avu): The word in the scrolls implies a "Lack of Desire." Saul and the army looked at the wealth of Amalek and saw "Utility" where God had commanded "Destruction." They were valuing the "Goodness" of the objects over the "Holiness" of the command. This is the definition of "Compromise."

3 Bleating of Sheep: This is a masterpiece of prophetic irony in the scrolls. Saul was claiming to have obeyed God’s "Voice," but Samuel could hear the "Voice" of the animals Saul was supposed to have destroyed. The evidence of Saul’s sin was literally making noise. He had a "Witness against him" in his own camp.

4 Obey is better than Sacrifice: This is one of the most foundational statements in the scrolls. Saul tried to use "Religious Ritual" (sacrifice) to cover "Moral Rebellion" (disobedience). Samuel exposes the heart of the matter: God doesn't want our "Stuff" if He doesn't have our "Surrender." Sacrifice without obedience is just "Bribing God."

5 One Better Than You: Samuel doesn't name David yet, but he defines the "Standard." The one "better" is the one who will prioritize the "Heart of God" over the "Opinion of the Soldiers." Saul’s excuse ("the soldiers brought them") revealed that he was a leader who was led by his followers rather than by God.

1 Samuel 21

1David went to Nob, to Ahimelech the priest. Ahimelech trembled when he met him, and asked, “Why are you alone? Why is no one with you?” 1
2David answered Ahimelech the priest, “The king sent me on a mission...”
4But the priest answered David, “I don’t have any ordinary bread on hand; however, there is some holy bread here—provided the men have kept themselves from women.”
6So the priest gave him the holy bread, since there was no bread there except the Bread of the Presence that had been removed from before the LORD... 2
7Now one of Saul’s servants was there that day, detained before the LORD; he was Doeg the Edomite, Saul’s chief shepherd. 3
8David asked Ahimelech, “Don’t you have a spear or a sword here?”
9The priest replied, “The sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you killed in the Valley of Elah, is here; it is wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod. If you want it, take it...” David said, “There is none like it; give it to me.” 4
10That day David fled from Saul and went to Achish king of Gath.
11But the servants of Achish said to him, “Isn’t this David, the king of the land? Isn’t he the one they sing about in their dances...”
12David took these words to heart and was very afraid of Achish king of Gath.
13So he pretended to be insane in their presence; and while he was in their hands he acted like a madman, making marks on the doors of the gate and letting saliva run down his beard. 5
15Achish said... “Am I so short of madmen that you have to bring this fellow here to carry on like this in front of me? Must this man come into my house?”

Translation Notes

1 Trembled (Vayye-cherad): In the scrolls, Ahimelech was terrified because David (the hero and general) appeared alone and unannounced. He knew that the political tension between Saul and David was high. David’s lie ("The king sent me") was intended to protect the priests, but it ultimately led to their tragedy.

2 Holy Bread (Lechem ha-Panim): Literally in the scrolls, "Bread of the Face." These twelve loaves were changed every Sabbath and were only for the priests. By giving them to David, Ahimelech was prioritizing "Human Need" over "Ritual Law." Jesus would later use this specific moment to teach that "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath."

3 Doeg the Edomite: This is a "Foreboding Note" in the scrolls. Doeg was an Edomite (an outsider) serving Saul. He was "detained before the LORD," possibly fulfilling a vow or ritual. His presence at the sanctuary while David was there is the "Silent Threat" that would eventually explode in Chapter 22.

4 The Sword of Goliath: In the scrolls, this is the ultimate "Symbol of Faith." David, who had previously refused Saul’s armor, now took the weapon of the giant he had killed with a stone. It served as a "Physical Reminder" of God’s previous deliverance. "There is none like it" was a confession of God’s unique power.

5 Pretended to be Insane: This is a low point for David’s dignity in the scrolls. He had fled to the very city Goliath was from (Gath). When he was recognized, he used "Acting" to survive. Scratching the gates and foaming at the mouth was a desperate attempt to look "Unthreatening." It shows that even the "Anointed King" can be reduced to "Pitiful Tactics" when driven by fear.

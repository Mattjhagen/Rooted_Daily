1 Samuel 29

1The Philistines gathered all their forces at Aphek, and Israel camped by the spring in Jezreel.
2As the Philistine rulers marched with their units of hundreds and thousands, David and his men were marching at the rear with Achish. 1
3The commanders of the Philistines asked, “What are these Hebrews doing here?” Achish replied... “Is this not David, who was an officer of Saul king of Israel?”
4But the Philistine commanders were angry with Achish... “Send the man back! He must not go with us into battle, or he will turn against us in the thick of the fight.” 2
“Isn’t this the David they sang about: ‘Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands’?”
6So Achish called David and said to him, “As surely as the LORD lives, you have been reliable... but the rulers don’t approve of you.”
8“What have I done?” asked David. “What have you found against your servant from the day I came to you until now? Why can’t I go and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?” 3
9Achish answered, “I know that you have been as pleasing in my eyes as an angel of God; nevertheless, the Philistine commanders have said, ‘He must not go up with us into battle.’”
11So David and his men got up early in the morning to go back to the land of the Philistines, and the Philistines went up to Jezreel. 4

Translation Notes

1 At the Rear (Ba-Acharonah): In the scrolls, this is a position of "Extreme Tension." David and his 600 men were marching as part of the Philistine invasion force against their own countrymen. David was in an impossible situation: if he fought, he was a traitor to Israel; if he didn't, he was a traitor to Achish. The "Rear" position allowed for a last-minute change of sides, which is exactly what the Philistine lords feared.

2 Providential Rejection: The "Anger of the Commanders" in the scrolls is the means by which God rescued David from his own strategic mistake. David’s flight to the Philistines (Chapter 27) had led him to this moral cliff. By being "Rejected" and "Sent Home," David was spared from the bloodguilt of fighting against Saul and Jonathan on the day of their death.

3 What have I done?: David’s protest in the scrolls is often seen by scholars as a "Masterful Performance." He had to act offended to maintain his cover with Achish. By asking "Why can't I fight your enemies?" he was sounding like a loyal mercenary while likely being relieved in his heart that he was being dismissed. He was "Playing the Part" until the very end.

4 Return to Philistine Land: This departure in the scrolls is timed perfectly. While David was heading back to Ziklag (where a crisis awaited him), the Philistines were heading to Jezreel to finalize the doom of Saul. The "Geography of the Scrolls" shows two paths diverging—one toward a new beginning (David) and one toward a tragic end (Saul).

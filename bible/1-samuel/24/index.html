1 Samuel 24

3Saul came to the sheep pens along the way; a cave was there, and he went in to relieve himself. David and his men were far back in the cave. 1
4The men said, “This is the day the LORD spoke of when he said to you, ‘I will give your enemy into your hands for you to deal with as you wish.’” Then David crept up unnoticed and cut off a corner of Saul’s robe.
5Afterward, David was conscience-stricken for having cut off a corner of his robe. 2
6He said to his men, “The LORD forbid that I should do such a thing to my master, the LORD’s anointed, or lay my hand on him; for he is the anointed of the LORD.”
8Then David went out of the cave and called out to Saul, “My lord the king!” When Saul looked behind him, David bowed down and prostrated himself with his face to the ground.
11“See, my father, look at this piece of your robe in my hand! I cut off the corner of your robe but did not kill you. Now understand and recognize that I am not guilty of wrongdoing or rebellion.” 3
“May the LORD judge between you and me. And may the LORD avenge the wrongs you have done to me, but my hand will not touch you.”
16When David finished saying this, Saul asked, “Is that your voice, David my son?” And he wept aloud.
17“You are more righteous than I,” he said. “You have treated me well, but I have treated you badly.” 4
20“I know that you will surely be king and that the kingdom of Israel will be established in your hands.”
22So David gave his oath to Saul. Then Saul returned home, but David and his men went up to the stronghold. 5

Translation Notes

1 Relieve himself (Le-ha-sek): Literally in the scrolls, "To cover his feet." This was a common euphemism. The setting is one of intense irony: the king who was hunting David for his life was at his most vulnerable and undignified, while David sat in the shadows of the same cave. It was the perfect "Opportunity" from a human perspective.

2 Conscience-Stricken (Vay-yak Lev-David): Literally in the scrolls, "David’s heart struck him." Even though he had only cut a piece of the robe, David felt he had committed an act of "Disrespect" against the office of the King. The robe was a symbol of authority (as seen with Jonathan in Chapter 18). David’s sensitivity to the Spirit was so high that even this "Minor Insult" felt like a sin.

3 See, My Father: David’s address to Saul in the scrolls is deeply respectful. By calling him "Father" (as Saul was his father-in-law), David was appealing to their personal relationship. The piece of the robe was "Exhibit A" in the court of heaven. It proved that David’s "Actions" matched his "Words."

4 You are more Righteous (Tzaddik): In the scrolls, Saul is forced to acknowledge the "Moral Superiority" of the man he was trying to kill. This was a moment of "Temporary Repentance." Saul saw the truth, but his character was not transformed. He was moved by the "Evidence," but not changed by the "Grace."

5 David went to the Stronghold: This final detail in the scrolls is significant. Even though Saul wept and called David "son," David did not go back to the palace with him. He "Trusted God’s Word" but did not "Trust Saul’s Emotions." He remained in the wilderness, knowing that Saul’s paranoia would eventually return.

1 Samuel 17

4A champion named Goliath, who was from Gath, came out of the Philistine camp. His height was six cubits and a span. 1
8Goliath stood and shouted to the ranks of Israel... “Choose a man and have him come down to me!”
11On hearing the Philistine’s words, Saul and all the Israelites were dismayed and terrified.
26David asked the men standing near him... “Who is this uncircumcised Philistine that he should defy the armies of the living God?” 2
32David said to Saul, “Let no one lose heart on account of this Philistine; your servant will go and fight him.”
37“The LORD who rescued me from the paw of the lion and the paw of the bear will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine.”
39“I cannot go in these,” David said to Saul, “because I am not used to them.” So he took them off.
40Then he took his staff in his hand, chose five smooth stones from the stream, put them in the pouch of his shepherd’s bag and, with his sling in his hand, approached the Philistine. 3
“You come against me with sword and spear and javelin,
but I come against you in the name of the LORD Almighty,
the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied.” 4
“All those gathered here will know that it is not by sword or spear that the LORD saves; for the battle is the LORD’s...”
48As the Philistine moved closer to attack him, David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet him.
49Reaching into his bag and taking out a stone, he slung it and struck the Philistine on the forehead. The stone sank into his forehead, and he fell facedown on the ground. 5
51David ran and stood over him. He took hold of the Philistine’s sword and drew it from the sheath. After he killed him, he cut off his head with the sword.
54David took the Philistine’s head and brought it to Jerusalem; he put the Philistine’s weapons in his own tent.

Translation Notes

1 Six Cubits and a Span: In the scrolls, this height (about 9 feet 9 inches) made Goliath a literal giant, likely a descendant of the Anakim. His armor was not just defensive; it was "Psychological Warfare." He looked like an invincible bronze tank. The scrolls emphasize that Israel was paralyzed because they were looking at the "Height" of the giant rather than the "Height" of their God.

2 Defy the Living God: This is David’s "Perspective Shift" in the scrolls. While the army saw a "Giant vs. Man" problem, David saw a "God vs. Idolater" problem. By calling God the "Living God," David was pointing out that Goliath’s gods were dead. David’s outrage was not based on personal insult, but on the "Defamation of the Name."

3 Five Smooth Stones: In the scrolls, these were not "Primitive Weapons." A skilled slinger in the ancient world could hit a bird in flight. David was using the "Technology" he had mastered as a shepherd. By choosing five, he was prepared for a sustained fight, though he only needed one. It shows a combination of "Faith" and "Practical Readiness."

4 I Come in the Name: This is the "Legal Basis" for David’s victory in the scrolls. He was acting as a "Proxy" for the King of Heaven. He was not fighting for his own glory or even for Israel’s safety, but to prove that "There is a God in Israel." This is the ultimate purpose of the Kingdom.

5 Struck on the Forehead: The scrolls note that the stone "sank" into the forehead. This was the only vulnerable spot in Goliath’s massive bronze helmet. It was a "Bullseye of Providence." David’s skill provided the "Accuracy," but God provided the "Impact." The giant fell "facedown"—the posture of a defeated idol before the Ark.

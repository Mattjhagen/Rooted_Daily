1 Samuel 1

1There was a certain man from Ramathaim, a Zuphite from the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Elkanah.
2He had two wives; one was called Hannah and the other Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had none. 1
6Because the LORD had closed Hannah’s womb, her rival kept provoking her in order to irritate her.
7This went on year after year... and Hannah wept and would not eat.
10In her deep anguish Hannah prayed to the LORD, weeping bitterly.
11And she made a vow, saying, “LORD Almighty, if you will only look on your servant’s misery and remember me... and give her a son, then I will give him to the LORD for all the days of his life, and no razor will ever be used on his head.” 2
12As she kept on praying to the LORD, Eli the priest observed her mouth.
13Hannah was praying in her heart, and her lips were moving but her voice was not heard. Eli thought she was drunk. 3
15“Not so, my lord,” Hannah replied, “I am a woman who is deeply troubled... I was pouring out my soul to the LORD.”
17Eli answered, “Go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant you what you have asked of him.”
19Early the next morning they arose and worshiped before the LORD... Elkanah made love to his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her.
20So in the course of time Hannah became pregnant and gave birth to a son. She named him Samuel, saying, “Because I asked the LORD for him.” 4
24After he was weaned, she took the boy with her... and brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh.
26She said to Eli, “Pardon me, my lord... I am the woman who stood here beside you praying to the LORD.”
27“I prayed for this child, and the LORD has granted me what I asked of him.”
28“So now I give him to the LORD. For his whole life he will be given over to the LORD.” And they worshiped the LORD there. 5

Translation Notes

1 Hannah and Peninnah: In the scrolls, this domestic conflict mirrors the story of Rachel and Leah. Hannah (which means "Grace") was the favored wife but was barren, while Peninnah (which means "Pearl" or "Jewel") was fruitful but unloved. This "Barrenness" in the first chapter of Samuel is a metaphor for the spiritual state of Israel—dry and unable to produce a leader until God intervenes.

2 Nazirite Vow: Hannah’s vow in the scrolls was an act of "Total Surrender." She wasn't just asking for a child for her own comfort; she was asking for a child to be a "Permanent Servant" of the LORD. Like Samson, Samuel was to be a lifelong Nazirite. But unlike Samson, Samuel would actually keep his vow and his integrity.

3 Lips were moving: In the scrolls, Eli’s reaction is a commentary on the "Formalism" of the time. Silent, heartfelt prayer was so rare that the High Priest of Israel assumed a woman praying in the Spirit was actually drunk. Eli’s spiritual discernment was already fading, matching his fading physical eyesight mentioned later.

4 Samuel (Shemu-el): The name in the scrolls is a play on two Hebrew concepts: *Shama* (to hear) and *El* (God). It literally means "God has heard." It was a constant reminder to Hannah and to Israel that God is not deaf to the cries of the suffering.

5 I Give Him (Hish-iltihu): Literally in the scrolls, "I have lent him." It is a wordplay on the name *Shaul* (Saul), which means "Asked For." Hannah is saying that because she "Asked" (*Shaul*) for him, she is now "Lending" or "Granting" him back to the LORD. It teaches that the highest form of receiving from God is the willingness to give the gift back to His service.

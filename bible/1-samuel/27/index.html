1 Samuel 27

1But David thought to himself, “One of these days I will be destroyed by the hand of Saul. The best thing I can do is to escape to the land of the Philistines.” 1
2So David and the six hundred men with him left and went over to Achish son of Maok king of Gath.
4When Saul was told that David had fled to Gath, he no longer searched for him.
5David said to Achish, “If I have found favor in your eyes, let a place be assigned to me in one of the country towns... why should your servant live in the royal city with you?”
6So on that day Achish gave him Ziklag, and it has belonged to the kings of Judah ever since. 2
7David lived in Philistine territory a year and four months.
8Now David and his men went up and raided the Geshurites, the Girzites and the Amalekites. (From ancient times these peoples had lived in the land extending to Shur and Egypt.) 3
9Whenever David attacked an area, he did not leave a man or woman alive, but took sheep and cattle, donkeys and camels, and clothes. Then he returned to Achish.
10When Achish asked, “Where did you go raiding today?” David would say, “Against the Negev of Judah...” 4
12Achish trusted David and said to himself, “He has become so obnoxious to his people, the Israelites, that he will be my servant for life.” 5

Translation Notes

1 David thought to himself (Amar el-Libo): Literally "Said to his heart." In the scrolls, this is a moment of "Spiritual Weariness." David had been on the run for years. He had seen God deliver him multiple times, but the constant pressure finally led him to a "Pragmatic Decision" rather than a "Prophetic Instruction." He chose the "Safety of the Enemy" over the "Insecurity of the Inheritance."

2 Achish gave him Ziklag: This is a major "Geopolitical Event" in the scrolls. Ziklag was originally assigned to the tribe of Simeon/Judah but was held by the Philistines. By giving it to David, Achish was unwittingly returning the land to Israel. David established a "Kingdom in Miniature" there, with its own laws and military.

3 Raided the Amalekites: In the scrolls, David was doing what Saul had failed to do (Chapter 15). He was using his time in exile to systematically eliminate the ancient enemies of God’s people. He was acting as the "Secret Protector" of Israel while pretending to be a "Traitor."

4 David’s Deception: This is a controversial aspect of the scrolls. David was lying to Achish about the direction of his raids. He told Achish he was attacking his own people (Judah), when he was actually attacking the Philistines’ allies. This "Double Life" allowed him to survive, but it placed him in a morally complex position.

5 Achish Trusted David: The scrolls note the total success of David’s "Information Operation." Achish believed that David had burned all his bridges with Israel. This trust would soon create a massive conflict of interest for David when the Philistines decided to launch a full-scale war against Saul.

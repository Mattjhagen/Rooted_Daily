1 Samuel 25

1Now Samuel died, and all Israel assembled and mourned for him; and they buried him at his home in Ramah. 1
2A certain man in Maon... was very wealthy. His name was Nabal and his wife’s name was Abigail. She was an intelligent and beautiful woman, but her husband was surly and mean in his dealings—he was a Calebite. 2
10Nabal answered David’s servants, “Who is this David? Who is this son of Jesse? Many servants are breaking away from their masters these days.”
13David said to his men, “Each of you strap on your sword!” So they did, and David strapped his on as well.

The Wisdom of Abigail

23When Abigail saw David, she quickly got off her donkey and bowed down before David with her face to the ground.
“And now, my lord, as surely as the LORD your God lives... let your enemies and all who intend to harm my lord be like Nabal.” 3
“When the LORD has fulfilled every good thing he promised... my lord will not have on his conscience the staggering burden of needless bloodshed.” 4
32David said to Abigail, “Praise be to the LORD, the God of Israel, who has sent you today to meet me.”
37In the morning, when Nabal was sober, his wife told him all these things, and his heart failed him and he became like a stone.
38About ten days later, the LORD struck Nabal and he died. 5
40When David’s servants arrived at Carmel... they said, “David has sent us to you to take you to become his wife.”
42Abigail quickly got on a donkey and, attended by her five female servants, went with David’s messengers and became his wife.

Translation Notes

1 Death of Samuel: The scrolls record this simply, but it marks the end of an era. Samuel was the last judge and the first great prophet of the monarchy. David lost his spiritual mentor, and Israel lost its "Moral Anchor." This context makes David’s subsequent loss of temper with Nabal more understandable—he was mourning.

2 Nabal: Literally in the scrolls, "Fool." This was not just a description but his name. In the ancient world, names were often prophetic. Nabal’s folly was his "Atheistic Materialism"—he behaved as if there was no God to whom he was accountable for his wealth. He treated the "Anointed King" as just another "Runaway Slave."

3 Be like Nabal: This is a sophisticated "Curse" in the scrolls. Abigail was subtly telling David that Nabal’s "Folly" was already destroying him. He was a "Dead Man Walking." By telling David to let God handle it, she was preserving David’s "Kingly Dignity."

4 Staggering Burden (Pik-kah-lon): The word in the scrolls refers to a "Stumbling Block" or "Grief of Heart." Abigail was a "Political Realist." She knew David would be king, and she knew that a king who started his reign with a "Civilian Massacre" would have a stained legacy. She was protecting David from his own "Wilderness Rage."

5 Heart became like a Stone: Most medical scholars of the scrolls identify this as a "Stroke" or a "Heart Attack" brought on by the combination of a hangover and the shock of his narrow escape. The scrolls explicitly state that "The LORD struck him," confirming that it was a "Divine Judgment" for his refusal of hospitality and his insult to the King.

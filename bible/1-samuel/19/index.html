1 Samuel 19

1Saul told his son Jonathan and all the attendants to kill David. But Jonathan was very fond of David
2and warned him, “My father Saul is looking for a chance to kill you. Be on your guard tomorrow morning; go into hiding and stay there.”
4Jonathan spoke well of David to Saul his father... “Let not the king do wrong to his servant David; he has not wronged you, and what he has done has benefited you greatly.”
6Saul listened to Jonathan and took this oath: “As surely as the LORD lives, David will not be put to death.”
9But an evil spirit from the LORD came on Saul as he was sitting in his house with his spear in his hand. While David was playing the lyre,
10Saul tried to pin him to the wall with his spear, but David eluded him as Saul drove the spear into the wall. That night David made his escape. 1
11Saul sent men to David’s house to watch it and to kill him in the morning. But Michal, David’s wife, warned him...
12So Michal let David down through a window, and he fled and escaped.
13Then Michal took an idol and laid it on the bed, putting some goat’s hair at the head and covering it with a garment. 2

The Prophetic Shield at Ramah

18When David had fled and made his escape, he went to Samuel at Ramah...
20...Saul sent men to capture him. But when they saw a group of prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing there as their leader, the Spirit of God came on Saul’s men, and they also prophesied. 3
23Finally Saul himself went to Ramah...
24He stripped off his garments and he too prophesied in Samuel’s presence. He lay naked all that day and all that night. This is why people say, “Is Saul also among the prophets?” 4

Translation Notes

1 Spear into the Wall: In the scrolls, this is a "Symbolic Miss." Saul was trying to "Fix" David in place (death), but instead, he only managed to fix his own weapon into his own wall. It shows that Saul’s power was becoming "Self-Destructive." He was physically in his palace, but mentally he was already losing his grip.

2 The Idol (Teraphim): This is a curious detail in the scrolls. Michal, the daughter of Saul and wife of David, apparently had a household idol (*Teraphim*) large enough to pass for a person. It shows the "Religious Inconsistency" of Saul’s household. She used this relic of paganism to save the man of God—a perfect example of the messy reality of the period.

3 They also Prophesied: In the scrolls, this was God’s "Non-Violent Defense." He didn't strike the soldiers dead; He simply "Overwhelmed" them with His Spirit. They came to "Arrest," but they stayed to "Worship." The "Anointing" on Samuel and the school of prophets was so strong that it created a "Sacred Atmosphere" that no one could resist.

4 Saul’s Nakedness: In the scrolls, this is the "Ultimate Humiliation." For a king to be stripped of his royal garments and lay vulnerable on the ground was a sign that his "Majesty" was gone. God was showing Saul that despite his spear and his army, he was nothing in the presence of the Spirit. It was a final, supernatural warning to stop his pursuit of David.

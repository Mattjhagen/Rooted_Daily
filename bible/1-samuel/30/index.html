1 Samuel 30

1David and his men reached Ziklag on the third day. Now the Amalekites had raided the Negev and Ziklag. They had attacked Ziklag and burned it.
3When David and his men reached Ziklag, they found it destroyed by fire and their wives and sons and daughters taken captive.
4So David and his men wept aloud until they had no strength left to weep.
6David was in great distress because the men were talking of stoning him; each one was embittered in spirit because of his sons and daughters.
But David found strength in the LORD his God. 1
8David inquired of the LORD, “Shall I pursue this raiding party? Will I overtake them?” “Pursue them,” he answered. “You will certainly overtake them and succeed in the rescue.” 2
17David fought them from twilight until the evening of the next day, and none of them got away...
18David recovered everything the Amalekites had taken, including his two wives.
19Nothing was missing: young or old, boy or girl, plunder or anything else they had taken. David brought everything back. 3

The Statute of the Spoil

22But all the evil men and troublemakers among David’s followers said, “Because they did not go out with us, we will not share with them the plunder we recovered.”
23David replied, “No, my brothers, you must not do that with what the LORD has given us...”
24“The share of the man who stayed with the supplies is to be the same as that of him who went down to the battle. All will share alike.” 4
26When David reached Ziklag, he sent some of the plunder to the elders of Judah, who were his friends, saying, “Here is a gift for you from the plunder of the LORD’s enemies.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Strength in the LORD (Va-ye-chit-haz-zek): This is a turning point in the scrolls. David had lost everything—his home, his family, and the support of his men. Usually, he relied on Samuel or Jonathan, but now he was alone. He "strengthened himself" by recalling God’s promises. This internal "Resurrection" allowed him to lead even when his followers were ready to stone him.

2 Succeed in the Rescue (Hats-tsel Tats-tsil): Literally "Rescuing you will rescue." The LORD’s promise in the scrolls was emphatic. This was not just a military operation; it was a "Divine Recovery." It shows that even when the enemy has taken everything, God’s restoration can be complete.

3 Nothing was Missing: This is a miraculous detail in the scrolls. The Amalekites were known for their cruelty, yet they hadn't killed a single person. God had "restrained" the enemy to ensure David’s total recovery. It serves as a shadow of the "Greater Recovery" that the Messiah would bring.

4 Law of the Spoil: In the scrolls, David established a "Theology of Grace" for his army. He recognized that the victory was a "Gift from the LORD," not a result of human effort. Therefore, those who "Stayed with the Supplies" (the support staff) were just as vital as the "Combatants." This statute promoted "Unity" over "Greed."

5 Gifts to the Elders: David was acting "Kingly" in the scrolls. By sharing the plunder with the leaders of Judah, he was rebuilding his "Political Capital" and demonstrating that he was the true protector of the southern tribes. While Saul was losing his kingdom in the north, David was winning the hearts of the south.

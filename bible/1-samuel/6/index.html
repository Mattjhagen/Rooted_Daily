1 Samuel 6

1When the ark of the LORD had been in Philistine territory seven months,
2the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners and asked, “What shall we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us how we should send it back to its place.”
3They answered, “If you return the ark... do not send it back empty, but by all means send a guilt offering to him.”
4The Philistines asked, “What guilt offering should we send?” They replied, “Five gold tumors and five gold rats, according to the number of the Philistine rulers, because the same plague has struck both you and your rulers.” 1

The Test of the Cows

7“Now then, get a new cart ready, with two cows that have calved and have never been yoked. Hitch the cows to the cart, but take their calves away and pen them up.”
9“But watch it: If it goes up to its own territory, toward Beth Shemesh, then the LORD has brought this great evil on us. But if it does not, then we will know... it was a chance.” 2
12Then the cows went straight up toward Beth Shemesh, keeping on the road and lowing all the way; they did not turn to the right or to the left.
13Now the people of Beth Shemesh were harvesting their wheat in the valley, and when they looked up and saw the ark, they rejoiced at the sight.
19But God struck down some of the inhabitants of Beth Shemesh, putting seventy of them to death, because they looked into the ark of the LORD. 3
20And the people of Beth Shemesh asked, “Who can stand in the presence of the LORD, this holy God? To whom will the ark go up from here?” 4
21Then they sent messengers to the people of Kiriath Jearim, saying, “The Philistines have returned the ark of the LORD. Come down and take it up to your town.”

Translation Notes

1 Gold Rats and Tumors: In the scrolls, this guilt offering was a form of "Sympathetic Magic." The Philistines were trying to "Mirror" the plague back to the God who sent it, hoping to appease Him. The mention of "rats" suggests that the "tumors" were indeed the bubonic plague, spread by rodents. It shows that even the pagans recognized the "Moral Link" between their suffering and the Ark.

2 The Test of the Cows: This was a "Rigged Test" in the scrolls. Biologically, two nursing cows that had never been yoked would naturally turn back to their calves in the pen. For them to go straight away from their young and straight toward a foreign city while pulling a heavy cart was a "Mathematical Impossibility." The Philistines were forcing God to prove He was behind the plague by overriding the laws of nature.

3 Looking into the Ark: In the scrolls, the Ark was the most sacred object on earth. It was only to be handled by priests and covered from view. The men of Beth Shemesh treated it with "Familiarity" rather than "Reverence." They opened it up as a "Curiosity." God’s judgment was a reminder that while the Philistines were punished for "Ignoring" Him, His own people would be judged for "Trivializing" Him.

4 Who can stand: This question in the scrolls is the central theme of the priestly system. The answer, developed elsewhere, is "He who has clean hands and a pure heart." The people of Beth Shemesh realized they were not spiritually prepared for the "Raw Presence" of God. They wanted the Ark removed for the same reason the Philistines did—it was "Too Hot to Handle."

1 Samuel 22

1David left Gath and escaped to the cave of Adullam. When his brothers and his father’s household heard about it, they went down to him there.
2All those who were in distress or in debt or discontented gathered around him, and he became their commander. About four hundred men were with him. 1
8Saul said, “None of you is concerned about me. My son has made a covenant with the son of Jesse, and none of you tells me about it...”
9But Doeg the Edomite, who was standing with Saul’s officials, said, “I saw the son of Jesse come to Ahimelech son of Ahitub at Nob.” 2

The Massacre at Nob

16The king said, “You will surely die, Ahimelech, you and your whole family.”
17Then the king ordered the guards at his side: “Turn and kill the priests of the LORD...” But the king’s officials were unwilling to raise a hand to strike the priests of the LORD. 3
18The king then ordered Doeg, “You turn and strike down the priests.” So Doeg the Edomite turned and struck them down. That day he killed eighty-five men who wore the linen ephod.
19He also put to the sword Nob, the town of the priests, with its men and women, its children and infants, and its cattle, donkeys and sheep. 4
20But one son of Ahimelech son of Ahitub, named Abiathar, escaped and fled to join David.
22David said to Abiathar... “I am responsible for the death of your whole family.”
23“Stay with me; don’t be afraid. The man who wants to kill you is trying to kill me too. You will be safe with me.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Distress, Debt, Discontented: In the scrolls, these were the "Social Casualties" of Saul’s failed administration. Saul was taxing them heavily and ruling with paranoia. These outcasts found in David a leader who shared their "Exile Status." This band of 400 would become the "Mighty Men" of David’s future kingdom. It teaches that God builds His kingdom with the "Rejected."

2 Doeg’s Betrayal: In the scrolls, Doeg was an Edomite. His lack of "Israelite Identity" allowed him to commit atrocities that no Israelite would touch. He used his knowledge of David’s visit to "Buy Favor" with Saul. He is the ultimate "Opportunistic Villain."

3 Unwilling to Raise a Hand: This is a powerful moment of "Civil Disobedience" in the scrolls. Saul’s own soldiers recognized that killing the priests of the LORD was a "Red Line" they would not cross. Even under the threat of their king, they chose "Religious Integrity" over "Blind Obedience."

4 Massacre at Nob: In the scrolls, Saul committed a *Cherem* (total destruction) against a "Holy City" of his own people. He did to the priests of God what he had refused to do to the Amalekites in Chapter 15. It shows the "Total Inversion" of his soul; he was now acting as the enemy of God’s people.

5 Safe with Me (Mish-meret): David’s promise to Abiathar in the scrolls is a "Covenant of Protection." David took responsibility for the tragedy, even though it was Saul’s crime. By welcoming the last priest of Nob, David was also welcoming the "Urim and Thummim" (the means of inquiring of God), which would be his "Spiritual Advantage" in the wilderness.

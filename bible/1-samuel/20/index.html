1 Samuel 20

1Then David fled from Naioth at Ramah and went to Jonathan and asked, “What have I done? What is my crime? How have I wronged your father, that he is trying to kill me?”
3David took an oath and said, “Your father knows very well that I have found favor in your eyes... as surely as the LORD lives... there is only a step between me and death.”
4Jonathan said to David, “Whatever you want me to do, I’ll do for you.” 1
14“But show me unfailing kindness like the LORD’s kindness as long as I live...
15“and do not ever cut off your kindness from my family—not even when the LORD has cut off every one of David’s enemies from the face of the earth.” 2
20“I will shoot three arrows to the side of it, as though I were shooting at a target.”
“But if I say to the boy, ‘Look, the arrows are beyond you,’ then you must go, because the LORD has sent you away.”
30Saul’s anger flared up at Jonathan and he said to him, “You son of a perverse and rebellious woman! ... As long as the son of Jesse lives on this earth, neither you nor your kingdom will be established.” 3
32“Why should he be put to death? What has he done?” Jonathan asked his father.
33But Saul hurled his spear at him to kill him. Then Jonathan knew that his father intended to kill David.
41...David got up from the south side of the stone and bowed down before Jonathan three times, with his face to the ground. Then they kissed each other and wept together—but David wept the most. 4
42Jonathan said to David, “Go in peace, for we have sworn friendship with each other in the name of the LORD...” 5

Translation Notes

1 Only a Step between me and Death: This famous phrase in the scrolls captures the "Fragility of Life" for David during this period. He wasn't just a soldier anymore; he was a "Target." He realized that his life was held together only by the "Thin Thread" of God’s protection and Jonathan’s loyalty.

2 Unfailing Kindness (Hesed): In the scrolls, Jonathan is asking for *Hesed*—the covenantal love that David would later fulfill with Mephibosheth (2 Samuel 9). Jonathan knew that David would be king, and he was essentially "Negotiating for Grace" for his future descendants. He trusted David’s character more than his father’s crown.

3 Son of a Perverse Woman: Saul’s insult in the scrolls was directed at Jonathan’s "Loyalty." He was accusing Jonathan of betraying his own blood and his own future. Saul’s worldview was entirely "Self-Centered" and "Dynastic," while Jonathan’s was "God-Centered." Saul couldn't understand a son who would favor a "Rival" over a "Kingdom."

4 David Wept the Most (Hig-dil): Literally in the scrolls, "David made [it] great." David’s grief was deeper because he was losing everything—his home, his wife, his mentor, and his best friend. He was becoming a "Fugitive in his own Land." This weeping is the emotional low point of the friendship.

5 Sworn Friendship (Be-Shem YHWH): The covenant between David and Jonathan in the scrolls was the "Anchor" that would hold David through the coming years of exile. It was a "Sacred Bond" that transcended politics and family. Their final parting is a "Transition of Hope"—Jonathan stays in the palace to manage the chaos, while David goes to the wilderness to wait for God’s timing.

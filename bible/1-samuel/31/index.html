1 Samuel 31

1Now the Philistines fought against Israel; the Israelites fled before them, and many fell slain on Mount Gilboa.
2The Philistines were in hot pursuit of Saul and his sons, and they killed his sons Jonathan, Abinadab and Malki-Shua. 1
3The fighting grew fierce around Saul, and when the archers overtook him, they wounded him critically.
4Saul said to his armor-bearer, “Draw your sword and run me through...” But his armor-bearer was terrified and would not do it; so Saul took his own sword and fell on it. 2
6So Saul and his three sons and his armor-bearer and all his men died together that same day.
8The next day, when the Philistines came to strip the dead, they found Saul and his three sons fallen on Mount Gilboa.
9They cut off his head and stripped off his armor...
10They put his armor in the temple of the Ashtoreths and fastened his body to the wall of Beth Shan. 3

The Bravery of Jabesh Gilead

11When the people of Jabesh Gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul,
12all their valiant men marched through the night to Beth Shan. They took down the bodies of Saul and his sons from the wall... 4
13Then they took their bones and buried them under a tamarisk tree at Jabesh, and they fasted seven days. 5

Translation Notes

1 Death of Jonathan: In the scrolls, this is the deepest tragedy of the book. Jonathan, the man of faith and integrity (Chapter 14, 20), died because he remained loyal to his father’s failing administration. His death marks the "Clearance of the Path" for David, though it was a path David never wanted to be opened by Jonathan’s blood.

2 Fell on his Sword: Saul’s suicide in the scrolls is the final act of a man who refused to trust God until the end. He was more afraid of "Abuse" by the Philistines than he was of the judgment of God. He died on the same mountain where he had once won great victories, proving that "Military Success" without "Spiritual Obedience" is a hollow shell.

3 Temple of the Ashtoreths: By placing Saul’s armor in the temple of a fertility goddess, the Philistines were making a "Theological Point" in the scrolls. They were claiming that their gods had defeated the God of Israel. The "Captivity of the King" had become a "Disgrace to the Name."

4 The Men of Jabesh Gilead: This is a beautiful "Closing of the Circle" in the scrolls. In Chapter 11, Saul’s first act as king was to rescue Jabesh Gilead from the Ammonites. Now, 40 years later, the men of Jabesh showed their "Long Memory" and "Gratitude" by risking their lives to rescue Saul’s body from desecration. It is the only note of "Honor" in an otherwise dark ending.

5 Fasted Seven Days: The scrolls conclude 1 Samuel with a period of "National Mourning." The first experiment with monarchy had ended in disaster. The king who was chosen for his "Height" had fallen to the ground. The book ends with Israel defeated and their king dead, leaving the reader with a desperate question: "Where is the Man after God’s Own Heart?"

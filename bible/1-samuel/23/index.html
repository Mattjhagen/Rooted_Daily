1 Samuel 23

1When David was told, “Look, the Philistines are fighting against Keilah and are looting the threshing floors,”
2he inquired of the LORD, “Shall I go and attack these Philistines?” The LORD answered him, “Go, attack the Philistines and save Keilah.” 1
5So David and his men went to Keilah, fought the Philistines and carried off their livestock. He inflicted heavy losses on the Philistines and saved the people of Keilah.
9When David learned that Saul was plotting against him, he said to Abiathar the priest, “Bring the ephod.” 2
11“Will the citizens of Keilah surrender me into his hands?” And the LORD said, “They will.”
13So David and his men, about six hundred in number, left Keilah and kept moving from place to place.
16And Saul’s son Jonathan went to David at Horesh and helped him find strength in God. 3
“Don’t be afraid,” he said. “My father Saul will not lay a hand on you. You will be king over Israel, and I will be second to you.”
18The two of them renewed their covenant before the LORD.

Sela Hammahlekoth: The Rock of Parting

26Saul was going along one side of the mountain, and David and his men were on the other side, hurrying to get away from Saul.
27Just then a messenger came to Saul, saying, “Come quickly! The Philistines are raiding the land.” 4
28Then Saul broke off his pursuit of David and went to meet the Philistines. That is why they call this place Sela Hammahlekoth.
29And David went up from there and lived in the strongholds of En Gedi. 5

Translation Notes

1 Inquired of the LORD (Vay-yish-al): In the scrolls, this is the mark of David’s leadership. Even though he was a fugitive, he was still "Acting as King" by protecting his people. He didn't ask "Is it safe for me?" but "Shall I go?" He was more concerned with the Philistines looting the threshing floors than with his own precarious position.

2 Bring the Ephod: This refers to the high priest’s garment in the scrolls, which contained the Urim and Thummim. By having the only surviving priest of Nob with him, David had direct access to "Divine Intelligence." Saul, having killed the priests, was now "Spiritually Blind." This "Information Gap" is why David was always a step ahead.

3 Strength in God (Va-ye-chaz-zek): Literally "He strengthened David’s hand in Elohim." This was the final meeting between the two friends in the scrolls. Jonathan was acting as David’s "Spiritual Support," reminding him of his destiny when David was most discouraged. Jonathan’s statement "I will be second to you" is the ultimate expression of his selflessness.

4 The Philistine Raid: This is a classic example of "Providential Intervention" in the scrolls. Just as Saul was about to close the circle around David, God used Israel’s national enemy to distract the king. It shows that God can use the "Chaos of the World" to protect His anointed.

5 En Gedi: Literally in the scrolls, "The Spring of the Young Goat." It is a lush oasis in the middle of the barren Judean desert. After the "Rock of Parting," David moved to this cave-filled region, setting the stage for the famous confrontation in the next chapter.

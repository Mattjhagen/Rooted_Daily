1 Samuel 16

1The LORD said to Samuel, “How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him as king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and be on your way; I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem. I have chosen one of his sons to be king.”
6When they arrived, Samuel saw Eliab and thought, “Surely the LORD’s anointed stands here before the LORD.”
7But the LORD said to Samuel, “Do not consider his appearance or his height, for I have rejected him. The LORD does not look at the things people look at. People look at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart.” 1
11Samuel asked, “Are these all the sons you have?” “There is still the youngest,” Jesse answered. “He is tending the sheep.” Samuel said, “Send for him; we will not sit down until he arrives.”
12So Jesse sent for him and had him brought in. He was glowing with health and had a fine appearance and handsome features. Then the LORD said, “Rise and anoint him; this is the one.” 2
13So Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the presence of his brothers, and from that day on the Spirit of the LORD came powerfully upon David. 3
14Now the Spirit of the LORD had departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD tormented him. 4
18One of the servants answered, “I have seen a son of Jesse of Bethlehem who knows how to play the lyre. He is a brave man and a warrior. He speaks well and is a fine-looking man. And the LORD is with him.”
21David came to Saul and entered his service. Saul liked him very much, and David became one of his armor-bearers.
23Whenever the spirit from God came on Saul, David would take up his lyre and play. Then relief would come to Saul; he would feel better, and the evil spirit would leave him. 5

Translation Notes

1 The Lord looks at the Heart (Yireh la-Levav): This is the "Thesis Statement" of David’s life in the scrolls. After the failure of Saul (who was chosen for his outward appearance), God explicitly states that His "Recruitment Criteria" are different. The heart (*Levav*) represents the "Inner Disposition" and "Spiritual Alignment." David was chosen because his internal compass was pointed toward God.

2 Glowing with Health (Admoni): Literally "Ruddy." This often referred to red hair or a reddish complexion. In the scrolls, this detail distinguishes David from his brothers. He was the "Unlikely Candidate," forgotten by his own fatherJesse didn't even think to include him in the sacrifice until Samuel asked. This "Forgotten Shepherd" was the "Royal Solution."

3 From that day on: In the scrolls, the "Transfer of Anointing" is immediate. Saul still held the "Title" of King, but David now held the "Authority" and the "Power" of the Spirit. For the next several chapters, the scrolls depict the tension between the "De Jure" king (Saul) and the "De Facto" king (David).

4 Evil Spirit from the LORD (Ruach Ra’ah): This is a difficult phrase in the scrolls. It suggests a "Spirit of Melancholy" or "Paranoia" that was sent as a judgment. When the Spirit of Grace (the Holy Spirit) left Saul, it left a "Vacuum" that was filled by a darker influence. It shows the psychological consequence of persistent rebellion.

5 The Lyre (Kinnor): David’s music in the scrolls was more than just "Entertainment." It was a "Spiritual Ministry." The lyre was an instrument of worship. By playing, David was bringing the "Presence of God" into Saul’s troubled room. It is ironic that the one who would replace Saul was the only one who could provide him with relief.

1 Samuel 10

1Then Samuel took a flask of olive oil and poured it on Saul’s head and kissed him, saying, “Has not the LORD anointed you ruler over his inheritance?” 1

The Three Signs of Confirmation

Sign 1: “You will meet two men near Rachel’s tomb... they will tell you the donkeys are found.”
Sign 2: “Three men going up to Bethel will meet you... they will give you two loaves of bread.”
Sign 3: “As you approach Gibeah... you will meet a procession of prophets... The Spirit of the LORD will come powerfully upon you, and you will be changed into a different person.” 2
9As Saul turned to leave Samuel, God changed Saul’s heart, and all these signs were fulfilled that day. 3
11When those who had formerly known him saw him prophesying with the prophets, they asked each other, “What is this that has happened to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?”

The Selection at Mizpah

17Samuel summoned the people of Israel to the LORD at Mizpah...
21Finally Saul son of Kish was chosen. But when they looked for him, he was not to be found.
22So they inquired further of the LORD, “Has the man come here yet?” And the LORD said, “Yes, he has hidden himself among the supplies.” 4
24Samuel said to all the people, “Do you see the man the LORD has chosen?” And the people shouted, “Long live the king!”
26Saul also went to his home in Gibeah, accompanied by valiant men whose hearts God had touched.
27But some scoundrels said, “How can this fellow save us?” They despised him and brought him no gifts. But Saul kept silent. 5

Translation Notes

1 Anointed (Mashach): In the scrolls, this is the origin of the word "Messiah" (*Mashiach*). Anointing with oil symbolized the "Set-Apartness" for a specific divine task. It was not just a political appointment; it was a "Sacerdotization" of the office of King. Saul was now a "Person of the Oil," carrying the responsibility of God’s reputation.

2 Changed into a different person: This is a crucial phrase in the scrolls. God was giving Saul a "New Capability" for leadership. He was being empowered by the Spirit of the LORD (*Ruach YHWH*) to act as a judge/prophet. It teaches that God never calls a person to a task without also providing the "Internal Shift" necessary to perform it.

3 God Changed his Heart: Literally in the scrolls, "God turned him another heart." This was the internal sign. While the external signs were for his "Mind" to believe, the change of heart was for his "Will" to obey. Sadly, as the story progresses, we see that having a changed heart is not the same as having a "Sustained Character."

4 Hidden among the Supplies (Kelim): In the scrolls, this detail is ambiguous. Was Saul being "Humble" or was he being "Fearful"? Given the later developments, many commentators see this as a sign of his "Indecisiveness." He was a king who looked like a giant but felt like a child. He was hiding from the responsibility that God had clearly placed on him.

5 Saul kept Silent (Kemach-arish): Literally "Like a deaf man." In the scrolls, this was Saul’s "Best Moment." He didn't use his new power to execute his critics. He showed the restraint of a true leader. This "Humble Silence" would soon be replaced by "Paranoid Rage" in the years to come.

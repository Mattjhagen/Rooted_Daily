1 Samuel 12

1Samuel said to all Israel, “I have listened to everything you said to me and have set a king over you.”
3“Here I stand. Testify against me in the presence of the LORD and his anointed. Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey have I taken? Whom have I cheated? Whom have I oppressed?” 1
4“You have not cheated or oppressed us,” they replied. “You have not taken anything from anyone’s hand.”
7“Now then, stand here, because I am going to confront you with evidence before the LORD as to all the righteous acts performed by the LORD for you and your ancestors.” 2
12“But when you saw that Nahash king of the Ammonites was moving against you, you said to me, ‘No, we want a king to rule over us’—even though the LORD your God was your king.”

The Sign of the Harvest Rain

16“Now then, stand still and see this great thing the LORD is about to do before your eyes!”
17“Is it not wheat harvest now? I will call on the LORD to send thunder and rain. And you will realize what an evil thing you did in the eyes of the LORD when you asked for a king.” 3
18Then Samuel called on the LORD, and that same day the LORD sent thunder and rain. So all the people stood in awe of the LORD and of Samuel.
20“Do not be afraid,” Samuel replied. “You have done all this evil; yet do not turn away from the LORD, but serve the LORD with all your heart.”
22“For the sake of his great name the LORD will not reject his people, because the LORD was pleased to make you his own.” 4
23“As for me, far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD by failing to pray for you. And I will teach you the way that is good and right.” 5
25“But if you persist in doing evil, both you and your king will be swept away.”

Translation Notes

1 Integrity Test: In the scrolls, Samuel’s challenge is a "Legal Clearing." He wanted it on the record that his leadership was purely based on service, not greed. This stands in direct contrast to the "Warning of the King" in Chapter 8, where he said the King would "Take." Samuel "Gave"; the King would "Take."

2 Confront with Evidence (Ishaph-tah): Literally in the scrolls, "I will judge you." Samuel was acting as the Prosecutor for God. He was showing them that their history was a cycle of "God’s Faithfulness" vs. "Israel’s Faithlessness." Their request for a king was the latest example of their failure to trust the God who had already rescued them dozens of times.

3 Thunder and Rain: In the scrolls, rain during the "Wheat Harvest" (May/June) was a disaster, not a blessing. It would destroy the crops. Samuel was using a "Nature Miracle" to show the "Unnaturalness" of their request for a king. It was a physical manifestation of God’s displeasure. The people’s terror proved they finally understood the gravity of their rejection.

4 For the Sake of His Great Name: This is a powerful theological note in the scrolls. God’s commitment to Israel was not based on their "Performance" but on His "Reputation" (*Shem*). He had linked His name to theirs. This provided the "Basis for Hope" even after their great sin.

5 Sin by failing to pray: This is a profound insight into the "Prophetic Office" in the scrolls. Samuel believed that "Intercession" was a mandatory duty, not an optional extra. To stop praying for a rebellious people was, in his eyes, a sin against God. He remained their "Spiritual Shield" even as he stepped down from "Political Rule."

1 Samuel 7

1So the men of Kiriath Jearim came and took up the ark of the LORD. They brought it to Abinadab’s house on the hill and consecrated Eleazar his son to guard the ark of the LORD.
2The ark remained at Kiriath Jearim a long time—twenty years in all—and all the people of Israel mourned and sought after the LORD. 1
3So Samuel said to all the Israelites, “If you are returning to the LORD with all your hearts, then rid yourselves of the foreign gods and the Ashtoreths and commit yourselves to the LORD and serve him only; and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines.” 2
4So the Israelites put away their Baals and Ashtoreths, and served the LORD only.
5Then Samuel said, “Assemble all Israel at Mizpah, and I will intercede with the LORD for you.”
6When they had assembled at Mizpah, they drew water and poured it out before the LORD. On that day they fasted and there they confessed, “We have sinned against the LORD.”
7When the Philistines heard that Israel had assembled at Mizpah, the rulers of the Philistines came up to attack them. When the Israelites heard of it, they were afraid...
10While Samuel was sacrificing the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to engage Israel in battle. But that day the LORD thundered with loud thunder against the Philistines and threw them into such a panic that they were routed before the Israelites. 3
12Then Samuel took a stone and set it up between Mizpah and Shen. He named it Ebenezer, saying, “Thus far the LORD has helped us.” 4
13So the Philistines were subdued and they stopped invading Israel’s territory. Throughout Samuel’s lifetime, the hand of the LORD was against the Philistines.
15Samuel continued as Israel’s leader all the days of his life.
16From year to year he went on a circuit from Bethel to Gilgal to Mizpah, judging Israel in all those places. 5

Translation Notes

1 All Israel Mourned (Vayye-nahu): The word in the scrolls implies a "Lamenting Search." After 20 years of living without the Ark in the Tabernacle (which was likely destroyed after Shiloh’s fall), the nation began to feel the spiritual void. They weren't just sad; they were "Looking for the Presence." This hunger for God is the necessary precursor to Samuel’s reform.

2 Rid yourselves of Foreign Gods: Samuel’s message in the scrolls is clear: "Revival" requires "Renunciation." You cannot seek God while keeping the idols of the culture. The Ashtoreths were fertility goddesses; the Baals were storm/grain gods. Samuel was demanding a "Total Monotheism." This was the first time the nation had actually obeyed this command since the days of Joshua.

3 The Thunder of the LORD: In the scrolls, this is the "Answer from Heaven." The Philistines worshiped Ba’al-Hadad, who was supposed to be the "Lord of the Thunder." By thundering against them, Yahweh was demonstrating His superiority over their own god. He used their own "Fear" against them. It was a victory won by prayer and sacrifice, not by military planning.

4 Ebenezer (Even ha-Ezer): Literally in the scrolls, "The Stone of Help." This was not just a memorial of a past event; it was a "Marker of Progress." By saying "Thus far," Samuel was acknowledging that the victory was a step in a journey. It is a confession that they are entirely dependent on God for every future success. Ebenezer became the name for "Faithful Providence."

5 Samuel’s Circuit: In the scrolls, Samuel established a "Model of Justice." He didn't wait for people to come to him; he went to them. He traveled to the historic centers of the faith (Bethel, Gilgal, Mizpah) to ensure the Law was being applied. He was the last of the Judges and the first of the regular Prophets, bridging the gap between "Crisis Leadership" and "Institutional Governance."

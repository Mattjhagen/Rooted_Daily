1 Samuel 14

6Jonathan said to his young armor-bearer, “Come, let’s go over to the outpost of those uncircumcised men. Perhaps the LORD will act in our behalf. Nothing can hinder the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few.” 1
13Jonathan climbed up, using his hands and feet, with his armor-bearer right behind him. The Philistines fell before Jonathan, and his armor-bearer followed and killed them.
15Then panic struck the whole Philistine army... and the ground shook. It was a panic sent by God.
24Now the Israelites were in distress that day, because Saul had bound the people under an oath, saying, “Cursed be anyone who eats food before evening, before I have avenged myself on my enemies!” 2
27But Jonathan had not heard that his father had bound the people with the oath, so he reached out the end of the staff that was in his hand and dipped it into the honeycomb. He raised his hand to his mouth, and his eyes brightened. 3
37So Saul asked God, “Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you give them into Israel’s hand?” But God did not answer him that day.
44Saul said, “May God deal with me, be it ever so severely, if you do not die, Jonathan.”
45But the men said to Saul, “Should Jonathan die—he who has brought about this great deliverance in Israel? Never! As surely as the LORD lives, not a hair of his head shall fall to the ground...” So the men rescued Jonathan, and he was not put to death. 4
47After Saul had assumed rule over Israel, he fought against their enemies on every side... Whatever way he turned, he inflicted punishment on them.
52All the days of Saul there was bitter war with the Philistines, and whenever Saul saw a mighty or brave man, he helped him into his service. 5

Translation Notes

1 Many or Few: This is a "Core Principle of the Kingdom" in the scrolls. Jonathan understood that victory was a matter of God’s "Will," not human "Mass." While his father Saul was sitting under a pomegranate tree (waiting for a perfect opportunity), Jonathan was acting on "Possibility." He is the first true "Warrior of Faith" in the monarchical era.

2 Saul’s Rash Oath: In the scrolls, this is a sign of Saul’s "Legalistic Superstition." He thought he could bribe God for a victory by forcing his army to fast. Instead of empowering them, he exhausted them, leading to them eventually sinning by eating meat with the blood. It shows a leader who is "Out of Touch" with both God and his men.

3 Eyes Brightened (Va-Ta-ornah): This is a beautiful physiological detail in the scrolls. The honey gave Jonathan an immediate surge of glucose, restoring his physical strength and clarity. But it also serves as a metaphor for "Enlightenment." Jonathan saw the foolishness of his father’s oath that the others were too afraid to voice.

4 The People Rescued Jonathan: This was a "Constitutional Crisis" in the scrolls. The people’s intervention saved the hero of the day from his father’s "Religious Ego." It shows that the "Divine Right of Kings" was already being balanced by the "Common Sense" of the community. Saul was ready to kill his own son to keep a foolish oath, showing his distorted priorities.

5 Saul’s Recruitment: The final verse of the scrolls in this chapter sets the stage for David’s entry. Saul was always looking for "Mighty Men." He was building an army based on "Human Prowess." While this made him militarily successful, it also made him vulnerable to the "Comparison Trap" that would eventually lead to his jealousy of David.

1 Samuel 2

Then Hannah prayed and said:
“My heart rejoices in the LORD;
in the LORD my horn is lifted high.”
“There is no one holy like the LORD;
there is no one besides you;
there is no Rock like our God.” 1
“The LORD brings death and makes alive;
he brings down to the grave and raises up.”
“The LORD sends poverty and wealth;
he humbles and he exalts.”
“He raises the poor from the dust
and lifts the needy from the ash heap;
he seats them with princes
and has them inherit a throne of honor.” 2
“The LORD will judge the ends of the earth.
He will give strength to his king
and exalt the horn of his anointed.”

The Sons of Eli

12Eli’s sons were scoundrels; they had no regard for the LORD. 3
17This sin of the young men was very great in the LORD’s sight, for they were treating the LORD’s offering with contempt.

The Boy Samuel

18But Samuel was ministering before the LORD—a boy wearing a linen ephod.
19Each year his mother made him a little robe and took it to him. 4
22Now Eli, who was very old, heard about everything his sons were doing... and how they slept with the women who served at the entrance to the tent of meeting.
25“If one person sins against another, God may mediate for the offender; but if anyone sins against the LORD, who will intercede for them?” His sons, however, did not listen to their father’s rebuke, for it was the LORD’s will to put them to death.
27Now a man of God came to Eli and said... “Why do you scorn my sacrifice and offering... honoring your sons more than me?”
34“‘And what happens to your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will be a sign to you—they will both die on the same day.’”
35“‘I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who will do according to what is in my heart and mind. I will firmly establish his priestly house...’” 5

Translation Notes

1 My Horn is Lifted: In the scrolls, the "Horn" (*Keren*) is a symbol of strength and dignity. For a barren woman, her "horn was in the dust." Hannah’s song is a celebration of "Reversed Fortunes." She recognizes that the Rock of Israel is the only one who can lift the humble and bring down the proud.

2 Lifts the Needy from the Ash Heap: This is the "Social Gospel" of the scrolls. Hannah understands that God is not a supporter of the status quo. He is the Great Equalizer who takes those discarded by society (on the ash heap) and places them in positions of power. This song provides the theological template for the Virgin Mary’s *Magnificat* centuries later.

3 Eli’s Sons (Bnei Belial): Literally in the scrolls, "Sons of Worthlessness." They were using their religious office for "Physical Appetite" and "Sexual Exploitation." They were treating the Tabernacle as their personal restaurant and bordello. Their "Contempt" for the offering was actually a contempt for God Himself.

4 The Little Robe (Me’il Katan): This is a touching detail in the scrolls. Every year, Hannah would bring Samuel a new robe as he grew. It shows that while she had dedicated him to the LORD, she never stopped being his mother. In the scrolls, the *Me’il* was also a garment of high status; she was literally "clothing him" for the greatness that God had planned for him.

5 A Faithful Priest: This prophecy in the scrolls has multiple layers. It refers immediately to Zadok (who replaced the line of Eli in the time of Solomon) and ultimately to the Messiah. It teaches that when the "Official Religious Institutions" become corrupt, God will bypass them to raise up something "Faithful" from the outside.

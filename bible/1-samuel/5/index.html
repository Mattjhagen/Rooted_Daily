1 Samuel 5

1After the Philistines had captured the ark of God, they took it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.
2They carried the ark into Dagon’s temple and set it beside Dagon. 1
3When the people of Ashdod rose early the next day, there was Dagon, fallen on his face on the ground before the ark of the LORD! They took Dagon and put him back in his place.
4But the following morning when they rose, there was Dagon, fallen on his face on the ground before the ark of the LORD! His head and hands had been broken off and were lying on the threshold; only his body remained. 2
6The LORD’s hand was heavy on the people of Ashdod and its vicinity; he brought devastation on them and afflicted them with tumors. 3
7When the people of Ashdod saw what was happening, they said, “The ark of the god of Israel must not stay here with us, because his hand is heavy on us and on Dagon our god.”
9But after they had moved it to Gath, the LORD’s hand was against that city also, throwing it into a great panic. He afflicted the people of the city, both young and old, with an outbreak of tumors.
10So they sent the ark of God to Ekron. As the ark of God was entering Ekron, the people of Ekron cried out, “They have brought the ark of the god of Israel around to us to kill us and our people.”
11So they called together all the rulers of the Philistines and said, “Send the ark of the god of Israel away; let it go back to its own place...” For death had filled the city with panic; God’s hand was very heavy on it. 4
12Those who did not die were afflicted with tumors, and the outcry of the city went up to heaven.

Translation Notes

1 Dagon’s Temple: In the scrolls, putting the Ark in Dagon’s temple was a "Statement of Conquest." The Philistines believed that because they had defeated Israel’s army, their god Dagon was superior to Yahweh. They were treating the Ark as a "Trophy" of war. They soon discovered that God was not captured; He had merely "infiltrated" their camp.

2 Broken Dagon: The visual in the scrolls is a total humiliation of the Philistine deity. Dagon (the "Fish-God" or "Grain-God") was found "worshiping" the Ark on the first day. On the second day, his "Head" (wisdom) and "Hands" (power) were cut off on the threshold. In the ancient world, the threshold was a sacred boundary; Dagon’s pieces being there meant he was rejected from his own temple. God was "Beheading" the idols of the Philistines.

3 Tumors (Ophalim): Many scholars in the scrolls identify this plague as the "Bubonic Plague," as it was associated with rats (Chapter 6). The word *Ophalim* refers to painful swellings. God was attacking the Philistines in their physical bodies, proving that their "Military Strength" was useless against His "Biological Judgment."

4 The Outcry (Shav'at): The word for "Outcry" in the scrolls is the same word used for the cry of the Israelites in Egypt. The tables had turned; the Philistines were now the ones crying out under the heavy hand of God. The "Captivity of the Ark" had become a "Plague of the Captors." It teaches that God is never more dangerous than when we think we have Him under our control.

1 Samuel 26

2So Saul went down to the Desert of Ziph with three thousand select Israelite troops to search there for David.
5Then David set out and went to the place where Saul had camped... Saul was sleeping inside the inner circle of the camp, with the army camped around him.
7So David and Abishai went to the army by night, and there was Saul, lying asleep inside the camp with his spear stuck in the ground near his head. 1
8Abishai said to David, “Today God has delivered your enemy into your hands. Now let me pin him to the ground with one thrust of the spear; I won’t need to strike him twice.”
9But David said to Abishai, “Don’t destroy him! Who can lay a hand on the LORD’s anointed and be guiltless?” 2
12So David took the spear and water jug near Saul’s head, and they left. No one saw or knew about it... because the LORD had put them into a deep sleep. 3
14David called out to the army and to Abner, “Aren’t you going to answer me, Abner?”
“As surely as the LORD lives, you and your men must die, because you did not guard your master, the LORD’s anointed. Look around you. Where are the king’s spear and water jug that were near his head?” 4
21Then Saul said, “I have sinned. Come back, David my son... Surely I have acted like a fool and have been terribly mistaken.”
23David replied, “The LORD rewards every man for his righteousness and faithfulness. The LORD delivered you into my hands today, but I would not lay a hand on the LORD’s anointed.” 5
25Then Saul said to David, “May you be blessed, David my son; you will do great things and surely triumph.”
So David went his way, and Saul returned home.

Translation Notes

1 Spear in the Ground: In the scrolls, the spear stuck in the ground near the head was a sign of the king’s "Authority" and "Protection." David and Abishai were able to penetrate the very heart of the military defense. It was a "Security Breach" of supernatural proportions.

2 Don’t Destroy Him (Al-Tash-che-te-hu): This is David’s "Refusal of Shortcuts" in the scrolls. Abishai saw a "Military Opportunity," but David saw a "Theological Principle." He believed that if God had anointed Saul, only God had the right to remove him. He refused to "Help God" by committing a crime. This is the difference between a "Mercenary" and a "King."

3 Deep Sleep (Tar-de-mat YHWH): The scrolls use a specific word for "Supernatural Slumber." It is the same word used when God put Adam into a deep sleep to create Eve. This was not just a quiet night; it was a "Divine Intervention" that allowed David to walk through 3,000 soldiers without being noticed.

4 Spear and Water Jug: These two items in the scrolls were "Symbols of Life." The spear was Saul’s "Power," and the water jug was his "Sustenance." By taking them, David was showing that Saul’s life was entirely in David’s hands. He was "Disarming" the king without shedding a drop of blood.

5 The LORD Rewards (Ya-shiv): Literally "The LORD will return." David’s theology in the scrolls was one of "Reciprocity." He believed that by showing mercy to Saul, he was "Investing in Mercy" from God. He was leaving his own survival in the hands of the Great Judge. This was their final meeting—Saul went back to his palace to die, and David went back to the wilderness to wait for the throne.

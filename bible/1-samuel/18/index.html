1 Samuel 18

1After David had finished talking with Saul, Jonathan became one in spirit with David, and he loved him as himself.
3And Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as himself.
4Jonathan took off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his tunic, and even his sword, his bow and his belt. 1
“Saul has slain his thousands,
and David his ten thousands.” 2
8Saul was very angry; this refrain displeased him greatly. “They have credited David with ten thousands,” he thought, “but me with only thousands. What more can he get but the kingdom?”
9And from that time on Saul kept a close eye on David.
10The next day an evil spirit from God came forcefully on Saul... While David was playing the lyre, as he usually did, Saul had a spear in his hand
11and he hurled it, saying to himself, “I’ll pin David to the wall.” But David eluded him twice. 3
12Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD was with David but had departed from Saul.
20Now Saul’s daughter Michal was in love with David...
21Saul thought, “I will give her to him, so that she may be a snare to him and so that the hand of the Philistines may be against him.” 4
25Saul replied, “The king wants no other price for the bride than a hundred Philistine foreskins, to take revenge on his enemies.”
27David and his men went out and killed two hundred Philistines. He brought their foreskins and presented the full number to the king... Then Saul gave him his daughter Michal in marriage. 5
29Saul became still more afraid of David, and he remained his enemy the rest of his days.

Translation Notes

1 Transfer of the Robe: In the scrolls, this was a "Legal and Symbolic Abdication." By giving David his robe and armor, Jonathan (the crown prince) was publicly acknowledging that David was the true heir to the throne. It was a remarkable act of "Friendship over Ambition." Jonathan loved the "Kingdom" more than his own "Position."

2 Song of Ten Thousands: This was the "Folk Liturgy" of the day in the scrolls. The women were simply stating a military fact, but to Saul’s insecure ears, it was a "Treasonous Comparison." This song became the catalyst for Saul’s "Paranoia." It shows the danger of "Human Praise"—it can make one person a hero and another a monster.

3 Saul’s Spear: In the scrolls, Saul’s javelin was the "Instrument of his Insanity." He was trying to "Forcefully End" what God had "Supernaturally Begun." The fact that David eluded him "twice" was a sign to Saul that David was under divine protection. Saul was literally "Fighting God."

4 Michal as a Snare: In the scrolls, Saul’s use of his daughters (Merab and Michal) as "Political Bait" shows how far he had fallen. He was no longer a "Father" but a "Manipulator." He hoped that David’s "Desire" for Michal would lead to David’s "Death" in battle. It was a "Passive-Aggressive Murder Attempt."

5 Two Hundred Foreskins: David doubled the required price in the scrolls. This was his "Over-Performance" to prove his loyalty and his prowess. Instead of David being killed, he became twice the hero he was before. Saul’s plan backfired, and he ended up having to give his daughter to the man he feared most.

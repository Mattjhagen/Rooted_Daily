1 Samuel 4

1Now the Israelites went out to fight against the Philistines. The Israelites camped at Ebenezer, and the Philistines at Aphek.
2The Philistines deployed their forces... and when the battle spread, they defeated Israel, killing about four thousand of them.
3When the soldiers returned to camp, the elders of Israel asked, “Why did the LORD bring defeat on us today before the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the LORD’s covenant from Shiloh, so that it may go with us and save us from the hand of our enemies.” 1
5When the ark of the LORD’s covenant came into the camp, all Israel raised such a great shout that the ground shook.
7The Philistines were afraid. “A god has come into the camp,” they said. “Oh no! Nothing like this has happened before.”
10So the Philistines fought, and the Israelites were defeated and every man fled to his tent. The slaughter was very great; Israel lost thirty thousand foot soldiers.
11The ark of God was captured, and Eli’s two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, died. 2
13When the man arrived, he found Eli sitting on his chair by the side of the road, watching, because his heart trembled for the ark of God.
17The man who brought the news replied, “Israel fled before the Philistines... and the ark of God has been captured.”
18When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backward off his chair by the side of the gate. His neck was broken and he died, for he was an old man, and he was heavy. He had led Israel forty years. 3
21She named the boy Ichabod, saying, “The Glory has departed from Israel”—because of the capture of the ark of God and the deaths of her father-in-law and her husband. 4
22She said, “The Glory has departed from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured.”

Translation Notes

1 Superstition of the Ark: In the scrolls, the elders’ logic is fatally flawed. They treated the Ark as a "Magic Charm" or a "Lucky Mascot" rather than the throne of a Holy God. They thought they could bring the "Box" and force God to win. They were trying to manipulate God’s power without submitting to God’s holiness.

2 The Death of the Sons: This was the literal fulfillment of the prophecy given to Eli in Chapter 2. Hophni and Phinehas died on the same day. In the scrolls, their presence with the Ark was the ultimate insult; they were priests who didn't know God, guarding an Ark they didn't respect.

3 Eli’s End: The detail in the scrolls that Eli was "heavy" and fell "backward" is a graphic representation of the weight of his failure. He was sitting on a "chair" (*Kisse*—the same word for a throne), but his rule was weak. His death marks the final end of the era of the Judges and the transition to the leadership of Samuel.

4 Ichabod (I-Kavod): Literally in the scrolls, "Where is the Glory?" or "No Glory." The *Kavod* (Glory) of God was the visible manifestation of His presence among the people. Without the Ark and without a faithful priesthood, the nation was spiritually "Empty." The birth of Ichabod is the low point of Israel’s history since the crossing of the Jordan.

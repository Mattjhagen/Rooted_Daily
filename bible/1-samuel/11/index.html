1 Samuel 11

1Nahash the Ammonite went up and besieged Jabesh Gilead. All the men of Jabesh said to him, “Make a treaty with us, and we will be subject to you.”
2But Nahash the Ammonite replied, “I will make a treaty with you only on the condition that I gouge out the right eye of every one of you and so bring disgrace on all Israel.” 1
5Just then Saul was returning from the fields, behind his oxen...
6When he heard their words, the Spirit of God came powerfully upon him, and he burned with anger. 2
7He took a pair of oxen, cut them into pieces, and sent the pieces by messengers throughout Israel, proclaiming, “This is what will be done to the oxen of anyone who does not follow Saul and Samuel.”
7...Then the terror of the LORD fell on the people, and they came out as one man. 3
8When Saul mustered them at Bezek, the men of Israel numbered three hundred thousand and those of Judah thirty thousand.
11The next day Saul separated his men into three companies; they broke into the Ammonite camp during the last watch of the night and slaughtered them until the heat of the day.
13But Saul said, “No one will be put to death today, for this day the LORD has rescued Israel.” 4
14Then Samuel said to the people, “Come, let us go to Gilgal and there reaffirm the kingship.”
15So all the people went to Gilgal and made Saul king in the presence of the LORD. There they sacrificed fellowship offerings before the LORD, and Saul and all the Israelites held a great celebration. 5

Translation Notes

1 Gouge out the Right Eye: This was a "Calculated Military Disgrace" in the scrolls. In ancient warfare, the shield covered the left eye, meaning a soldier needed his right eye to aim and fight. By blinding their right eyes, Nahash was making them militarily useless while keeping them alive as slaves. It was a total insult to the "Honor of Israel."

2 Spirit of God / Burned with Anger: This is a unique combination in the scrolls. The "Anger" (*Af*) of Saul was not a fleshly rage, but a "Divinely Inspired Indignation." The Spirit of God empowered his emotions to match God’s own feelings about the injustice. This is Saul’s "Peak"—he is acting as the perfect instrument of the Covenant.

3 Terror of the LORD (Pachad YHWH): In the scrolls, this is the supernatural "Unity" that fell on the nation. 330,000 men responded immediately. This was not just loyalty to Saul; it was a realization that God was moving. The "Dismembered Oxen" was a graphic reminder of the covenant curses, which sparked a "Holy Fear."

4 No one put to death today: Again, the scrolls show Saul at his most gracious. He refused to use his victory to settle personal scores with the "scoundrels" who had previously doubted him. He gave the "Rescuing Credit" entirely to the LORD. At this point, Saul is the "Ideal King."

5 Reaffirm the Kingship: This ceremony at Gilgal was the "Official Coronation." In the scrolls, there is a distinction between the "Private Choice" (Chapter 9), the "Lot Selection" (Chapter 10), and the "Military Validation" (Chapter 11). The nation was finally "All In" on Saul’s leadership.

1 Samuel 3

1The boy Samuel ministered before the LORD under Eli. In those days the word of the LORD was rare; there were not many visions. 1
2One night Eli, whose eyes were becoming so weak that he could barely see, was lying down in his usual place. 2
3The lamp of God had not yet gone out, and Samuel was lying down in the house of the LORD, where the ark of God was.
4Then the LORD called Samuel. Samuel answered, “Here I am.”
5And he ran to Eli and said, “Here I am; you called me.” But Eli said, “I did not call; go back and lie down.” So he went and lay down.
7Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD: The word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him. 3
8A third time the LORD called, “Samuel!” And Samuel got up and went to Eli... Then Eli realized that the LORD was calling the boy.
9So Eli told Samuel, “Go and lie down, and if he calls you, say, ‘Speak, LORD, for your servant is listening.’”
10The LORD came and stood there, calling as at the other times, “Samuel! Samuel!” Then Samuel said, “Speak, for your servant is listening.” 4
11And the LORD said to Samuel: “See, I am about to do something in Israel that will make the ears of everyone who hears about it tingle.”
13“For I told him that I would judge his family forever because of the sin he knew about; his sons made themselves contemptible, and he failed to restrain them.”
18So Samuel told him everything, hiding nothing from him. Then Eli said, “He is the LORD; let him do what is good in his eyes.”
19The LORD was with Samuel as he grew up, and he let none of Samuel’s words fall to the ground. 5
20And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba recognized that Samuel was attested as a prophet of the LORD.

Translation Notes

1 Rare Word (Yakar): The word in the scrolls implies something "Precious" or "Scarce." Because of the corruption in the priesthood (Chapter 2), the communication channel between God and His people was blocked. Visions were not "breaking through" (*Nifratz*). This is the setting for Samuel’s call—a world starving for a fresh word from God.

2 Eli’s Eyesight: In the scrolls, Eli’s "weak eyes" are a metaphor for his "Spiritual Blindness." He could not see the corruption in his own house, and he was failing to see the vision of God. The transition from the old priest (who can't see) to the young prophet (who can hear) is the central theme of the chapter.

3 Did not yet know the LORD: Samuel knew *about* God from his service in the Tabernacle, but he had not yet had a "Personal Encounter." In the scrolls, this distinction is vital. One can be religious and serve in the church without having "Known the Voice." God was moving Samuel from "Information" to "Revelation."

4 The LORD came and stood there: This is a "Theophany" in the scrolls. God did not just send a sound; He manifested His presence. Samuel’s response, "Speak, for your servant is listening," is the "Master Key" to prophetic authority. He puts himself in the position of a servant (*Eved*) before he is ever called a prophet.

5 None of his words fell to the ground: In the scrolls, this is the mark of a "True Prophet." Every word Samuel spoke was backed by the power of God and came to pass. His "Accuracy" was 100%. Because he was faithful to listen to God in the dark, God was faithful to validate his words in the light.

Numbers 17

1The LORD said to Moses,
2“Speak to the Israelites and get twelve staffs from them, one from the leader of each ancestral tribe. Write the name of each man on his staff.”
3“On the staff of Levi write Aaron’s name, for there must be one staff for the head of each ancestral tribe.”
4“Place them in the tent of meeting in front of the ark of the Covenant Law, where I meet with you.”
5“The staff belonging to the man I choose will sprout, and I will rid myself of this constant grumbling against you by the Israelites.” 1
6So Moses spoke to the Israelites, and their leaders gave him twelve staffs, one for the leader of each ancestral tribe, and Aaron’s staff was among them.
7Moses placed the staffs before the LORD in the tent of the Covenant Law.
8The next day Moses entered the tent and saw that Aaron’s staff, which represented the tribe of Levi, had not only sprouted but had budded, blossomed and produced ripe almonds. 2
9Then Moses brought out all the staffs from the LORD’s presence to all the Israelites. They looked at them, and each leader took his own staff.
10The LORD said to Moses, “Put back Aaron’s staff in front of the ark of the Covenant Law, to be kept as a sign to the rebellious. This will put an end to their grumbling against me, so that they will not die.” 3
11Moses did just as the LORD commanded him.
12The Israelites said to Moses, “We will die! We are lost, we are all lost!”
13“Anyone who even comes near the tabernacle of the LORD will die. Are we all going to die?”

Translation Notes

1 The Man I Choose: In the scrolls, this miracle was the final answer to Korah's claim that "the whole community is holy." While the whole community was indeed "set apart," God had a specific *functional* design for the Priesthood. The miracle was meant to end the lethal grumbling that was destroying the nation.

2 Ripe Almonds (Shaked): The choice of almonds in the original scrolls is significant. The Hebrew word for almond, *Shaked*, comes from a root meaning "to watch" or "to be wakeful," because the almond tree is the first to bloom in late winter. It symbolizes that God is "watching over" His word to perform it. The staff went through a whole season of growth (buds, blossoms, fruit) in a single night—a sign of God’s life-giving power vs. the death brought by the rebels.

3 Sign to the Rebellious: The staff was placed inside or in front of the Ark of the Covenant, alongside the Tablets of the Law and the jar of Manna. It served as a permanent witness. In the scrolls, it is a reminder that true authority is not "seized" by force, but "buds" from the life of God.

Numbers 27

1The daughters of Zelophehad son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Makir, the son of Manasseh, belonged to the clans of Manasseh son of Joseph. The names of the daughters were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milkah and Tirzah. They stepped forward
2and stood before Moses, Eleazar the priest, the leaders and the whole assembly at the entrance to the tent of meeting and said,
3“Our father died in the wilderness. He was not among Korah’s followers, who banded together against the LORD, but he died for his own sin and left no sons.”
4“Why should our father’s name disappear from his clan because he had no son? Give us property among our father’s relatives.” 1
5So Moses brought their case before the LORD,
6and the LORD said to him,
7“What Zelophehad’s daughters are saying is right. You must certainly give them property as an inheritance among their father’s relatives and give their father’s inheritance to them.”
8“Say to the Israelites, ‘If a man dies and leaves no son, give his inheritance to his daughter.”
9“‘If he has no daughter, give his inheritance to his brothers.”
10“‘If he has no brothers, give it to his father’s brothers.”
11“‘If his father had no brothers, give his inheritance to the nearest relative in his clan, that he may possess it. This is to have the force of law for the Israelites, as the LORD commanded Moses.’”
12Then the LORD said to Moses, “Go up this mountain in the Abarim range and see the land I have given the Israelites.”
13“After you have seen it, you too will be gathered to your people, as your brother Aaron was,”
14“for when the community rebelled at the waters in the Desert of Zin, both of you disobeyed my command to honor me as holy before their eyes.” (These were the waters of Meribah Kadesh, in the Desert of Zin.)
15Moses said to the LORD,
16“May the LORD, the God who gives breath to all living things, appoint someone over this community” 2
17“to go out and come in before them, one who will lead them out and bring them in, so the LORD’s people will not be like sheep without a shepherd.”
18So the LORD said to Moses, “Take Joshua son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit of leadership, and lay your hand on him.” 3
19“Have him stand before Eleazar the priest and the entire assembly and commission him in their presence.”
20“Give him some of your authority so the whole Israelite community will obey him.”
21“He is to stand before Eleazar the priest, who will obtain decisions for him by inquiring of the Urim before the LORD. At his command he and the entire community of the Israelites will go out, and at his command they will come in.” 4
22Moses did as the LORD commanded him. He took Joshua and had him stand before Eleazar the priest and the whole assembly.
23Then he laid his hands on him and commissioned him, as the LORD instructed through Moses.

Translation Notes

1 Daughters of Zelophehad: This is a landmark moment in the scrolls for the status of women. In most ancient cultures, land could not be inherited by daughters. These five women showed remarkable faith and courage by bringing their case directly to Moses. They didn't see themselves as victims; they saw themselves as legal stakeholders in the Promised Land. God not only grants their request but turns it into a permanent law.

2 God who gives breath: In the original scrolls, Moses addresses God as *Elohei ha-ruchot l'chol basar* (God of the spirits of all flesh). It is a title that acknowledges God as the source of life and the Knower of human hearts. Even when faced with his own impending death, Moses’ first concern is for the "sheep"—the community he led for forty years.

3 Spirit of Leadership: The scrolls describe Joshua as a man *ashir ruach bo*—one in whom there is spirit. Moses was commanded to "lay his hand" on Joshua. This is the origin of *Semikhah* (ordination). Moses was "pouring" his authority into Joshua so that the transition would be visible and undisputed by the people.

4 The Urim: In the scrolls, the method of leadership for Joshua was different than for Moses. Moses spoke with God "mouth to mouth." Joshua would receive guidance through the High Priest using the *Urim* (lights) and *Thummim* (perfections) on the priestly breastplate. It represents a transition from a "Charismatic" leader to an "Institutional" leader, where the office of the Prophet and the Priest work together.

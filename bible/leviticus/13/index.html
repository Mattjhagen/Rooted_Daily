Leviticus 13

1The LORD said to Moses and Aaron,
2“When anyone has a swelling or a rash or a shiny spot on their skin that may be a defiling skin disease, they must be brought to Aaron the priest or to one of his sons who is a priest.” 1
3“The priest is to examine the sore on the skin, and if the hair in the sore has turned white and the sore appears to be more than skin deep, it is a defiling skin disease. When the priest examines that person, he shall pronounce them ceremonially unclean.”
4“If the spot on the skin is white but does not appear to be more than skin deep and the hair in it has not turned white, the priest is to isolate the affected person for seven days.”
5“On the seventh day the priest is to examine them, and if he sees that the sore is unchanged and has not spread on the skin, he is to isolate them for another seven days.”
6“On the seventh day the priest is to examine them again, and if the sore has faded and has not spread on the skin, the priest shall pronounce them clean; it is only a rash. They must wash their clothes, and they will be clean.”
7“But if the rash spreads on their skin after they have shown themselves to the priest to be pronounced clean, they must appear before the priest again.”
8“The priest is to examine them, and if the rash has spread on the skin, he shall pronounce them unclean; it is a defiling skin disease.”
9“When anyone has a defiling skin disease, they must be brought to the priest.”
10“The priest is to examine them, and if there is a white swelling in the skin that has turned the hair white and if there is raw flesh in the swelling,”
11“it is a chronic skin disease and the priest shall pronounce them unclean. He is not to isolate them, because they are already unclean.”
12“If the disease breaks out all over their skin and, so far as the priest can see, it covers all the skin of the affected person from head to foot,”
13“the priest is to examine them, and if the disease has covered their whole body, he shall pronounce them clean. Since it has all turned white, they are clean.” 2
14“But whenever raw flesh appears on them, they will be unclean.”
15“When the priest sees the raw flesh, he shall pronounce them unclean. The raw flesh is unclean; it is a defiling disease.”
16“If the raw flesh changes and turns white, they must go to the priest.”
17“The priest is to examine them, and if the sores have turned white, the priest shall pronounce the affected person clean; then they will be clean.”
18“When someone has a boil on their skin and it heals,”
19“and in the place where the boil was, a white swelling or a reddish-white spot appears, they must present themselves to the priest.”
20“The priest is to examine it, and if it appears to be more than skin deep and the hair in it has turned white, the priest shall pronounce them unclean. It is a defiling skin disease that has broken out where the boil was.”
21“But if, when the priest examines it, there is no white hair in it and it is not more than skin deep and has faded, then the priest is to isolate them for seven days.”
22“If it is spreading on the skin, the priest shall pronounce them unclean; it is a defiling disease.”
23“But if the spot is unchanged and has not spread, it is only a scar from the boil, and the priest shall pronounce them clean.”
24“When someone has a burn on their skin and a reddish-white or white spot appears in the raw flesh of the burn,”
25“the priest is to examine the spot, and if the hair in it has turned white, and it appears to be more than skin deep, it is a defiling disease that has broken out in the burn. The priest shall pronounce them unclean; it is a defiling skin disease.”
26“But if the priest examines it and there is no white hair in the spot and it is not more than skin deep and has faded, then the priest is to isolate them for seven days.”
27“On the seventh day the priest is to examine them, and if it is spreading on the skin, the priest shall pronounce them unclean; it is a defiling skin disease.”
28“If, however, the spot is unchanged and has not spread on the skin but has faded, it is a swelling from the burn, and the priest shall pronounce them clean; it is only a scar from the burn.”
29“If a man or woman has a sore on their head or chin,”
30“the priest is to examine the sore, and if it appears to be more than skin deep and the hair in it is yellow and thin, the priest shall pronounce them unclean; it is a defiling skin disease of the head or chin.”
31“But if, when the priest examines the sore, it does not appear to be more than skin deep and there is no black hair in it, then the priest is to isolate the affected person for seven days.”
32“On the seventh day the priest is to examine the sore, and if it has not spread and there is no yellow hair in it and it does not appear to be more than skin deep,”
33“then the person must shave themselves, except for the sore, and the priest is to isolate them for another seven days.”
34“On the seventh day the priest is to examine the sore, and if it has not spread on the skin and does not appear to be more than skin deep, the priest shall pronounce them clean. They must wash their clothes, and they will be clean.”
35“But if the sore spreads on the skin after they are pronounced clean,”
36“the priest is to examine them, and if the sore has spread on the skin, the priest does not need to look for yellow hair; they are unclean.”
37“If, however, the sore is unchanged so far as the priest can see, and if black hair has grown in it, the sore is healed. They are clean, and the priest shall pronounce them clean.”
38“When a man or woman has white spots on the skin,”
39“the priest is to examine them, and if the spots are dull white, it is a harmless rash that has broken out on the skin; they are clean.”
40“A man who has lost his hair and is bald is clean.”
41“If he has lost his hair from the front of his scalp and has a bald forehead, he is clean.”
42“But if he has a reddish-white sore on his bald head or forehead, it is a defiling disease breaking out on his head or forehead.”
43“The priest is to examine him, and if the swollen sore on his head or forehead is reddish-white like a defiling skin disease,”
44“the man is diseased and is unclean. The priest shall pronounce him unclean because of the sore on his head.”
45“Anyone with such a defiling disease must wear torn clothes, let their hair be unkempt, cover the lower part of their face and cry out, ‘Unclean! Unclean!’” 3
46“As long as they have the disease they remain unclean. They must live alone; they must live outside the camp.”
47“As for any fabric that has a defiling mold in it, whether it is wool or linen,”
48“whether woven or knitted material of linen or wool, or any leather or anything made of leather,”
49“if the affected area in the fabric or the leather, whether woven or knitted material or any leather article, is greenish or reddish, it is a defiling mold and must be shown to the priest.”
50“The priest is to examine the affected area and isolate the article for seven days.”
51“On the seventh day he is to examine it, and if the mold has spread in the fabric, whether woven or knitted material, or leather, whatever its use, it is a persistent defiling mold; the article is unclean.”
52“He must burn the fabric, whether woven or knitted material of wool or linen, or any leather article that has the defiling mold, because the mold is persistent; the article must be burned.”
53“But if, when the priest examines it, the mold has not spread in the fabric, whether woven or knitted material, or leather article,”
54“he shall order that the affected article be washed. Then he is to isolate it for another seven days.”
55“After the affected article has been washed, the priest is to examine it, and if the mold has not changed its appearance, even though it has not spread, it is unclean. Burn it, no matter which side of the fabric has been affected.”
56“If, when the priest examines it, the mold has faded after the article has been washed, he is to tear the affected part out of the fabric, leather, or woven or knitted material.”
57“But if it reappears in the fabric, in the woven or knitted material, or in the leather article, it is a spreading mold; whatever has the mold must be burned.”
58“Any fabric, woven or knitted material, or any leather article that has been washed and is rid of the mold, must be washed again. Then it will be clean.”
59“These are the regulations concerning defiling molds in woolen or linen fabrics, woven or knitted material, or any leather article, for pronouncing them clean or unclean.”

Translation Notes

1 Tzara'at (Defiling Disease): In the original scrolls, the term *Tzara'at* is often mistranslated as leprosy. However, it covers a wide range of skin conditions and even mold in houses. According to tradition, it was often a spiritual malady (associated with *Lashon Hara* or evil speech) that manifested physically, forcing the offender into isolation to reflect on their impact on the community.

2 Covered all the skin: This seems counter-intuitive in the scrolls. If the disease spreads to the entire body, the person is pronounced clean. Ancient commentators suggest this is because once it covers everything, the internal "poison" has been fully expelled, or because the state is no longer a "sore" but a "change in skin color." It emphasizes that the priest is looking for specific ritual signs, not just a general sense of sickness.

3 Unclean! Unclean!: The cry of the sufferer in the scrolls served two purposes: it warned others to keep their distance to avoid ritual defilement, and it expressed the deep sorrow of being cut off from the Tabernacle and the community. It is a vivid picture of the isolating power of sin and death.

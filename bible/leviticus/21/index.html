Leviticus 21

1The LORD said to Moses, “Speak to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and say to them: ‘A priest must not make himself ceremonially unclean for any of his people who die,”
2“‘except for a close relative, such as his mother or father, his son or daughter, his brother,”
3“‘or an unmarried sister who is dependent on him since she has no husband—for her he may make himself unclean.”
4“‘He must not make himself unclean for people related to him by marriage, and so profane himself.”
5“‘Priests must not shave their heads or shave off the edges of their beards or cut their bodies.”
6“‘They must be holy to their God and must not profane the name of their God. Because they present the food offerings to the LORD, the food of their God, they are to be holy.” 1
7“‘They must not marry women defiled by prostitution or divorced from their husbands, because priests are holy to their God.”
8“‘Regard them as holy, because they offer up the food of your God. Consider them holy, because I the LORD am holy—I who make you holy.”
9“‘If a priest’s daughter defiles herself by becoming a prostitute, she disgraces her father; she must be burned in the fire.”
10“‘The high priest, the one among his brothers who has had the anointing oil poured on his head and who has been ordained to wear the priestly garments, must not let his hair become unkempt or tear his clothes.”
11“‘He must not enter a place where there is a dead body. He must not make himself unclean, even for his father or mother,”
12“‘nor leave the sanctuary of his God or profane it, because he has been dedicated by the anointing oil of his God. I am the LORD.” 2
13“‘The woman he marries must be a virgin.”
14“‘He must not marry a widow, a divorced woman, or a woman defiled by prostitution, but only a virgin from his own people,”
15“‘so he will not defile his offspring among his people. I am the LORD, who makes him holy.’”
16The LORD said to Moses,
17“Say to Aaron: ‘For the generations to come none of your descendants who has a defect may come near to offer the food of his God.” 3
18“‘No man who has any defect may come near: no one who is blind or lame, disfigured or deformed;”
19“‘no one with a crippled foot or hand,”
20“‘or who is a hunchback or a dwarf, or who has any eye defect, or who has festering or running sores or damaged testicles.”
21“‘No descendant of Aaron the priest who has any defect is to come near to present the food offerings to the LORD. He has a defect; he must not come near to offer the food of his God.”
22“‘He may eat the most holy food of his God, as well as the holy food;”
23“‘yet because of his defect, he must not go near the curtain or approach the altar, and so profane my sanctuary. I am the LORD, who makes them holy.’”
24So Moses told this to Aaron and his sons and to all the Israelites.

Translation Notes

1 Food of their God (Lechem Eloheihem): This metaphor in the scrolls does not mean that God eats. Instead, it signifies that the priests are like the domestic staff of a Royal Palace. Just as a king’s servants must be impeccable, the priests who handle the "Table of the LORD" must maintain the highest standard of personal purity.

2 Dedicated by the Anointing Oil (Nezer): The original scrolls use the word *Nezer*, which also means "crown." The anointing oil is seen as an invisible crown of holiness. Because the High Priest wears this crown, he is permanently "in service" and can never abandon his post, even for the funeral of his own parents. His life is entirely subsumed by his sacred office.

3 Physical Perfection: Modern readers often find the exclusion of people with defects from the altar troubling. However, in the scrolls, this is not a statement about the value of the person (the priest with a defect was still supported by the community and ate the holy food). Instead, it was about the symbolism of the ritual. The Tabernacle was a place of wholeness and life; therefore, the mediator had to represent physical "completeness" to mirror the perfection of God.

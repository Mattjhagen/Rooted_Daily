Ezekiel 31

The Majestic Cedar and the Depth of the Pit.

The Cedar in Lebanon

“Consider a cedar in Lebanon, with beautiful branches and forest shade; its top was among the thick foliage.” 1

The Envy of Eden

“All the trees of Eden in the garden of God envied it.” 2

Towering Pride

“Because it towered high and became proud of its height, I handed it over.” 3

Down to the Realm of the Dead

“I made the nations tremble at the sound of its fall when I brought it down to the realm of the dead.” 4
“This is Pharaoh and all his hordes, declares the Sovereign Lord.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Top among the Foliage (way-hī bhēn a-bhō-thīm tza-mer-tō): Literally "And its top was between the clouds/thick boughs." The scrolls highlight the "Arboreal Root of Ambition." "Humility" is "Uprooted" as the "Cedar" (*Erez*) seeks the "Sky." It signifies "The Spiritual Danger of Vertical Growth without Roots."

2 Envied it (way-qan-e-ū-hū): The scrolls highlight the "Competitive Root of Beauty." Even the "Trees of Eden" (*Trees of Pleasure*) were "Rooted" in "Jealousy" (*Qana*) toward it. It signify "The Aesthetic of Peerless Splendor."

3 Handed it Over (nā-tha-tī ō-thō be-yadh): Literally "I gave him into the hand." The scrolls highlight the "Judicial Root of Transference." The "Tree" is "Uprooted" from the "Waters" and "Rooted" in "Judgment." It signifies "The Divine Response to Self-Exaltation."

4 Realm of the Dead (she-ō-lāh): The scrolls highlight the "Subterranean Root of the End." The "Height" is "Uprooted" and replaced by the "Depth" (*Sheol*). It signify "The Gravity of Eternal Loss."

5 This is Pharaoh (hū Phar-a-ōh): The scrolls conclude with this "Identificatory Root of the Allegory." The "Cedar" is "Rooted" in the "King." It marks the transition to "The Lament for Pharaoh" in *Ezekiel 32*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Sunken Cedar." Down to the pit.

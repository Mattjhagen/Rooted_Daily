Ezekiel 17

The Two Eagles and the Majestic Cedar.

The First Eagle

“A great eagle with powerful wings and full plumage came to Lebanon and took the top of a cedar.” 1

The Turning Vine

“This vine now sent out its roots toward another great eagle.” 2
“Will it not be uprooted and stripped of its fruit?”

Broken Covenant

“He despised the oath by breaking the covenant... he will not escape.” 3

The Majestic Cedar

“I myself will take a shoot from the very top of a cedar.”
“It will produce branches and bear fruit and become a majestic cedar.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Top of a Cedar (eth-tza-me-reth ha-e-rez): In the scrolls, this is the "Arboreal Root of Royalty." Lebanon represents the "Forest of Kings." The "Top" (*Jehoiachin*) is "Uprooted" by "Babylon" (*The Eagle*). It signifies "The End of the Old Dynasty."

2 Sent out its Roots (shi-le-chāh she-rā-she-yā): The scrolls highlight the "Disloyal Root of the Vine." Zedekiah "Roots" his "Security" in "Egypt" (*The Second Eagle*) instead of "God’s Covenant." It signify "The Aesthetic of Treachery."

3 Breaking the Covenant (ha-phēr be-rīth): The scrolls highlight the "Contractual Root of Doom." "Truth" is "Uprooted" by "Perjury." It signifies "The Moral Failure of the Puppet King."

4 Majestic Cedar (e-rez ad-dīr): The scrolls conclude with this "Messianic Root of Hope." God will "Re-Root" a "Shoot" (*Messiah*) on a "High Mountain." It marks the transition to "Individual Responsibility" in *Ezekiel 18*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The High Mountain." I will plant it.

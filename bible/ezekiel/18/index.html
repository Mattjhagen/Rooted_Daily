Ezekiel 18

Individual Responsibility and the Choice of Life.

Every Soul is Mine

“Every living soul belongs to me... The soul who sins is the one who will die.” 1

Sour Grapes

“The parents eat sour grapes, and the children’s teeth are set on edge.” 2

No Pleasure in Death

“Do I take any pleasure in the death of the wicked?” 3

Repent and Live

“Get a new heart and a new spirit.”
“Repent and live!” 4

Translation Notes

1 Soul who Sins (han-ne-phesh ha-chō-tēth): The scrolls highlight the "Individual Root of Mortality." "Collective Guilt" is "Uprooted." Each "Soul" (*Nephesh*) is "Rooted" in its own "Actions." It signifies "The Moral Autonomy of the Individual."

2 Teeth on Edge (shi-nē ha-bhā-nīm thikh-le-nāh): The scrolls highlight the "Sensory Root of Injustice." The popular proverb "Roots" the "Suffering" in the "Ancestors." God "Uproots" this proverb. It signify "The Aesthetic of Personal Responsibility."

3 No Pleasure in Death (he-chā-phōtz ech-pōtz): Literally "Delighting, do I delight?" The scrolls highlight the "Affective Root of God." "Malice" is "Uprooted." God "Roots" His "Will" in "Life" (*Chay*). It signifies "The Heart of the Divine Judge."

4 Repent and Live (we-ha-shī-bhū wi-che-yū): The scrolls conclude with this "Imperative Root of Survival." "Death" is "Uprooted" through "Return" (*Shubh*). It marks the transition to "The Lament for the Princes" in *Ezekiel 19*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Choice of Life." Choose to live.

Ruth 2

1Now Naomi had a relative on her husband’s side, a man of standing from the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz.
2And Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, “Let me go to the fields and pick up the leftover grain behind anyone in whose eyes I find favor.” Naomi said to her, “Go ahead, my daughter.” 1
3So she went out, entered a field and began to glean behind the harvesters. As it turned out, she was working in a field belonging to Boaz, who was from the clan of Elimelech.
4Just then Boaz arrived from Bethlehem and greeted the harvesters, “The LORD be with you!” “The LORD bless you!” they answered. 2
8So Boaz said to Ruth, “My daughter, listen to me. Don’t go and glean in another field and don’t go away from here. Stay here with the women who work for me.”
9“Watch the field where the men are harvesting, and follow along after the women. I have told the men not to lay a hand on you. And whenever you are thirsty, go and get a drink from the water jars the men have filled.”
10At this, she bowed down with her face to the ground. She asked him, “Why have I found such favor in your eyes that you notice me—a foreigner?”
12“May the LORD repay you for what you have done. May you be richly rewarded by the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge.” 3
15As she got up to glean, Boaz gave orders to his men, “Let her gather among the sheaves and don’t reprimand her.”
16“Even pull out some stalks from the bundles for her and leave them for her to gather, and don’t rebuke her.” 4
19Her mother-in-law asked her, “Where did you glean today? Where did you work? Blessed be the man who took notice of you!”
20“The LORD bless him!” Naomi said to her daughter-in-law. “He has not stopped showing his kindness to the living and the dead.” She added, “That man is our close relative; he is one of our guardian-redeemers.” 5
23So Ruth stayed close to the women of Boaz to glean until the barley and wheat harvests were finished. And she lived with her mother-in-law.

Translation Notes

1 Gleaning (Leket): In the scrolls, this refers to the Law of Leviticus 19:9-10 and Deuteronomy 24:19. Farmers were commanded not to harvest the corners of their fields or pick up what they dropped, so that the poor and the foreigner could eat. It was God’s "Welfare System" that preserved the dignity of the poor by allowing them to work for their food.

2 The Greeting of Boaz: The first words of Boaz in the scrolls reveal his character. He greets his workers with the name of the LORD. In a time when "everyone did as they saw fit," Boaz was a man who integrated his faith into his business. His fields were a "Sacred Space" where even the workers were treated with spiritual dignity.

3 Under His Wings: This is a powerful poetic image in the scrolls (*Kanafayim*). It refers to the protection of a mother bird over her chicks, but also to the corners of a garment (fringes). Ruth had come to Israel to seek the protection of God; Boaz, as a righteous man, would soon become the "Physical Instrument" through which those wings provided shelter.

4 Purposely Pull Out: Boaz goes beyond the letter of the Law. The Law said "don't pick up what you drop," but Boaz told his men to "drop it on purpose." In the scrolls, this is the definition of *Hesed* (extraordinary kindness). He wanted to ensure Ruth had more than enough without making her feel like a beggar.

5 Guardian-Redeemer (Go’el): This is the central concept of the book of Ruth in the scrolls. A *Go’el* was a close relative who had the right and responsibility to buy back land sold by a poor relative and to marry the widow of a deceased relative to preserve the family line. Naomi realizes that Boaz is the "Solution" to their family’s survival.

Ruth 4

1Meanwhile Boaz went up to the town gate and sat down there just as the guardian-redeemer he had mentioned came by. 1
2Boaz took ten of the elders of the town and said, “Sit here,” and they did so.
3Then he said to the guardian-redeemer, “Naomi... is selling the piece of land that belonged to our relative Elimelech.”
4“I thought I should bring the matter to your attention... If you will redeem it, do so. But if you will not, tell me...” “I will redeem it,” he said.
5Then Boaz said, “On the day you buy the land from Naomi, you also acquire Ruth the Moabite, the dead man’s widow, in order to maintain the name of the dead with his property.”
6At this, the guardian-redeemer said, “Then I cannot redeem it because I might endanger my own inheritance. You redeem it yourself. I cannot do it.” 2
7(Now in earlier times in Israel, for the redemption and transfer of property to become final, one party took off his sandal and gave it to the other. This was the method of legalizing transactions in Israel.) 3
9Then Boaz announced to the elders and all the people, “Today you are witnesses that I have bought from Naomi all the property of Elimelech, Kilion and Mahlon.”
10“I have also acquired Ruth the Moabite, Mahlon’s widow, as my wife, in order to maintain the name of the dead with his property, so that his name will not disappear from among his family or from his hometown. Today you are witnesses!”
13So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife. When he made love to her, the LORD enabled her to conceive, and she gave birth to a son.
14The women said to Naomi: “Praise be to the LORD, who this day has not left you without a guardian-redeemer. May he become famous throughout Israel!”
15“He will renew your life and sustain you in your old age. For your daughter-in-law, who loves you and who is better to you than seven sons, has given him birth.” 4

The Royal Line

Translation Notes

1 The City Gate: In the scrolls, the gate was the local courthouse. It was where all official business, property transfers, and legal disputes were settled. Boaz’s promptness in going there "today" shows his integrity and his eagerness to fulfill his promise to Ruth.

2 Endanger my own inheritance: The unnamed relative (literally "Mr. So-and-So" in the scrolls) was willing to buy the land as an investment, but he was unwilling to fulfill the "Levirate Marriage" duty. If he had a son with Ruth, that son would be considered the heir of Mahlon, not himself. He chose his own "Financial Security" over "Family Loyalty." This highlights the superior character of Boaz, who was willing to take the "Risk of Kindness."

3 The Sandal: This custom in the scrolls symbolizes the "Right to Walk on the Land." By giving Boaz his sandal, the relative was formally handing over his "Possession" and "Authority" to redeem the property. It was a public, non-verbal signature on a contract.

4 Better than Seven Sons: This is a massive compliment in the scrolls. In ancient Near Eastern culture, seven sons was the pinnacle of a woman's blessing. To say that Ruth (the Moabite widow) was better than seven sons was a total "Revaluation of Worth." It proved that Ruth’s *Hesed* (loyalty) had completely integrated her into the heart of Israel.

5 The Genealogy: The book ends with a bridge from the "Era of the Judges" to the "Era of the Kings." In the scrolls, this genealogy proves that David’s family line was preserved through the faithfulness of an ordinary man (Boaz) and an outsider (Ruth). It teaches that God is working through "Ordinary Lives" to bring about "Extraordinary Purposes."

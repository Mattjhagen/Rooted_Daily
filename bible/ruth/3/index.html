Ruth 3

1One day Ruth’s mother-in-law Naomi said to her, “My daughter, I must find a home for you, where you will be well provided for.”
2“Now Boaz, with whose women you have been working, is a relative of ours. Tonight he will be winnowing barley on the threshing floor.”
3“Wash, put on perfume, and get dressed in your best clothes. Then go down to the threshing floor, but don’t let him know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking.”
4“When he lies down, note the place where he is lying. Then go and uncover his feet and lie down. He will tell you what to do.” 1
7When Boaz had finished eating and drinking and was in good spirits, he went over to lie down at the far end of the grain pile. Ruth approached quietly, uncovered his feet and lay down.
8In the middle of the night something startled the man; he turned—and there was a woman lying at his feet! 2
9“Who are you?” he asked. “I am your servant Ruth,” she said. “Spread the corner of your garment over me, since you are a guardian-redeemer.” 3
10“The LORD bless you, my daughter,” he replied. “This kindness is greater than that which you showed earlier: You have not run after the younger men, whether rich or poor.”
11“And now, my daughter, don’t be afraid. I will do for you all you ask. All the people of my town know that you are a woman of noble character.” 4
12“Although it is true that I am a guardian-redeemer, there is another who is more closely related than I.”
15He also said, “Bring me the shawl you are wearing and hold it out.” When she did so, he poured into it six measures of barley and placed the bundle on her. 5
18Then Naomi said, “Wait, my daughter, until you find out what happens. For the man will not rest until he has settled the matter today.”

Translation Notes

1 Uncover his feet: In the scrolls, this was a symbolic act of "Submission" and "Appeal." By uncovering his feet and lying there, Ruth was putting herself in the position of a servant. It was a culturally understood way of asking for protection. Naomi’s plan was bold and risky, but it relied on the known character of Boaz.

2 Startled (He-Cherad): The word in the scrolls implies a "Trembling Fear." Boaz woke up in the dark and found a person at his feet. In the ancient world, this could have been a thief or a dangerous situation. His reaction shows his vulnerability and the high stakes of the encounter.

3 Spread the corner of your garment: This is a direct reference back to Boaz’s blessing in Ruth 2:12. Ruth was essentially saying, "You prayed that I would find refuge under God's wings; now, be the wings!" In the scrolls, the "Corner" of the garment (*Kanaf*) is the same word for "Wing." She was asking for marriage—the ultimate form of legal and physical protection.

4 Woman of Noble Character (Eshet Chayil): This is the same title given to the ideal woman in Proverbs 31. Boaz recognizes that Ruth’s actions are not those of a desperate seductress, but of a faithful daughter-in-law trying to preserve her family name. Her "Hesed" (loyalty) impressed him more than her appearance.

5 Six Measures of Barley: This was a "Sign" for Naomi. In the scrolls, the number six often suggests "Incompleteness" waiting for the seventh (perfection). Boaz was saying, "I am sending you back with food now, but the full redemption is coming." He was guaranteeing his commitment to settle the matter immediately.

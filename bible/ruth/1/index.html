Ruth 1

1In the days when the judges ruled, there was a famine in the land. So a man from Bethlehem in Judah, together with his wife and two sons, went to live for a while in the country of Moab. 1
2The man’s name was Elimelech, his wife’s name was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Kilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem, Judah. And they went to Moab and lived there.
3Now Elimelech, Naomi’s husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.
4They married Moabite women, one named Orpah and the other Ruth. After they had lived there about ten years,
5both Mahlon and Kilion also died, and Naomi was left without her two sons and her husband. 2
14At this they wept aloud again. Then Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye, but Ruth clung to her.
15“Look,” said Naomi, “your sister-in-law is going back to her people and her gods. Go back with her.”
16But Ruth replied, “Don’t urge me to leave you or to turn back from you. Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God.” 3
17“Where you die I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD deal with me, be it ever so severely, if even death separates you and me.”
19So the two women went on until they came to Bethlehem. When they arrived in Bethlehem, the whole town was stirred because of them, and the women exclaimed, “Can this be Naomi?”
20“Don’t call me Naomi,” she told them. “Call me Mara, because the Almighty has made my life very bitter.” 4
21“I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi? The LORD has afflicted me; the Almighty has brought misfortune upon me.”
22So Naomi returned from Moab accompanied by Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, arriving in Bethlehem as the barley harvest was beginning. 5

Translation Notes

1 Famine in the House of Bread: There is deep irony in the scrolls here. Bethlehem literally means "House of Bread" (*Beit-Lechem*). A famine in the House of Bread is a sign of severe spiritual and physical judgment. Elimelech’s decision to move to Moab (a traditional enemy of Israel) was seen by many commentators as a lack of faith in God’s provision in the land.

2 The Names: The names in the scrolls are often descriptive. Elimelech means "My God is King." Mahlon and Kilion are related to words for "Sickness" and "Pining Away"—suggesting their fragile nature or early deaths. Naomi means "Pleasant." The tragedy of this chapter is the stripping away of all these identities until Naomi feels she is nothing but "Mara."

3 Ruth’s Vow (Hesed): This is the most famous declaration of loyalty in the scrolls. Ruth, a Moabite, was not just choosing a person; she was choosing a "Covenant." She was abandoning her own national identity and gods to join the people of Yahweh. It is an act of *Hesed*—the covenant-love and faithfulness that God shows to His people, now shown by a foreigner to an aging widow.

4 Mara (Bitterness): Naomi was experiencing "Grief-Induced Identity Loss." She could no longer see the "Pleasantness" of God’s previous blessings, only the current "Bitterness" of her loss. In the scrolls, her complaint is honest and raw, but it misses the fact that God had already provided her with Ruth, the first seed of her restoration.

5 Barley Harvest: This is a "Providential Marker" in the scrolls. They arrived at the exact moment when the laws of "Gleaning" (designed by God to protect the poor and the widow) were about to become active. The famine was over, and the stage was set for Ruth to meet the one who would redeem her family line.

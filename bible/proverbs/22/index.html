Proverbs 22

A Good Name and the Sayings of the Wise.

The Desirable Name

“A good name is more desirable than great riches; to be esteemed is better than silver or gold.” 1

Train Up a Child

“Start children off on the way they should go, and even when they are old they will not turn from it.” 2
“The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender.” 3

Sayings of the Wise

“Pay attention and turn your ear to the sayings of the wise; apply your heart to what I teach.” 4
“Do not move an ancient boundary stone set up by your ancestors.”

Service Before Kings

“Do you see someone skilled in their work? They will serve before kings; they will not serve before officials of low rank.” 5

Translation Notes

1 A Good Name (Shem Ni-bhar): Literally "A chosen name." In the scrolls, "Reputation" (*Shem*) is the "Chosen Root" of social value. To be "Esteemed" (*Chen/Grace*) is a "Durable Root" compared to the "Uprootable" nature of "Silver and Gold." It signifies "Intangible Capital."

2 Train up a Child (Cha-nok lan-Na-ar): Literally "Dedicate/Inaugurate the youth." The scrolls highlight the "Developmental Root of Trajectory." The "Way" (*De-rekh*) set in childhood "Roots" the "Direction" of age. It signify "Primary Formation."

3 Slave to the Lender (E-bed lo-Ish Mal-weh): The scrolls highlight the "Servitude Root of Debt." "Borrowing" (*Lo-weh*) "Uproots" "Personal Sovereignty." It signifies "Economic Bondage."

4 Sayings of the Wise (Dib-rey Cha-ka-mim): The scrolls highlight the "Oral Tradition Root." This marks the transition to a specific sub-collection. "Applying the Heart" (*Ta-shith Lib-be-kha*) is the "Root of Internalization." It signify "Academic Devotion."

5 Skilled in Work (Ma-hir bi-me-lakh-to): The scrolls conclude with this "Excellence Root." "Skill" (*Mahir*) "Roots" a person in "Royal Service" (*Li-phney-Me-la-khim*). It marks the transition to the "Table of the Ruler" in *Proverbs 23*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "The Skilled Life." Excel and serve.

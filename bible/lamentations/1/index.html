Lamentations 1

The Widowhood of Zion and the Tears of the Night.

How Deserted Lies the City

“How deserted lies the city, once so full of people!” 1
“How like a widow is she!” 2

Night Tears

“Bitterly she weeps at night, tears are on her cheeks.” 3
“Among all her lovers there is no one to comfort her.”

The Mourning Roads

“The roads to Zion mourn, for no one comes to her appointed festivals.” 4

Is it Nothing to You?

“Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by? Look around and see.” 5
“Is any suffering like my suffering?”

Translation Notes

1 How Deserted (ē-khāh yāsh-bhā bhā-dhādh): The opening word of Lamentations (*Eikhah* - "How") is the traditional cry of a funeral dirge. In the scrolls, this is the "Exclamatory Root of Grief." "Community" is "Uprooted" and "Solitude" (*Bhadhadh*) is "Rooted." It signifies "The Shock of the Empty City."

2 Like a Widow (ha-ye-thāh ke-al-mā-nāh): The scrolls highlight the "Marital Root of Abandonment." Jerusalem is "Uprooted" from her "Husband" (*God*) and "Rooted" in "Widowhood." It signify "The Loss of Protection and Identity."

3 Bitterly she Weeps (bā-khō thibh-keh): Literally "Weeping she weeps." The scrolls highlight the "Recursive Root of Sorrow." "Tears" are "Rooted" on the "Cheeks" (*Lekhi*) of the city. It signifies "The Endless Cycle of Pain."

4 The Roads to Zion Mourn (dar-khē Tziy-yōn a-bhē-lōth): The scrolls highlight the "Geographic Root of Absence." The "Inanimate Roads" are "Rooted" in "Mourning" because the "Human Root" of "Worship" is "Uprooted." It signify "The Silence of the Sacred Space."

5 Is it Nothing to You? (lō a-lē-khem): Literally "Not to you." The scrolls conclude with this "Observational Root of Indifference." The "Sufferer" is "Uprooted" from "Empathy." It marks the transition to "The Lord as Enemy" in *Lamentations 2*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "Zion’s Widowhood." Look and see.

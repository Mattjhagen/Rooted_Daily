Joshua 20

1Then the LORD said to Joshua:
2“Tell the Israelites to designate the cities of refuge, as I instructed you through Moses,”
3“so that anyone who kills a person accidentally and unintentionally may flee there and find protection from the avenger of blood.” 1
4“When they flee to one of these cities, they are to stand in the entrance of the city gate and state their case before the elders of that city. Then the elders are to admit them into their city and give them a place to live with them.”
5“If the avenger of blood comes in pursuit, the elders must not surrender the fugitive, because the fugitive killed their neighbor unintentionally and without malice aforethought.”
6“They are to stay in that city until they have stood trial before the assembly and until the death of the high priest who is serving at that time. Then they may go back to their own home in the town from which they fled.” 2

9Any of the Israelites or any foreigner residing among them who killed someone accidentally could flee to these designated cities and not be killed by the avenger of blood prior to standing trial before the assembly. 3

Translation Notes

1 Avenger of Blood (Goel ha-Dam): In ancient Near Eastern culture, if a person was killed, the nearest relative (*Goel*) was legally obligated to kill the slayer to maintain the family honor and "atone" for the blood spilled. The scrolls introduce the Cities of Refuge to break this cycle of "Private Vengeance." It was the beginning of a "State Justice System" where the motive (intent) of the slayer mattered.

2 Death of the High Priest: This is a unique legal principle in the scrolls. The death of the High Priest acted as a "Statutory Limitation" or a "National Atonement." His death cleared the record for all accidental slayers in the cities of refuge, allowing them to return home safely. It suggests that the High Priest’s life was representatively valuable for the whole nation.

3 Foreigner Residing Among Them: The scrolls emphasize that this protection applied even to non-Israelites (*Ger*). The God of Israel demanded "Equal Justice" for everyone living in the land. This prevented the land from being "Defiled by Blood" regardless of who the victim or the slayer was.

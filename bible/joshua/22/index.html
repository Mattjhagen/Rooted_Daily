Joshua 22

1Then Joshua summoned the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh
2and said to them, “You have done all that Moses the servant of the LORD commanded, and you have obeyed me in everything I commanded.”
3“For a long time now—to this very day—you have not deserted your fellow Israelites but have carried out the mission the LORD your God gave you.”
4“Now that the LORD your God has given them rest as he promised, return to your homes in the land that Moses the servant of the LORD gave you on the other side of the Jordan.”
5“But be very careful to keep the commandment and the law that Moses the servant of the LORD gave you: to love the LORD your God, to walk in obedience to him, to keep his commands, to hold fast to him and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul.” 1
6Then Joshua blessed them and sent them away, and they went to their homes.
10When they came to Geliloth near the Jordan in the land of Canaan, the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh built an imposing altar there by the Jordan.
11And when the Israelites heard that they had built the altar...
12the whole assembly of Israel gathered at Shiloh to go to war against them. 2
13The Israelites sent Phinehas son of Eleazar, the priest, to the land of Gilead—to Reuben, Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh.
16They said: “The whole assembly of the LORD says: ‘How could you break faith with the God of Israel like this? How could you turn away from the LORD and build yourselves an altar in rebellion against him now?’”
21Then Reuben, Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh replied to the heads of the clans of Israel:
22“The Mighty One, God, the LORD! The Mighty One, God, the LORD! He knows! And let Israel know!” 3
26“That is why we said, ‘Let us get ready and build an altar—but not for burnt offerings or sacrifices.’”
27“On the contrary, it is to be a witness between us and you and the generations that follow, that we will worship the LORD at his sanctuary with our burnt offerings, sacrifices and fellowship offerings.” 4
30When Phinehas the priest and the leaders of the community... heard what Reuben, Gad and Manasseh had to say, they were pleased.
31And Phinehas son of Eleazar, the priest, said... “Today we know that the LORD is with us, because you have not been unfaithful to the LORD in this matter.”
34And the Reubenites and the Gadites gave the altar this name: A Witness Between Us—that the LORD is God.

Translation Notes

1 To Hold Fast (U-le-davka): This word in the scrolls literally means "to cling" or "to glue yourself to." It describes the highest level of intimacy and loyalty. Joshua’s final command to the returning tribes was that their distance from the Tabernacle must not lead to a distance from God. Geography should not dictate spirituality.

2 Crisis of Unity: The scrolls record a near-catastrophic misunderstanding. Because the Law commanded only *one* altar for sacrifice (Deuteronomy 12), the western tribes saw a second altar as an act of treason. Their readiness for war shows how seriously they took the spiritual purity of the nation. They had learned from the sin of Achan that one person’s rebellion could destroy everyone.

3 The Mighty One, God, the LORD (El Elohim YHWH): This triple-title invocation in the scrolls is an oath of the highest order. They were calling upon the Creator, the Judge, and the Covenant-Keeper to witness their intentions. It is a powerful defense of their "Orthodoxy."

4 Altar of Witness (Ed): The Eastern tribes were worried that in the future, the Western tribes would say, "You have no part in the LORD" because of the Jordan river barrier. The altar was built not for *ritual*, but for *memory*. It was a physical copy of the altar at Shiloh, built to prove they belonged to the same spiritual community. It teaches that "Symbols" can be used for unity rather than division.

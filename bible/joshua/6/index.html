Joshua 6

1Now the gates of Jericho were securely barred because of the Israelites. No one went out and no one came in.
2Then the LORD said to Joshua, “See, I have delivered Jericho into your hands, along with its king and its fighting men.”
3“March around the city once with all the armed men. Do this for six days.”
4“Have seven priests carry trumpets of rams’ horns in front of the ark. On the seventh day, march around the city seven times, with the priests blowing the trumpets.” 1
5“When you hear them sound a long blast on the trumpets, have all the people give a loud shout; then the wall of the city will collapse and the people will go up, every man straight in.”
8When Joshua had spoken to the people, the seven priests carrying the seven trumpets before the LORD went forward, blowing their trumpets, and the ark of the LORD’s covenant followed them.
10But Joshua had commanded the people, “Do not give a war cry, do not raise your voices, do not say a word until the day I tell you to shout. Then shout!” 2
11So he had the ark of the LORD carried around the city, circling it once. Then the people returned to camp and spent the night there.
14So on the second day they marched around the city once and returned to the camp. They did this for six days.
15On the seventh day, they got up at daybreak and marched around the city seven times in the same manner, except that on that day they circled the city seven times.
16The seventh time around, when the priests sounded the trumpet blast, Joshua commanded the people, “Shout! For the LORD has given you the city!”
17“The city and all that is in it are to be devoted to the LORD. Only Rahab the prostitute and all who are with her in her house shall be spared, because she hid the spies we sent.” 3
18“But keep away from the devoted things, so that you will not bring about your own destruction by taking any of them. Otherwise you will make the camp of Israel liable to destruction and bring trouble on it.”
19“All the silver and gold and the articles of bronze and iron are sacred to the LORD and must go into his treasury.”
20When the trumpets sounded, the people shouted, and at the sound of the trumpet, when the people gave a loud shout, the wall collapsed; so every man charged straight in, and they took the city. 4
21They devoted the city to the LORD and destroyed with the sword every living thing in it—men and women, young and old, cattle, sheep and donkeys.
22Joshua said to the two men who had spied out the land, “Go into the prostitute’s house and bring her out and all who belong to her, in accordance with your oath to her.”
23So the young men who had done the spying went in and brought out Rahab, her father and mother, her brothers and sisters and all who belonged to her. They brought out her entire family and put them in a place outside the camp of Israel.
25But Joshua spared Rahab the prostitute, with her family and all who belonged to her, because she hid the men Joshua had sent as spies to Jericho—and she lives among the Israelites to this day.
26At that time Joshua pronounced this solemn oath: “Cursed before the LORD is the one who undertakes to rebuild this city, Jericho: At the cost of his firstborn son will he lay its foundations; at the cost of his youngest will he set up its gates.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Rams' Horns (Shofarot ha-Yovelim): The scrolls specify that these were the same horns used to announce the "Year of Jubilee." This signaled that the conquest was not just a military action, but a spiritual "Restoration" of the land to its true Owner. The sound of the shofar was the sound of God’s sovereignty.

2 The Silent March: This is a unique psychological tactic in the scrolls. For six days, the people were commanded to be absolutely silent. This prevented them from complaining, mocking, or debating the strategy. It also likely terrified the people of Jericho, who had never seen an army move in such eerie, disciplined silence. It teaches that "Waiting on God" often requires the closing of the mouth.

3 Devoted Thing (Cherem): The word *Cherem* in the scrolls means something set apart exclusively for God, either for use in the sanctuary or for total destruction. Jericho was the "Firstfruits" of the land; therefore, everything in it belonged to God. Taking plunder from Jericho was not just theft; it was a "Sacrilege"—taking what God had claimed for Himself.

4 The Wall Collapsed (Tichpal ha-Chomah): Archaeological evidence at Tel es-Sultan (ancient Jericho) shows that the city walls literally fell outward and downward, creating a "ramp" for invaders to run up into the city. This matches the biblical description in the scrolls precisely. It was a "Structural Miracle."

5 The Curse on Rebuilding: In the scrolls, Jericho was to remain a ruin as a permanent memorial to God’s judgment. To rebuild it would be an act of "Rebellion" against that memorial. The fulfillment of this curse is later recorded in 1 Kings 16:34, during the reign of King Ahab.

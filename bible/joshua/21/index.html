Joshua 21

1Now the family heads of the Levites approached Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun, and the heads of the other tribal families of Israel at Shiloh in Canaan
2and said to them, “The LORD commanded through Moses that you give us towns to live in, with pasturelands for our livestock.” 1
3So, as the LORD had commanded, the Israelites gave the Levites the following towns and pasturelands out of their own inheritance:

The Kohathites: 23 towns (v.4-19)

The Gershonites: 13 towns (v.27-33)

The Merarites: 12 towns (v.34-40)

Total: 48 towns with their pasturelands. (v.41)

41The towns of the Levites in the territory held by the Israelites were forty-eight in all, together with their pasturelands.
42Each of these towns had pasturelands surrounding it; this was true for all these towns.
43So the LORD gave Israel all the land he had sworn to give their ancestors, and they took possession of it and settled there.
44The LORD gave them rest on every side, just as he had sworn to their ancestors. Not one of their enemies withstood them; the LORD gave all their enemies into their hands.
45Not one of all the LORD’s good promises to Israel failed; every one was fulfilled. 2

Translation Notes

1 Levitical Cities: In the scrolls, the Levites were intentionally scattered throughout all the tribes. By living in 48 different locations, the spiritual leaders were never more than a few miles away from any Israelite. They served as "Local Teachers" and "Judges," ensuring that the knowledge of the Law was distributed evenly across the nation. They were the "Spiritual Nervous System" of Israel.

2 Every one was fulfilled (Ha-kol ba): Literally in the scrolls, "Everything came." This is the theological climax of the Book of Joshua. It answers the question posed in Genesis: "Will God keep His promise to Abraham?" The answer is a definitive "Yes." While there were still battles to be fought and territory to be cleared, the "Constitutional Grant" of the land was now complete. God had done His part; the remaining struggle would be a test of Israel's faithfulness.

Joshua 24

1Then Joshua assembled all the tribes of Israel at Shechem. He summoned the elders, leaders, judges and officials of Israel, and they presented themselves before God.
2Joshua said to all the people, “This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘Long ago your ancestors, including Terah the father of Abraham and Nahor, lived beyond the Euphrates River and worshiped other gods.’” 1
3“‘But I took your father Abraham from the land beyond the Euphrates and led him throughout Canaan and gave him many descendants.’”
13“‘So I gave you a land on which you did not toil and cities you did not build; and you live in them and eat from vineyards and olive groves that you did not plant.’”
14“Now fear the LORD and serve him with all faithfulness. Throw away the gods your ancestors worshiped beyond the Euphrates River and in Egypt, and serve the LORD.”
15“But if serving the LORD seems undesirable to you, then choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve, whether the gods your ancestors served beyond the Euphrates, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you are living.”
“But as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.” 2
16Then the people answered, “Far be it from us to forsake the LORD to serve other gods!”
19Joshua said to the people, “You are not able to serve the LORD. He is a holy God; he is a jealous God. He will not forgive your rebellion and your sins.” 3
21But the people said to Joshua, “No! We will serve the LORD.”
24And the people said to Joshua, “We will serve the LORD our God and obey him.”
25On that day Joshua made a covenant for the people, and there at Shechem he reaffirmed for them decrees and laws.
26And Joshua recorded these things in the Book of the Law of God. Then he took a large stone and set it up there under the oak near the holy place of the LORD. 4
27“See!” he said to all the people. “This stone will be a witness against us. It has heard all the words the LORD has said to us. It will be a witness against you if you are untrue to your God.”
29After these things, Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of a hundred and ten.
31Israel served the LORD throughout the lifetime of Joshua and of the elders who outlived him and who had experienced everything the LORD had done for Israel.
32And Joseph’s bones, which the Israelites had brought up from Egypt, were buried at Shechem in the tract of land that Jacob bought... it became the inheritance of Joseph’s descendants. 5
33Eleazar son of Aaron died and was buried at Gibeah, which had been given to his son Phinehas in the hill country of Ephraim.

Translation Notes

1 Worshiped other gods: In the scrolls, Joshua begins his final speech with a "Reality Check." Even the great ancestors (Abraham’s family) were idolaters before God called them. It teaches that the covenant is based entirely on "God’s Initiative" and "Grace," not on the inherent righteousness of the family line.

2 Me and my household (Ani u-veiti): This is the famous declaration of "Personal Leadership." Joshua doesn't wait to see what the crowd will do; he sets the pace. In the scrolls, the "Household" (*Bayit*) was the smallest unit of the nation. If every head of household made this choice, the entire nation would be secure. It is the definition of "Grassroots Faithfulness."

3 You are not able: This is a shocking statement in the scrolls. Joshua is testing their sincerity. He knows that human enthusiasm is fickle. He warns them that God is "Holy" and "Jealous"—He will not accept a half-hearted or divided loyalty. By saying "you are not able," he is driving them to a deeper level of commitment and dependence on divine help.

4 The Oak at Shechem: This location in the scrolls is deeply symbolic. It was at this same oak that Abraham first received the promise of the land (Genesis 12:6-7) and where Jacob buried his family’s idols (Genesis 35:4). By returning to this spot, Joshua was closing a "Historical Circle." The promise was now fulfilled, and the idols were once again to be put away.

5 Joseph’s Bones: The burial of Joseph’s bones marks the final end of the Exodus journey. Joseph had died in Egypt 400 years earlier, but he had insisted that his body be returned to the land (Genesis 50:25). His burial in Shechem is the "Final Receipt" of the land inheritance. The journey was truly over.

Joshua 23

1After a long time had passed and the LORD had given Israel rest from all their enemies around them, Joshua, by then a very old man,
2summoned all Israel—their elders, leaders, judges and officials—and said to them: “I am very old.”
3“You yourselves have seen everything the LORD your God has done to all these nations for your sake; it was the LORD your God who fought for you.”
6“Be very strong; be careful to obey all that is written in the Book of the Law of Moses, without turning aside to the right or to the left.” 1
7“Do not associate with these nations that remain among you; do not invoke the names of their gods or swear by them. You must not serve them or bow down to them.”
8“But you are to hold fast to the LORD your God, as you have until now.”
10“One of you routs a thousand, because the LORD your God fights for you, just as he promised.” 2
11“So be very careful to love the LORD your God.”
12“But if you turn away and ally yourselves with the survivors of these nations that remain among you and if you intermarry with them and associate with them,”
13“then you may be sure that the LORD your God will no longer drive out these nations before you. Instead, they will become snares and traps for you, whips on your backs and thorns in your eyes, until you perish from this good land, which the LORD your God has given you.” 3
14“Now I am about to go the way of all the earth. You know with all your heart and soul that not one of all the good promises the LORD your God gave you has failed. Every promise has been fulfilled; not one has failed.”
15“But just as every good promise of the LORD your God has come true, so the LORD will bring on you all the evil he has threatened, until he has destroyed you from this good land he has given you.”
16“If you violate the covenant of the LORD your God... then the LORD’s anger will burn against you, and you will quickly perish from the good land he has given you.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Turning Aside: In the scrolls, "Right or Left" refers to the two extremes of disobedience. One can turn aside by *adding* to the Law (legalism) or by *subtracting* from it (licentiousness). Joshua emphasizes that the path of the "Upright" (*Yashar*) is the narrow middle way of exact obedience.

2 One Routs a Thousand: This is the "Mathematics of the Spirit" in the scrolls. When God fights for His people, their physical numbers are irrelevant. A single person aligned with the divine will can exert an influence that outweighs a thousand opponents. It is a promise of "Asymmetric Spiritual Warfare."

3 Snares and Traps: This is a powerful metaphor for "Cultural Assimilation" in the scrolls. Joshua warns that the enemies they *refuse* to drive out will become the instruments of their own destruction. "Whips on your backs" refers to political oppression, while "Thorns in your eyes" refers to spiritual blindness. If you tolerate what God has condemned, it will eventually blind and bind you.

4 Covenant Violation: The scrolls present the Covenant as a "Two-Edged Sword." Just as God was faithful to fulfill every good promise, He will be equally faithful to fulfill every warned judgment. The stability of the land is not unconditional; it is tied to the "Moral Integrity" of the nation.

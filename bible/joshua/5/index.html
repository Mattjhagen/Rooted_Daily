Joshua 5

1Now when all the Amorite kings west of the Jordan and all the Canaanite kings along the coast heard how the LORD had dried up the Jordan before the Israelites until they had crossed over, their hearts melted in fear and they no longer had the courage to face the Israelites.
2At that time the LORD said to Joshua, “Make flint knives and circumcise the Israelites again.”
3So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at Gibeath Haaraloth. 1
4Now this is why he did so: All those who came out of Egypt—all the men of military age—had died in the wilderness on the way after leaving Egypt.
5All the people who came out had been circumcised, but all the people born in the wilderness during the journey from Egypt had not.
8And after the whole nation had been circumcised, they remained where they were in camp until they were healed.
9Then the LORD said to Joshua, “Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you.” So the place has been called Gilgal to this day. 2
10On the evening of the fourteenth day of the month, while camped at Gilgal on the plains of Jericho, the Israelites celebrated the Passover.
11The day after the Passover, that very day, they ate some of the produce of the land: unleavened bread and roasted grain.
12The manna stopped the day after they ate this food from the land; there was no longer any manna for the Israelites, but that year they ate the produce of Canaan. 3
13Now when Joshua was near Jericho, he looked up and saw a man standing in front of him with a drawn sword in his hand. Joshua went up to him and asked, “Are you for us or for our enemies?”
14“Neither,” he replied, “but as commander of the army of the LORD I have now come.” 4
Then Joshua fell facedown to the ground in reverence and asked him, “What message does my Lord have for his servant?”
15The commander of the LORD’s army replied, “Take off your sandals, for the place where you are standing is holy.” And Joshua did so. 5

Translation Notes

1 Flint Knives: Even though the Israelites were in the Bronze Age, the scrolls specify *flint* knives for circumcision. This is likely to maintain continuity with the patriarchal era. Circumcision at this moment was a "Tactical Vulnerability"—the entire army was incapacitated for several days in the shadow of the enemy’s walls. It shows that their primary security was in God, not their physical health.

2 Reproach of Egypt (Cherpat Mitzrayim): In the scrolls, "Gilgal" is linked to the verb *Galal* (to roll). By circumcising the new generation, they were finally removing the shame of their slave history and the "uncircumcised" status they held in Egypt. They were no longer "Egyptian fugitives" but "Covenant Citizens."

3 The Manna Stopped: This is a major transition in the scrolls. The "Survival Food" of the wilderness ended the moment they ate the "Inheritance Food" of the land. It teaches that God provides miracles for the *journey*, but expects us to live off the *fruit* of our inheritance once we arrive. The season of dependence shifted to a season of stewardship.

4 Commander of the LORD’s Army: Joshua’s question ("For us or them?") was binary. The Commander's answer ("Neither") was revolutionary. It teaches that God is not a "Tribal Deity" who automatically takes sides. Rather, we must decide if we are on *His* side. The Commander represents the heavenly forces that operate beyond human politics.

5 Take off your sandals: This command in the scrolls links Joshua directly to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:5). It was a "Validation" of Joshua’s leadership. It also reminded him that although he was the human general, he was standing on ground that belonged to God. The conquest was to be a "Holy War," not a secular land grab.

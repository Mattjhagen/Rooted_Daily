Joshua 19

1The second lot came out for the tribe of Simeon according to its clans. Its inheritance lay within the territory of Judah. 1
9The inheritance of the Simeonites was taken from the share of Judah, because Judah’s portion was more than they needed. So the Simeonites received their inheritance within the territory of Judah.

v.10-16: Zebulun's Inheritance. The third lot. It extended toward Sarid and the sea.

v.17-23: Issachar's Inheritance. The fourth lot. It included Jezreel and Shunem.

v.24-31: Asher's Inheritance. The fifth lot. It reached Great Sidon and the fortified city of Tyre.

v.32-39: Naphtali's Inheritance. The sixth lot. In the northern mountains, reaching the Jordan at the Sea of Galilee.

v.40-48: Dan's Inheritance. The seventh lot. They had difficulty taking their allotted land, so they went up and attacked Leshem, captured it, and called it Dan. 2

49When they had finished dividing the land into its separate territories, the Israelites gave Joshua son of Nun an inheritance among them,
50as the LORD had commanded. They gave him the town he asked for—Timnath Serah in the hill country of Ephraim. And he built up the town and settled there. 3
51...And so they finished dividing the land.

Translation Notes

1 Simeon within Judah: In the scrolls, this arrangement is the fulfillment of Jacob’s prophecy that Simeon would be "scattered in Israel" (Genesis 49:7). Because Judah had received a portion that was "more than they needed," they shared it with their brothers. It teaches that "Abundance" in one tribe should be used to provide for the "Lack" in another.

2 Dan’s Migration: The scrolls record that Dan struggled to hold their territory against the Philistines and Amorites. Instead of persevering, they migrated to the far north. This created the traditional boundaries of Israel: "From Dan to Beersheba." It shows how human struggle can redefine the map of a nation.

3 Joshua’s Inheritance (The Servant’s Portion): Notice the timing in the scrolls: Joshua was the *last* person to receive an inheritance. Although he was the leader, he ensured that everyone else was settled before he took anything for himself. He didn't take the best city (Jericho or Hebron), but asked for a ruin (Timnath Serah) and "built it up." It is the ultimate model of "Servant Leadership."

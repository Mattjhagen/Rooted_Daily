Joshua 15

1The allotment for the tribe of Judah, according to its clans, extended down to the territory of Edom, to the Desert of Zin in the extreme south.
12The western boundary was the coastline of the Mediterranean Sea. These are the boundaries around the people of Judah by their clans.
13In accordance with the LORD’s command to him, Joshua gave to Caleb son of Jephunneh a portion in Judah—Kiriath Arba, that is, Hebron. (Arba was the forefather of Anak.)
14From Hebron Caleb drove out the three Anakites—Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai, descendants of Anak. 1
15From there he marched against the people living in Debir (formerly called Kiriath Sepher).
16And Caleb said, “I will give my daughter Acsah in marriage to the man who attacks and captures Kiriath Sepher.”
17Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s brother, took it; so Caleb gave him his daughter Acsah in marriage.
18One day when she came to Othniel, she urged him to ask her father for a field. When she got off her donkey, Caleb asked her, “What can I do for you?”
19She replied, “Do me a special favor. Since you have given me land in the Negev, give me also springs of water.” So Caleb gave her the upper and lower springs. 2
20This is the inheritance of the tribe of Judah, according to its clans:
21The southernmost towns of the tribe of Judah in the Negev toward the boundary of Edom were: Kabzeel, Eder, Jagur... (The list continues with 115 cities).
63Judah could not dislodge the Jebusites, who were living in Jerusalem; to this day the Jebusites live there with the people of Judah. 3

Translation Notes

1 Caleb drove out the Anakites: The scrolls record that Caleb personally finished the job he had advocated for 45 years earlier. Hebron was the burial place of the Patriarchs (Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob). By reclaiming it from the "Giants," Caleb was returning the family cemetery to the family. It was a victory of "History over Fear."

2 Springs of Water (Gullot Mayim): In the scrolls, Acsah is presented as a "Wise Steward." Land in the Negev (the south) was dry and difficult. She realized that "Possession" without "Resources" was a burden. By asking for the springs, she ensured the long-term viability of her inheritance. It teaches that we should pray not just for "Status," but for the "Life-Giving Resources" needed to sustain our responsibilities.

3 The Jebusites in Jerusalem: This is a crucial historical note in the scrolls. Jerusalem was a "Highland Fortress" that proved too difficult for the initial conquest. It remained a foreign enclave in the heart of Israelite territory for centuries until the time of King David (2 Samuel 5). It serves as a reminder that the conquest was a "Process," not a single event.

Joshua 14

1Now these are the areas the Israelites received as an inheritance in the land of Canaan, which Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun and the heads of the tribal clans of Israel allotted to them.
2Their inheritances were assigned by lot to the nine-and-a-half tribes, as the LORD had commanded through Moses. 1
5So the Israelites divided the land, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.
6Now the people of Judah approached Joshua at Gilgal, and Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite said to him, “You know what the LORD said to Moses the man of God at Kadesh Barnea about you and me.”
7“I was forty years old when Moses the servant of the LORD sent me from Kadesh Barnea to spy out the land. And I brought him back a report according to my convictions.”
8“But my fellow Israelites who went up with me made the hearts of the people melt in fear. I, however, followed the LORD my God wholeheartedly.” 2
9“So on that day Moses swore to me, ‘The land on which your feet have walked will be your inheritance and that of your children forever, because you have followed the LORD my God wholeheartedly.’”
10“Now then, just as the LORD promised, he has kept me alive for forty-five years since the time he spoke this word to Moses... So here I am today, eighty-five years old!”
11“I am still as strong today as the day Moses sent me out; I’m just as vigorous to go out to battle now as I was then.” 3
12“Now give me this hill country that the LORD promised me that day. You yourself heard then that the Anakites were there and their cities were large and fortified, but, the LORD helping me, I will drive them out just as he said.” 4
13Then Joshua blessed Caleb son of Jephunneh and gave him Hebron as his inheritance.
14So Hebron has belonged to Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite ever since, because he followed the LORD, the God of Israel, wholeheartedly.
15(Hebron used to be called Kiriath Arba after Arba, who was the greatest man among the Anakites.) Then the land had rest from war.

Translation Notes

1 Assigned by Lot (Goral): The scrolls show that the division of the land was not left to human favoritism or negotiation. By using "Lots," the people were acknowledging that God is the one who determines the boundaries of our lives. It prevented envy among the tribes because the result was seen as a divine decision.

2 Wholeheartedly (Mille acharei): Literally in the scrolls, "He filled after the LORD." It means Caleb followed God "to the full"—without holding anything back. While the other ten spies were filled with the "conviction of the enemy's strength," Caleb was filled with the "conviction of God's presence."

3 Vigor of Caleb: Caleb’s statement in the scrolls is a testament to "Divine Preservation." Most of his generation had died in the desert, but Caleb was preserved in a state of youthfulness. He was 85, but he didn't want a retirement home; he wanted a battlefield. It teaches that God can sustain our physical strength to match our spiritual calling.

4 Give me this hill country: Caleb did not ask for the easy, flat land. He specifically asked for the "Highlands" where the giants (Anakites) still lived in fortified cities. He wanted the hardest task because it offered the greatest opportunity to demonstrate God’s power. Caleb’s faith was "Anakite-proof."

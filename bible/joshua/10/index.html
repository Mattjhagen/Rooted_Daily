Joshua 10

1Now Adoni-Zedek king of Jerusalem heard that Joshua had taken Ai and totally destroyed it... and that the people of Gibeon had made a treaty of peace with Israel and were living near them.
2He and his people were very much alarmed at this, because Gibeon was an important city, like one of the royal cities; it was larger than Ai, and all its men were good fighters.
3So Adoni-Zedek king of Jerusalem appealed to Hoham king of Hebron, Piram king of Jarmuth, Japhia king of Lachish and Debir king of Eglon.
4“Come up and help me attack Gibeon,” he said, “because it has made a treaty of peace with Joshua and the Israelites.”
6The Gibeonites sent word to Joshua in the camp at Gilgal: “Do not abandon your servants. Come up to us quickly and save us! Help us, because all the Amorite kings from the hill country have joined forces against us.”
7So Joshua marched up from Gilgal with his entire army, including all the best fighting men.
8The LORD said to Joshua, “Do not be afraid of them; I have given them into your hand. Not one of them will be able to withstand you.”
9After an all-night march from Gilgal, Joshua took them by surprise.
11As they fled before Israel on the road down from Beth Horon to Azekah, the LORD hurled large hailstones down on them, and more of them died from the hailstones than were killed by the swords of the Israelites. 1
12On the day the LORD gave the Amorites over to Israel, Joshua said to the LORD in the presence of Israel:
“Sun, stand still over Gibeon,
and you, moon, over the Valley of Aijalon.”
13So the sun stood still, and the moon stopped, till the nation avenged itself on its enemies, as it is written in the Book of Jashar. 2
The sun stopped in the middle of the sky and delayed going down about a full day.
14There has never been a day like it before or since, a day when the LORD listened to a human being. Surely the LORD was fighting for Israel! 3
16Now the five kings had fled and hidden in the cave at Makkedah.
22Joshua said, “Open the mouth of the cave and bring those five kings out to me.”
24When they had brought these kings to Joshua, he summoned all the men of Israel and said to the army commanders who had come with him, “Come here and put your feet on the necks of these kings.” So they came forward and placed their feet on their necks. 4
25Joshua said to them, “Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged. Be strong and courageous. This is what the LORD will do to all the enemies you are going to fight.”
40So Joshua subdued the whole region, including the hill country, the Negev, the western foothills and the mountain slopes, together with all their kings. He left no survivors. He totally destroyed all who breathed, just as the LORD, the God of Israel, had commanded.
42All these kings and their lands Joshua conquered in one campaign, because the LORD, the God of Israel, fought for Israel. 5

Translation Notes

1 Hailstones (Avnim Gedolot): In the scrolls, this is an act of "Divine Artillery." The kings had formed a coalition to fight Israel, but God used the elements to fight for them. It demonstrates that the Creator is the "Lord of Hosts"—the commander of the heavenly and natural forces.

2 Book of Jashar (Sefer ha-Yashar): This is a lost ancient book mentioned in the scrolls (also in 2 Samuel 1:18). It was likely a collection of heroic poems and songs celebrating the "Upright ones" (*Yashar*) of Israel. It shows that the miracle of the Sun was so significant it was recorded in the secular and poetic annals of the nation.

3 Sun, stand still: This is the most famous miracle of the conquest. In the scrolls, it is presented as a response to "Joshua’s Boldness." God "listened to a human being" and suspended the cosmic order to complete the victory. It teaches that God is willing to break His own natural laws to preserve His covenantal people.

4 Feet on the Necks: This was a standard ancient Near Eastern ritual of "Total Submission." By having his commanders do this, Joshua was building their confidence. He wanted them to see that the "Terrifying Kings" were now under their feet. It was a visual lesson in the fulfillment of God’s promises.

5 Conquered in one campaign: The scrolls emphasize that the speed of the conquest was not due to Joshua’s brilliance, but to "God’s Agency." Because the LORD fought for Israel, they achieved in one season what would have normally taken a generation of siege warfare.

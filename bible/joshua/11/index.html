Joshua 11

1When Jabin king of Hazor heard of this, he sent word to Jobab king of Madon, to the kings of Shimron and Akshaph,
2and to the kings who were in the northern mountains... and in Naphoth Dor on the west.
4They came out with all their troops and a large number of horses and chariots—a huge army, as numerous as the sand on the seashore.
5All these kings joined forces and encamped together at the Waters of Merom to fight against Israel.
6The LORD said to Joshua, “Do not be afraid of them, because by this time tomorrow I will hand all of them over to Israel, slain. You are to hamstring their horses and burn their chariots.” 1
7So Joshua and his whole army came against them suddenly at the Waters of Merom and attacked them,
8and the LORD gave them into the hand of Israel. They defeated them and pursued them... until no survivors were left.
9Joshua did to them as the LORD had directed: He hamstrung their horses and burned their chariots.
10At that time Joshua turned back and captured Hazor and put its king to the sword. (Hazor had been the head of all these kingdoms.)
11Everyone in it they put to the sword. They totally destroyed them, not sparing anyone that breathed, and he burned Hazor itself. 2
15As the LORD commanded his servant Moses, so Moses commanded Joshua, and Joshua did it; he left nothing undone of all that the LORD commanded Moses. 3
18Joshua waged war against all these kings for a long time.
21At that time Joshua went and destroyed the Anakites from the hill country: from Hebron, Debir and Anab, from all the hill country of Judah, and from all the hill country of Israel. Joshua totally destroyed them and their towns. 4
22No Anakites were left in Israelite territory; only in Gaza, Gath and Ashdod did any survive.
23So Joshua took the entire land, just as the LORD had directed Moses, and he gave it as an inheritance to Israel according to their tribal divisions. Then the land had rest from war. 5

Translation Notes

1 Hamstring their horses: This is a very specific command in the scrolls. To "hamstring" means to cut the tendon of the horse's leg, making it unfit for war. God did not want Israel to develop a "Chariot Corps." By destroying the enemy’s superior technology instead of adopting it, Israel was forced to remain dependent on God’s power rather than their own military machinery.

2 Burned Hazor: Archaeology confirms that Hazor (the largest city in the region) was indeed burned in the late Bronze Age. The scrolls emphasize that Hazor was the "Head" of the kingdoms. By burning the "Head," Joshua was signaling the total end of the old Canaanite power structure.

3 Nothing Undone: This is the ultimate "Summary of Faithfulness" for Joshua in the scrolls. He didn't just do the easy parts; he did *everything* commanded. It teaches that true leadership is defined by comprehensive obedience to the revealed Word.

4 Anakites: These were the "Giants" that had terrified the previous generation (Numbers 13). Joshua’s victory over them shows that what was once a source of "National Panic" was now a "Routine Conquest" because of the presence of God. Faith had shrunk the giants down to size.

5 Rest from War (Shaketah ha-Aretz): Literally, the land "was quiet." This marks the end of the first phase of the conquest—the breaking of the major regional powers. Although individual pockets of resistance remained, the "Inheritance" was now ready to be divided among the tribes. It was the first time since leaving Egypt that the people had a home.

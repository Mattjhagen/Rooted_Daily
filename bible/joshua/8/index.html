Joshua 8

1Then the LORD said to Joshua, “Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged. Take the whole army with you, and go up and attack Ai. For I have delivered into your hands the king of Ai, his people, his city and his land.”
2“You shall do to Ai and its king as you did to Jericho and its king, except that you may carry off their livestock and plunder for yourselves. Set an ambush behind the city.” 1
3So Joshua and the whole army moved out to attack Ai. He chose thirty thousand of his best fighting men and sent them out at night.
18Then the LORD said to Joshua, “Hold out toward Ai the javelin that is in your hand, for into your hand I will deliver the city.” So Joshua held out toward the city the javelin that was in his hand.
19As soon as he did this, the men in the ambush rose quickly from their position and rushed forward. They entered the city and captured it and quickly set it on fire.
20When the men of Ai looked back and saw the smoke of the city rising, they had no chance to escape in any direction; the Israelites who had been fleeing toward the wilderness had turned back against their pursuers.
26For Joshua did not draw back the hand that held out his javelin until he had completely destroyed all who lived in Ai. 2
28So Joshua burned Ai and made it a permanent heap of ruins, a desolate place to this day.
29He impaled the body of the king of Ai on a pole and left it there until evening. At sunset, Joshua ordered them to take the body from the pole and throw it down at the entrance of the city gate. And they raised a large pile of rocks over it, which remains to this day.
30Then Joshua built on Mount Ebal an altar to the LORD, the God of Israel, 3
31as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded the Israelites. He built it according to what is written in the Book of the Law of Moses—an altar of uncut stones, on which no iron tool had been used. On it they offered to the LORD burnt offerings and sacrificed fellowship offerings.
32There, in the presence of the Israelites, Joshua wrote on stones a copy of the law of Moses.
33All the Israelites, with their elders, officials and judges, were standing on both sides of the ark of the covenant of the LORD... half of the people stood in front of Mount Gerizim and half of them in front of Mount Ebal.
34Afterward, Joshua read all the words of the law—the blessings and the curses—exactly as it is written in the Book of the Law. 4
35There was not a word of all that Moses had commanded that Joshua did not read to the whole assembly of Israel, including the women and children, and the foreigners who lived among them.

Translation Notes

1 Plunder for yourselves: In the scrolls, this is a significant change from Jericho. Jericho was "Firstfruits" and belonged entirely to God. Ai, however, was opened for the people to take booty. This shows that the command for "Total Devotion" (*Cherem*) was specific to Jericho as a spiritual milestone. God provides for the needs of His army through the spoils of subsequent battles.

2 The Javelin (Kidon): Joshua’s outstretched javelin in the scrolls acts as a "Battle Standard." It mirrors Moses holding up his staff during the battle with Amalek (Exodus 17). It represents the persistence of faith; as long as the leader’s focus and hand remained directed toward the objective, the victory continued. It was a visual sign to the army that God’s presence was with them.

3 Renewal at Ebal: This fulfill’s the command of Deuteronomy 27. Joshua marched the entire nation (millions of people) deep into enemy territory to reach Shechem. This was a "Sacred Interruption" of the military campaign. It teaches that worship and Law-reading are more important than military momentum. They were "Pausing to Reconnect" with the source of their strength.

4 Read all the words: This is a powerful moment of "National Education" in the scrolls. No one was excluded—not children, and not even the "foreigners" who had joined the camp. The Law was the great equalizer. By hearing the curses and blessings in the very geography where they were commanded, the words became "Real" and "Territorial" to the new generation.

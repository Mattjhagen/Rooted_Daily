Joshua 13

1When Joshua had grown old, the LORD said to him, “You are now very old, and there are still very large areas of land to be taken over.” 1
2“This is the land that remains: all the regions of the Philistines and Geshurites,”
6“As for all the inhabitants of the mountain regions... I myself will drive them out before the Israelites. Be sure to allocate this land to Israel for an inheritance, as I have instructed you,”
7“and divide it as an inheritance among the nine tribes and half of the tribe of Manasseh.”
8The other half of Manasseh, the Reubenites and the Gadites had received the inheritance that Moses had given them east of the Jordan, as he, the servant of the LORD, had assigned it to them.
13But the Israelites did not drive out the people of Geshur and Maakah, so they continue to live among the Israelites to this day. 2
14But to the tribe of Levi he gave no inheritance of land, because the food offerings presented to the LORD, the God of Israel, are their inheritance, as he promised them. 3

Reuben's Portion: From Aroer on the rim of the Arnon Gorge... Heshbon and all its towns. (v.15-23)

Gad's Portion: The territory of Jazer, all the towns of Gilead and half the Ammonite country. (v.24-28)

Half-Manasseh's Portion: All of Bashan, the entire kingdom of Og. (v.29-31)

22In addition to those slain in battle, the Israelites put to the sword Balaam son of Beor, who practiced divination. 4
33But to the tribe of Levi Moses had given no inheritance; the LORD, the God of Israel, is their inheritance, as he promised them.

Translation Notes

1 You are now very old: In the scrolls, this is a moment of "Generational Reality." Joshua had broken the back of the enemy resistance, but the "Granular Conquest" of every town was too much for one man’s lifetime. The work of God is always larger than any one leader. The division of the land was a way to empower the individual tribes to finish the job locally.

2 Did not drive out: The scrolls begin to note the failures of the tribes to complete the conquest. By allowing pockets of Canaanite culture to remain, they were inviting future spiritual compromise. It is a recurring warning: an incomplete obedience leads to a complicated future.

3 Levitical Inheritance: The scrolls repeat this twice (v.14 and v.33). The Levites were not given a block of territory so that they would not be tied to the "Economy of Agriculture." Their survival was tied directly to the spiritual health of the nation (the offerings). If the people stopped worshiping, the Levites would starve. This kept the spiritual leaders focused on their primary mission.

4 Balaam son of Beor: The scrolls record the final end of the prophet-for-hire from the Book of Numbers. Although he had blessed Israel under divine constraint, he had later advised the Midianites on how to seduce Israel into idolatry (Numbers 31:16). His death in battle shows that "Spiritual Gifts" without "Moral Integrity" lead to destruction.

Joshua 17

1This was the allotment for the tribe of Manasseh as Joseph’s firstborn...
3Now Zelophehad son of Hepher... had no sons but only daughters, whose names were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milkah and Tirzah.
4They went to Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun and the leaders and said, “The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our relatives.” So Joshua gave them an inheritance along with the brothers of their father. 1
7The territory of Manasseh extended from Asher to Mikmethath east of Shechem.
12Yet the Manassites were not able to occupy these towns, for the Canaanites were determined to live in that region. 2
13However, when the Israelites grew stronger, they subjected the Canaanites to forced labor but did not drive them out completely.
14The people of Joseph said to Joshua, “Why have you given us only one allotment and one portion for an inheritance? We are a numerous people, and the LORD has blessed us abundantly.”
15“If you are so numerous,” Joshua answered, “and if the hill country of Ephraim is too small for you, go up into the forest and clear land for yourselves there in the land of the Perizzites and Rephaites.” 3
16The people of Joseph replied, “The hill country is not enough for us, and all the Canaanites who live in the plain have iron chariots, both those in Beth Shan and its settlements and those in the Valley of Jezreel.”
17But Joshua said to the tribe of Joseph—to Ephraim and Manasseh—“You are numerous and very powerful. You will have not only one allotment”
18“but the forested hill country as well. Clear it, and its farthest limits will be yours; though the Canaanites have iron chariots and though they are strong, you can drive them out.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Daughters of Zelophehad: This case in the scrolls is a landmark for "Justice and Inheritance Rights." It shows that the Law was dynamic and could respond to specific family tragedies to ensure that no family line was blotted out of the land. It emphasizes the importance of "Persistent Faith"—these women had to claim what was promised to them.

2 Determined to live: The scrolls use a word that implies the Canaanites "dug in" and refused to move. It shows that the conquest was not just a series of miraculous victories, but a long, grinding struggle against a stubborn enemy. The "Inheritance" had to be fought for city by city.

3 The Complaint: The Josephites (Ephraim and Manasseh) were powerful and numerous, but they had a "Spirit of Entitlement." They wanted Joshua to give them more "Ready-Made" land rather than working to clear the forests or fighting the difficult enemies. Joshua’s response was a call to "Maturity"—if they were truly so numerous, they should use that strength to create their own space.

4 Iron Chariots: In the scrolls, "Iron Chariots" were the "High-Tech Weapons" of the era. The people were terrified of them, but Joshua reminded them that their spiritual power was superior to any technology. It teaches that our "Numerousness" and "Power" are only useful if they are coupled with the "Courage to Drive Out" the obstacles in our way.

Deuteronomy 23

1No one who has been emasculated by crushing or cutting may enter the assembly of the LORD.
2No one of illegitimate birth may enter the assembly of the LORD; even to the tenth generation, none of their descendants may enter.
3No Ammonite or Moabite or any of their descendants may enter the assembly of the LORD, not even to the tenth generation. 1
4For they did not come to meet you with bread and water on your way when you came out of Egypt, and they hired Balaam son of Beor from Pethor in Aram Naharaim to pronounce a curse on you.
5However, the LORD your God would not listen to Balaam but turned the curse into a blessing for you, because the LORD your God loves you.
6Do not seek a treaty of friendship with them as long as you live.
7Do not despise an Edomite, for the Edomite is your relative. Do not despise an Egyptian, because you would have been a foreigner in his land.
8The third generation of children born to them may enter the assembly of the LORD.
9When you are encamped against your enemies, keep away from everything impure.
10If one of your men is unclean because of a nocturnal emission, he is to go outside the camp and stay there.
11But as evening approaches he is to bathe himself, and at sunset he may return to the camp.
12Designate a place outside the camp where you can go to relieve yourself.
13As part of your equipment have something to dig with, and when you relieve yourself, dig a hole and cover up your excrement. 2
14For the LORD your God moves about in your camp to protect you and to deliver your enemies to you. Your camp must be holy, so that he will not see among you anything indecent and turn away from you.
15If a slave has taken refuge with you, do not hand them over to their master. 3
16Let them live among you wherever they like and in whatever town they choose. Do not oppress them.
17No Israelite man or woman is to become a shrine prostitute.
18You must not bring the earnings of a female prostitute or of a male prostitute into the house of the LORD your God to pay any vow, for the LORD your God detests them both.
19Do not charge a fellow Israelite interest, whether on money or food or anything else that may earn interest. 4
20You may charge a foreigner interest, but not a fellow Israelite, so that the LORD your God may bless you in everything you put your hand to in the land you are entering to possess.
21If you make a vow to the LORD your God, do not be slow to pay it, for the LORD your God will certainly demand it of you and you will be guilty of sin.
22But if you refrain from making a vow, you will not be guilty.
23Whatever your lips utter you must be faithful to do, because you made your vow freely to the LORD your God with your own mouth.
24If you enter your neighbor’s vineyard, you may eat all the grapes you want, but do not put any in your basket.
25If you enter your neighbor’s standing grain, you may pluck the ears with your hands, but you must not put a sickle to their standing grain.

Translation Notes

1 Exclusion from the Assembly: In the scrolls, "Entering the Assembly" (*Kahal*) refers to full citizenship and the right to hold office. The exclusion of Ammon and Moab was not about ethnicity, but about their history of "Active Hostility" (hiring Balaam). Contrast this with Edom and Egypt, who were to be treated with relative kindness because of kinship or historical hospitality. It teaches that national relationships are shaped by history.

2 Camp Hygiene: This is a unique law in the ancient world. While other armies lived in filth, the scrolls commanded strict sanitation. The reason given is "Spiritual" rather than "Medical": God "moves about in your camp." It teaches that God cares about the physical details of our environment. Cleanliness is here literally linked to the Divine Presence.

3 Escaped Slaves: This law is a massive departure from the "Code of Hammurabi" and other ancient laws, which required the return of runaway slaves on pain of death. In the scrolls, Israel was to be a "Sanctuary State." Because they had been slaves in Egypt, they were never to return an escaped slave to a master. It is a radical law of "Human Liberation."

4 Interest (Neshech): The word for interest in the scrolls is *Neshech*, which means "to bite." Charging interest to a brother was seen as taking a "bite" out of their survival. The goal was to prevent a "Debt Trap" within the community. Business with foreigners followed standard international trade rules, but within the nation, the economy was to be based on "Mutual Aid" rather than "Profit from Poverty."

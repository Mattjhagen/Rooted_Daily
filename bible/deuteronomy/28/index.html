Deuteronomy 28

1If you fully obey the LORD your God and carefully follow all his commands I give you today, the LORD your God will set you high above all the nations on earth.
2All these blessings will come on you and accompany you if you obey the LORD your God:

The City & Field: You will be blessed in the city and blessed in the country. (v.3)

The Household: The fruit of your womb, the crops of your land, and the young of your livestock will be blessed. (v.4)

The Provision: Your basket and your kneading trough will be blessed. (v.5)

The Journey: You will be blessed when you come in and blessed when you go out. (v.6)

7The LORD will grant that the enemies who rise up against you will be defeated before you. They will come at you from one direction but flee from you in seven.
12The LORD will open the heavens, the storehouse of his bounty, to send rain on your land in season and to bless all the work of your hands. You will lend to many nations but will borrow from none.
13The LORD will make you the head, not the tail. If you pay attention to the commands of the LORD your God that I give you this day and carefully follow them, you will always be at the top, never at the bottom. 1
15However, if you do not obey the LORD your God and do not carefully follow all his commands and decrees I am giving you today, all these curses will come on you and overtake you:

The Reversal: You will be cursed in the city and cursed in the country. Your basket and kneading trough will be cursed. You will be cursed when you come in and cursed when you go out. (v.16-19)

20The LORD will send on you curses, confusion and rebuke in everything you put your hand to, until you are destroyed and come to sudden ruin because of the evil you have done in forsaking him.
23The sky over your head will be bronze, the ground beneath you iron. 2
25The LORD will cause you to be defeated before your enemies. You will come at them from one direction but flee from them in seven, and you will become a thing of horror to all the kingdoms on earth.
28The LORD will afflict you with madness, blindness and confusion of mind.
37You will become an object of horror, a byword and an object of ridicule among all the peoples where the LORD will drive you.
43The foreigner who resides among you will rise above you higher and higher, but you will sink lower and lower. 3
44He will lend to you, but you will not lend to him. He will be the head, but you will be the tail.
53Because of the suffering your enemy will inflict on you during the siege, you will eat the fruit of the womb, the flesh of the sons and daughters the LORD your God has given you.
64Then the LORD will scatter you among all nations, from one end of the earth to the other. There you will worship other gods—gods of wood and stone, which neither you nor your ancestors have known.
65Among those nations you will find no repose, no resting place for the sole of your foot. There the LORD will give you an anxious mind, eyes weary with longing, and a despairing heart. 4
66You will live in constant suspense, filled with dread both night and day, never sure of your life.

Translation Notes

1 Head and the Tail: In the original scrolls, this is a metaphor for "Causation" and "Leadership." To be the head is to set the direction; to be the tail is to be dragged along by the decisions of others. God promised that an obedient Israel would be the world's leader in wisdom, economy, and justice. They would be the "Influencers," not the "Influenced."

2 Sky of Bronze, Ground of Iron: This is a terrifying image of "Environmental Closure" in the scrolls. In a land that "drinks rain from heaven," a sky that acts like bronze means no rain can penetrate it, and a ground like iron means no seed can break through it. It describes a complete breakdown of the life-support system of the Land.

3 The Foreigner will Rise: In the scrolls, this is a specific social curse. Israel was commanded to care for the foreigner as a "Vulnerable Guest." The curse is that the guest becomes the master, and the host becomes the servant. It is the loss of sovereignty within one's own borders.

4 Anxious Mind (Lev Ragaz): Literally in the scrolls, a "Trembling Heart." This is perhaps the most tragic of the curses—the loss of internal peace. Even if they survived the wars and the sieges, the people in exile would live with a permanent sense of dread and uncertainty. It teaches that the ultimate consequence of breaking the covenant is the disintegration of the human psyche.

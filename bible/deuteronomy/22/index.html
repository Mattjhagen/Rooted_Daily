Deuteronomy 22

1If you see your fellow Israelite’s ox or sheep straying, do not ignore it but be sure to take it back to its owner.
2If they do not live near you or if you do not know who they are, take it home with you and keep it until they come looking for it. Then give it back.
3Do the same if you find their donkey or cloak or anything else they have lost. Do not ignore it. 1
4If you see your fellow Israelite’s donkey or ox fallen on the road, do not ignore it. Help the owner get it to its feet.
5A woman must not wear men’s clothing, nor a man wear women’s clothing, for the LORD your God detests anyone who does this.
6If you come across a bird’s nest beside the road, either in a tree or on the ground, and the mother is sitting on the young or on the eggs, do not take the mother with the young. 2
7You may take the young, but be sure to let the mother go, so that it may go well with you and you may have a long life.
8When you build a new house, make a parapet around your roof so that you may not bring the guilt of bloodshed on your house if someone falls from the roof. 3
9Do not plant two kinds of seed in your vineyard; if you do, not only the crops you plant but also the fruit of the vineyard will be defiled.
10Do not plow with an ox and a donkey yoked together.
11Do not wear clothes of wool and linen woven together.
12Make tassels on the four corners of the cloak you wear.
13If a man takes a wife and, after sleeping with her, dislikes her
14and slanders her and gives her a bad name, saying, “I married this woman, but when I approached her, I did not find proof of her virginity,”
15then the young woman’s father and mother shall bring to the town elders at the gate proof that she was a virgin... (The legal process for marital disputes continues in verses 16-30).

Translation Notes

1 Do not ignore it (Lo tuchal l'hit'alem): Literally in the scrolls, "You are not able to hide yourself." This is a law of "Positive Duty." It wasn't enough to not *steal* a neighbor's property; you were forbidden from simply "looking the other way" when their property was at risk. It teaches that indifference is a form of sin. A holy community is one where everyone looks out for the interests of others.

2 The Mother Bird: In the scrolls, this is the "Smallest Commandment" with the "Greatest Reward" (long life). By letting the mother go, the person preserves the *source* of the life while taking the *fruit*. It is a law against "Greed-driven Extinction." It teaches that even in small things, we must show compassion and think of the future sustainability of the world.

3 Parapet on the Roof (Ma'akeh): In the scrolls, roofs were flat and used as living spaces. This is a law of "Public Safety" and "Liability." If you own property, you are responsible for ensuring it is not a hazard to others. It is the biblical basis for building codes. Holiness is not just about prayer; it's about making sure your balcony is safe.

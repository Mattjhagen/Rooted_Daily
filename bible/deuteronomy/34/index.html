Deuteronomy 34

1Then Moses climbed Mount Nebo from the plains of Moab to the top of Pisgah, across from Jericho. There the LORD showed him the whole land—from Gilead to Dan, 1
2all of Naphtali, the territory of Ephraim and Manasseh, all the land of Judah as far as the Mediterranean Sea,
3the Negev and the whole region from the Valley of Jericho, the City of Palms, as far as Zoar.
4Then the LORD said to him, “This is the land I promised on oath to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob when I said, ‘I will give it to your descendants.’ I have let you see it with your eyes, but you will not cross over into it.”
5And Moses the servant of the LORD died there in Moab, as the LORD had said.
6He buried him in Moab, in the valley opposite Beth Peor, but to this day no one knows where his grave is. 2
7Moses was a hundred and twenty years old when he died, yet his eyes were not weak nor his strength gone.
8The Israelites grieved for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days, until the time of weeping and mourning was over.
9Now Joshua son of Nun was filled with the spirit of wisdom because Moses had laid his hands on him. So the Israelites listened to him and did what the LORD had commanded Moses. 3
10Since then, no prophet has risen in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face, 4
11who did all those signs and wonders the LORD sent him to do in Egypt—to Pharaoh and to all his officials and to his whole land.
12For no one has ever shown the mighty power or performed the awesome deeds that Moses did in the sight of all Israel.

Translation Notes

1 The View from Nebo: In the scrolls, God "showed" Moses the land. This was likely a supernatural vision, as one cannot physically see all these locations from a single peak. It was a final act of grace, allowing the shepherd to see the destination of his flock before he laid down his staff.

2 The Secret Grave: The scrolls record that God Himself buried Moses. The location was kept secret to prevent the grave from becoming a site of idolatry or a pagan shrine. Moses was to be remembered for his *Word*, not his *Bones*. It teaches that the servant is less important than the Message.

3 Spirit of Wisdom: In the scrolls, the transition of leadership was accomplished by the "Laying on of Hands" (*Semichah*). This was not just a political appointment, but a transfer of spiritual authority and capacity. Joshua was prepared because he had been "filled" through his long apprenticeship with Moses.

4 Face to Face (Panim el-Panim): This is the ultimate epitaph in the scrolls. While other prophets received visions and dreams, Moses had a unique, conversational intimacy with the Creator. He is the standard against which all other leaders are measured. The Torah ends not with a conquest, but with the memory of a relationship.

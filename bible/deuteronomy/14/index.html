Deuteronomy 14

1You are the children of the LORD your God. Do not cut yourselves or shave the front of your heads for the dead,
2for you are a people holy to the LORD your God. Out of all the peoples on the face of the earth, the LORD has chosen you to be his treasured possession. 1
3Do not eat any detestable thing.

Clean and Unclean: The scrolls list specific categories for a "Holy Diet":

  • Land Animals: Any animal that has a divided hoof and chews the cud (Cow, sheep, goat, deer, etc.). The camel, rabbit, and pig are forbidden.
  • Water Creatures: Anything that has both fins and scales.
  • Birds: A list of forbidden scavengers and predators (Eagles, vultures, ravens, owls, storks, bats).
  • Winged Insects: Only those with jointed legs for hopping are allowed (Locusts).
21Do not eat anything you find already dead. You may give it to the foreigner residing in any of your towns, and they may eat it, or you may sell it to any other foreigner. But you are a people holy to the LORD your God. Do not cook a young goat in its mother’s milk. 2
22Be sure to set aside a tenth of all that your fields produce each year.
23Eat the tithe of your grain, new wine and olive oil, and the firstborn of your herds and flocks in the presence of the LORD your God at the place he will choose as a dwelling for his Name, so that you may learn to revere the LORD your God always.
24But if that place is too far and you have been blessed by the LORD your God and cannot carry your tithe (because the place where the LORD will choose to put his Name is so far away),
25then exchange your tithe for silver, and take the silver with you and go to the place the LORD your God will choose.
26Use the silver to buy whatever you like: cattle, sheep, wine or other fermented drink, or anything you wish. Then you and your household shall eat there in the presence of the LORD your God and rejoice.
27And do not neglect the Levites living in your towns, for they have no allotment or inheritance of their own.
28At the end of every three years, bring all the tithes of that year’s produce and store it in your towns, 3
29so that the Levites (who have no allotment or inheritance of their own) and the foreigners, the fatherless and the widows who live in your towns may come and eat and be satisfied, and so that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands.

Translation Notes

1 Holy Distinction: In the scrolls, "Holiness" (*Kadosh*) means "Separated." These laws were not just about hygiene, but about identity. By eating differently and mourning differently than the surrounding nations, the Israelites were constantly reminded that they belonged to a different Kingdom. Their daily choices—even what they put in their mouths—were acts of worship.

2 Mother’s Milk: This cryptic command in the scrolls is the basis for the Jewish laws of *Kashrut* (separating meat and dairy). In the ancient world, cooking a kid in its mother's milk was a pagan fertility ritual. God is commanding a "Reverence for Life": one should not use the very substance meant to *nurture* a life to *cook* that life. It is a prohibition against cruelty and the mixing of life and death.

3 The Social Tithe: In the scrolls, the "Tithe of the Third Year" was a beautiful social safety net. Instead of taking the produce to the central sanctuary, it was stored locally for the vulnerable members of the community. It teaches that God’s blessing on our produce is intended to be shared. The prosperity of the nation was measured by how well the "Foreigner, Fatherless, and Widow" were fed.

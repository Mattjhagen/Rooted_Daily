Deuteronomy 32

1Listen, you heavens, and I will speak; hear, you earth, the words of my mouth.
2Let my teaching fall like rain and my words descend like dew, like showers on new grass, like abundant rain on tender plants.
3I will proclaim the name of the LORD. Oh, praise the greatness of our God!
4He is the Rock, his works are perfect, and all his ways are just. A faithful God who does no wrong, upright and just is he. 1
5They have acted corruptly toward him; to their shame they are no longer his children, but a warped and crooked generation.
6Is this the way you repay the LORD, you foolish and unwise people? Is he not your Father, your Creator, who made you and formed you?
7Remember the days of old; consider the generations long past. Ask your father and he will tell you, your elders, and they will explain to it to you.
8When the Most High gave the nations their inheritance, when he divided all mankind, he set up boundaries for the peoples according to the number of the sons of God. 2
9For the LORD’s portion is his people, Jacob his allotted inheritance.
10In a desert land he found him, in a barren and howling waste. He shielded him and cared for him; he guarded him as the apple of his eye,
11like an eagle that stirs up its nest and hovers over its young, that spreads its wings to catch them and carries them aloft.
12The LORD alone led him; no foreign god was with him.
15Jeshurun grew fat and kicked; filled with food, they became heavy and sleek. They abandoned the God who made them and rejected the Rock their Savior. 3
17They sacrificed to false gods, which are not God—gods they had not known, gods that recently appeared, gods your ancestors did not fear.
18You deserted the Rock, who fathered you; you forgot the God who gave you birth.
21They made me jealous by what is no god and angered me with their worthless idols. I will make them envious by those who are not a people; I will make them angry by a nation that has no understanding.
35It is mine to avenge; I will repay. In due time their foot will slip; their day of disaster is near and their doom rushes upon them.
39“See now that I myself am he! There is no god besides me. I put to death and I bring to life, I have wounded and I will heal, and no one can deliver out of my hand.” 4
43Rejoice, you nations, with his people, for he will avenge the blood of his servants; he will take vengeance on his enemies and make atonement for his land and people.
44Moses came with Joshua son of Nun and spoke all the words of this song in the hearing of the people.
45When Moses finished reciting all these words to all Israel,
46he said to them, “Take to heart all the words I have solemnly declared to you this day, so that you may command your children to obey carefully all the words of this law.”
47“They are not just idle words for you—they are your life. By them you will live long in the land you are crossing the Jordan to possess.”
48On that same day the LORD said to Moses,
49“Go up into the Abarim Range to Mount Nebo in Moab, across from Jericho, and view Canaan, the land I am giving the Israelites as their own possession.”
50“There on the mountain that you have climbed you will die and be gathered to your people, just as your brother Aaron died on Mount Hor and was gathered to his people.”

Translation Notes

1 The Rock (HaTzur): This is the first time in the scrolls that God is systematically called "The Rock." It emphasizes His immutability, stability, and reliability in contrast to the "fickle" nature of the people. The word implies that God is the foundational reality that does not shift with the times.

2 Sons of God (Bnei Elohim): In the oldest Hebrew manuscripts (and the Dead Sea Scrolls), this verse says "according to the number of the *Sons of God*" rather than "the sons of Israel" (which is found in the later Masoretic text). This suggests an ancient theology where God assigned heavenly guardians or lower "elohim" over the other nations, while keeping Israel for Himself. It is a glimpse into a very ancient biblical worldview.

3 Jeshurun: This is a poetic title for Israel in the scrolls, derived from the word *Yashar* (Upright). It is used ironically here: "Upright-one grew fat and kicked." It describes the tragedy of a people who, once they achieve the prosperity God promised, use that very strength to rebel against Him.

4 I put to death and I bring to life: This is the ultimate statement of "Monotheistic Power" in the scrolls. God claims total sovereignty over the life-cycle and the course of history. He is not just one god among many who handles "death" or "healing"; He is the sole proprietor of both. It teaches that nothing—not even tragedy—is outside of His jurisdiction.

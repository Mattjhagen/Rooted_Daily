Deuteronomy 9

1Hear, Israel: You are now about to cross the Jordan to go in and dispossess nations greater and stronger than you, with large cities that have walls up to the sky.
2The people are strong and tall—Anakites! You know about them and have heard it said: “Who can stand up against the Anakites?”
3But be assured today that the LORD your God is the one who goes across ahead of you like a devouring fire. He will destroy them; he will subdue them before you. And you will drive them out and annihilate them quickly, as the LORD has promised you.
4After the LORD your God has driven them out before you, do not say to yourself, “The LORD has brought me here to take possession of this land because of my righteousness.” No, it is on account of the wickedness of these nations that the LORD is going to drive them out before you. 1
5It is not because of your righteousness or your integrity that you are going in to take possession of their land; but on account of the wickedness of these nations, the LORD your God will drive them out before you, to accomplish what he swore to your ancestors, to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.
6Understand, then, that it is not because of your righteousness that the LORD your God is giving you this good land to possess, for you are a stiff-necked people. 2
7Remember this and never forget how you aroused the anger of the LORD your God in the wilderness. From the day you left Egypt until you arrived here, you have been rebellious against the LORD.
8At Horeb you aroused the LORD’s wrath so that he was angry enough to destroy you.
9When I went up on the mountain to receive the tablets of stone, the tablets of the covenant that the LORD had made with you, I stayed on the mountain forty days and forty nights; I ate no bread and drank no water.
10The LORD gave me two stone tablets inscribed by the finger of God. On them were all the commandments the LORD proclaimed to you on the mountain out of the fire, on the day of the assembly.
11At the end of the forty days and forty nights, the LORD gave me the two stone tablets, the tablets of the covenant.
12Then the LORD told me, “Go down from here at once, because your people whom you brought out of Egypt have become corrupt. They have turned away quickly from what I commanded them and have made an idol for themselves.”
13And the LORD said to me, “I have seen this people, and they are a stiff-necked people indeed!”
14“Let me alone, so that I may destroy them and blot out their name from under heaven. And I will make you into a nation stronger and more numerous than they.”
15So I turned and went down from the mountain while it was ablaze with fire. And the two tablets of the covenant were in my hands.
16When I looked, I saw that you had sinned against the LORD your God; you had made for yourselves an idol cast in the shape of a calf. You had turned away quickly from the way that the LORD had commanded you.
17So I took the two tablets and threw them out of my hands, breaking them to pieces before your eyes. 3
18Then once again I fell facedown before the LORD for forty days and forty nights; I ate no bread and drank no water, because of all the sin you had committed, doing evil in the LORD’s sight and so arousing his anger.
19I feared the anger and wrath of the LORD, for he was angry enough with you to destroy you. But again the LORD listened to me.
20And the LORD was angry enough with Aaron to destroy him, but at that time I prayed for Aaron too.
21Also I took that sinful thing of yours, the calf you had made, and burned it in the fire. Then I crushed it and ground it to powder as fine as dust and threw the dust into a stream that flowed down the mountain.
22You also made the LORD angry at Taberah, at Massah and at Kibroth Hattaavah.
23And when the LORD sent you out from Kadesh Barnea, he said, “Go up and take possession of the land I have given you.” But you rebelled against the command of the LORD your God. You did not trust him or obey him.
24You have been rebellious against the LORD ever since I have known you.
25I lay facedown before the LORD those forty days and forty nights because the LORD had said he would destroy you.
26I prayed to the LORD and said, “Sovereign LORD, do not destroy your people, your own inheritance that you redeemed by your great power and brought out of Egypt with a mighty hand.” 4
27“Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Overlook the stubbornness of this people, their wickedness and their sin.”
28“Otherwise, the country from which you brought us will say, ‘Because the LORD was not able to take them into the land he had promised them, and because he hated them, he brought them out to put them to death in the wilderness.’”
29“But they are your people, your inheritance that you brought out by your great power and your outstretched arm.”

Translation Notes

1 Wickedness of the Nations: In the scrolls, the conquest is described as a "cleaning of the house." The Canaanites were not being removed because Israel was "good," but because they were "ripe for judgment" (Genesis 15:16). Their cultures involved institutionalized child sacrifice and extreme sexual deviancy. Israel was merely the instrument of a much larger cosmic justice.

2 Stiff-Necked (Am k'shei oref): This is a powerful agricultural metaphor in the scrolls. It refers to an ox that refuses to turn its head to the yoke of its master. It suggests a deep-seated stubbornness that resists direction. Moses is puncturing the balloon of Israel's national pride before they even cross the river.

3 Breaking the Tablets: In the original scrolls, this was more than a fit of temper. In ancient Near Eastern law, a contract was "broken" physically to show it was legally void. By breaking the tablets, Moses was showing the people that their covenant with God was dead because of the Golden Calf. He then spent the next forty days begging God to "renew" the contract.

4 The Intercession of Moses: The scrolls record a profound logic in Moses' prayer. He doesn't defend Israel's behavior; he appeals to God's *Reputation* and God's *Ancestry*. He argues that if God destroys them, the Egyptians will mock God's power. And he reminds God of the promise made to the patriarchs. It is a model of "Kingdom-Centered Prayer"—praying for God’s name to be honored above all else.

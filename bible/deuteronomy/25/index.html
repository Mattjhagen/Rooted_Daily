Deuteronomy 25

1When people have a dispute, they are to take it to court and the judges will decide the case, acquitting the innocent and condemning the guilty.
2If the guilty person deserves to be beaten, the judge shall make them lie down and have them flogged in his presence with the number of lashes the crime deserves,
3but the judge must not give more than forty lashes. If the guilty person is flogged more than that, your fellow Israelite will be degraded in your eyes. 1
4Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain. 2
5If brothers are living together and one of them dies without a son, his widow must not marry outside the family. Her husband’s brother shall take her and marry her and fulfill the duty of a brother-in-law to her.
6The first son she bears shall carry on the name of the dead brother so that his name will not be blotted out from Israel.
7However, if a man does not want to marry his brother’s wife, she shall go to the elders at the town gate and say, “My husband’s brother refuses to carry on his brother’s name in Israel. He will not fulfill the duty of a brother-in-law to me.”
8Then the elders of his town shall summon him and talk to him. If he persists in saying, “I do not want to marry her,”
9his brother’s wife shall go up to him in the presence of the elders, take off one of his sandals, spit in his face and say, “This is what is done to the man who will not build up his brother’s family line.” 3
10That man’s line shall be known in Israel as The Family of the Unsandaled.
11If two men are fighting and the wife of one of them comes to rescue her husband from his assailant, and she reaches out and seizes him by his private parts,
12you shall cut off her hand. Show her no pity.
13Do not have two differing weights in your bag—one heavy, one light.
14Do not have two differing measures in your house—one large, one small.
15You must have accurate and honest weights and measures, so that you may live long in the land the LORD your God is giving you. 4
16For the LORD your God detests anyone who does these things, anyone who deals dishonestly.
17Remember what the Amalekites did to you along the way when you came out of Egypt. 5
18When you were weary and worn out, they met you on your journey and attacked all who were lagging behind; they had no fear of God.
19When the LORD your God gives you rest from all the enemies around you in the land he is giving you to possess as an inheritance, you shall blot out the name of Amalek from under heaven. Do not forget!

Translation Notes

1 Forty Lashes: In the original scrolls, this is a law of "Human Dignity." Even a criminal was still a "fellow Israelite." To flog someone excessively was to treat them like an animal and degrade their humanity. Jewish tradition later set the limit at 39 lashes to ensure the biblical limit was never accidentally exceeded (2 Corinthians 11:24).

2 Muzzle an Ox: This is a profound law of "Empathy for Creation." If an animal is working to provide food for a human, it should be allowed to enjoy the fruit of its labor. It teaches that God hates greed that exploits the weak, even in the animal kingdom. Paul later applies this to the right of spiritual workers to be supported (1 Corinthians 9:9).

3 The Sandal Ceremony (Chalitzah): This ritual in the scrolls was a "Public Shame" for a man who refused to protect his family’s legacy. By removing his sandal, the widow was taking back the "right to walk on the land" that he had abdicated. It was a legal way for her to be released from the family bond so she could marry elsewhere. It highlights the importance of "Family Responsibility."

4 Honest Weights (Even Shelemah): The scrolls link "Marketplace Honesty" directly to "Longevity in the Land." A society based on deception cannot survive. If a merchant has two sets of weights—one for buying and one for selling—he is attacking the very fabric of social trust. God is described as "detesting" Dishonesty.

5 Remember Amalek: Amalek represents the "Cowardly Predator" in the scrolls. Instead of fighting the army, they attacked the "lagging behind"—the elderly, the sick, and the children. Because they targeted the vulnerable, they incurred a divine sentence of erasure. It is a warning that God specifically notices and judges those who prey on the weak.

Deuteronomy 33

1This is the blessing that Moses the man of God pronounced on the Israelites before his death.
2He said: “The LORD came from Sinai and dawned over them from Seir; he shone forth from Mount Paran. He came with myriads of holy ones from the south, from his mountain slopes.” 1
3“Surely it is you who love the people; all the holy ones are in your hand. At your feet they all bow down, and from you they receive instruction,”
4“the law that Moses gave us, the possession of the assembly of Jacob.”
5“He was king over Jeshurun when the leaders of the people assembled, along with the tribes of Israel.”

Reuben: “Let Reuben live and not die, nor his people be few.” (v.6)

Judah: “Hear, LORD, the cry of Judah; bring him to his people. With his own hands he defends his cause. Oh, be his help against his foes!” (v.7)

Levi: “Your Thummim and Urim belong to your faithful servant... He watched over your word and guarded your covenant.” (v.8-9)

Benjamin: “Let the beloved of the LORD rest secure in him, for he shields him all day long, and the one the LORD loves rests between his shoulders.” (v.12)

Joseph: “May the LORD bless his land with the precious dew from heaven above and with the deep waters that lie below; with the best the sun brings forth and the finest the moon yields.” (v.13-14)

Zebulun & Issachar: “Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out, and you, Issachar, in your tents.” (v.18)

Gad: “Blessed is he who enlarges Gad’s domain! Gad lives there like a lion, tearing at arm or head.” (v.20)

Dan: “Dan is a lion’s cub, springing out of Bashan.” (v.22)

Naphtali: “Naphtali is abounding with the favor of the LORD and is full of his blessing; he will inherit southward to the lake.” (v.23)

Asher: “Most blessed of sons is Asher; let him be favored by his brothers, and let him dip his foot in oil.” (v.24)

26“There is no one like the God of Jeshurun, who rides on the heavens to help you and on the clouds in his majesty.” 2
27“The eternal God is your refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms. He will drive out your enemies before you, saying, ‘Destroy them!’” 3
28“So Israel will live in safety; Jacob will dwell secure in a land of grain and new wine, where the heavens drop dew.”
29“Blessed are you, Israel! Who is like you, a people saved by the LORD? He is your shield and helper and your glorious sword.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Fiery Law (Esh-Dat): This is a very difficult Hebrew phrase in the scrolls. Some translate it as "fire of a law" or "fiery stream." It describes the revelation at Sinai as a marriage of "Fire" (Power) and "Law" (Order). It suggests that the Word of God is both illuminating and purifying.

2 God of Jeshurun: In the scrolls, this title emphasizes God's relationship to His people as their Protector and Helper. The image of God "riding the clouds" was a direct polemic against the Canaanite god Baal, who was also called "The Cloud Rider." The scrolls are asserting that the God of Israel is the true master of the elements.

3 Everlasting Arms (Zro'ot Olam): This is one of the most comforting images in the scrolls. It describes God’s support as something that is "Underneath"—meaning that no matter how far a person or a nation falls, they cannot fall beyond the reach of His support. It is the biblical definition of "Grace."

4 A People Saved by the LORD: The scrolls conclude the blessing by defining Israel’s unique identity. They are not defined by their military power or their wealth, but by their *Relationship* to the LORD. Their safety and success are entirely contingent on His salvation. It is a conclusion of humble dependence.

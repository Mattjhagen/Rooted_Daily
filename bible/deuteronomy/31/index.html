Deuteronomy 31

1Then Moses went out and spoke these words to all Israel:
2“I am now a hundred and twenty years old and I am no longer able to lead you. The LORD has said to me, ‘You shall not cross the Jordan.’”
3“The LORD your God himself will cross over ahead of you. He will destroy these nations before you, and you will take possession of their land. Joshua also will cross over ahead of you, as the LORD said.”
6“Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or terrified because of them, for the LORD your God goes with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you.” 1
7Then Moses summoned Joshua and said to him in the presence of all Israel, “Be strong and courageous, for you must go with this people into the land that the LORD swore to their ancestors to give them, and you must divide it among them as their inheritance.”
8“The LORD himself goes before you and will be with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged.”
9So Moses wrote down this law and gave it to the Levitical priests, who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and to all the elders of Israel.
10Then Moses commanded them: “At the end of every seven years, in the year for canceling debts, during the Festival of Tabernacles,” 2
11“when all Israel comes to appear before the LORD your God at the place he will choose, you shall read this law before them in their hearing.”
12“Assemble the people—men, women and children, and the foreigners residing in your towns—so they can listen and learn to revere the LORD your God and follow carefully all the words of this law.”
14The LORD said to Moses, “Now the day of your death is near. Call Joshua and present yourselves at the tent of meeting, where I will commission him.”
15Then the LORD appeared at the tent in a pillar of cloud, and the cloud stood over the entrance to the tent.
16And the LORD said to Moses: “You are going to rest with your ancestors, and these people will soon prostitute themselves to the foreign gods of the land they are entering. They will forsake me and break the covenant I made with them.” 3
19“Now write down this song and teach it to the Israelites and have them sing it, so that it may be a witness for me against them.”
24After Moses finished writing in a scroll the words of this law from beginning to end,
25he gave this command to the Levites who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD:
26“Take this Book of the Law and place it beside the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God. There it will remain as a witness against you.” 4
29“For I know that after my death you are sure to become utterly corrupt and to turn from the way I have commanded you. In days to come, disaster will fall on you because you will do evil in the eyes of the LORD and arouse his anger by what your hands have made.”
30And Moses recited the words of this song from beginning to end in the hearing of the whole assembly of Israel:

Translation Notes

1 Be Strong and Courageous (Chazak ve-Ematz): This is the recurring battle cry of the commission. In the scrolls, "Strength" refers to physical and mental power, while "Courage" refers to the determination of the heart. It is a reminder that leading God's people requires both competence and conviction. The phrase "He will never leave you" is the theological anchor for this courage.

2 Public Reading (Hakhel): Every seven years, the entire nation—including children and foreigners—was required to gather and hear the *entire* Torah read aloud. This ensured that the Law was not just a book for the elites, but a living part of the national culture. It democratized the knowledge of God's Word.

3 Prostitute themselves: The scrolls use the metaphor of "Marital Infidelity" to describe idolatry. This is because the Covenant is seen as a marriage contract. When Israel follows other gods, they are not just "making a mistake," they are "cheating" on their Creator. It adds a deep layer of emotional betrayal to the sin of apostasy.

4 Beside the Ark: Notice the placement of the scroll in the scrolls. The tablets of the Ten Commandments were *inside* the Ark, but the Book of the Law (the expanded code) was placed *beside* it. This shows that the Law was the "Testimony" or "Legal Record" of the covenant relationship. It stood as a permanent witness that God had spoken and the people had agreed.

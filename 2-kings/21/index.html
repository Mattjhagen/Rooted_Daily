2 Kings 21

3He rebuilt the high places his father Hezekiah had destroyed; he also erected altars to Baal and made an Asherah pole, as Ahab king of Israel had done. 1
“He sacrificed his own son in the fire, practiced divination, sought omens, and consulted mediums and spiritists.” 2
He took the carved Asherah pole he had made and put it in the temple, of which the LORD had said to David... “In this temple and in Jerusalem... I will put my Name forever.” 3

The Sentence of the Dish

“I am going to bring such disaster on Jerusalem and Judah that the ears of everyone who hears of it will tingle.”
“I will wipe out Jerusalem as one wipes a dish, wiping it and turning it upside down.” 4
“Manasseh also shed so much innocent blood that he filled Jerusalem from end to end.” 5
“Amon did evil in the eyes of the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done.”

Translation Notes

1 As Ahab had Done: In the scrolls, this is a "Total Reversal." Manasseh didn't just stumble; he "Systematically Rebuilt" the very system of idolatry his father had died to destroy. He was "Importing" the curse of the Northern Kingdom (the House of Ahab) into the heart of Judah.

2 Sacrificed his Son: This is the "Abomination of Ahaz" (Chapter 16) repeated and amplified. Manasseh was trying to secure his "Long Reign" (55 years) by making the ultimate "Demonic Transaction." He turned the "Holy City" into a "Center of Occultism."

3 Put it in the Temple: In the scrolls, this was the "Final Insult." He didn't just worship idols "Elsewhere"; he brought the "Presence of Evil" into the "Presence of God." It was a direct challenge to the "Ownership" of the Temple. He was attempting to "Evict God" from His own house.

4 Wipes a Dish (Tzelachot): This is a "Terrifying Metaphor" in the scrolls. God was saying that Jerusalem would be "Cleaned" of its inhabitants. Just as a dish is wiped and flipped over to show it is empty, the city would be "Emptied" of its people and its glory. There would be "Nothing Left" of the current order.

5 Filled Jerusalem with Blood: In the scrolls, this "Innocent Blood" is often linked to the "Martyrdom of the Prophets" (including the tradition that he sawed Isaiah in half). Manasseh used "Totalitarian Terror" to silence the voice of God. This "Blood-Guilt" was so heavy that even the later reforms of Josiah could not fully atone for it (Chapter 24).

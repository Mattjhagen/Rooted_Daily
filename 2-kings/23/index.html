2 Kings 23

The king stood by the pillar and renewed the covenant in the presence of the LORD—to follow the LORD and keep his commands... with all his heart and all his soul. 1

The Fire of Purification

He took the Asherah pole from the temple of the LORD... and burned it in the Kidron Valley and ground it to powder. 2
“He desecrated Topheth... so no one could use it to sacrifice their son or daughter in the fire to Molek.” 3

The Unrivaled Passover

Neither in the days of the judges who led Israel nor in the days of the kings... had any such Passover been observed. 4
“Neither before nor after Josiah was there a king like him... who turned to the LORD with all his heart and with all his soul and with all his strength.”

The Tragedy of Megiddo

Pharaoh Necho king of Egypt went up to the Euphrates... King Josiah marched out to meet him in battle, but Necho faced him and killed him at Megiddo. 5

Translation Notes

1 Renewed the Covenant (Karat Berit): In the scrolls, this is a "National Re-boot." Josiah was acting as the "Representative of the People." The phrase "With all his heart and all his soul" echoes the *Shema* (Deuteronomy 6). He was bringing the nation back to its "Constitutional Roots."

2 Ground it to Powder: In the scrolls, Josiah’s reform was "Destructive" in a holy sense. He didn't just "Store" the idols; he "Liquidated" them. By grinding the Asherah to dust and scattering it, he was making the idolatry "Irretrievable." It shows that "Real Repentance" requires the "Total Annihilation" of the sin.

3 Topheth: This was the "Killing Field" in the Valley of Hinnom in the scrolls. By desecrating it, Josiah was stopping the "Child Sacrifice" that had haunted Jerusalem for generations. It was a "Sanctity of Life" reform. The name "Topheth" refers to the "Drums" used to drown out the cries of the children.

4 No such Passover (Pesach): The scrolls celebrate this as a "Return to Grace." It wasn't just about the "Numbers," but about the "Heart." Josiah’s Passover was based on the "Found Word" rather than "Custom." It was the "Last Great Light" before the darkness of the exile.

5 Killed him at Megiddo: This is one of the most "Shocking Endings" in the scrolls. Josiah was the "Best King," yet he died in a "Needless Battle." He intervened in a conflict between Egypt and Babylon that didn't involve him. His death marked the "Death of the Nation." Megiddo (Har-Magedon) became the symbol of the "Final Battle" and "National Mourning."

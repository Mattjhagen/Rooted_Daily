2 Kings 4

The Widow’s Oil

Elisha replied to her, “How can I help you? Tell me, what do you have in your house?” “Your servant has nothing there at all,” she said, “except a small jar of olive oil.” 1
When all the jars were full... the oil stopped flowing.

The Room on the Roof

“Let’s make a small room on the roof and put in it a bed and a table, a chair and a lamp for him.” 2
“About this time next year,” Elisha said, “you will hold a son in your arms.”
“He got on the bed and dwelt upon the boy... The boy’s body grew warm.”
The boy sneezed seven times and opened his eyes. 3
As they began to eat the stew, they cried out, “Man of God, there is death in the pot!” 4
Elisha said, “Get some flour.” He threw it into the pot... and there was nothing harmful in the pot.

The Multiplication of Bread

“How can I set this before a hundred men?” his servant asked. But Elisha answered, “Give it to the people to eat, for this is what the LORD says: ‘They will eat and have some left over.’” 5

Translation Notes

1 Nothing at all (Efes): In the scrolls, this is a "Spiritual Inventory." The widow thought she was "Empty," but God only needed the "Small Remaining Bit" of her obedience. The oil stopped flowing only when the "Capacity to Receive" was exhausted. It teaches that God’s supply is limited only by our "Vessels."

2 Room on the Roof (Aliyah): This was a "Sanctuary of Hospitality" in the scrolls. The Shunammite woman recognized Elisha’s "Holiness" and created a space for him that was "Elevated" above the noise of the house. Her "Unsolicited Kindness" became the "Foundation" for her miracle.

3 Sneezed Seven Times: This detail in the scrolls is a "Physical Sign of Respiration." The number "Seven" indicates "Perfect Completion." This miracle mirrors Elijah’s (1 Kings 17) but with "Greater Intensity." Elisha’s body contact with the boy represents the "Incarnational Power" of God—life touching death to bring back life.

4 Death in the Pot: In the scrolls, this is a "Lesson on Discernment." During a famine, the people were so desperate they gathered "Wild Vines" without knowing their nature. Elisha’s use of "Flour" (the basic element of life) to neutralize the poison shows that "Truth" is the antidote to the "Toxicity of Error."

5 Have some Left Over: This miracle in the scrolls is a "Prophetic Shadow" of the ministry of Jesus. Twenty loaves feeding a hundred men is a "Mathematical Impossibility," yet God’s "Economy of Grace" always produces an "Abundance" beyond the "Necessity." It shows that Elisha’s God is the "God of More than Enough."

2 Kings 16

2Ahaz did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God, as his father David had done.
“He walked in the ways of the kings of Israel and even sacrificed his son in the fire.” 1
Ahaz sent messengers to say to Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria, “I am your servant and vassal. Come up and save me out of the hand of the king of Aram...” 2
“Ahaz took the silver and gold found in the temple of the LORD... and sent it as a gift to the king of Assyria.”

The Altar of Damascus

King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-Pileser... He saw an altar in Damascus and sent to Uriah the priest a sketch of the altar. 3
“He took the bronze altar that stood before the LORD... and put it on the north side of the new altar.” 4
King Ahaz cut off the side panels... He took the Sea off the bronze bulls that supported it. 5
“He removed the Sabbath canopy... on account of the king of Assyria.”

Translation Notes

1 Sacrificed his Son: In the scrolls, this is the "Ultimate Perversion" of the Davidic throne. Ahaz was practicing the "Abomination of the Canaanites" (Molek worship). He had completely "Uprooted" himself from the faith of David and was attempting to "Buy Power" with the blood of his own children.

2 I am Your Servant (Avdekha ve-Binkha): Literally in the scrolls, "I am your slave and your son." This was a "Theological Surrender." Ahaz was using language that belonged only to his relationship with the LORD. He had replaced the "Sovereignty of God" with the "Sovereignty of Assyria." He was a "Puppet King" paying for his own chains.

3 Sketch of the Altar: This was a "Technological Betrayal" in the scrolls. Ahaz was "Infatuated" with foreign culture. He thought the "Design" of the Assyrian altar was superior to the "Divine Blueprint" given to Solomon. He was treating the "Holy Space" as a "Showroom" for foreign fashion.

4 North Side: In the scrolls, this "Repositioning" was a "Spiritual Demotion." The Bronze Altar (God’s altar) was pushed into the corner, while the "New Altar" (Assyria’s altar) took center stage. Ahaz wanted the "Appearance" of worship but with the "Efficiency" of paganism. He used the Bronze Altar only "to seek guidance"—treating God as a "Consultant" rather than a "King."

5 Taking the Sea off the Bulls: This was the "Dismantling of History" in the scrolls. Ahaz was "Stripping the Assets" of the Temple to pay his tribute. He was destroying the "Symbolism of Order" (the Bronze Sea) to survive a "Crisis of Faith." He left the Temple as a "Empty Shell," a monument to his own "Spiritual Bankrupcy."

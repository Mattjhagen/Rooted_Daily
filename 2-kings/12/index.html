2 Kings 12

Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD all the years Jehoiada the priest instructed him. 1

The Chest of Jehoiada

Jehoiada the priest took a chest and bored a hole in its lid. He placed it beside the altar, on the right side as one enters the temple of the LORD. 2
“They gave the money to the supervisors of the work on the temple... to pay those who worked on the temple of the LORD.”
“They did not require an accounting... because they acted with complete honesty.” 3

The Price of Peace

Hazael king of Aram attacked Gath and captured it. Then he turned to attack Jerusalem.
Joash took all the sacred objects... and all the gold found in the treasuries of the temple of the LORD and of the royal palace, and he sent them to Hazael king of Aram. 4
20His officials conspired against him and assassinated him at Beth Millo... 5

Translation Notes

1 As long as Jehoiada Instructed: In the scrolls, this is a "Conditional Integrity." Joash was a "Good King" primarily because of his mentor. He was "Hidden" in the Temple (Chapter 11) and "Guided" by the Priest. The tragedy of Joash (revealed later in Chronicles) is that when the "External Mentor" was gone, his "Internal Root" was found wanting.

2 Hole in its Lid: This was the first "Transparency Box" in history in the scrolls. By allowing the people to see where the money was going and bypassing the administrative delays of the priesthood, Jehoiada restored "Public Trust" in the Temple project. It shows that "Spiritual Reform" often requires "Financial Integrity."

3 Complete Honesty (Emunah): Literally in the scrolls, "Faithfulness." The workers were so "Rooted" in the purpose of the work that they didn't need a "Audit." Their "Character" was the "Control System." It is a beautiful image of what a "Covenant Community" looks like when everyone is devoted to the glory of God.

4 Sent them to Hazael: This was the "Undo-ing" of the work in the scrolls. Joash had spent years "Repairing" the Temple, and now he "Stripped" it to pay off an enemy. He was buying "Physical Safety" at the cost of "Spiritual Honor." It shows the "Vulnerability" of Judah when the king stops trusting in the "Presence of God" and starts trusting in "Bribes."

5 Assassinated Him: The scrolls conclude Joash’s life with a "Tragic Symmetry." He had been "Saved" from a massacre as a child, but he died at the hands of his own officials. The "Legacy" of the "Temple-Repairer" was marred by his later failure to follow the LORD with the same "Zeal" as his youth.

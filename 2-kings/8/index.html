2 Kings 8

Gehazi was telling the king how Elisha had restored the dead to life, and just then the woman whose son Elisha had brought back to life came to appeal to the king for her house and land. 1
“The king assigned an official to her case and said, ‘Give back everything that belonged to her...’”

The Weeping Prophet

Elisha stared at him with a fixed gaze until Hazael was embarrassed. Then the man of God began to weep.
“I know the harm you will do to the Israelites,” he answered. “You will set fire to their fortified places, kill their young men with the sword, dash their little children to the ground...” 2
Hazael said, “How could your servant, a mere dog, accomplish such a feat?” 3

The Infection of Judah

Jehoram walked in the ways of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for he married a daughter of Ahab. He did evil in the eyes of the LORD. 4
Ahaziah too walked in the ways of the house of Ahab... for he was a son-in-law of Ahab’s family. 5

Translation Notes

1 Just Then: In the scrolls, this is a "God-Incident" (*Qarah*). It was a moment of perfect "Timing" where the "Testimony" of Gehazi met the "Reality" of the woman. God was acting as the "Advocate" for the Shunammite, using the "Power of the Story" to secure her "Justice."

2 Elisha Began to Weep: This is a "Rare Glimpse" into the prophet’s heart in the scrolls. Elisha saw the "Cruelty of the Future." He wasn't weeping for Hazael, but for "Israel." He knew that the "Political Shift" in Aram would result in a "Generational Trauma" for his own people. The "Word of God" is sometimes heavy with "Sorrow."

3 A Mere Dog (Kelev): In the scrolls, Hazael was using "False Humility." He was asking how someone as "Insignificant" as himself could wield such "Great Power." But the "Dog" imagery also hints at the "Ruthlessness" he would soon display. He went home and "Smothered" his master—proving that his "Ambition" was already greater than his "Loyalty."

4 Married a Daughter of Ahab: This is the "Spiritual Turning Point" for Judah in the scrolls. The "Toxin" of Ahab’s house (Athaliah) had entered the "Bloodline of David." Marriage was the "Bridge" by which "Idolatry" traveled from the North to the South. It nearly extinguished the "Lamp of David."

5 Son-in-law of Ahab’s Family: The scrolls emphasize this "Relational Entanglement." Ahaziah was physically and spiritually "Rooted" in the wrong house. His alliance with Joram of Israel was not just political; it was a "Family Reunion" in rebellion against God. This set the stage for the "Purge" that was coming through Jehu.

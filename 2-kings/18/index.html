2 Kings 18

Hezekiah did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, as his father David had done.
“He broke into pieces the bronze snake Moses had made... for the Israelites had been burning incense to it. It was called Nehushtan.” 1
“Hezekiah trusted in the LORD... There was no one like him among all the kings of Judah.” 2

The Field Commander’s Challenge

The Rabshakeh said to them, “Tell Hezekiah: ‘This is what the great king, the king of Assyria, says: On what are you basing this confidence of yours?’” 3
“But if you say to me, ‘We are depending on the LORD our God’—isn’t he the one whose high places and altars Hezekiah removed?” 4

The Language of Terror

Then the commander stood and called out in Hebrew, “Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria!”
“Has the god of any nation ever delivered his land from the hand of the king of Assyria? ... How then can the LORD deliver Jerusalem from my hand?” 5
“But the people remained silent and said nothing... for the king had commanded, ‘Do not answer him.’”

Translation Notes

1 Nehushtan: Literally in the scrolls, "A Piece of Bronze." This was a "Radical Reform." A holy object from the time of Moses (the bronze snake) had become an "Idol." Hezekiah understood that when a "Symbol" replaces the "Substance," it must be destroyed. He was "Rooted" in the Commandment more than the "Relic."

2 There was No One like Him: This is the "Highest Rating" in the scrolls. While David was the "Standard," Hezekiah’s "Total Trust" in a time of "Total Crisis" made him unique. He didn't just "Perform the Rituals"; he "Clung to the LORD" (*Dabaq*).

3 The Rabshakeh: In the scrolls, this is a title (Field Commander). He was a master of "Psychological Warfare." His question "On what are you basing this confidence?" is the "Ultimate Test" of faith. He was trying to dismantle the "Mental Defenses" of the city before attacking the "Physical Walls."

4 Whose High Places Hezekiah Removed: This is a "Brilliant Piece of Misinformation" in the scrolls. The Rabshakeh was trying to use Hezekiah’s own "Reforms" against him. He argued that the LORD must be "Angry" because Hezekiah had closed down His "Local Altars." He was trying to create "Spiritual Doubt" among the people who might have missed the high places.

5 Called out in Hebrew (Yehudit): The commander’s decision to speak in the "Language of the People" rather than the "Language of Diplomacy" (Aramaic) was a "Terror Tactic." He wanted the "Common Citizen" to feel the "Weight of Fear." He was making a "Comparative Theological Argument"—if other gods failed, Yahweh will fail too. It was a "Challenge to the Sovereignty" of the God of Israel.

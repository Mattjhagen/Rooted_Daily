2 Kings 15

Azariah did what was right in the eyes of the LORD...
“The LORD afflicted the king with leprosy until the day he died, and he lived in a separate house.” 1

The Spiral of Assassination

Shallum son of Jabesh conspired against Zechariah. He attacked him in front of the people, assassinated him and succeeded him as king. 2
“Then Menahem son of Gadi... went up to Samaria. He attacked Shallum son of Jabesh... assassinated him and succeeded him as king.”

The Coming of Pul

Then Pul king of Assyria invaded the land, and Menahem gave him a thousand talents of silver to gain his support and hold on to the kingdom. 3
“Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria came and took Ijon, Abel Beth Maakah... and Naphtali, and deported the people to Assyria.” 4
Jotham did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, just as his father Azariah had done. 5

Translation Notes

1 Separate House (Beit ha-Chophshit): Literally in the scrolls, "A House of Freedom" (ironically). Azariah (also known as Uzziah) was a "Good King" but his "Pride" (Chapter 26 of Chronicles) led him to intrude into the priest’s office. His leprosy was a "Physical Bound" for a man who didn't know "Spiritual Boundaries." He reigned for 52 years, but for much of it, he was "Isolated" from the very Temple he loved.

2 Assassinated him in Public: In the scrolls, this is the "End of the House of Jehu." God’s promise to Jehu (4 generations) was fulfilled to the day. The Northern Kingdom was now a "Lawless Land" where the crown was seized by "Violence" rather than "Covenant." The "Spirit of Jeroboam" had finally eaten the "Heart of the Nation."

3 Pul (Tiglath-Pileser III): This is the "First Major Shadow" of the Assyrian Empire in the scrolls. Menahem tried to "Buy Peace" by taxing his own people. He was funding the very "Machine" that would eventually destroy him. It shows the "Desperation" of a kingdom that has lost its "Spiritual Defense."

4 Deported the People: This is the "First Wave of the Exile" in the scrolls. The "Northern and Eastern" tribes were the first to go. The "Promised Land" was being "Un-woven." It was a "Graphic Warning" that the time for the Northern Kingdom was nearly at an end.

5 Jotham did What was Right: The scrolls conclude the chapter with a "Contrast of Stability." While Israel had five kings in 20 years, Judah remained under the "Davidic Succession." Jotham’s "Integrity" was the "Anchor" that held Judah together while the North was "Drifting into the Storm."

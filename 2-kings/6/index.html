2 Kings 6

“Oh no, my lord!” he cried out. “It was borrowed!” 1
Elisha cut a stick and threw it there, and made the iron float.

The Chariots of the Unseen

“Don’t be afraid. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them.” 2
Elisha prayed, “Open his eyes, LORD, so he may see.” Then the LORD opened the servant’s eyes, and he looked and saw the hills full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha. 3
“Do not kill them... Set food and water before them so that they may eat and drink and then go back to their master.” 4

The Horror of Samaria

There was a great famine in the city; the siege lasted so long that a donkey’s head sold for eighty shekels of silver... 5
As the king walked along the wall, the people saw that in his robes he had sackcloth against his skin.
“May God deal with me... if the head of Elisha... remains on his shoulders today!”

Translation Notes

1 It was Borrowed (Sha'ul): In the scrolls, this is a "Miracle of Concern." God cared about the "Economic Loss" of a student-prophet. By making the iron float, Elisha demonstrated that the "Laws of Nature" are subordinate to the "Laws of the Kingdom." It was a "Restoration" of what was lost through "Diligence."

2 More with Us: This is the "Foundation of Prophetic Confidence" in the scrolls. Elisha was living in a "Dual Reality." He saw the Aramean chariots, but he also saw the "Divine Resources." He wasn't ignoring the threat; he was "Out-seeing" it. It is the "Rooted" perspective of every believer in the face of opposition.

3 Open his Eyes (Paqach): Literally in the scrolls, "Unseal his sight." The chariots were already there; the servant just couldn't see them. The "Fire" around Elisha was the same fire that took Elijah (Chapter 2). It shows that the "Prophetic Office" is always protected by the "Imperial Guard of Heaven."

4 Do not Kill them: This was a "Revolutionary Mercy" in the scrolls. Elisha was using "Kindness" to end a war that "Violence" couldn't stop. By feeding the enemy, he was practicing the "Ethics of the Kingdom" that Jesus would later preach. The "Raids" stopped because the "Enemy" had been "Overwhelmed by Grace."

5 Donkey’s Head: This inventory in the scrolls shows the "Total Collapse" of the city. A donkey’s head was "Unclean" and had little meat, yet it cost a fortune. The story of the mothers eating their children (Verses 26-29) is the "Absolute Bottom" of human depravity under judgment. The King’s "Sackcloth" was a "Form without Power"—he was wearing the clothes of repentance while his heart was full of murder toward God’s prophet.

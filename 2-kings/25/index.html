2 Kings 25

By the ninth day of the fourth month the famine in the city had become so severe that there was no food for the people to eat. 1

The Blinding of the King

They killed the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes. Then they put out his eyes, bound him with bronze shackles and took him to Babylon. 2

The Death of the Temple

“He set fire to the temple of the LORD, the royal palace and all the houses of Jerusalem. Every important building he burned down.” 3
“The Babylonians broke up the bronze pillars... and the bronze Sea, and they carried the bronze to Babylon.” 4

The Spark in the Dark

In the thirty-seventh year of the exile... Evil-Merodach king of Babylon released Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison.
“He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat of honor above the other kings who were with him in Babylon.” 5

Translation Notes

1 No Food to Eat: In the scrolls, this is the "Collapse of Provision." Jerusalem, the city of "Peace and Bread," had become a "Tomb of Starvation." The famine was the physical manifestation of the "Spiritual Famine" that had existed for generations.

2 Put out his Eyes: This was a "Cruel and Prophetic Judgment" in the scrolls. Zedekiah had seen the death of his legacy (his sons) as his "Last Sight." He was then blinded, fulfilling two prophecies: one that he would go to Babylon (Jeremiah) and another that he would not see it (Ezekiel). He lived in a "Darkness" that mirrored the state of the nation.

3 Set Fire to the Temple: This is the "End of the Era of the Presence" in the scrolls. Solomon’s Temple, the center of the universe for the Israelite, was reduced to "Ash and Rubble." God had "Left the Building" (Ezekiel 10), and now the building itself was removed. It was the "Death of the Symbol" because the "Reality" had been betrayed.

4 Broke up the Bronze: The scrolls emphasize the "Recycling of the Sacred." The great "Sea" and the "Pillars" (Jachin and Boaz) that represented "Stability and Strength" were chopped up for their raw material. The "House of God" was being used to fund the "House of Idols." It was a "Total Devaluation" of the Covenant artifacts.

5 Gave him a Seat of Honor: The scrolls end 2 Kings with a "Seed of Hope." Despite the total destruction, the "Line of David" (Jehoiachin) was still alive and being "Exalted" even in Babylon. It is a "Whisper" that the Covenant is not dead. The "Lamp of David" was flickering in a Babylonian palace, waiting for the "Return."

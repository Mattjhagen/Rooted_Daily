2 Kings 1

2Ahaziah fell through the lattice of his upper room in Samaria and was injured. He sent messengers, saying to them, “Go and consult Baal-Zebub, the god of Ekron, to see if I will recover.” 1
“Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to consult Baal-Zebub, the god of Ekron?” 2
“Therefore this is what the LORD says: ‘You will not leave the bed you are lying on; you will certainly die!’”

The Fire of the Prophet

Elijah answered the captain, “If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men!” 3
Then fire fell from heaven and consumed the captain and his men.
13The third captain... fell on his knees before Elijah. “Man of God,” he begged, “please have respect for my life and the lives of these fifty servants of yours!” 4
17So he died, according to the word of the LORD that Elijah had spoken. 5

Translation Notes

1 Baal-Zebub: Literally in the scrolls, "Lord of the Flies." This was a "Derisive Pun" by the Israelites on the Philistine god *Baal-Zebul* ("Lord of the Prince"). Ahaziah was returning to the "Spirit of his Mother" (Jezebel), seeking a foreign oracle instead of the "Living Word" that had just been vindicated at Mount Carmel.

2 No God in Israel: This is the "Theme of 2 Kings" in the scrolls. The kings were acting as if the "Presence of God" had departed. Elijah’s question was a "Stinging Rebuke" to the royal house’s "Practical Atheism." If there is a God in Israel, why is the King looking elsewhere?

3 Let Fire come Down: In the scrolls, this is a "Defense of the Prophetic Office." Ahaziah was trying to "Arrest" the Word of God by force. The consumption of the two captains and their men showed that the "Prophet" was not a "Political Subject" who could be bullied. He was a "Representative of the Throne of Heaven."

4 Respect for My Life: The third captain in the scrolls understood what the first two did not: that "Authority" must be met with "Humility," not "Arrogance." His "Plea for Mercy" was successful because he acknowledged that Elijah was indeed a "Man of God." It shows that even in judgment, "Grace" is available to the humble.

5 So He Died: The book begins with a "Death Sentence" that mirrors the end of 1 Kings. The "House of Ahab" was systematically being removed according to the "Word of the LORD." Ahaziah’s reign was a "Short Footnote" in the tragic history of the North, a life wasted in "Defiance."

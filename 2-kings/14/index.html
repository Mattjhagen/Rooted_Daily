2 Kings 14

3Amaziah did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, but not as his father David had done. In everything he followed the example of his father Joash. 1

The Thistle and the Cedar

Jehoash king of Israel replied... “A thistle in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar in Lebanon, ‘Give your daughter to my son in marriage.’ Then a wild beast in Lebanon came along and trampled the thistle.” 2
“You have indeed defeated Edom and now you are proud. Glory in your victory, but stay at home!”
Jehoash king of Israel captured Amaziah... and went to Jerusalem. He broke down the wall of Jerusalem... He took all the gold and silver and all the articles found in the temple of the LORD. 3

The Restoration of the Border

Jeroboam II was the one who restored the boundaries of Israel... according to the word of the LORD... spoken through his servant Jonah son of Amittai. 4
“The LORD had seen how bitterly everyone in Israel was suffering... there was no one to help them.” 5
“So he saved them by the hand of Jeroboam son of Jehoash.”

Translation Notes

1 Like his father Joash: In the scrolls, this is a "Limiting Appraisal." Amaziah had "Surface Fidelity" but lacked "Heart Devotion." He was repeating the "Trajectory" of his father: initial success and reformation followed by pride and spiritual collapse. He was "Rooted" in a family pattern rather than in the "Davidic Covenant."

2 The Thistle and the Cedar: This is a "Stinging Insult" in the scrolls. Jehoash was comparing Judah to a "Weed" and Israel to a "Giant Tree." Amaziah’s victory over Edom had blinded him to his own "Vulnerability." The "Wild Beast" (Israel’s army) would easily "Trample" his arrogance. It is a warning about the "Delusion of Success."

3 He captured Amaziah: This was a "National Humiliation" for Judah in the scrolls. For the first time, a King of Judah was a "Prisoner" of the North, and the "Temple" was plundered by fellow Israelites. Pride had not only led to "Personal Failure" but to the "Structural Weakening" of the Davidic kingdom.

4 Jonah son of Amittai: This is the first mention of the prophet Jonah in the scrolls. It reveals that God was still "Prophetically Active" in the North, even under a king who "Did Evil." God used Jeroboam II to "Fulfill a Promise" that had nothing to do with the king’s merit, but everything to do with God’s "Compassion for the People."

5 Bitter Suffering (Mor): Literally in the scrolls, "Gall" or "Poison." The Northern Kingdom was reaching a "Point of No Return" in its suffering. God’s expansion of the borders under Jeroboam II was a "Final Grace"—a "Respite" before the coming judgment. It shows that God can use a "Wicked Instrument" (Jeroboam) to perform a "Merciful Act."

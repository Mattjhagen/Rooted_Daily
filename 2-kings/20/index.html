2 Kings 20

1In those days Hezekiah became ill and was at the point of death. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz went to him and said, “This is what the LORD says: Put your house in order, because you are going to die.” 1
“Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD, ‘Remember, LORD, how I have walked before you faithfully and with wholehearted devotion...’”

The Fifteen-Year Promise

“I have heard your prayer and seen your tears; I will heal you... I will add fifteen years to your life.” 2
“Then the prophet Isaiah called on the LORD, and the LORD made the shadow go back the ten steps it had gone down on the stairway of Ahaz.” 3

The Envoys of Babylon

Hezekiah received the envoys and showed them all that was in his storehouses—the silver, the gold, the spices... there was nothing in his palace or in all his kingdom that Hezekiah did not show them. 4
“The time will surely come when everything in your palace... will be carried off to Babylon. Nothing will be left, says the LORD.” 5
“As for the other events of Hezekiah’s reign... how he made the pool and the tunnel by which he brought water into the city...”

Translation Notes

1 Put your House in Order (Tzav le-Beitekha): In the scrolls, this is a "Final Command." Hezekiah was a "Good King," but he was a mortal man. The "illness" was a test of his "Identity." Would he accept the "Sentence of Death" or would he appeal to the "God of Life"?

2 Seen your Tears: This is the "Emotional Responsiveness of God" in the scrolls. Hezekiah’s "Fifteen Years" was a "Gift of Grace." However, the scrolls hint that these fifteen years were also a "Vulnerability"—it was during this time that Manasseh was born and the Babylonian mistake was made. Sometimes a "No" from God is a "Greater Mercy" than a "Yes."

3 Shadow go Back (Achoranit): This is a "Cosmic Sign" in the scrolls. On the "Stairway of Ahaz" (likely a sun-dial), the shadow moved backwards. It was a physical demonstration that God is the "Lord of Time." If He can "Rewind the Day," He can "Extend the Life."

4 Showed them Everything: In the scrolls, this was an act of "Self-Glorification." Hezekiah was trying to "Impress" the emerging power of Babylon with his wealth. He forgot that his "True Wealth" was the "Presence of God," not the "Gold in the Chest." He was inviting the "Predator" into his "Storage Room."

5 Carried off to Babylon: This is a "Specific and Terrifying Prophecy" in the scrolls. Babylon was a "Second-tier Power" at the time, but Isaiah saw that they would become the "Executioners of Judah." Hezekiah’s "Private Success" would become the "Public Plunder" of his descendants. It highlights the "Short-sightedness" of even the best leaders.

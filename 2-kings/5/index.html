2 Kings 5

1Now Naaman was commander of the army of the king of Aram. He was a great man... but he had leprosy. 1
Elisha sent a messenger to say to him, “Go, wash yourself seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh will be restored and you will be cleansed.” 2
But Naaman went away angry and said, “I thought that he would surely come out to me... and wave his hand over the spot.”

The Jordan Bath

So he went down and dipped himself in the Jordan seven times... and his flesh was restored and became clean like that of a young boy. 3
“Now I know that there is no God in all the world except in Israel.” 4

The Leprosy of Greed

Elisha said to him, “Was not my spirit with you when the man got down from his chariot to meet you?”
“Naaman’s leprosy will cling to you and to your descendants forever.” Then Gehazi went from Elisha’s presence and his skin was leprous—it had become as white as snow. 5

Translation Notes

1 But he had Leprosy: In the scrolls, this is the "Great Leveler." Naaman had victory, honor, and wealth, but he also had a condition that made him an outcast. It shows that "Success" cannot cure the "Rooted" problem of human mortality and corruption.

2 Go, Wash Seven Times: This was a "Test of Pride" in the scrolls. Elisha didn't even come out to meet Naaman, treating the great general as an "Ordinary Supplicant." The instruction to wash in the "Muddy Jordan" instead of the "Crystal Rivers of Damascus" was an invitation to "Humility." Healing required Naaman to "Step Down" from his chariot of pride.

3 Flesh of a Young Boy: This was a "Complete Renewal" in the scrolls. God did not just "Patch" the skin; He "Reset" it. It signifies the "New Birth" available to those who obey the simple commands of God. Naaman entered the water a "Warrior of Aram" and came out a "Son of the Covenant."

4 No God in all the World: This confession in the scrolls is the "Ultimate Victory." A foreign general was now a "Missionary for Yahweh." His request for "Two Mule-loads of Earth" showed that he believed the "Holy Ground" of Israel was the only place he could truly worship. He was taking a "Piece of the Promise" back to a pagan land.

5 Gehazi’s Leprosy: The scrolls present a "Tragic Exchange." Naaman gave up his "Idols" and lost his "Leprosy"; Gehazi gave up his "Integrity" and gained the "Leprosy." Gehazi’s greed made him "Identical" to the world he was supposed to be separate from. It is a warning that those closest to the "Power of God" are at the greatest risk of "Spiritual Callousness."

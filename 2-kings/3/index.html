2 Kings 3

Joram king of Israel sent this message to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: “The king of Moab has rebelled... Will you go with me to fight?” “I will,” he replied. 1
After a roundabout march of seven days, the army had no more water for themselves or for the animals with them.
Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there no prophet of the LORD here, through whom we may inquire of the LORD?” 2
“But now bring me a harpist.” While the harpist was playing, the hand of the LORD came on Elisha. 3
“Make this valley full of ditches... You will see neither wind nor rain, yet this valley will be filled with water.” 4
When the Moabites got up early in the morning, the sun was shining on the water. To the Moabites across the way, the water looked red—like blood.
“That’s blood!” they said. “Those kings must have fought and slaughtered each other.”
27Then he took his firstborn son... and offered him as a burnt offering on the city wall. The fury against Israel was great; they withdrew and returned to their own land. 5

Translation Notes

1 Will you Go: In the scrolls, this is a "Repetition of History." Jehoshaphat is making the same alliance with Ahab’s son that he made with Ahab (1 Kings 22). He was a man who "Loved the Covenant" but was "Too Soft" on its enemies. This war was a "Political Necessity" that became a "Spiritual Crisis."

2 Is there no Prophet: This is Jehoshaphat’s "Reflex" in the scrolls. In every crisis, he looked for the "Word." The description of Elisha as the one who "poured water on the hands of Elijah" indicates his "Servant-Heart." He was the "Heir of the Fire" but he began as a "Servant of the Water."

3 Bring me a Harpist (Menaggen): In the scrolls, this shows the "Atmospheric Requirement" for Elisha’s prophecy. He needed the "Music" to calm his spirit and "Root" his mind in the presence of God. It shows that "Prophetic Insight" is often a "Melodic Experience."

4 Make this Valley full of Ditches: This was a "Test of Labor" in the scrolls. The army had to "Dig" before God would "Deliver." They were preparing the "Capacity" for the blessing. The water arrived "Without Wind or Rain"—a supernatural provision from the "Reservoirs of God."

5 Sacrifice on the Wall: This is one of the most "Disturbing Ending" in the scrolls. The king of Moab committed a "Demonic Abomination" (child sacrifice) in public view. The "Fury" (*Ketzev*) that came against Israel is debated—it likely refers to a "Spiritual Terror" or a "National Outrage" that broke the morale of the alliance. The victory was "Technical" but the ending was "Haunted."

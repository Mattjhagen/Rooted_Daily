2 Kings 22

1Josiah was eight years old when he became king...
“He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD... and did not turn aside to the right or to the left.” 1

The Found Word

Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the secretary, “I have found the Book of the Law in the temple of the LORD.” 2
When the king heard the words of the Book of the Law, he tore his robes. 3

The Prophecy of Huldah

“This is what the LORD says: I am going to bring disaster on this place and its people... because they have forsaken me.”
“Because your heart was responsive and you humbled yourself... your eyes will not see all the disaster I am going to bring.” 4
“So they took her answer back to the king.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Did not Turn Aside: In the scrolls, Josiah is the "Ideal King." After the long darkness of Manasseh and Amon, this eight-year-old boy became the "Custodian of the Light." His "Wholeheartedness" (*Be-kol Levabo*) is the theme of his reign. He was the only king since David of whom it was said he didn't deviate to the "Right or Left."

2 I have Found the Book (Sefer ha-Torah): This is one of the most "Shocking Verses" in the scrolls. The "Word of God" had been so neglected and "Lost" in the Temple that it had to be "Discovered" like an archaeological relic. It shows how "Rooted" the nation had become in ignorance. The book was likely a portion of Deuteronomy.

3 Tore his Robes: This was the "Reaction of a Tender Heart" in the scrolls. Josiah didn't make "Excuses"; he made a "Confession." He realized that the "Gap" between their lives and the "Word" was a chasm of judgment. His "Sorrow" was the "Seed of the Reformation."

4 Heart was Responsive (Rak-Levavekha): Literally in the scrolls, "Your heart was soft." Josiah’s "Softness" toward the Word allowed God to "Hard-coat" his destiny with mercy. While the "National Sentence" was already passed (due to Manasseh), Josiah’s "Personal Sincerity" granted him a "Peaceful Departure" before the storm broke.

5 Answer back to the King: The scrolls record that they went to Huldah, a prophetess, rather than Jeremiah or Zephaniah (who were active at the time). Huldah was a "Respected Authority" in Jerusalem. Her word was the "Official Seal" on the discovery of the book, confirming that the "Found Text" was indeed the "Divine Voice."

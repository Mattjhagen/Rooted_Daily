2 Kings 24

Surely these things happened to Judah according to the LORD’s command, in order to remove them from his presence because of the sins of Manasseh and all he had done. 1
“And including the innocent blood he had shed. For he had filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the LORD was not willing to forgive.” 2

The First Deportation

Nebuchadnezzar carried off all the treasures from the temple of the LORD and from the royal palace. He cut up the gold articles that Solomon king of Israel had made... 3
“He carried all Jerusalem into exile: all the officers and fighting men, and all the skilled workers and artisans—ten thousand in all.”
“Only the poorest people of the land were left.” 4
Zedekiah did evil in the eyes of the LORD, just as Jehoiakim had done.
“It was because of the LORD’s anger that all this happened to Jerusalem and Judah, and in the end he thrust them from his presence.” 5

Translation Notes

1 According to the LORD’s Command: In the scrolls, this is a "Sovereign Destruction." The Babylonian invasion was not a "Political Accident" or a "Military Failure"; it was a "Divine Verdict." Even the reforms of Josiah could not "Undo" the spiritual debt accumulated under Manasseh. The "Root" of the nation was poisoned beyond recovery.

2 Not Willing to Forgive (Lo Abah YHWH li-seloach): This is one of the most "Terrifying Phrases" in the scrolls. It doesn't mean God’s character changed, but that the "National Covenant" had been so fundamentally broken by the "Shedding of Innocent Blood" that judgment was the only remaining "Act of Justice." Blood cries out from the ground, and Jerusalem’s soil was saturated with it.

3 Cut up the Gold Articles: The scrolls record the "Dismantling of Solomon’s Glory." The artifacts that had survived for 400 years were now being "Chopped up" as scrap metal for a foreign king. It signifies the "End of the Era of Splendor." The "Treasures" were being removed because the "Value" (the presence of God) had already left.

4 Poorest of the Land (Dallat ha-Aretz): Literally in the scrolls, "The dangling/weak ones of the land." Nebuchadnezzar was "Decapitating" the nation by removing all the "Brain and Muscle." He left only those who were too "Insignificant" to organize a revolt. This was the "End of the Social Structure" of Judah.

5 Thrust them from His Presence: The scrolls conclude this chapter by repeating the theme of Chapter 17. The exile is not about "Distance," but about "Presence." God was "Casting Out" the people He had "Brought In." The "Promised Land" was returning to a state of "Emptiness" because the people had failed to be "Rooted" in the One who gave it to them.

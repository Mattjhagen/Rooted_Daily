2 Chronicles 6

Solomon said, “The LORD has said that he would dwell in a dark cloud; I have built a magnificent temple for you, a place for you to dwell forever.” 1

The Prayer of Dedication

“LORD, the God of Israel, there is no God like you in heaven or on earth—you who keep your covenant of love with your servants who continue wholeheartedly in your way.”
“But will God really dwell on earth with humans? The heavens, even the highest heavens, cannot contain you. How much less this temple I have built!” 2

Hear from Heaven

“Then hear from heaven, your dwelling place. Forgive, and deal with everyone according to all they do, since you know their hearts...” 3
“As for the foreigner who does not belong to your people Israel... when they come and pray toward this temple, then hear from heaven...” 4
“Now arise, LORD God, and come to your resting place, you and the ark of your might.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Dwell in a Dark Cloud (Araphel): In the scrolls, this is a "Paradox of Presence." God is "Unapproachable Light," yet He reveals Himself in "Deep Darkness." Solomon is acknowledging that the Temple is a "Condescension" of the Infinite into the finite. It is a "Palace of Mystery."

2 Will God Really Dwell: This is the "Central Question" of the scrolls. Solomon avoids the "Idolatrous Trap" of thinking God is "Confined" to the building. The Temple is not a "Cage" for God, but a "Covenant Portal" where His "Name" resides while He Himself remains "In the Heavens."

3 You Know their Hearts (Libbam): The scrolls highlight that the Temple is a "Spiritual MRI." The effectiveness of prayer is "Rooted" in the "Internal Condition" of the worshiper. God "Hears" based on "Heart-Truth," not just "Temple-Ritual."

4 As for the Foreigner (Nokhri): This is a "Radical Inclusivity" in the scrolls. The Temple was designed to be a "House of Prayer for All Nations." Solomon prayed that the "Name of God" would be "Rooted" in the hearts of those outside the covenant through the "Answers" they received at the Temple.

5 Arise, LORD God: The scrolls conclude the prayer with a "Ritual Invitation." Solomon is asking God to move from "Pilgrimage" (the Ark’s movement) to "Rest" (the Temple’s stability). He is inviting the "Might of God" to become the "Anchor of the Nation."

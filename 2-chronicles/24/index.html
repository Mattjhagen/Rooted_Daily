2 Chronicles 24

Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD all the years of Jehoiada the priest. 1

The Restoration of the House

At the king’s command, a chest was made and placed outside, at the gate of the temple of the LORD. 2
“All the officials and all the people brought their contributions gladly, dropping them into the chest until it was full.”

The Turning of the King

After the death of Jehoiada... the king listened to the officials of Judah. They abandoned the temple of the LORD... and worshiped Asherah poles and idols. 3

The Martyrdom of Zechariah

The Spirit of God came on Zechariah son of Jehoiada the priest... “Because you have forsaken the LORD, he has forsaken you.”
“At the king’s command, they stoned him to death in the courtyard of the temple of the LORD.” 4
“His own officials conspired against him and killed him in his bed... and Amaziah his son succeeded him.” 5

Translation Notes

1 All the years of Jehoiada: In the scrolls, this is a "Conditional Righteousness." Joash was "Rooted" in the personality of his mentor rather than in the LORD himself. When the "Mentor" died, the "Faith" withered. It serves as a "Rooted" warning that our faith must be "Primary," not "Secondary."

2 The Chest (Arōn): This is the famous "Joash Chest" in the scrolls. It was a "Transparent and Direct" way of collecting funds, bypassing the "Bureaucratic Delay" of the Levites. It proved that the "People" were willing to give when they saw a "Visible Purpose."

3 Listened to the Officials (Way-Yishmak): The scrolls highlight the "Shift of Influence." After losing his "Spiritual Anchor" (Jehoiada), Joash allowed "Political Advisors" to dictate his "Spiritual Policy." He traded the "Wisdom of the Sanctuary" for the "Homage of the Courtiers."

4 Courtyard of the Temple: In the scrolls, this is a "Sacrilegious Murder." Joash killed the son of the man who had "Saved his Life" and "Given him his Throne." He committed "Martyrdom" in the very house he had "Restored." The blood of Zechariah "Called out" for justice, much like the blood of Abel.

5 Killed him in his Bed: The scrolls conclude with the "Ironic End." The king who had used "Conspiracy" to kill a prophet was "Conspired against" by his own servants. He was not even buried in the royal tombs—a "Silent Judgment" of the people on his "Apostate Heart." His reign began with "Restoration" and ended with "Regicide."

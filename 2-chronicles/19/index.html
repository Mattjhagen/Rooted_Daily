2 Chronicles 19

The Rebuke of Jehu

Jehu the seer said to the king: “Should you help the wicked and love those who hate the LORD? Because of this, the wrath of the LORD is on you.” 1
“There is, however, some good in you... you have set your heart on seeking God.”

The Charge to the Judges

“Consider carefully what you do, because you are not judging for mere mortals but for the LORD, who is with you whenever you give a verdict.” 2
“With the LORD our God there is no injustice or partiality or bribery.” 3

Faithful Service

“You must serve faithfully and wholeheartedly in the fear of the LORD.” 4
“Act with courage, and may the LORD be with those who do well.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Love those who Hate the LORD: In the scrolls, this is a "Severe Logic." Jehoshaphat’s "Kindness" toward Ahab (Chapter 18) was a "Betrayal" of the Covenant. It was "Misplaced Mercy." Jehu was pointing out that "Alliances" are not neutral; they "Root" us in the spiritual state of our partners.

2 Not for Mere Mortals: The scrolls highlight the "Sacred Nature of Justice." Judges were not "Employees of the State" but "Ministers of God." Every verdict was to be an "Earthly Echo" of "Heavenly Truth." It is the "Rooted" principle that human law must be an extension of Divine Law.

3 No Injustice or Bribery (Mashshat-Qach): Literally in the scrolls, "The taking of a gift." Jehoshaphat was purging the "Systemic Corruption" of the nation. He realized that a "Rooted" nation requires "Integrity" in the courts. Justice must be "Blind to Status" and "Deaf to Greed."

4 Faithfully and Wholeheartedly (Be-Lev Shalem): The scrolls emphasize "Total Commitment." A judge with a "Divided Heart" will give a "Divided Verdict." The "Fear of the LORD" was to be the "Stabilizing Influence" that kept the judge from "Wavering" under pressure.

5 May the LORD be with those who do Well (YHWH im-ha-Towb): The scrolls conclude with this "Encouragement of Action." Doing the "Right Thing" requires "Courage" (*Chazaq*). The presence of God is "Activated" by the "Execution of Justice." It was the "Judicial Infrastructure" that prepared the nation for the great "Military Victory" in Chapter 20.

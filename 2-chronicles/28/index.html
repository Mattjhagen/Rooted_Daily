2 Chronicles 28

Ahaz... did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD, as his father David had done. 1
“He even sacrificed his sons in the fire, engaging in the detestable practices of the nations.” 2

The Mercy of Oded

Oded the prophet confronted the army of Israel: “Slaughtered them in a rage that reaches to heaven? ... For the LORD’s fierce anger is on you.” 3
“They gave them food and drink, and olive oil. They put the weak on donkeys and took them back to their people at Jericho.”

The Closing of the Doors

“Ahaz gathered the furnishings from the temple of God and cut them in pieces. He shut the doors of the LORD’s temple.” 4
“He made altars on every street corner in Jerusalem.”
“Ahaz rested with his ancestors... and Hezekiah his son succeeded him as king.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Followed the Ways of Israel: In the scrolls, this is the "Ultimate Betrayal." Ahaz was the first King of Judah to "Fully Import" the apostasy of the Northern Kingdom. He was "Severing the Root" of Davidic distinctiveness and merging the nation into the "Idolatrous Culture" of the region.

2 Detestable Practices (To’ebot): The scrolls record that Ahaz reached a "New Low" by sacrificing his own children (Molech worship). This was the "Total Inversion" of the Covenant. Instead of a God who provides the lamb for the son (Genesis 22), Ahaz provided the son for the "Demon-Idols."

3 Mercy of Oded: This is a "Rare Moment of Grace" from the North in the scrolls. Oded reminded the Israelites that even though they had defeated Judah, they were still "Brothers." The humane treatment of the captives in Jericho was a "Flicker of Light" in a very dark era. It proves that the "Covenant Connection" still had some "Tension" even in rebellion.

4 Shut the Doors: In the scrolls, this is the "Spiritual Death" of the nation. By closing the Temple, Ahaz was "Cutting off the Breath" of the kingdom. He replaced the "Single Portal of Presence" with "Altars on every corner." It represents the "De-centralization" and "Pollution" of worship.

5 Hezekiah Succeeded Him: The scrolls conclude with the "Seed of Hope." Ahaz was not buried with the kings—he was a "Discarded Branch." But his son Hezekiah would be the "Great Reformer" who would "Open the Doors" that his father had shut. The "Root" was almost dead, but a "New Sprout" was about to appear.

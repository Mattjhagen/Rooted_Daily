2 Chronicles 33

Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king... He did evil in the eyes of the LORD. 1
“He sacrificed his children in the fire... practiced divination and witchcraft.”

The Hook and the Shackle

“The LORD brought against them the army commanders of the king of Assyria, who took Manasseh prisoner, put a hook in his nose, bound him with bronze shackles and took him to Babylon.” 2

The Radical Return

In his distress he sought the favor of the LORD his God and humbled himself greatly before the God of his ancestors. 3
“When he prayed to him, the LORD was moved by his entreaty... then Manasseh knew that the LORD is God.” 4

The Folly of Amon

“He did not humble himself before the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done; Amon increased his guilt.” 5

Translation Notes

1 He did Evil (ha-Ra): In the scrolls, Manasseh is portrayed as the "Anti-Hezekiah." He didn't just "Forget" his father’s reforms; he "Systematically Reversed" them. He was "Uprooting" the very foundations of the Temple. His 55-year reign (the longest in Judah’s history) was the "Great Spiritual Drought."

2 Hook in his Nose (Be-Chochim): Literally in the scrolls, "With Hooks." This was a "Barbaric Humiliation" common in Assyrian warfare for rebellious kings. The king who had "Exalted Himself" was "Dragged like an Animal" to Babylon. It was a "Rooted" lesson in the "Weight of Pride."

3 Humbled Himself Greatly (Way-Yikkana): This is the "Turning Point" of the scrolls. Chronicles is the only book that records Manasseh’s repentance. It signifies that there is "No Sin too Deep" and "No Heart too Hard" for the grace of God. Manasseh "Found his Roots" in the darkness of a Babylonian prison.

4 Manasseh Knew: In the scrolls, this is the "Moment of Enlightenment." He had grown up in the Temple, but he didn't "Know" God until he was in the "Shackles." His "Restoration" to the throne was a "Miracle of Mercy" that redefined the "God of Israel" as the "God of Second Chances."

5 Amon Increased his Guilt: The scrolls conclude with the "Warning of the Son." Amon saw his father’s "Sins" and his "Repentance," yet he chose only to "Imitate the Sins." He refused the "Humility" that had saved his father. His "Uprooting" was swift—he reigned only two years before being assassinated. It proves that "Mercy" is not a "License" for the next generation.

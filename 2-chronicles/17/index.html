2 Chronicles 17

The LORD was with Jehoshaphat because he followed the ways of his father David in his early years. 1
“His heart was devoted to the ways of the LORD.” 2

The Teaching Reform

They taught throughout Judah, taking with them the Book of the Law of the LORD; they went around to all the towns of Judah and taught the people. 3

The Fear of the LORD

“The fear of the LORD fell on all the kingdoms of the lands surrounding Judah, so that they did not go to war against Jehoshaphat.” 4

The Mighty Army

“Jehoshaphat became more and more powerful... he had a large army of fighting men in Jerusalem.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Ways of David (Ha-Derekh ha-Rishonot): Literally in the scrolls, "The First Ways." Jehoshaphat was "Re-rooting" the nation in the original passion of the Davidic dynasty. He was bypassing the recent failures and reaching back to the "Source" of the kingdom’s strength.

2 Heart was Devoted (Gabhah Libbo): Literally in the scrolls, "His heart was lifted up." Usually, a "Lifted Heart" refers to pride, but here it is "Pride in the LORD." He was "Bold" and "Exalted" in his pursuit of holiness. It signifies a "Rooted Confidence" that does not shrink from spiritual reform.

3 Taking with them the Book (Sepher Torat YHWH): This is a "Revolutionary Strategy" in the scrolls. Instead of expecting the people to come to the Temple, Jehoshaphat sent the "Word" to the people. It was the first "National Literacy and Spiritual Education" program. It ensured that the "Roots of Faith" were planted in every local town.

4 Fear of the LORD (Pachad YHWH): In the scrolls, this is the "Ultimate Deterrent." Because Judah was "Rooted in the Word," the surrounding nations perceived a "Supernatural Protection." Peace was not won by "Diplomacy," but by "Devotion." The "Spiritual Climate" of the nation protected its "Geopolitical Status."

5 Large Army: The scrolls record the "Overflow of Power." Jehoshaphat’s army (over a million men) was a "Sign of Prosperity." The "Strength" of the nation was a "Fruit" of its "Spiritual Alignment." However, the chapter ends with the "Warning" of this great power—it can lead to dangerous alliances (Chapter 18).

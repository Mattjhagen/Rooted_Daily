2 Chronicles 13

The Covenant of Salt

“Don’t you know that the LORD, the God of Israel, has given the kingship of Israel to David and his descendants forever by a covenant of salt?” 1
“As for us, the LORD is our God, and we have not abandoned him.” 2

The Cry of Judah

“When Judah turned around, they were being attacked from both front and rear. Then they cried out to the LORD.”
“As the men of Judah raised the battle cry, God routed Jeroboam and all Israel.” 3
The Israelites were subdued on that occasion, and the people of Judah were victorious because they relied on the LORD, the God of their ancestors. 4
“Abijah grew strong... he married fourteen wives and had twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Covenant of Salt (Berit Melach): In the scrolls, salt is a "Preservative" and a "Permanent Element." A covenant of salt is an "Inviolable Agreement." Abijah was reminding the rebels that God’s choice of David was not a "Passing Fad" but an "Enduring Decree." To fight David was to fight the "Salt of the Promise."

2 The LORD is our God: This is a "Declaration of Identity" in the scrolls. While Jeroboam had created "Calf-Gods" for political convenience, Judah remained "Rooted" in the "Name of Yahweh." Abijah’s confidence was not in his "Army" (which was half the size of the enemy’s) but in his "God."

3 Battle Cry (Hari’u): In the scrolls, this is the "Sacramental Shout." It was the same cry that brought down the walls of Jericho. It was not just a "Military Signal," but a "Spiritual Appeal" to the God of the Covenant. When the priests blew the trumpets and the people shouted, the "Presence of God" intervened.

4 Relied on the LORD (Nish'anu): Literally in the scrolls, "They leaned." They weren't just "Believing" in God; they were "Weighting" their entire existence on His faithfulness. This "Reliance" is the secret of the "Rooted" life—transferring the burden of the battle to the One who is truly "Mighty."

5 Abijah Grew Strong: The scrolls conclude with the "Expansion of the House." Despite his short reign (3 years), Abijah’s victory secured the "Stability" of Judah. His "Household" (14 wives) reflected the "Blessing of God" that follows "Victory." He was the "Bridge" between the failures of Rehoboam and the reforms of Asa.

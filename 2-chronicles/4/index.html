2 Chronicles 4

The Bronze Altar

He made a bronze altar twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide and ten cubits high. 1

The Sea of Bronze

He made the Sea of cast metal... It stood on twelve bulls, three facing north, three facing west, three facing south and three facing east. 2
“The Sea was for the priests to wash in.”

The Light of the Sanctuary

He made ten gold lampstands according to the specifications set for them and placed them in the temple. 3
“Solomon made all these things in such great quantities that the weight of the bronze could not be determined.” 4
“The floral work and lamps and tongs were of solid gold.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Bronze Altar (Mizbach Nechoshet): In the scrolls, the size of this altar (30 feet by 30 feet) is "Massive." It was significantly larger than the altar in the Tabernacle. It signifies that the "Scale of Sacrifice" had increased to match the "Scale of the Kingdom." It was the "Place of Reconciliation" for the entire nation.

2 Twelve Bulls (Baqar): The scrolls highlight the "Symbolism of the Sea." The twelve bulls represent the "Twelve Tribes of Israel" supporting the "Purity of the Sanctuary." The "Sea" itself (the large water basin) was for the "Ritual Cleansing" of the priests. It shows that "Purity" is the foundation of "Service."

3 Ten Gold Lampstands (Menorot): In the scrolls, the number of lampstands increased from one (in the Tabernacle) to ten. This signifies the "Greater Light" of the Solomon era. The "Light of God" was being "Multiplied" in the nation. They were made of "Pure Gold," reflecting the "Refinement" of divine truth.

4 Weight could not be Determined: The scrolls record this "Uncountable Abundance." It was not a "Scarcity Economy" but a "Kingdom of Surplus." The bronze work was done in the "Clay Molds" of the Jordan valley, showing the "Industrial Might" of Solomon’s project. It signifies that "God’s Provision" exceeded "Human Calculation."

5 Solid Gold (Zahav Sagur): Literally in the scrolls, "Closed Gold." This refers to the "Highest Grade" of gold that has been perfectly refined. Even the smallest tools (tongs) were made of this material. It shows that in the "House of God," even the "Commonest Tool" is "Precious and Refined."

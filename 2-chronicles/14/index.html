2 Chronicles 14

Asa did what was good and right in the eyes of the LORD his God. 1
“He commanded Judah to seek the LORD, the God of their ancestors, and to obey his laws and commands.”
“The land is still ours, because we have sought the LORD our God... he has given us rest on every side.” 2

The Million-Man Army

Zerah the Cushite marched out against them with an army of thousands upon thousands and three hundred chariots. 3

Asa’s Prayer

“LORD, there is no one like you to help the powerless against the mighty. Help us, LORD our God, for we rely on you.” 4
“LORD, you are our God; do not let mere mortals prevail against you.”
“The LORD struck down the Cushites... they were crushed before the LORD and his forces.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Good and Right (Ha-Towb we-ha-Yashar): In the scrolls, this is the "Standard of the King." Asa didn't just follow "Tradition"; he followed the "Ethos of the Covenant." His "Reforms" were not just "Policy Changes," but a "Root-Level" cleansing of the culture.

2 The Land is still Ours: This is a "Spiritual Claim" in the scrolls. Asa recognized that "Possession of the Land" was tied to "Seeking the LORD." The "Rest" (Menuchah) they enjoyed was not "Geopolitical Luck" but "Covenant Reward."

3 Thousands upon Thousands (Eleph Alaphim): Literally in the scrolls, "A Thousand Thousands"—one million. This was a "Tidal Wave" of humanity coming from the South. It was a "Faith-Shattering" force designed to prove that Judah’s "Fortifications" were useless. It was a "Rooted" test of Asa’s trust.

4 Help the Powerless (En Koach): Literally in the scrolls, "Without Strength." Asa’s prayer is a "Masterclass in Humility." He acknowledges that in the face of a million men, his 580,000 were "Powerless." He makes the battle "God’s Battle" by saying "Do not let mere mortals prevail against YOU."

5 Crushed before the LORD: The scrolls record this as a "Total Annihilation." The Cushites were not just "Defeated"; they were "Shattered." The plunder they left behind was "Vast," providing the "Economic Resources" for the next phase of Asa’s reforms (Chapter 15). It proves that when we "Rely on the Source," the "Obstacle" becomes the "Opportunity."

2 Chronicles 2

“The house I am about to build will be great, because our God is greater than all other gods.” 1
“But who is able to build a temple for him, since the heavens, even the highest heavens, cannot contain him?”

The Letter to Tyre

“Send me, therefore, a man skilled to work in gold and silver, bronze and iron, and in purple, crimson and blue yarn, and experienced in engraving...” 2

The Reply of Hiram

“Because the LORD loves his people, he has made you their king.” 3
“Praise be to the LORD, the God of Israel, who made heaven and earth!” 4
“We will cut all the logs from Lebanon that you need and will float them as rafts by sea to Joppa. You can then take them up to Jerusalem.” 5

Translation Notes

1 God is Greater: In the scrolls, this is the "Alogetic Purpose" of the Temple. Solomon wasn't building for "Utility" but for "Testimony." The "Scale" of the building was a "Sign" of the "Supremacy" of Yahweh over the idols of the nations. However, Solomon’s humble admission (Verse 6) shows he knew that no "Box" could truly hold the "Infinite."

2 Engraving (Pittuach): The scrolls highlight the "Artistic Requirement" for the Sanctuary. Solomon sought a "Master Craftsman" (*Chakam*) who could work across multiple mediums. It shows that the "Holy Space" was to be a "Marriage of Skill and Material," reflecting the "Beauty of Holiness."

3 Because the LORD Loves His People: This "Foreign Recognition" in the scrolls is a major theme of Chronicles. Hiram, a pagan king, recognized that Solomon’s "Wisdom" was a "Gift of Love" from God to Israel. It shows that "Covenant Blessing" has a "Fragrance" that the world notices.

4 God... Who Made Heaven and Earth: In the scrolls, Hiram’s blessing uses the highest title for God. He is acknowledging the "Universal Creator." It is a "Rooted" moment where the "Nations" join in the "Praise of the Creator" because of the "Order" they see in Israel.

5 Float them as Rafts (Doberot): The scrolls record the "Technological Logistics" of the project. Lebanon’s cedars were moved 250 miles by sea. It was an "International Project" requiring coordination between "Kings and Workers." The Temple was "Built in Jerusalem," but its "Roots" stretched into the mountains of Lebanon and the skills of Tyre.

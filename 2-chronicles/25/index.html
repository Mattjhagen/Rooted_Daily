2 Chronicles 25

“He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, but not wholeheartedly.” 1

The Cost of Trust

Amaziah asked the man of God, “But what about the hundred talents I paid for these Israelite troops?”
“The man of God replied, ‘The LORD can give you much more than that.’” 2

The Defeated Gods

“After Amaziah returned from slaughtering the Edomites, he brought back the gods of the people of Seir. He set them up as his own gods...” 3

The Thistle and the Cedar

“Jehoash king of Israel replied... ‘A thistle in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar in Lebanon... then a wild beast in Lebanon came along and trampled the thistle.’” 4
“You have defeated Edom and now you are arrogant and proud... Why ask for trouble?”
“Jehoash... broke down the wall of Jerusalem from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate—a section about four hundred cubits long.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Not Wholeheartedly (Belo Lev Shalem): In the scrolls, this is the "Tragedy of the Divided Heart." Amaziah did the "Right Things" (*ha-Yashar*) but without the "Right Passion." He was a king of "Formalities" rather than "Roots." His obedience was "Mechanical," which left him vulnerable to the next temptation.

2 Much More than That (Harbeh mi-Zeh): This is one of the most "Rooted" promises in the scrolls. Amaziah was worried about his "Sunk Cost" (the 100 talents). God was reminding him that the "Source" of wealth is greater than any "Loss" incurred in the path of obedience. We don't "Lose" by following God; we "Exchange" the temporary for the eternal.

3 Brought back the Gods: In the scrolls, this is the "Absurdity of Apostasy." Amaziah worshipped the "Defeated Gods" of Edom. He chose the "Weakness" of the conquered over the "Strength" of the Conqueror. It shows how "Pride in Victory" can lead to "Blindness in Spirit."

4 The Thistle and the Cedar: This "Fable" in the scrolls was a "Brutal Insult." Jehoash was telling Amaziah that he was a "Lowly Thistle" trying to marry into the "Royal Cedar" of the North. It was a "Geopolitical Reality Check" that Amaziah, in his "Apostate Pride," refused to hear.

5 Broke down the Wall: The scrolls record this "Physical Humiliation." Because Amaziah had "Broken the Covenant Wall," God allowed the "Physical Wall" of Jerusalem to be broken. The 600-foot gap in the wall was a "Visible Scar" of Amaziah’s "Spiritual Failure." He ended his reign as a "Broken Man" in a "Broken City."

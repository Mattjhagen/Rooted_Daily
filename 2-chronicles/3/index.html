2 Chronicles 3

Then Solomon began to build the temple of the LORD in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where the LORD had appeared to his father David. 1

The House of Gold

He adorned the temple with precious stones. And the gold he used was gold of Parvaim. 2
“He built the Most Holy Place... and he overlaid the inside with six hundred talents of fine gold.”

The Cherubim of the Sanctuary

In the Most Holy Place he made two sculpted cherubim and overlaid them with gold. 3
“The wingspan of these cherubim was twenty cubits. They stood on their feet and faced the main hall.”

Jachin

“He Will Establish”

The Two Pillars 4

Boaz

“In Him is Strength”

“He made the veil of blue, purple and crimson yarn and fine linen, with cherubim worked into it.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Mount Moriah: In the scrolls, this is the "Place of Substitution." It is the same mountain where Abraham offered Isaac (Genesis 22). It represents the "Root" of the sacrificial system—where God provides the lamb. The Temple was built on a site "Consecrated by Sacrifice."

2 Gold of Parvaim: The scrolls mention this "Mysterious Gold." Scholars believe it refers to a region known for its "Refined Quality." It signifies that only the "Best of the Earth" was used for the "House of the King." The abundance of gold made the interior "Reflective," symbolizing the "Unapproachable Light" of God.

3 Cherubim Faced the Hall: In the scrolls, this is a "Change of Orientation." In the Tabernacle, the cherubim on the Ark faced each other. These massive new cherubim (15 feet tall) faced "Outward" toward the people. It symbolizes God’s "Watchful Presence" looking toward His worshipers.

4 Jachin and Boaz: The names of the pillars in the scrolls are a "Theological Statement." Jachin (*Yakhin*) means "He will establish"; Boaz (*Bo’az*) means "In Him is strength." Together they declare: "By His Strength, He will Establish this House." They were the "Sentinels" of the Covenant.

5 The Veil (Parokhet): In the scrolls, the veil was the "Boundary of Holiness." It separated the "Holy Place" from the "Most Holy Place." The blue, purple, and crimson colors were "Royal Colors," indicating that one was entering the "Presence of the High King." The "Cherubim" on the veil acted as "Spiritual Guards."

2 Chronicles 1

Solomon son of David established himself firmly over his kingdom, for the LORD his God was with him and made him exceedingly great. 1

The Night at Gibeon

That night God appeared to Solomon and said to him, “Ask for whatever you want me to give you.” 2
“Give me wisdom and knowledge, that I may lead this people, for who is able to govern this great people of yours?” 3

The Divine Response

“Since this is your heart’s desire and you have not asked for wealth... but for wisdom and knowledge...”
“Wisdom and knowledge will be given to you. And I will also give you wealth, possessions and honor.” 4
“The king made silver and gold as common in Jerusalem as stones.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Established Himself Firmly (Way-Yithchazzeq): Literally in the scrolls, "He strengthened himself." It indicates that the transition from David was not automatic; it required Solomon to "Step into the Anointing." The "Strength" was internal as much as political.

2 Ask for Whatever (She'al): In the scrolls, this is a "Test of the Heart." God was giving Solomon a "Blank Check." The "Ask" revealed the "Roots" of Solomon’s character. He didn't ask for "Survival" or "Revenge," but for "Utility" in the Kingdom.

3 Wisdom and Knowledge (Chokmah u-Madda): In the scrolls, *Chokmah* is "Skill in Living/Ruling" and *Madda* is "Insight/Learning." Solomon realized that the "Great People" belonged to God ("of yours"), and he was just a steward. His "Humility" was the "Gateway" to his "Greatness."

4 Wealth, Possessions and Honor: The scrolls show that "Secondary Blessings" follow "Primary Priorities." Because Solomon sought the "Order of the Kingdom" (Wisdom), God added the "Resources of the Kingdom" (Wealth). It is the "Rooted" principle that "Success" is a by-product of "Seeking."

5 Silver like Stones: This is a "Hyperbolic Description" in the scrolls of the "Prosperity of Wisdom." It signifies that under Solomon, the "Value of the Rare" was diminished because of the "Abundance of the Presence." However, the scrolls also hint at the "Danger" of such wealth—it can make one forget the "Source."

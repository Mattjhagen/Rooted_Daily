2 Chronicles 35

The Passover of Restoration

Josiah celebrated the Passover to the LORD in Jerusalem... 1
“The Passover had not been observed like this in Israel since the days of Samuel the prophet.” 2

The Warning from Egypt

Pharaoh Necho sent messengers: “What quarrel is there between you and me? ... Stop opposing God, who is with me, or he will destroy you.” 3
“Josiah, however, would not turn away from him... he went to fight on the plain of Megiddo.”

The Fall of the Reformer

Archers shot King Josiah, and he told his officers, “Take me away; I am badly wounded.” 4
“Jeremiah composed laments for Josiah, and to this day all the male and female singers commemorate Josiah in the laments.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Josiah Celebrated: In the scrolls, this is the "Last Great Peak" before the Exile. Josiah’s Passover was the "Fruit" of the found Law (Chapter 34). It was an act of "Radical Obedience" to the ancient instructions. He wasn't just "Following Tradition"; he was "Reviving Reality."

2 Since the Days of Samuel: This is a "Stunning Comparison" in the scrolls. It places Josiah’s devotion above even David’s or Solomon’s in terms of "Paschal Integrity." It signifies a "Rooted Purity" that hadn't been seen for centuries. It was the "Perfect Worship" of a "Dying Monarchy."

3 God, who is with Me: In the scrolls, this is the "Paradox of Prophecy." God spoke to the King of Judah through the King of Egypt. Josiah, who was usually so "Responsive" to the Word (Chapter 34), failed to "Hear" God when he used a "Foreign Vessel." His "Zeal" for Judah’s independence blinded him to the "Divine Directive."

4 Badly Wounded (He-Chaleiti): Literally in the scrolls, "I am made sick/weak." This is the "End of the Era of Reform." Megiddo (Armageddon) became the "Grave of the Hope" of the nation. Josiah’s "Disguise" (*Verse 22*) could not protect him from a judgment he had "Invited" by his disobedience. It is a "Rooted" warning that even a "Good King" must stay "Subject" to the Word.

5 Jeremiah Composed Laments: The scrolls record this "National Grief." Josiah was the "Breath of our Nostrils" (Lamentations 4:20). With his death, the "Spiritual Shield" of the nation was removed. The "Female Singers" (*Verse 25*) were the "Custodians of Memory," ensuring that the "Loss of the Reformer" would never be forgotten. It marks the "Beginning of the End."

2 Chronicles 9

When the queen of Sheba heard of Solomon’s fame, she came to Jerusalem to test him with hard questions. 1
“Indeed, not even half the greatness of your wisdom was told me; you have far exceeded the report I heard.” 2

The Ivory Throne

The king made a great ivory throne and overlaid it with pure gold. 3
“The throne had six steps, and a gold footstool was attached to it... Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one at either end of each step. Nothing like it had ever been made for any other kingdom.”

The Greatest on Earth

“King Solomon was greater in riches and wisdom than all the other kings of the earth.” 4
“All the kings of the earth sought audience with Solomon to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart.”
“Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years. Then he rested with his ancestors and was buried in the City of David his father.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Hard Questions (Chidot): Literally in the scrolls, "Riddles." The Queen was not just asking factual questions; she was testing his "Discernment" and "Spiritual Insight." Solomon’s ability to "Explain the Unexplained" was what truly "Breathless" (*Verse 4*) her. It signifies that "Rooted Wisdom" can solve the "Riddles of Life."

2 Not even Half: The scrolls highlight the "Gap between Report and Reality." No matter how great the "Fame" of the Kingdom, the "Experience" of the Presence was always greater. It is a "Rooted" truth that the "Fame of God" is only a shadow of the "Presence of God."

3 Ivory Throne (Kisse Shen): In the scrolls, this is the "Ultimate Symbol of Sovereignty." Ivory (elephant tusks) was a "Rare and Costly" import. Overlaying it with gold made it a "Marriage of Strength and Splendor." The "Twelve Lions" represented the "Twelve Tribes" guarding the authority of the King.

4 Greater in Riches and Wisdom: The scrolls conclude the narrative of Solomon by "Pairing these two attributes." His wealth was the "Fruit" of his wisdom. The world sought him not for his "Chariots" but for his "Heart." He was the "Magnet of the Nations."

5 Rested with his Ancestors (Way-Yishkab): The scrolls end the chapter with the "Sunset of the Golden Age." Solomon’s reign was the "High-Water Mark" of the united monarchy. His "Death" set the stage for the "Testing of the Roots" in the next generation. The "Peace" he built would now be challenged by the "Pride" of his son.

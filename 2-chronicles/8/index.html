2 Chronicles 8

At the end of twenty years, during which Solomon built the temple of the LORD and his own palace...
“He also built Tadmor in the desert and all the store cities he had built in Hamath.” 1

The Holiness of Zion

Solomon brought Pharaoh’s daughter up from the City of David to the palace he had built for her, for he said, “My wife must not live in the palace of David king of Israel, because the places the ark of the LORD has entered are holy.” 2

The Prescribed Service

“In keeping with the ordinance of his father David, he appointed the divisions of the priests for their duties, and the Levites to lead the praise...” 3
“All Solomon’s work was carried out, from the day the foundation of the temple of the LORD was laid until its completion.”

The Gold of Ophir

“Hiram sent him ships... they sailed to Ophir and brought back four hundred and fifty talents of gold, which they delivered to King Solomon.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Tadmor in the Desert: In the scrolls, this represents Solomon’s "Expansion of Influence." By building in the desert, he was securing trade routes and establishing a "Presence" in regions that were previously untamed. It signifies a "Rooted" kingdom that extends its order into the wilderness.

2 Places the Ark entered are Holy: The scrolls highlight Solomon’s "Sensitivity to Sanctity." Even though he had married Pharaoh’s daughter (a political alliance), he recognized that the "Space of David" was "Contagiously Holy." He wanted to maintain the "Spiritual Purity" of the royal residence. It shows that "Covenant Space" is different from "Secular Space."

3 Ordinance of his Father David: The scrolls emphasize "Continuity." Solomon didn't "Re-invent" the service; he "Implemented" the vision of the previous generation. It is a "Rooted" principle that the "Success" of one generation depends on the "Faithfulness" to the foundation laid by the previous one.

4 Four Hundred and Fifty Talents: This "Staggering Wealth" in the scrolls (approx. 17 tons of gold) was the "Result of Peace." Because Solomon was not at war, he could focus on "Commerce and Exploration." Ophir represents the "Ends of the Earth." The "Kingdom of God" was attracting the wealth of the nations. It is a "Fulfillment" of the promise in Chapter 1.

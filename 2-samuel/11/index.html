2 Samuel 11

1In the spring, at the time when kings go off to war... David sent Joab out with the king’s men and the whole Israelite army... But David remained in Jerusalem. 1
2One evening David got up from his bed and walked around on the roof of the palace. From the roof he saw a woman bathing. The woman was very beautiful... 2
4Then David sent messengers to get her. She came to him, and he slept with her... Then she went back home.
5The woman conceived and sent word to David, saying, “I am pregnant.”
11Uriah said to David, “The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in tents... How could I go to my house to eat and drink and make love to my wife? As surely as you live, I will not do such a thing!” 3

The Letter of Death

14In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it with Uriah. 4
“Put Uriah out in front where the fighting is fiercest. Then withdraw from him so he will be struck down and die.”
17...and Uriah the Hittite died.
26When Uriah’s wife heard that her husband was dead, she mourned for him.
27After the time of mourning was over, David had her brought to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son.
But the thing David had done displeased the LORD. 5

Translation Notes

1 David Remained (Va-ye-shev David): The scrolls start this tragedy with a "Strategic Absence." As king, David should have been leading his men (Chapter 5, 8, 10). By staying behind while his men were "In Tents," he created the "Idle Environment" where temptation could take root. His palace had become a "Gilded Cage" of complacency.

2 Walked around on the Roof: In the scrolls, this is the "Vantage Point of Entitlement." From the height of the palace, David looked down on his subjects. His sin started with a "Look," progressed to an "Inquiry," and ended in "Seizing." He treated the wife of his most loyal soldier as a "Resource" to be consumed.

3 Uriah’s Integrity: This is the most painful contrast in the scrolls. Uriah, a "Hittite" (convert), demonstrated more "Covenant Loyalty" than the King of Israel. He refused the "Comforts of Home" while the Ark of God was in the field. His refusal to go home forced David into the even darker path of murder.

4 Sent it with Uriah: In the scrolls, this is the height of "Cruelty." Uriah was carrying his own "Death Warrant." David trusted Uriah’s integrity so much that he knew the soldier would not read the letter. He used Uriah’s "Virtue" to accomplish his "Vice."

5 Displeased the LORD (Va-ye-ra Be-ene YHWH): Literally in the scrolls, "The thing was evil in the eyes of the LORD." David thought he had covered his tracks. He had the woman, the child, and the dead husband. But the scrolls remind us that "The Eyes of the LORD" see through the walls of the palace. This single sentence sets the stage for the "Sword" that would never leave David’s house.

2 Samuel 13

1In the course of time, Amnon son of David fell in love with Tamar, the beautiful sister of Absalom...
3Now Amnon had an adviser named Jonadab... a very shrewd man. 1
“No, my brother!” she said to him. “Don’t force me! Such a thing should not be done in Israel! Don’t do this wicked thing!” 2
14But he refused to listen to her, and since he was stronger than she, he raped her.
15Then Amnon hated her with intense hatred. In fact, he hated her more than he had loved her. 3
19Tamar put ashes on her head and tore the ornate robe she was wearing. She put her hands on her head and went away, weeping aloud as she went.
20And Tamar lived in her brother Absalom’s house, a desolate woman. 4

Absalom’s Vengeance

28Absalom ordered his men, “Listen! When Amnon is in high spirits from drinking wine... strike him down and kill him. Don’t be afraid!”
29So Absalom’s men did to Amnon what Absalom had ordered. Then all the king’s sons got up, mounted their mules and fled. 5
38After Absalom fled and went to Geshur, he stayed there three years.

Translation Notes

1 Shrewd Man (Ish Chakam Me-od): Literally in the scrolls, "A very wise man." But his "wisdom" was dark and manipulative. Jonadab is a "Foil" to Nathan. Nathan used a parable to bring David to repentance; Jonadab used a plot to lead Amnon into sin. It shows that "Intellectual Brilliance" without "Moral Guidance" is a deadly weapon.

2 Don’t do this Wicked Thing (Nebalah): The word in the scrolls refers to a "Senseless, Disgraceful Act." Tamar’s plea was an appeal to the "Covenant Identity" of Israel. She was reminding Amnon that they were supposed to be different from the surrounding nations. Her rape was a violation of the very "Purity" the kingdom was meant to protect.

3 Intense Hatred: The scrolls note this "Psychological Shift." Amnon didn't love Tamar; he was "In Lust" with the "Idea" of her. Once he had "Seized" her, she became the symbol of his guilt. His hatred was a "Self-Defense Mechanism" to push away the mirror of his own crime.

4 Desolate Woman (Shomemah): Literally in the scrolls, "Isolated" or "Ruined." Tamar’s life as a royal princess was over. She was "Covenantally Dead" in terms of marriage and future. Her brother Absalom’s silence ("Be quiet now") was not comfort, but a "Strategic Suppression." He was already planning his own move.

5 Absalom’s Men: In the scrolls, this is the "Echo of David’s Letter." Just as David used Joab to kill Uriah (Chapter 11), Absalom used his men to kill Amnon. The "Sword" had truly arrived in the house. This was not just a family feud; it was a "Succession Crisis." Amnon was the heir apparent; his death made Absalom the primary claimant to the throne.

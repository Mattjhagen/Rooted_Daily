2 Samuel 10

2David thought, “I will show kindness to Hanun son of Nahash...” So David sent a delegation to express his sympathy.
4So Hanun seized David’s envoys, shaved off half of each man’s beard, cut off their garments in the middle at the buttocks, and sent them away. 1
6When the Ammonites realized that they had become obnoxious to David, they hired twenty thousand Aramean foot soldiers... 2

The Courage of Joab

11Joab said, “If the Arameans are too strong for me, then you are to come to my rescue; but if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will come to rescue you.”
12“Be strong, and let us fight bravely for our people and the cities of our God. The LORD will do what is good in his sight.” 3
18But the Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven hundred of their charioteers and forty thousand of their horsemen. 4
19When all the kings who were vassals of Hadadezer saw that they had been routed by Israel, they made peace with the Israelites and became subject to them. 5

Translation Notes

1 Shaved off the Beards: In the scrolls, this was an "Extreme Cultural Insult." For an Israelite man, the beard was a symbol of "Dignity" and "Manhood." By shaving half of it and cutting their robes, Hanun was not just insulting the men, but the "King" they represented. It was a "Public Mockery" that made war inevitable.

2 Hired the Arameans: The Ammonites knew they couldn't stand against David alone, so they created an "International Coalition" in the scrolls. They used their wealth to bring in mercenaries from the north (modern-day Syria). This escalated a local dispute into a "Regional War."

3 Be Strong (Chazaq): This is one of the most powerful "Battle Speeches" in the scrolls. Joab, though often ruthless, here demonstrates a "Profound Faith." He understood that while they must fight with all their might, the "Final Outcome" belonged to the LORD. This balance of "Human Effort" and "Divine Sovereignty" is a central theme of the history.

4 Seven Hundred Charioteers: In the scrolls, David demonstrated that his "Infantry-Based Army" could defeat a "Chariot-Based Superpower." God’s previous instruction to "hamstring the horses" (Chapter 8) did not leave Israel vulnerable; it forced them to rely on "Divine Strategy."

5 Became Subject: The conclusion of this war in the scrolls marks the peak of David’s "Empire." The Arameans, who would later become a major thorn in Israel’s side, were now "Tributaries." This peace in the north allowed David to focus on the final siege of the Ammonite capital, which provides the dark background for the events of Chapter 11.

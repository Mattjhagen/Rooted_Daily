2 Samuel 12

1The LORD sent Nathan to David. When he came to him, he said, “There were two men in a certain town, one rich and the other poor...” 1
“The poor man had nothing but one little ewe lamb he had bought. He raised it, and it grew up with him and his children. It ate from his plate, drank from his cup and even slept in his arms.”
“A traveler came to the rich man, but the rich man refrained from taking one of his own sheep... Instead, he took the ewe lamb that belonged to the poor man.”
David burned with anger against the man and said to Nathan, “As surely as the LORD lives, the man who did this must die!”
Then Nathan said to David, “YOU ARE THE MAN!” 2
“Now, therefore, the sword will never depart from your house, because you despised me and took the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your own.”
Then David said to Nathan, “I have sinned against the LORD.” 3
18On the seventh day the child died.
“But now that he is dead, why should I go on fasting? Can I bring him back again? I will go to him, but he will not return to me.” 4
24Then David comforted his wife Bathsheba, and he went to her and made love to her. She gave birth to a son, and they named him Solomon. The LORD loved him. 5

Translation Notes

1 Nathan’s Parable: In the scrolls, this is a masterpiece of "Prophetic Trap-Setting." Nathan knew that David’s "Justice Reflex" was still intact even if his "Self-Awareness" was clouded. By telling a story about a "Little Ewe Lamb," he bypassed David’s defenses and got him to pronounce his own sentence.

2 You are the Man (Attah ha-Ish): These four words in the scrolls are the most shattering in the Davidic narrative. In an instant, the "Gilded Mask" of the king was stripped away. Nathan was holding up a mirror to David’s soul. David had become the very tyrant he used to fight against. This is the moment where "Power" meets "Truth."

3 I have Sinned (Chatati): In the scrolls, this is the shortest but most significant confession. Unlike Saul, who made excuses (1 Samuel 15), David took "Full Responsibility." He didn't blame the woman or the stress of the war. This "Brokenness" is why David is called a "Man after God’s Own Heart"—not because he was perfect, but because he was "Repentant."

4 I will go to him: This is a rare glimpse into the "Afterlife Theology" of the scrolls. David found comfort not in the hope of the child’s return, but in the certainty of a "Future Reunion." It shows David’s deep trust in the character of God even in the midst of painful judgment.

5 The LORD Loved Him (Jedidiah): In the scrolls, the birth of Solomon is the "Silver Lining of Grace." Despite the horrific nature of the sin, God did not abandon the line of David. The name *Jedidiah* means "Loved by Yah." It signifies that while the "Sword" was now in the house, the "Covenant" was still in force. Solomon would become the builder of the house David was not permitted to build.

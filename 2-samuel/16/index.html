2 Samuel 16

1When David had gone a short distance beyond the summit, there was Ziba, the steward of Mephibosheth, waiting to meet him...
3The king then asked, “Where is your master’s grandson?” Ziba said to him, “He is staying in Jerusalem, because he thinks, ‘Today the house of Israel will give me back my grandfather’s kingdom.’” 1

The Curse at Bahurim

5As King David approached Bahurim, a man from the same clan as Saul’s family came out... His name was Shimei son of Gera, and he cursed as he came. 2
“Get out, get out, you man of blood, you rogue! The LORD has repaid you for all the blood you shed in the household of Saul...”
10But the king said... “If he is cursing because the LORD said to him, ‘Curse David,’ who can ask, ‘Why do you do this?’” 3
20Absalom said to Ahithophel, “Give us your advice. What should we do?”
Ahithophel answered, “Sleep with your father’s concubines whom he left to take care of the palace. Then all Israel will hear that you have made yourself obnoxious to your father...” 4
22So they pitched a tent for Absalom on the roof, and he slept with his father’s concubines in the sight of all Israel.
23Now in those days the advice Ahithophel gave was like that of one who inquires of God. 5

Translation Notes

1 Ziba’s Deception: In the scrolls, this is a masterclass in "Opportunism." Ziba was exploiting David’s "Emotional Vulnerability" during the flight to gain control of Mephibosheth’s land. David’s impulsive decision to give the land to Ziba (without a trial) shows how his "Judgment" was impaired by the stress of the coup.

2 Man of Blood (Ish ha-Damim): Shimei’s insult in the scrolls was directed at David’s "Military History." He was accusing David of being a "Usurper" who had climbed to power over the bodies of Saul’s house. While David was innocent of Saul’s blood (Chapter 1), he was indeed a "Man of Blood" regarding Uriah (Chapter 11). Shimei’s words were "Cruel" but they contained a "Stinging Grain of Truth."

3 Leave Him Alone: David’s response in the scrolls is a high point of "Spiritual Submission." He refused to let Abishai kill Shimei, not because Shimei was right, but because David was willing to accept "Any Humiliation" if it came from the hand of God. He was leaving his "Vindication" entirely to the LORD.

4 Obnoxious to your Father: Ahithophel’s advice in the scrolls was a "Strategic Necessity" for the rebellion. In the ancient world, taking the king’s harem was the ultimate "Declaration of Sovereignty." It also made the breach between father and son "Irreparable," forcing the people to choose sides permanently. It was a cold, calculated move to solidify Absalom’s power.

5 Advice like that of God: This closing note in the scrolls highlights the "Intellectual Weight" of the conspiracy. Ahithophel was the greatest mind in the kingdom. David was not just fighting a son; he was fighting his own "Best Counselor." This makes David’s prayer in the previous chapter ("Turn Ahithophel’s counsel into foolishness") the most critical spiritual battle of the war.

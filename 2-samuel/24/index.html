2 Samuel 24

1Again the anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he incited David... saying, “Go and take a census of Israel and Judah.” 1
But Joab replied to the king, “May the LORD your God multiply the troops a hundred times over... but why does my lord the king want to do such a thing?”
10David was conscience-stricken after he had counted the fighting men, and he said to the LORD, “I have sinned greatly in what I have done... I have done a very foolish thing.” 2
David said to Gad, “I am in deep distress. Let us fall into the hands of the LORD, for his mercy is great; but do not let me fall into human hands.” 3
16When the angel stretched out his hand to destroy Jerusalem, the LORD was grieved because of the calamity and said... “Enough! Withdraw your hand.”
17David said... “I am the one who has sinned and done wrong. These are but sheep. What have they done?”

The Cost of Worship

“No, I insist on paying you for it. I will not sacrifice to the LORD my God burnt offerings that cost me nothing.” 4
David built an altar to the LORD there and sacrificed burnt offerings and fellowship offerings. Then the LORD answered his prayer in behalf of the land, and the plague on Israel was stopped. 5

Translation Notes

1 Incited David: The scrolls present a complex "Providential Mystery." Why was God angry? Likely because of the lingering divisions and rebellions (Chapters 15, 20). The census was a sin because it shifted David’s "Trust" from God’s power to his "Military Numbers." David was treating Israel as a "Human Resource" to be exploited rather than a "Covenant People."

2 Conscience-Stricken (Va-yak Lev David): Literally in the scrolls, "David’s heart struck him." David’s sensitivity to the Spirit was his "Saving Grace." Even when he failed, his "Heart" wouldn't let him rest in his pride. He recognized the "Folly" of trying to quantify the "Blessing of God."

3 Fall into the hands of the LORD: David’s choice in the scrolls is a "Theological Masterstroke." He knew that human enemies (the other options) are often "Cruel" and "Relentless," but God is "Merciful" even in His discipline. He chose the path that kept him closest to the "Heart of the Creator."

4 Offerings that cost me nothing: This is a "Universal Principle of Worship" in the scrolls. Real worship requires "Sacrifice." David refused the "Easy Path" of a free gift from Araunah because he understood that worship is not about "Convenience" but about "Devotion." It must "Cost" something to mean anything.

5 Araunah’s Threshing Floor: In the scrolls, this is a "Prophetic Location." This site (Mount Moriah) was where Abraham had offered Isaac, and it would become the site where Solomon would build the "First Temple." The book of 2 Samuel ends not with a "Military Victory," but with an "Altar of Mercy," signifying that the kingdom’s true foundation is "Sacrificial Reconciliation."

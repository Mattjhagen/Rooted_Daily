2 Samuel 17

1Ahithophel said to Absalom, “I would choose twelve thousand men and set out tonight in pursuit of David.”
“I would attack him while he is weary and weak. I would strike down only the king and bring all the people back to you.” 1
7Hushai said to Absalom, “The advice Ahithophel has given is not good this time.”
“You know your father and his men; they are fighters, and as fierce as a wild bear robbed of her cubs in the field.” 2
“I advise that all Israel, from Dan to Beersheba... be gathered to you. Then you personally should lead them into battle.” 3
For the LORD had determined to frustrate the good advice of Ahithophel in order to bring disaster on Absalom.
18But a boy saw them and told Absalom... so the two of them went quickly to the house of a man in Bahurim. He had a well in his courtyard, and they climbed down into it. 4
22So David and all the people with him set out and crossed the Jordan. By daybreak, no one was left who had not crossed the Jordan.
23When Ahithophel saw that his advice had not been followed... he went home to his own town, put his house in order and hanged himself. 5
28They brought beds and basins and articles of pottery. They also brought wheat and barley, flour and roasted grain...
For they said, “The people have become hungry and tired and thirsty in the wilderness.”

Translation Notes

1 Strike down only the King: In the scrolls, Ahithophel’s advice was "Strategically Perfect." He knew that David’s army was held together only by David’s presence. A swift, surgical strike would have ended the war with minimal casualties. His brilliance was his lack of "Sentimentality."

2 Bear robbed of her Cubs: Hushai used "Psychological Imagery" in the scrolls to play on Absalom’s secret fear of his father. David was indeed a "War-Bear," but Hushai was exaggeratedly painting him as an "Invincible Force" to make Absalom hesitate. He was fighting a war of "Visions."

3 You personally should lead: This was the "Ego-Trap" in the scrolls. Hushai knew that Absalom’s vanity was his weak point. By suggesting a "Massive Parade" led by Absalom himself, he was offering the prince the "Glory" he craved, while secretly giving David the "Time" he needed to escape.

4 The Well at Bahurim: This suspenseful scene in the scrolls shows the "Invisible Support Network" David still had. From a servant girl to a woman with a well, the "Common People" were risking their lives to protect the King’s messengers. It shows that while the "Nobles" followed Absalom, the "Heart of the Land" was still with David.

5 Ahithophel’s Suicide: In the scrolls, this is the "Rationality of Despair." Ahithophel was so brilliant that he knew exactly how the war would end once his advice was rejected. He saw the "Failure of the Coup" as a mathematical certainty. He didn't hang himself out of "Anger," but out of "Calculated Avoidance" of the inevitable execution that would follow David’s return.

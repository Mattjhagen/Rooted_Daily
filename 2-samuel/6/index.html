2 Samuel 6

3They set the ark of God on a new cart and brought it from the house of Abinadab on the hill. 1
6When they came to the threshing floor of Nakon, Uzzah reached out and took hold of the ark of God, because the oxen stumbled.
7The LORD’s anger burned against Uzzah because of his irreverent act; therefore God struck him down, and he died there beside the ark of God. 2
11The ark of the LORD remained in the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite for three months, and the LORD blessed him and his entire household.

The Celebration of Zion

14Wearing a linen ephod, David was dancing before the LORD with all his might. 3
15While he and all Israel were bringing up the ark of the LORD with shouts and the sound of trumpets.
16As the ark of the LORD was entering the City of David, Michal daughter of Saul watched from a window. And when she saw King David leaping and dancing before the LORD, she despised him in her heart. 4
“How the king of Israel has distinguished himself today, disrobing in the sight of the slave girls... as any vulgar fellow would!”
21David said to Michal, “It was before the LORD, who chose me rather than your father... I will celebrate before the LORD.”
“I will become even more undignified than this, and I will be humiliated in my own eyes.” 5
23And Michal daughter of Saul had no children to the day of her death.

Translation Notes

1 New Cart: In the scrolls, this was a "Ritual Error." The Philistines had used a cart (1 Samuel 6), but the Law (Numbers 4) required the Ark to be carried on the shoulders of the Levites. David was copying the "Pagan Method" rather than the "Divine Mandate." This lack of "Covenant Precision" led to the tragedy.

2 Death of Uzzah: The "Irreverent Act" in the scrolls was the "Presumption of Control." Uzzah thought he could protect God’s holiness with his own "Common Hands." God was teaching the nation that while He was coming to live among them, He was still "Uncontrollable" and "Holy." The Ark was not a piece of furniture; it was the Throne.

3 Linen Ephod: In the scrolls, this is a highly significant detail. David had laid aside his "Royal Robes" and dressed as a "Priest." He was showing that in the presence of God, he was just another worshiper. His dance was not "Vulgar," but an act of "Sacrificial Joy." He was "Swept Up" in the presence of the King of Kings.

4 Michal Despised Him: In the scrolls, Michal was acting like "Saul’s Daughter" rather than "David’s Wife." She valued "Royal Dignity" and "Public Image" over "Spiritual Passion." She wanted a king who stayed in the palace and looked impressive. She couldn't understand a king who would "Abase" himself for the glory of God.

5 Undignified (Naqalloti): The word in the scrolls implies "being light" or "small." David was willing to be "Small" in the eyes of the world if it meant God was "Great." His response to Michal is a "Manifesto of Worship." He refused to let "Social Expectation" dictate his "Spiritual Expression." The result for Michal (barrenness) symbolized the "Spiritual Dead End" of a heart that refuses to worship.

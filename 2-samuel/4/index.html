2 Samuel 4

1When Ish-Bosheth son of Saul heard that Abner had died in Hebron, he lost courage and all Israel was alarmed.
4(Jonathan son of Saul had a son who was lame in both feet. He was five years old when the news about Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel. His nurse picked him up and fled, but as she hurried to leave, he fell and became disabled. His name was Mephibosheth.) 1
5Now Rekab and Baanah... set out for the house of Ish-Bosheth, and they arrived there in the heat of the day while he was taking his noonday rest.
7They went into the house while he was lying on the bed in his bedroom. After they stabbed and killed him, they cut off his head. 2
8They brought the head of Ish-Bosheth to David at Hebron and said... “Here is the head of Ish-Bosheth... Today the LORD has avenged my lord the king against Saul and his offspring.”
9David answered... “As surely as the LORD lives, who has delivered me out of every trouble,”
11“how much more, when wicked men have killed an innocent man in his own house and on his own bed! Shall I not now require his blood from your hands and rid the earth of you?” 3
12So David gave an order to his men, and they killed them. They cut off their hands and feet and hung the bodies by the pool in Hebron. 4
But they took the head of Ish-Bosheth and buried it in Abner’s tomb at Hebron. 5

Translation Notes

1 Mephibosheth (Merib-Baal): The scrolls include this parenthetical note to explain why David did not have to deal with Jonathan’s son as a rival. A "disabled" person in that culture was considered unfit for the throne. It also sets up David’s future "Covenant Kindness" toward this boy. His name in the scrolls, *Mephibosheth*, likely means "He Who Scatters Shame."

2 Stabbed on the Bed: In the scrolls, this was a "Cowardly Assassination." Rekab and Baanah were Ish-Bosheth’s own officers. They were Benjamites (Saul’s own tribe), which made their betrayal even deeper. They were "Opportunists" who thought they could buy David’s favor with the head of his rival.

3 Wicked men... Innocent man: David’s verdict in the scrolls is a masterpiece of "Moral Distinction." He recognized that Ish-Bosheth, while a political rival, was an "Innocent Man" (*Ish Tzaddik*) who had done nothing to deserve such a brutal end. David refused to let "Political Expediency" justify "Moral Evil."

4 Cut off Hands and Feet: In the scrolls, this was a "Legal Punishment" for their crime. Their hands had committed the murder and their feet had carried the head to David. By hanging their bodies by the pool, David was making a "Public Declaration" that his kingdom would not be built on treason or assassination. He was protecting the "Dignity of the Throne" even when it belonged to his enemy.

5 Buried in Abner’s Tomb: This final act of "Respect" in the scrolls shows David’s commitment to the "Unity of Israel." He didn't treat Ish-Bosheth’s remains as a trophy, but as the remains of a "King of Israel." He linked the last of Saul’s house with the great general Abner, signifying the end of the civil war and the beginning of a unified future.

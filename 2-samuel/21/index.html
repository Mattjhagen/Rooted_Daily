2 Samuel 21

1During the reign of David, there was a famine for three successive years... The LORD said, “It is on account of Saul and his blood-stained house; it is because he put the Gibeonites to death.” 1

The Vigil of Rizpah

10Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it out for herself on a rock... She did not let the birds of the air touch the bodies by day or the wild animals by night. 2
After that, God answered prayer in behalf of the land.

The Last of the Giants

15Once again there was a battle... David went down with his men to fight the Philistines, and he became exhausted.
Ishbi-Benob, one of the descendants of Rapha, whose bronze spearhead weighed three hundred shekels... intended to kill David.
But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to David’s rescue; he struck the Philistine down and killed him. 3
“Never again will you go out with us to battle, so that the lamp of Israel will not be extinguished.” 4
22These four were descendants of Rapha in Gath, and they fell at the hands of David and his men. 5

Translation Notes

1 Account of Saul: In the scrolls, this is a lesson on "Covenant Integrity." Saul had violated the 400-year-old oath Joshua had made to the Gibeonites (Joshua 9). Even though Saul was dead, the "National Sin" remained. David had to resolve the "Legal Debt" before the blessing (rain) could return to the land.

2 Vigil of Rizpah: This is one of the most moving scenes in the scrolls. Rizpah was Saul’s concubine (Chapter 3). Her silent, persistent "Mother’s Grief" over the bodies of her sons eventually moved David to act with "Compassion" for the entire house of Saul. She represents the "Silent Witnesses" of history whose suffering eventually forces a change in policy.

3 He became Exhausted: The scrolls show David’s "Human Vulnerability." He was no longer the young boy who killed Goliath. The "Giants" were still there, but David now needed his "Mighty Men" to protect him. It shows the "Transition of Leadership" from the individual hero to the established kingdom.

4 Lamp of Israel (Ner Yisrael): In the scrolls, David’s life had become the "Spiritual Light" of the nation. If he died, the nation would go into "Spiritual Darkness." This is why his men forbade him from the front lines. He was now more valuable as a "Symbol of Unity" than as a "Combatant."

5 Descendants of Rapha: These giants (*Rephaim*) in the scrolls were the "Last Remnants" of an ancient, anti-covenant lineage. By defeating them, David and his men were finishing the work of Moses and Joshua. The "Giants of Gath" were finally extinguished, signifying the total victory of the "Kingdom of God" over the "Lineage of Pride."

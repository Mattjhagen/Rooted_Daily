2 Samuel 20

1Now a troublemaker named Sheba son of Bikri... happened to be there. He sounded the trumpet and shouted, “We have no share in David, no part in Jesse’s son! Every man to his tent, Israel!” 1
2So all the men of Israel deserted David to follow Sheba son of Bikri. But the men of Judah stayed favorable to their king all the way from the Jordan to Jerusalem.

The Murder of Amasa

9Joab said to Amasa, “Is it well with you, my brother?” Then Joab took Amasa by the beard with his right hand to kiss him. 2
10Amasa was not on his guard against the dagger in Joab’s hand, and Joab stabbed him in the stomach... and he died.
12Amasa wallowed in his blood in the middle of the road... 3

The Wise Woman of Abel

16Then a wise woman called from the city... “We are the peaceful and faithful in Israel. You are trying to destroy a city that is a mother in Israel.” 4
“His head will be thrown to you from the wall.”
22Then the woman went to all the people with her wise advice, and they cut off the head of Sheba son of Bikri and threw it to Joab.
23Joab was over Israel’s entire army; Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Kerethites and Pelethites... 5

Translation Notes

1 No Share in David: In the scrolls, this is the "Slogan of Secession." Sheba was exploiting the "Tribal Rivalry" between the North and South that had flared up in Chapter 19. His words ("Every man to his tent") were a rejection of the Davidic covenant and a call for a return to the "Anarchy" of the Judges period.

2 By the Beard: This is a "Treacherous Kiss" in the scrolls. Joab used the appearance of "Brotherly Affection" to get close enough to kill. It mirrors his murder of Abner (Chapter 3). Joab was a "Political Surgeon" who removed anyone who threatened his command. He knew that David had replaced him with Amasa, and he simply "Deleted" the competition.

3 Wallowed in his Blood: The scrolls note the "Disturbing Public Display" of Amasa’s body. The soldiers were stopping to look, which hindered the pursuit of Sheba. Joab’s ruthlessness was "Strategically Effective" but "Morally Repulsive." It shows the "Vicious Atmosphere" that now surrounded David’s inner circle.

4 A Mother in Israel: In the scrolls, this refers to a "Metropolis" or a city of "Ancient Wisdom." The woman’s appeal to Joab was based on "Civic Identity." She saved the city by sacrificing the one "Traitor" inside. This is the second "Wise Woman" (the first was in Chapter 14) who intervened to resolve a crisis through "Intellect" rather than "Violence."

5 Joab was Over the Army: This closing summary in the scrolls is a "Cynical Conclusion." Joab had successfully murdered his way back to the top. David was king, but Joab was the "Necessary Evil" that maintained the kingdom’s physical security. The "Anointed King" was once again "Weak" against the "Sons of Zeruiah."

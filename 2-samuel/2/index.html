2 Samuel 2

1In the course of time, David inquired of the LORD, “Shall I go up to one of the towns of Judah?” “Go up,” the LORD said. David asked, “Where shall I go?” “To Hebron,” the LORD answered. 1
4Then the men of Judah came to Hebron, and there they anointed David king over the tribe of Judah.
8Meanwhile, Abner son of Ner, the commander of Saul’s army, had taken Ish-Bosheth son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim. 2
9He made him king over... all Israel.
11David was king in Hebron over Judah for seven years and six months.

The Pool of Gibeon

14Then Abner said to Joab, “Let’s have some of the young men get up and fight hand to hand in front of us.”
16Then each man grabbed his opponent by the head and thrust his dagger into his opponent’s side, and they fell down together. 3
17The battle that day was very fierce, and Abner and the Israelites were defeated by David’s men.
18The three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai and Asahel. Now Asahel was as fleet-footed as a wild gazelle. 4
21Abner said to him, “Turn aside... take his armor for yourself.” But Asahel would not stop pursuing him.
23But he refused to give up; so Abner thrust the butt of his spear into Asahel’s stomach, and the spear came out through his back. There he fell and died on the spot.
26Abner called out to Joab, “Must the sword devour forever? Don’t you realize that this will end in bitterness?” 5

Translation Notes

1 To Hebron: In the scrolls, Hebron was the ancient center of the tribe of Judah and the burial place of the patriarchs. By going there first, David was grounding his kingship in the "Ancestral Roots" of the nation. He was not rushing to take the whole kingdom by force; he was waiting for the tribes to recognize him organically.

2 Ish-Bosheth: Literally in the scrolls, "Man of Shame." His original name was likely *Esh-Baal* ("Man of the Lord/Baal"), but the later scribes of the scrolls often changed "Baal" to "Bosheth" (Shame) to distance him from the pagan god. He was a "Puppet King" controlled by Abner, maintaining the "Illusion of the Dynasty" while David ruled in the south.

3 Dagger into Side: This "Tournament" in the scrolls was a "Representative Combat" that turned into a massacre. All 24 participants died simultaneously. It shows the "Fruitlessness of Violence" as a means of resolving the political split. The place was named *Helkath Hazzurim* ("Field of Daggers").

4 Fleet-footed as a Gazelle: In the scrolls, Asahel’s "Gift" became his "Snare." He was so fast that he outran his own "Common Sense." Abner, a seasoned veteran, tried to avoid killing him to prevent a blood-feud with Joab, but Asahel’s "Youthful Ambition" led him to his death. This event created the "Vendetta" that would eventually lead to Abner’s own murder.

5 Must the Sword Devour: Abner’s plea in the scrolls is a cry for "National Reconciliation." He realized that "Brother-on-Brother" violence was only weakening the nation against the Philistines. Joab’s decision to stop the pursuit was a "Tactical Truce," but the "Bitterness" Abner warned of would haunt the kingdom for the next several chapters.

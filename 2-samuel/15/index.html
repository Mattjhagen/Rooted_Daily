2 Samuel 15

1In the course of time, Absalom provided himself with a chariot and horses and with fifty men to run ahead of him.
5Also, whenever anyone approached him to bow down before him, Absalom would reach out his hand, take hold of him and kiss him.
6Absalom behaved in this way toward all the Israelites who came to the king asking for justice, and so he stole the hearts of the people of Israel. 1
10Then Absalom sent secret messengers throughout the tribes of Israel to say, “As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpets, then say, ‘Absalom is king in Hebron.’” 2
While Absalom was offering sacrifices, he also sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David’s counselor... And so the conspiracy gained strength.
14Then David said to all his officials who were with him in Jerusalem, “Come! We must flee, or none of us will escape from Absalom. We must leave immediately...”
21But Ittai the Gittite replied to the king, “As surely as the LORD lives... wherever my lord the king may be, whether it means life or death, there will your servant be.” 3
25Then the king said to Zadok, “Take the ark of God back into the city. If I find favor in the LORD’s eyes, he will bring me back...”
“But if he says, ‘I am not pleased with you,’ then I am ready; let him do to me whatever seems good to him.” 4
30But David continued up the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went; his head was covered and he was barefoot. All the people with him covered their heads too and were weeping as they went up. 5

Translation Notes

1 Stole the Hearts (Va-ye-gan-nev): Literally in the scrolls, "Absalom kidnapped the hearts." This is a "Political Seduction." Absalom was using "Populist Rhetoric" to undermine David’s authority. He wasn't solving problems; he was simply making people feel that their problems were David’s fault. It is the archetype of the "Anti-Christ" figure—one who has the "Look" of the king but none of his "Substance."

2 In Hebron: The scrolls note the irony of Absalom launching his rebellion from the very city where David was first anointed (Chapter 2). He was trying to "Hijack David’s History." By gaining the support of Ahithophel (David’s most brilliant strategist), he made the coup look inevitable.

3 Ittai the Gittite: This is a powerful moment of "Foreign Faith" in the scrolls. Ittai was a Philistine mercenary from Gath. While David’s own son was betraying him, this "Gentile Outsider" demonstrated "Covenant Loyalty" that surpassed all of Israel. His statement mirrors Ruth’s vow to Naomi.

4 Let Him do to Me: David’s refusal to use the Ark as a "Lucky Charm" in the scrolls shows his "Spiritual Maturity." Unlike in 1 Samuel 4, David knew that the Ark did not control God. He was submitting himself entirely to "Divine Sovereignty." If God wanted him back on the throne, the Ark would be there; if not, he accepted the judgment.

5 The Mount of Olives: In the scrolls, this is the "Valley of the Shadow of Death" for David. His weeping and barefoot walk was an act of "Public Repentance." He recognized that this rebellion was part of the judgment Nathan had predicted (Chapter 12). This path—up the Mount of Olives in sorrow—prefigures the path of the "Greater Son of David" (Jesus) in Gethsemane.

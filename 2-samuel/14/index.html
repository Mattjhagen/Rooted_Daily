2 Samuel 14

1Joab son of Zeruiah knew that the king’s heart longed for Absalom.
2So Joab sent to Tekoa and brought a wise woman from there...
“They would quench my last burning coal and leave my husband neither name nor descendant on the face of the earth.” 1
“Why then have you devised a thing like this against the people of God? When the king says this, does he not convict himself, for the king does not bring back his banished son?”
“Like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be recovered, so we must die. But God does not take away life; instead, he devises ways so that a banished person may not remain estranged from him.” 2
25In all Israel there was not a man so highly praised for his handsome appearance as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him. 3
“Whenever he cut the hair of his head... it weighed two hundred shekels by the royal standard.” 4
32Absalom said to Joab... “Why have I come from Geshur? It would be better for me if I were still there! Now let me see the king’s face...”
33So Joab went to the king and told him this. Then the king summoned Absalom, and he came in and bowed down with his face to the ground before the king. And the king kissed Absalom. 5

Translation Notes

1 Last Burning Coal: In the scrolls, this is a vivid metaphor for "Hope" and "Legacy." By presenting her son’s life as the final spark of her husband’s line, she appealed to David’s own concern for the "House" God promised him in Chapter 7. She was using David’s "Covenant Language" to manipulate his "Personal Grief."

2 Water Spilled on the Ground: This is a profound "Theological Argument" in the scrolls. She was arguing that since life is fragile and death is inevitable, the king should prioritize "Restoration" over "Punishment." Her statement that "God devises ways" is a perfect description of the "Gospel of Grace," yet she was using it for a "Political End."

3 No Blemish: The scrolls use the same language for Absalom as they do for "Sacrificial Animals." He was physically "Perfect," which was a sign of his "Royal Potential." However, the scrolls also hint at the "Surface-Level" nature of his perfection. He had the "Look" of a king, but his "Heart" was already starting to rot.

4 Hair of Absalom: This detail in the scrolls is not just about vanity; it’s about "Weight" and "Burden." His hair, which was his "Glory," would eventually become his "Snare" (Chapter 18). The "Two Hundred Shekels" (about 5 lbs) is a physical manifestation of his "Pride."

5 The King Kissed Absalom: This was a "Formal Pardon," but in the scrolls, it was an "Incomplete Reconciliation." David allowed him back into the city, but for two years he wouldn't see him. This "Half-Forgiveness" created a "Festering Resentment" in Absalom. The "Kiss" was too late to heal the breach, and it only gave Absalom the "Access" he needed to plan his coup.

Translation Notes

1 Light of Morning: In the scrolls, this is the "Ideal of Kingship." David is describing a leader whose "Righteousness" brings life and growth to the people, just as the sun brings life to the grass. It is a vision of "Government as a Blessing" rather than a "Burden." This "Morning Light" ultimately points to the "Dayspring from on High" (Luke 1).

2 Everlasting Covenant (Berit Olam): This is David’s "Final Confidence" in the scrolls. Even though his family was a mess and his personal failures were many, he rested in the "Covenant of God" which was "Arranged and Secured." It shows that David’s hope was not in his own "Consistency" but in God’s "Faithfulness."

3 Hand Froze to the Sword: In the scrolls, this is a vivid picture of "Total Commitment." Eleazar had fought so long and so hard that his hand became "One" with the weapon. It signifies the "Synergy" between the warrior and the "Sword of the LORD." The victory was from God, but it required the "Frozen Grip" of the man.

4 Poured it out: This was a "Libation Offering" in the scrolls. David recognized that the water, won at such risk, was too "Sacred" for a human to consume. By pouring it out, he was "Consecrating" the bravery of his men to God. It shows David’s deep "Respect" for his warriors; he didn't treat them as "Expendable Resources."

5 Killed a Lion on a Snowy Day: The scrolls emphasize the "Adversity" of this feat. Lions are dangerous enough, but a "Snowy Day" implies slippery ground and poor visibility. Benaiah was a man who "Sought Out the Fight" rather than avoiding it. He would later become the commander of Solomon’s army.

6 Uriah the Hittite: The scrolls end the list of heroes with this name as a "Stinging Rebuke." Uriah was one of David’s most loyal and elite warriors, yet David had betrayed him. The inclusion of his name in the "Honor Roll" ensures that David’s sin—and Uriah’s virtue—will never be forgotten. It is a "Silent Indictment" that hangs over the end of David’s reign.

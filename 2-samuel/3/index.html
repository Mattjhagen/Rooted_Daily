2 Samuel 3

1The war between the house of Saul and the house of David lasted a long time. David grew stronger and stronger, while the house of Saul grew weaker and weaker. 1
7Now Saul had had a concubine named Rizpah... and Ish-Bosheth said to Abner, “Why did you sleep with my father’s concubine?” 2
8Abner was very angry... “Am I a dog’s head—on Judah’s side? All this time I have been loyal to the house of your father Saul... and now you accuse me of a misdemeanor!”
10“I will transfer the kingdom from the house of Saul and establish David’s throne over Israel and Judah...”
12Then Abner sent messengers on his behalf to say to David, “Whose land is it? Make a league with me, and I will help you bring all Israel over to you.”
13“Good,” said David. “But I require one thing of you: Do not come into my presence unless you bring Michal daughter of Saul...” 3

The Assassination of Abner

27Now when Abner returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside into an inner chamber, as if to speak with him privately, and there he stabbed him in the stomach. So he died because of the blood of Joab’s brother Asahel. 4
31Then David said to Joab and all the people with him, “Tear your clothes and put on sackcloth and walk in mourning in front of Abner.” King David himself walked behind the bier.
38Then the king said to his men, “Do you not realize that a prince and a great man has fallen in Israel today?”
39“And today, though I am the anointed king, I am weak, and these sons of Zeruiah are too strong for me. May the LORD repay the evildoer according to his evil deeds!” 5

Translation Notes

1 Stronger and Weaker (Holekh ve-Chazeq): This phrase in the scrolls describes a "Gradual Shift of Power." It wasn't a single battle that won the day, but a "Momentum of the Spirit." David’s house was built on the "Promise of God," while Saul’s house was built on the "Legacy of the Flesh." One was living, the other was dying.

2 Sleeping with a Concubine: In the scrolls, this was a "Political Move," not just a moral scandal. In the ancient Near East, taking the concubine of a previous king was a public "Claim to the Throne." Abner was essentially saying he was the real power in the north. Ish-Bosheth’s challenge to him was the final straw that broke their alliance.

3 Reclaiming Michal: David’s demand in the scrolls was a "Legal and Strategic Necessity." By taking back Saul’s daughter, he was reinforcing his "Family Claim" to Saul’s throne. It was also an act of personal "Restoration" of what had been stolen from him. It showed the north that David was still Saul’s legitimate son-in-law.

4 Joab’s Private Murder: In the scrolls, Joab’s act was a "Breach of International Law." Abner was there under a "Treaty of Peace." Joab’s motive was "Personal Revenge" for his brother Asahel (Chapter 2), but his action threatened the entire "National Unification." Joab represents the "Ruthless Pragmatism" that David often had to struggle to control.

5 I am Weak (Anokhi Rakh): This is a startling confession from the king in the scrolls. Even though David was the "Anointed," he found himself technically "Powerless" to punish his own general, Joab. The "Sons of Zeruiah" (Joab and Abishai) were too vital to his military to be discarded, yet their "Spirit of Violence" was contrary to David’s heart. It shows the "Administrative Agony" of David’s early reign.

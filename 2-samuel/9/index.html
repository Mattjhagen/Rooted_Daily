2 Samuel 9

1David asked, “Is there anyone still left of the house of Saul to whom I can show kindness for Jonathan’s sake?” 1
3Ziba answered the king, “There is still a son of Jonathan; he is lame in both feet.”
4“Where is he?” the king asked. Ziba answered, “He is at the house of Makir son of Ammiel in Lo Debar.” 2
6When Mephibosheth son of Jonathan, the son of Saul, came to David, he bowed down to pay him honor. David said, “Mephibosheth!” “At your service,” he replied.
7“Don’t be afraid,” David said to him, “for I will surely show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan. I will restore to you all the land that belonged to your grandfather Saul, and you will always eat at my table.” 3
8Mephibosheth bowed down and said, “What is your servant, that you should notice a dead dog like me?” 4
11So Mephibosheth ate at David’s table like one of the king’s sons.
12Mephibosheth had a young son named Mika, and all the members of Ziba’s household were servants of Mephibosheth.
13And Mephibosheth lived in Jerusalem, because he always ate at the king’s table; he was lame in both feet. 5

Translation Notes

1 Kindness for Jonathan’s sake (Hesed Elohim): Literally in the scrolls, "The Kindness of God." David was not just showing "Human Favor," but "Covenantal Grace." This was the fulfillment of the oath he made to Jonathan in 1 Samuel 20. It shows that David’s word was a "Bondsman’s Word," enduring even after the death of his friend.

2 Lo Debar: In the scrolls, this place name literally means "No Word" or "No Pasture." It was a barren, isolated place in Gilead. Mephibosheth was living in "Spiritual and Physical Obscurity," hiding from a king he assumed would want him dead. David reached out to the "Place of Nothing" to bring him to the "Place of Everything."

3 Always eat at my Table: This is a "Status Elevation" in the scrolls. David didn't just give him money; he gave him "Fellowship." By seating the son of his rival at his table, David was demonstrating that his kingdom was built on "Reconciliation" rather than "Purging." It was a visible sign of the "Peace of God."

4 A Dead Dog: This is the language of "Total Self-Abasement" in the scrolls. Mephibosheth saw himself as not only useless (lame) but dangerous (a son of Saul). He expected "Judgment," but he received "Adoption." His question reflects the "Shock of Grace."

5 Lame in both Feet: The scrolls conclude the chapter by repeating this detail. It serves as a "Closing Irony." In the presence of the King, Mephibosheth’s "Disability" did not matter. He sat at the table where his feet were hidden, but his "Heart" was restored. It is a powerful picture of the "Restoration of the Broken" in the Kingdom of God.

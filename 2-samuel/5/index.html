2 Samuel 5

1All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, “We are your own flesh and blood.”
2“In the past... you were the one who led Israel on their military campaigns. And the LORD said to you, ‘You will shepherd my people Israel, and you will become their ruler.’” 1
3When all the elders of Israel had come to King David at Hebron, the king made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel.

The Conquest of Zion

6The king and his men marched to Jerusalem to attack the Jebusites... The Jebusites said to David, “You will not get in here; even the blind and the lame can ward you off.” 2
7Nevertheless, David captured the fortress of Zion—which is the City of David.
8On that day David said, “Anyone who conquers the Jebusites will have to use the water shaft...” 3
10And David became more and more powerful, because the LORD God Almighty was with him.

The Breakthrough at Baal Perazim

19David inquired of the LORD, “Shall I go and attack the Philistines?” The LORD answered him, “Go, for I will surely deliver the Philistines into your hands.”
20“As waters break out, the LORD has broken out against my enemies before me.” So that place was called Baal Perazim. 4
24“As soon as you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the poplar trees, move quickly, because that will mean the LORD has gone out in front of you to strike the Philistine army.” 5
25So David did as the LORD commanded him, and he struck down the Philistines all the way from Geba to Gezer.

Translation Notes

1 Shepherd My People: This is the first time in the scrolls that the kingly office is explicitly linked to the "Shepherd" metaphor. David’s background as a shepherd (Chapter 16) was not an accident; it was a "Divine Internship." God wanted a king who would care for the people as a "Flock" rather than as "Subjects."

2 The Blind and the Lame: In the scrolls, the Jebusites were using "Psychological Warfare." Jerusalem was naturally defended by steep valleys, making it nearly "Impenetrable." They mocked David by saying that even the most disabled residents could defend the walls. David’s capture of the city was a "Military Miracle" that proved his anointing.

3 The Water Shaft (Tzinnur): This is a key archaeological detail in the scrolls. Jerusalem’s water came from the Gihon Spring via a vertical tunnel. David’s "Special Ops" team used this shaft to bypass the walls and open the gates from the inside. It shows David’s "Strategic Brilliance" and his ability to find the "Weak Point" in an "Impenetrable Defense."

4 Baal Perazim: Literally in the scrolls, "The Lord of Breakthroughs." This battle established David’s "Military Sovereignty" over the Philistines. The image of "Breaking Waters" represents an unstoppable "Flash Flood" of divine power. It marked the end of the Philistine dominance that had lasted since the days of Eli.

5 Sound of Marching (Qol Tze-adah): In the scrolls, this was a "Supernatural Signal." David was instructed to wait for the "Lord’s Army" to move first. The sound in the treetops was the "Wind of the Spirit" leading the way. It teaches that victory is a "Partnership" where the king moves only after the King of Kings has gone before him.

2 Samuel 8

1In the course of time, David defeated the Philistines and subdued them, and he took Metheg Ammah from the control of the Philistines. 1
2David also defeated the Moabites... The Moabites became subject to David and brought him tribute.
4David captured a thousand of his chariots, seven thousand charioteers and twenty thousand foot soldiers. He hamstrung all but a hundred of the chariot horses. 2
6The LORD gave David victory wherever he went. 3

The Justice of David

15David reigned over all Israel, doing what was just and right for all his people. 4
16Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder;
17Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar were priests; Seraiah was secretary;
18Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Kerethites and Pelethites; and David’s sons were royal advisers. 5

Translation Notes

1 Metheg Ammah: Literally in the scrolls, "The Bridle of the Mother City." This refers to the city of Gath, the "mother city" of the Philistines. By taking the "bridle," David was taking the "control" or the "leash" off the enemy that had tormented Israel for generations. It was the final defeat of the Philistine threat.

2 Hamstrung the Horses: This seems cruel to modern readers, but in the scrolls, it was an act of "Theological Obedience." The Law (Deuteronomy 17) forbade the king from accumulating horses (the primary "Weapon of War" for empires). By disabling the horses, David was refusing to build a "Chariot-Based Empire" and was instead choosing to "Trust in the Name of the LORD" for defense.

3 Victory wherever he went (Va-yosah YHWH): Literally "The LORD saved David." The scrolls emphasize that David’s military success was not a result of his own genius, but of God’s "Preservation." David was the "Sword of the LORD." This summary covers decades of expansion that brought Israel to its greatest geographical extent.

4 Just and Right (Mishpat u-Tzedaqah): In the scrolls, these are the two pillars of a godly kingdom. *Mishpat* is the "Legal Justice" of the courts, and *Tzedaqah* is the "Social Righteousness" that cares for the vulnerable. David wasn't just a "Conqueror"; he was a "Legislator" who ensured that even the poorest in Israel had a fair hearing. This was his "True Greatness."

5 Kerethites and Pelethites: In the scrolls, these were David’s "Elite Bodyguards." They were likely of Philistine/Cretan origin (mercenaries) who were personally loyal to David rather than to a specific tribe. This allowed David to have a "Neutral Force" to maintain order in a nation still struggling with tribal divisions.

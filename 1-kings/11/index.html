1 Kings 11

1King Solomon, however, loved many foreign women besides Pharaoh’s daughter... 1
He had seven hundred wives of royal birth and three hundred concubines, and his wives led him astray.
4As Solomon grew old, his wives turned his heart after other gods, and his heart was not fully devoted to the LORD his God, as the heart of David his father had been. 2
So the LORD said to Solomon, “Since this is your attitude... I will most certainly tear the kingdom away from you and give it to one of your subordinates.” 3

The Rising Storm

14Then the LORD raised up against Solomon an adversary, Hadad the Edomite...
23And God raised up against Solomon another adversary, Rezon son of Eliada...

The Prophecy of Ahijah

26Also, Jeroboam son of Nebat... rebelled against the king.
Ahijah took hold of the new cloak he was wearing and tore it into twelve pieces. 4
“Take ten pieces for yourself, for this is what the LORD says: ‘See, I am going to tear the kingdom out of Solomon’s hand and give you ten tribes.’”
42Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years. 5
Then he rested with his ancestors... and Rehoboam his son succeeded him as king.

Translation Notes

1 Loved many Foreign Women: In the scrolls, this is the final violation of the "King’s Law" (Deuteronomy 17). Solomon had already multiplied horses and gold; now he multiplied wives. These marriages were "Political Alliances," but they brought "Spiritual Pollution" into the heart of the palace. He was trying to "Keep the Peace" by compromising the "Faith."

2 Heart was not Fully Devoted (Shalem): Literally in the scrolls, "His heart was not whole." Solomon was living a "Double Life." He was maintaining the Temple rituals while building high places for the "Abominations" of his wives. His wisdom had become "Cynical"—thinking he could balance God and idols.

3 Tear the Kingdom (Qaro’ Eqra’): This is a "Violent Metaphor" in the scrolls. The kingdom that David had united with such blood and tears was now going to be "Ripped" apart. It was a "Divine Sentence" that couldn't be revoked, though its full execution was delayed for David’s sake.

4 The Torn Cloak: This "Prophetic Action" in the scrolls is highly significant. The "New Cloak" represented the "New Kingdom." By tearing it, Ahijah was showing that the "Unity" of Israel was artificial and could be easily broken. Jeroboam, a man of "Action" and "Labor," was chosen to be the "Instrument of Judgment."

5 Forty Years: The scrolls conclude Solomon’s reign with the same timeframe as David and Saul. It was a "Complete Generation." Solomon’s reign began with the "Gold of Ophir" and ended with the "Shadow of Idols" and the "Threat of Rebellion." The "Golden Age" was over; the "Age of Division" had begun.

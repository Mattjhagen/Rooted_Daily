1 Kings 9

2The LORD appeared to him a second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon. 1
“I have heard the prayer and plea you have made before me... My eyes and my heart will always be there.”

The Conditional Covenant

“But if you or your descendants turn away from me and do not observe the commands... and go to serve other gods and worship them,”
“then I will cut off Israel from the land I have given them and will reject this temple I have consecrated for my Name.” 2
When Hiram went from Tyre to see the towns Solomon had given him, he was not pleased with them.
“What kind of towns are these, my brother?” he asked. And he called them the Land of Kabul to this day. 3

The Imperial Expansion

15Here is the account of the forced labor King Solomon conscripted to build the LORD’s temple, his own palace, the terraces, the wall of Jerusalem, and Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer. 4
26King Solomon also built a fleet of ships at Ezion Geber... on the shore of the Red Sea. 5
They sailed to Ophir and brought back four hundred and twenty talents of gold.

Translation Notes

1 Appeared a Second Time: In the scrolls, this is a "Spiritual Check-in." Twenty years had passed since the first vision at Gibeon (Chapter 3). Solomon was now at the height of his power. God was reminding him that the "Source of his Success" was still the Covenant, not his own "Architectural Achievements."

2 Reject this Temple: This is a "Shattering Warning" in the scrolls. Solomon had just spent seven years building a "Magnificent House," but God was making it clear that a "Building" cannot hold Him if the "Heart" is gone. If the people turned to idols, the Temple would become a "Heap of Rubble"—a prophecy that came true centuries later.

3 Land of Kabul: Literally in the scrolls, "As Nothing" or "Good for Nothing." This was a "Diplomatic Blunder." Solomon had given Hiram towns in the Galilee that were likely underdeveloped or agriculturally poor. Hiram, a wealthy king of a trading empire, felt insulted. It shows the "Strains of the Empire" as Solomon began to struggle to pay for his massive projects.

4 Forced Labor (Mas): The scrolls list the major cities Solomon fortified (Hazor, Megiddo, Gezer). These were "Strategic Choke-points" in the land. However, the cost of these projects was the "Liberty of the People." The "Mas" (conscription) was becoming a permanent feature of Solomon’s administration, creating the "Internal Pressure" that would lead to the kingdom’s collapse.

5 Fleet of Ships: This was a "New Frontier" for Israel in the scrolls. Traditionally a land-based people, they were now engaging in "Global Trade" with the help of the Phoenicians. The "Gold of Ophir" brought immense wealth into the treasury, making silver as common as stones in Jerusalem, but also introducing the "Temptations of Luxury."

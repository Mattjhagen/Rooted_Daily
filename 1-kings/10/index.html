1 Kings 10

1When the queen of Sheba heard about the fame of Solomon and his relationship to the name of the LORD, she came to test him with hard questions. 1
3Solomon answered all her questions; nothing was too hard for the king to explain to her.
She said to the king, “The report I heard in my own country... was true. But I did not believe these things until I came and saw with my own eyes.”
“Indeed, not even half was told me; in wisdom and wealth you have far exceeded the report I heard.” 2
“Praise be to the LORD your God... Because of the LORD’s eternal love for Israel, he has made you king to maintain justice and righteousness.”

The Golden Empire

14The weight of the gold that Solomon received yearly was six hundred and sixty-six talents... 3
18Then the king made a great throne covered with ivory and overlaid with pure gold. 4
“All King Solomon’s drinking vessels were gold... Nothing was made of silver, because silver was considered of little value in Solomon’s days.”
26Solomon accumulated chariots and horses; he had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horses. 5

Translation Notes

1 Hard Questions (Chidot): Literally in the scrolls, "Riddles" or "Puzzles." The Queen of Sheba was not just looking for facts, but for "Metaphysical Wisdom." She was testing whether Solomon’s mind could handle the "Complexity of Reality." His ability to answer showed that his wisdom was "Divine" rather than just "Educational."

2 Not even Half: This is a key phrase in the scrolls. It indicates that the "Reality" of God’s blessing always exceeds the "Reputation." The Queen’s "Overwhelmed Spirit" (Chapter 10:5) was the reaction of a pagan ruler seeing the "Physical Manifestation of Spiritual Truth."

3 Six Hundred Sixty-Six Talents: The scrolls record this specific number (about 23 tons of gold). While it signified "Ultimate Wealth," later biblical writers would associate this number with the "Limit of Human Power" and the "Spirit of the Beast." It hints that Solomon’s wealth was approaching a "Dangerous Threshold" where it might eclipse his "Dependence on God."

4 Throne of Ivory: The throne with its six steps and twelve lions was a "Masterpiece of Political Symbolism" in the scrolls. It represented the "Order" and "Sovereignty" of the kingdom. It was meant to be the most impressive seat of judgment in the known world.

5 Chariots and Horses: The scrolls conclude the description of Solomon’s wealth by mentioning his "Military Accumulation." The Law (Deuteronomy 17) explicitly forbade the king from multiplying horses from Egypt. Solomon was now "Technically Disobedient," placing his "National Security" in chariots rather than in the "Name of the LORD." This sets the stage for the tragic fall in Chapter 11.

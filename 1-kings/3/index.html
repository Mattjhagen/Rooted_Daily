1 Kings 3

1Solomon made an alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt and married his daughter. 1
5At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon during the night in a dream, and God said, “Ask for whatever you want me to give you.”
“Give your servant a discerning heart to govern your people and to distinguish between right and wrong. For who is able to govern this great people of yours?” 2
“I will do what you have asked. I will give you a wise and discerning heart, so that there will never have been anyone like you, nor will there ever be.”

The Wisdom of the Sword

16Now two prostitutes came to the king and stood before him...
24Then the king said, “Bring me a sword.” So they brought a sword for the king.
“Cut the living child in two and give half to one and half to the other.” 3
The woman whose son was alive was filled with compassion for her son... “Please, my lord, give her the living baby! Don’t kill him!” 4
When all Israel heard the verdict the king had given, they held the king in awe, because they saw that he had wisdom from God to administer justice. 5

Translation Notes

1 Alliance with Pharaoh: In the scrolls, this is the first hint of "Compromise." While it showed Solomon’s international prestige, the Law (Deuteronomy 17) warned against going back to Egypt or multiplying wives. Solomon was trying to secure the kingdom through "Diplomacy" alongside "Devotion."

2 Discerning Heart (Lev Shomea): Literally in the scrolls, "A Hearing Heart." Solomon wasn't asking for high IQ or technical knowledge; he was asking for the ability to "Listen" to the Spirit and to the people. He saw himself as a "Little Child" (*Na’ar Qaton*) who was overwhelmed by the responsibility of the throne.

3 Cut the Child in Two: This was a "Psychological Test" in the scrolls. Solomon wasn't planning to kill the baby; he was using the "Threat of Death" to reveal the "Truth of Love." He knew that the real mother would prioritize the child’s "Life" over her own "Possession." It shows that wisdom is the ability to see through "Lies" into the "Motive of the Heart."

4 Filled with Compassion (Nikmeru Rachameha): Literally in the scrolls, "Her womb grew warm." The real mother’s reaction was "Visceral." The other woman’s reaction ("Cut him in two!") revealed the "Spite" of one who had already lost everything and wanted others to lose as well. Wisdom identified "Nurture" over "Neglect."

5 Wisdom from God (Chokmat Elohim): The scrolls emphasize that this was not "Human Talent." The people saw that Solomon’s justice had a "Supernatural Dimension." This awe was the "Foundation of Solomon’s Authority," ensuring that the nation was united not just by fear of the sword, but by respect for the truth.

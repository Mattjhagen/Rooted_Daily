1 Kings 5

1When Hiram king of Tyre heard that Solomon had been anointed king to succeed his father David, he sent his envoys to Solomon... 1
4“But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side, and there is no adversary or evil occurrence.”
“I intend, therefore, to build a temple for the Name of the LORD my God...”
“Give orders that cedars of Lebanon be cut for me... for you know that we have no one so skilled in felling timber as the Sidonians.” 2
Hiram replied... “I will do all you desire in providing the cedar and juniper logs.”

The Great Mobilization

13King Solomon conscripted laborers from all Israel—thirty thousand men. 3
15Solomon had seventy thousand carriers and eighty thousand stonecutters in the hills.
17At the king’s command they quarried large blocks of high-grade stone to provide a foundation of dressed stone for the temple. 4
12The LORD gave Solomon wisdom, just as he had promised him. There were peaceful relations between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a treaty. 5

Translation Notes

1 Hiram of Tyre: In the scrolls, Hiram is a "Covenant-Friendly Foreigner." He had been a close ally of David, and his continued support for Solomon was a sign of "International Recognition." The Phoenicians (Tyrians) were the master builders and sailors of the ancient world; their collaboration was essential for the "Quality" of the Temple.

2 Skilled Sidonians: The scrolls acknowledge that while Israel had the "Vision," they lacked the "Technical Skill" for high-end carpentry. By hiring the Sidonians, Solomon was "Incorporating the Best of the Nations" into the worship of Yahweh. It prefigures the "Global Scope" of the Temple as a house of prayer for all nations.

3 Conscripted Laborers (Mas): This is a heavy word in the scrolls. It refers to a "Labor Tax." While the people were "Happy" (Chapter 4), the reality of these massive building projects required a "Forced Commitment" of time. This "Mas" would eventually become the flashpoint of rebellion under Solomon’s son.

4 Foundation of Dressed Stone: In the scrolls, the "Preparation" was as important as the "Building." The stones were quarried and "Dressed" (cut to size) in the hills so that when they arrived at the site, they fit together perfectly. It shows a "High Level of Engineering" and a desire for "Silent Reverence" at the Temple site (as noted in Chapter 6).

5 Treaty (Berit): Literally in the scrolls, "A Covenant." This was more than a trade deal; it was a "Peace Treaty" that secured Israel’s northern border. Solomon used his "Wisdom" to create a "Mutual Benefit" that kept the region stable for decades. It was the "Diplomatic Foundation" for the "Spiritual Foundation."

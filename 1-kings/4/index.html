1 Kings 4

20The people of Judah and Israel were as numerous as the sand on the seashore; they ate, they drank and they were happy. 1
25During Solomon’s lifetime Judah and Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, lived in safety, everyone under their own vine and under their own fig tree. 2

The Royal Table

22Solomon’s daily provisions were thirty cors of the finest flour and sixty cors of meal...
ten fattened cattle, twenty range cattle and a hundred sheep and goats, besides deer, gazelles, roebucks and choice fowl. 3

The Measureless Mind

29God gave Solomon wisdom and very great insight, and a breadth of understanding as measureless as the sand on the seashore. 4
32He spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs numbered a thousand and five.
“He spoke about plant life, from the cedar of Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of walls. He also spoke about animals and birds, reptiles and fish.” 5
From all nations people came to listen to Solomon’s wisdom, sent by all the kings of the world, who had heard of his wisdom.

Translation Notes

1 Sand on the Seashore: This is the fulfillment of the "Abrahamic Promise" (Genesis 22) in the scrolls. The nation had reached the point where they could no longer be counted. The phrase "ate, drank, and were happy" indicates a period of "Economic Stability" that was rare in the ancient world.

2 Vine and Fig Tree: This is a powerful "Utopian Idiom" in the scrolls. It describes a society where every family has "Property Rights" and "Peace." No one was being conscripted into wars or fleeing from invaders. It was the "Shalom" that the Davidic kingdom was intended to produce.

3 The Daily Provisions: The scrolls include these "Inventory Details" to show the "Scale of the Administration." The feeding of the royal household required a massive "Logistics Network" involving the twelve districts. While it showed prosperity, it also hinted at the "Burden of the Crown" that would later lead to the kingdom’s split.

4 Measureless Understanding (Rochav Lev): Literally in the scrolls, "Breadth of Heart." It implies an "Infinite Capacity" to absorb and categorize information. Solomon’s wisdom was not just "Legal," but "Encyclopedic." He was the "First Scientist" of Israel, seeking to understand the "Order of Creation."

5 Cedar to Hyssop: This "Biological Catalog" in the scrolls shows the "Range of Wisdom." He understood the greatest (Cedar) and the smallest (Hyssop). He was studying the "Book of Nature" as an extension of the "Book of the Law." It shows that "True Wisdom" sees the hand of God in the "Botany" as much as in the "Bible."

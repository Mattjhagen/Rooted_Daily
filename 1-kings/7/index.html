1 Kings 7

1It took Solomon thirteen years, however, to complete the construction of his own palace. 1
13King Solomon sent to Tyre and brought Huram... 2
“He was filled with wisdom and understanding and skill in all kinds of bronze work. He came to King Solomon and did all the work assigned to him.”

The Pillars of the Portal

15He cast two bronze pillars, each eighteen cubits high...
He erected the pillars at the portico of the temple. The pillar to the south he named Jachin and the one to the north Boaz. 3

The Molten Sea

23He made the Sea of cast metal, circular in shape, measuring ten cubits from rim to rim... It was five cubits high. 4
25The Sea stood on twelve bulls, three facing north, three facing west, three facing south and three facing east.
51When all the work King Solomon had done for the temple of the LORD was finished, he brought in the things his father David had dedicated... and placed them in the treasuries. 5

Translation Notes

1 Thirteen Years: The scrolls note a "Quiet Contrast" here. Solomon spent seven years on God’s house (Chapter 6) but thirteen years on his own. While this showed his "Royal Grandeur," it also hinted at a shift in "Priorities." His palace complex was much larger than the Temple, reflecting the "Expanding State."

2 Huram (Hiram): This master craftsman in the scrolls was of mixed heritage (Israelite mother, Tyrian father). He represents the "Union of Skill and Spirit." He was "Filled with Wisdom" (the same word used for Solomon), showing that "Craftsmanship" is a spiritual gift as much as "Leadership."

3 Jachin and Boaz: These names in the scrolls are a "Theological Declaration." *Jachin* means "He establishes" and *Boaz* means "In Him is strength." Everyone entering the Temple had to walk between these two concepts: that the kingdom was "Established" by God and was "Supported" by His strength. They were silent sentinels of the covenant.

4 The Sea: This massive basin in the scrolls held over 10,000 gallons of water. It was used for "Priestly Purification." Standing on twelve bulls (representing the twelve tribes), it signified that the "Purity of the Priests" was supported by the "Unity of the Nation." The sheer weight of the bronze was a testament to the "Heavy Glory" of God.

5 Things David Dedicated: The scrolls conclude the construction by linking it back to the "Wars of David." The silver and gold won in battle (2 Samuel 8) were now used for "Peaceful Worship." It shows that David’s "Suffering" and "Conquests" were the "Financial Foundation" for Solomon’s "Prosperity." One man planted, the other man watered, but God gave the increase.

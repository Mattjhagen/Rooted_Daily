1 Kings 18

17When Ahab saw Elijah, he said to him, “Is that you, you troubler of Israel?” 1
“I have not made trouble for Israel,” Elijah replied. “But you and your father’s family have. You have abandoned the LORD’s commands and have followed the Baals.”
“How long will you waver between two opinions? If the LORD is God, follow him; but if Baal is God, follow him.” 2
27At noon Elijah began to taunt them. “Shout louder!” he said. “Surely he is a god! Perhaps he is deep in thought, or busy, or traveling. Maybe he is sleeping and must be awakened.” 3

The Fire of Yahweh

“Answer me, LORD, answer me, so these people will know that you, LORD, are God, and that you are turning their hearts back again.”
Then the fire of the LORD fell and burned up the sacrifice, the wood, the stones and the soil, and also licked up the water in the trench. 4
“The LORD—he is God! The LORD—he is God!”
“Go, eat and drink, for there is the sound of a heavy rain.” 5
“The sky grew black with clouds, the wind rose, a heavy rain started falling...”

Translation Notes

1 Troubler of Israel (Okher Yisrael): In the scrolls, Ahab was accusing Elijah of causing the drought. He was using Elijah as a "Scapegoat" for the national suffering. Elijah’s response correctly identifies that the "Trouble" was not the word of the prophet, but the "Disobedience" of the King. Real trouble starts when the covenant is broken.

2 Waver between two Opinions: Literally in the scrolls, "Limping on two branches." It’s the image of someone trying to walk on two different paths at once. The people were trying to "Synchronize" Yahweh and Baal. Elijah was forcing a "Crisis of Choice"—you cannot have two centers of gravity.

3 Elijah’s Taunt: In the scrolls, this is a "Sarcastic Deconstruction" of idolatry. If a god can be "Busy" (*Sig*) or "Sleeping," he is no god at all. Elijah was exposing the "Human Projections" of the Baal cult. It was a "Comedy of Errors" intended to show that the prophets were screaming at a "Silent Void."

4 Licked up the Water: This detail in the scrolls is crucial. Elijah had made the miracle "Humanly Impossible" by drenching the altar with 12 jars of water. The fire did not just burn the wood; it "Consumed the Inorganic" (stones and soil). It was an "Inverse Sacrifice"—God was consuming the very evidence of human effort to show His total "Sovereignty."

5 Sound of Heavy Rain (Hammon ha-Geshem): Literally "The Roar of the Rain." While the sky was still clear, Elijah "Heard" the answer by faith. He was "Rooted" in the promise of God (Verse 1). The small cloud "the size of a man’s hand" was enough for the prophet to know that the "Storm of Grace" was breaking the "Drought of Judgment."

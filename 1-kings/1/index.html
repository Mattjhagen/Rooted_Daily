1 Kings 1

1King David was now very old, and though they covered him with blankets, he could not keep warm. 1
3Then they searched throughout Israel for a beautiful young woman and found Abishag, a Shunammite... She took care of the king and waited on him, but the king had no sexual relations with her.
5Now Adonijah... put himself forward and said, “I will be king.” So he got chariots and horses ready, with fifty men to run ahead of him. 2
His father had never interfered with him by asking, “Why do you behave as you do?”
11Nathan asked Bathsheba... “Have you not heard that Adonijah... has become king, and our lord David does not know it?”
17She said to him, “My lord, you yourself swore to me by the LORD your God: ‘Solomon your son shall be king after me...’”

The Anointing of the Heir

“Set Solomon my son on my own mule and take him down to Gihon.” 3
39Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the sacred tent and anointed Solomon. Then they sounded the trumpet and all the people shouted, “Long live King Solomon!”
50But Adonijah, in fear of Solomon, went and took hold of the horns of the altar. 4
52Solomon replied, “If he shows himself to be a worthy man, not a hair of his head will fall to the ground; but if evil is found in him, he will die.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Could not keep warm: In the scrolls, this is a "Symbol of Fading Power." David’s physical "Coldness" mirrored his diminishing "Executive Control." The search for Abishag was not just for comfort, but a "Medical Practice" of the time. The detail that he had no relations with her is crucial to the upcoming conflict in Chapter 2.

2 Adonijah’s Coup: Adonijah was repeating Absalom’s "Playbook" (2 Samuel 15)—the chariots, the runners, the "Self-Anointing." The scrolls note David’s "Parental Failure" here; his lack of discipline ("never interfered") created the environment where Adonijah felt he could seize the throne with impunity.

3 The King’s Mule: In the scrolls, riding the "King’s own Mule" was the ultimate "Public Declaration" of Solomon’s legitimacy. It was a visible sign to all of Jerusalem that David had personally chosen his successor. This "Mule Ceremony" would later be echoed in the "Triumphal Entry" of the greater King into Jerusalem.

4 Horns of the Altar: This was the ancient equivalent of "Sanctuary" in the scrolls. By grabbing the horns of the bronze altar, Adonijah was appealing to "Divine Mercy." It was a "Holy Place" where even a criminal could find temporary protection from immediate execution.

5 If he shows himself Worthy (Ben Chayil): Solomon’s first recorded words in the scrolls demonstrate a "Measured Wisdom." He offered Adonijah a "Conditional Pardon." He wasn't seeking "Vengeance," but "Stability." However, he was also making it clear that the "Age of Impunity" was over; Adonijah’s future depended entirely on his own "Character."

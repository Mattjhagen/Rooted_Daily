1 Kings 2

1When the time drew near for David to die, he gave a charge to Solomon his son.
“I am about to go the way of all the earth. So be strong, act like a man.” 1
“Observe what the LORD your God requires: Walk in obedience to him, and keep his decrees and commands... so that you may prosper in all you do.”
10Then David rested with his ancestors and was buried in the City of David.
11He had reigned forty years over Israel.

The Consolidation of the Throne

25So King Solomon gave orders to Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he struck down Adonijah and he died. 2
34So Benaiah son of Jehoiada went up and struck down Joab and killed him... 3
46Then the king gave the order to Benaiah... and he struck down Shimei and he died. 4
The kingdom was now firmly established in Solomon’s hands. 5

Translation Notes

1 Act like a Man (Ve-hayita le-Ish): In the scrolls, this was not just about gender, but about "Moral Maturity" and "Decisive Leadership." David was telling Solomon that being king required a "Steely Resolve" that went beyond the "Wisdom" he already possessed. It was a call to "Take the Reins" of the covenant.

2 Death of Adonijah: In the scrolls, Adonijah’s request for Abishag (the king’s "Warmer") was a "Thinly Veiled Power Play." In the ancient Near East, taking the king’s concubine was a public "Claim to the Throne." Solomon recognized the "Treason" immediately and acted with "Surgical Precision" to remove the threat.

3 Gray Head to the Grave: David’s instruction regarding Joab in the scrolls was a matter of "Unfinished Justice." Joab had murdered two innocent generals in cold blood (Abner and Amasa). David had been too "Weak" to punish him (2 Samuel 3), but he didn't want the "Blood-Guilt" to haunt Solomon’s reign. Joab’s death at the altar was the final payment for his "Spirit of Violence."

4 Shimei’s Fate: Solomon’s handling of Shimei in the scrolls is a masterpiece of "Staged Justice." He gave Shimei a chance for life through "Obedience" (staying in Jerusalem). When Shimei broke the "House Arrest" for the sake of two runaway slaves, he proved that his own "Self-Interest" was still greater than his "Oath to the King."

5 Firmly Established: This closing sentence in the scrolls marks the end of the "Davidic Wars" and the beginning of the "Pax Solomonica." The rivals were gone, the priests and generals were loyal, and the "Covenant of David" was now in the hands of the "Prince of Peace."

Translation Notes

1 Place in the Inner Sanctuary: In the scrolls, the Ark was finally "Resting." For centuries it had been a "Mobile Presence" (2 Samuel 7). Its placement under the wings of the giant cherubim (Chapter 6) signified that the "Covenant of God" was now the protected center of the nation.

2 The Cloud (Shekinah): In the scrolls, this was the "Divine Acceptance." Just as the cloud filled the Tabernacle (Exodus 40), it now filled the Temple. The "Glory" (*Kavod*) was so heavy and tangible that the priests had to stop their work. It proved that God was not "Locked" in the box, but "Present" in the building.

3 Highest Heaven cannot Contain You: This is the "Theological Core" of the scrolls. Solomon was acknowledging that the Temple was not a "Cage" for God. God is "Infinite" and "Omnipresent." The Temple was merely a "Point of Contact"—a place where the "Name" of God resided so that humans could focus their worship.

4 The Foreigner: In the scrolls, this is a "Radical Inclusion." Solomon was praying that the Temple would be a "Beacon" for the entire world. He wanted the "Testimony of Israel" to result in the "Worship of the Nations." It shows that the Davidic kingdom was intended to have a "Global Mission."

5 Never Leave Us: Solomon’s blessing in the scrolls is a "Covenant Prayer." He understood that the Temple itself could not save them; only the "Presence of God" could. The building was a "Symbol," but the "Relationship" was the substance. This prayer would haunt the nation in later centuries when the Temple remained but the Presence departed.

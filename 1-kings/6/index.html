1 Kings 6

1In the four hundred and eightieth year after the Israelites came out of Egypt... in the fourth year of Solomon’s reign, he began to build the temple of the LORD. 1
7In building the temple, only blocks dressed at the quarry were used, and no hammer, chisel or any other iron tool was heard at the temple site while it was being built. 2

The Golden Sanctuary

20The inner sanctuary was twenty cubits long, twenty wide and twenty high. He overlaid the inside with pure gold. 3
22He overlaid the whole interior with gold, until the entire temple was finished.
23For the inner sanctuary he made a pair of cherubim out of olive wood, each ten cubits high. 4
“Their wings touched each other in the middle of the room... and he overlaid the cherubim with gold.”
29On the walls all around the temple... he carved cherubim, palm trees and open flowers. 5
38The temple was finished in all its details according to its specifications. He had spent seven years building it.

Translation Notes

1 480th Year: In the scrolls, this is a "Chronological Anchor." It explicitly links the building of the Temple back to the Exodus. The Temple was the "Destination" of the journey that began with Moses. It was the "Settling" of the presence of God that had been "Moving" in a tent for centuries.

2 No Iron Tool Heard: This is the "Silence of the Stones" in the scrolls. Because the stones were "Dressed" at the quarry (Chapter 5), the Temple site was a place of "Reverence" and "Peace." It teaches that the "Worship of God" should be a place of order and "Internal Silence," where the "Noise of the World" is left outside.

3 Pure Gold (Zahav Sagur): Literally "Enclosed" or "Refined Gold." The scrolls describe an interior that was "Total Light." Since there were only narrow windows, the gold would reflect the "Light of the Lampstand," creating a "Supernatural Glow." It was intended to be a "Reflection of Heaven" on earth.

4 Cherubim: These were not small figurines, but "Massive Statues" in the scrolls. Their wingspan of 20 cubits (about 30 feet) filled the entire width of the Most Holy Place. They represented the "Guardians of the Presence." By placing them there, Solomon was acknowledging that God is "Enthroned between the Cherubim."

5 Palm Trees and Flowers: This "Garden Imagery" in the scrolls is highly symbolic. The Temple was designed to look like "Eden Restored." It was a place where humanity could once again walk with God in a "Paradise of Purity." Every carving was a "Promise of Restoration."

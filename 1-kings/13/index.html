1 Kings 13

1By the word of the LORD a man of God came from Judah to Bethel, as Jeroboam was standing by the altar to make an offering.
“O altar, altar! This is what the LORD says: ‘A son named Josiah will be born to the house of David. On you he will sacrifice the priests of the high places...’” 1
4When King Jeroboam heard what the man of God cried out... he stretched out his hand from the altar and said, “Seize him!” But the hand he stretched out toward the man shriveled up. 2

The Deception of the Old Prophet

The old prophet answered, “I too am a prophet, as you are. And an angel said to me by the word of the LORD: ‘Bring him back with you to your house...’” 3
(But he was lying to him.)

The Judgment of the Lion

24As he went on his way, a lion met him on the road and killed him, and his body was left lying on the road, with both the donkey and the lion standing beside it. 4
33Even after this, Jeroboam did not change his evil ways... 5

Translation Notes

1 A Son named Josiah: In the scrolls, this is a "Specific and Long-Range Prophecy." Josiah wouldn't be born for another 300 years. It was a declaration that the "Davidic Covenant" still held authority over the North, even if the North had rejected the King. The altar of Jeroboam was "Sentenced" from the moment it was built.

2 Hand Shriveled Up: This was a "Physical Parable" in the scrolls. Jeroboam’s power was "Withered" when he tried to silence the Word of God. His subsequent healing was a "Mercy" intended to lead to repentance, but it only resulted in a temporary fear.

3 But he was Lying: The scrolls present a "Difficult Moral Lesson" here. Why did the old prophet lie? Perhaps out of "Professional Jealousy" or a desire for "Association" with a true man of God. The tragedy is that the man of God allowed a "Human Voice" to override a "Divine Command." In the prophetic office, "Second-hand Revelation" is no excuse for disobedience.

4 Lion and the Donkey: This scene in the scrolls is "Supernatural." Usually, a lion would eat the body or the donkey. By standing still, the lion proved it was an "Executioner for God" rather than a "Hungry Predator." It was a "Sign" that the prophecy against the man of God (for his disobedience) was as certain as the prophecy against the altar.

5 Did not Change: This is the "Cynicism of Power" in the scrolls. Jeroboam had seen the altar split, his hand shrivel, and a man of God killed by a lion—and he still continued his "Religious Strategy." He had become "Hardened" by his political position, choosing "Stability" over "Spirituality."

1 Kings 15

3Abijah committed all the sins his father had done... his heart was not fully devoted to the LORD his God.
4Nevertheless, for David’s sake the LORD his God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem by raising up a son to succeed him... 1

The Reformation of Asa

Asa did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, as his father David had done. 2
“He expelled the shrine prostitutes from the land and got rid of all the idols his ancestors had made.”
“He even deposed his grandmother Maakah from her position as queen mother, because she had made a repulsive Asherah pole.” 3

The Bloody North

27Baasha son of Ahijah... conspired against Nadab and struck him down...
“As soon as he began to reign, he killed Jeroboam’s whole family. He did not leave Jeroboam anyone that breathed.” 4
Baasha did evil in the eyes of the LORD, walking in the ways of Jeroboam and in his sin. 5

Translation Notes

1 A Lamp in Jerusalem (Nir): In the scrolls, this is a "Covenant Idiom." Even though the kings of Judah were failing, God maintained the "Light" of the Davidic line. Jerusalem remained the "Center of Hope" because of the promise, not because of the merit of the current ruler.

2 As David had Done: This is the "Highest Praise" in the scrolls. After two generations of decline (Rehoboam, Abijah), Asa "Rooted" himself back in the lifestyle of his great-grandfather. He is the first "Good King" mentioned in the book of Kings.

3 Repulsive Asherah (Miphletzet): Literally in the scrolls, "A Horrible Thing" or "A Shuddering Object." Maakah (the queen mother) had used her power to embed Canaanite fertility cults in the palace. Asa’s "Moral Courage" to remove his own grandmother from power showed that his loyalty to God was greater than his loyalty to "Family Tradition."

4 Killed Jeroboam’s whole Family: In the scrolls, this was the "Brutal Fulfillment" of Ahijah’s prophecy (Chapter 14). Baasha was a "Usurper" who thought he was securing his own throne, but he was actually the "Scalpel of Judgment" removing a "Cursed Lineage."

5 Ways of Jeroboam: This becomes the "Standard of Failure" in the scrolls. Every northern king is measured against Jeroboam’s "Altar of Convenience." Baasha had destroyed Jeroboam’s family, but he kept Jeroboam’s "Sin." He wanted the "Throne" but not the "Truth."

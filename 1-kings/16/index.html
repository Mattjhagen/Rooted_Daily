1 Kings 16

8Elah son of Baasha became king of Israel...
9Zimri, one of his officials... plotted against him. Elah was in Tirzah at the time, getting drunk in the home of Arza. 1
Zimri came in, struck him down and killed him... and he succeeded him as king.

The Dynasty of Omri

24He bought the hill of Samaria from Shemer for two talents of silver and built a city there... 2
“But Omri did evil in the eyes of the LORD and sinned more than all those before him.”

The Rise of Ahab

Ahab son of Omri did more evil in the eyes of the LORD than any of those before him. 3
“He married Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians, and began to serve Baal and worship him.” 4
34In Ahab’s time, Hiel of Bethel rebuilt Jericho. He laid its foundations at the cost of his firstborn son Abiram... 5

Translation Notes

1 Getting Drunk in the Home: In the scrolls, this is the "Ugliness of the Northern Throne." Elah was a "Weak King" who was assassinated while incapacitated by wine. It shows the "Moral Vacuum" at the top of the nation. The Northern Kingdom had become a "Game of Thrones" where power was seized by whoever was the most ruthless at the moment.

2 Hill of Samaria: This was a "Strategic Military Decision" in the scrolls. Omri was a "Pragmatic General" who understood that Tirzah was too vulnerable (Zimri had just burned the palace there). By building Samaria on a high, isolated hill, he gave the Northern Kingdom a "Fortress Capital" that would stand for centuries. It was a "Worldly Success" built on a "Spiritual Failure."

3 More Evil than Any: This is a "Staggering Indictment" in the scrolls. After a line of increasingly wicked kings, Ahab "Broke the Scale." His sin was not just the "Convenience" of Jeroboam, but the "Active Promotion" of foreign, demonic deities.

4 Jezebel (Izebel): Her name in the scrolls carries a "Dark Weight." She was not just a queen, but a "Missionary for Baal." Her marriage to Ahab was the "Unholy Alliance" that brought the Sidonian cult of Baal (the storm god) into the heart of Israel, attempting to replace Yahweh entirely. This sets the stage for the "Prophetic Resistance" of Elijah.

5 Cost of his Firstborn: The rebuilding of Jericho in the scrolls is a "Test of the Word." 500 years earlier, Joshua had cursed anyone who rebuilt the city (Joshua 6). Hiel’s attempt to ignore the curse resulted in the "Sacrifice of his Sons." It was a "Graphic Illustration" that even after centuries, the "Word of the LORD" remains active and dangerous to those who defy it.

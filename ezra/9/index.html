Ezra 9

The Holy Seed Compromised

“They have taken some of their daughters as wives... and have mingled the holy seed with the peoples around them.” 1
“When I heard this, I tore my tunic and cloak, pulled hair from my head and beard and sat down appalled.” 2
“Everyone who trembled at the words of the God of Israel gathered around me.”

The Prayer of Confession

“I am too ashamed and disgraced, my God, to lift up my face to you, because our sins are higher than our heads.” 3
“But now, for a brief moment, the LORD our God has been gracious in leaving us a remnant and giving us a firm stake in his holy place.” 4
“Here we are before you in our guilt, though because of it not one of us can stand in your presence.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Holy Seed (Zera ha-Qodesh): In the scrolls, this is the "Biological and Spiritual Integrity" of the nation. Intermarriage wasn't about "Racism," but about "Rootedness." By marrying into idolatrous cultures, the "Holy Remnant" was "Diluting" its commitment to the Covenant. They were "Mixing the Soil" of the promise with the "Poisons" of the Baals.

2 Sat down Appalled (Meshomem): Literally in the scrolls, "Desolate" or "Stunned." Ezra was in a "State of Spiritual Shock." He realized that the very sin that had caused the Exile (idolatry through unholy alliances) was being repeated by the first generation of the return. He was "Mourning the Death of the Future."

3 Higher than our Heads (Rabu le-Ma’lah Rosh): This is a "Stunning Image" in the scrolls. Ezra is picturing "Sin" as a "Flood" that has risen above the level of their breathing. He isn't just confessing "Personal Sin," but "Corporate Sin." He is "Rooting" himself in the "Guilt" of his people.

4 A Firm Stake (Yated): Literally in the scrolls, "A Tent Peg." The return was a "Small and Fragile" thing. A single tent peg in a holy place. Ezra was arguing that they shouldn't "Pull up the Peg" by their unfaithfulness. It signifies the "Vulnerability" of the restoration.

5 Not one of us can Stand: The scrolls conclude this prayer with "Absolute Vulnerability." Ezra doesn't "Offer a Solution" yet; he just "Offers the Truth." He was "Rooting" the nation’s only hope in the "Sovereign Mercy" of a God whose "Presence" they had just offended. It is the "Nakedness of True Confession."

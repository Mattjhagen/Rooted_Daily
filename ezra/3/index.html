Ezra 3

The Altar of Sacrifice

Despite their fear of the peoples around them, they built the altar on its foundation and sacrificed burnt offerings on it to the LORD. 1

The Foundation Stone

When the builders laid the foundation of the temple of the LORD... the priests and Levites took their places to praise the LORD. 2
“He is good; his love to Israel endures forever.”

Shouting and Weeping

Many of the older priests and Levites and family heads, who had seen the former temple, wept aloud when they saw this foundation... while many others shouted for joy. 3
“No one could distinguish the sound of the shouts of joy from the sound of weeping.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Despite their Fear (Be-Emah): Literally in the scrolls, "Because of the Terror." They were surrounded by enemies who hated the return of Judah. Instead of building a "Wall" first, they built the "Altar." It is the "Rooted" principle of "Spiritual Priority"—security is found in the "Presence," not the "Perimeter."

2 Laid the Foundation (Yissad): In the scrolls, this is the "Moment of Tangible Hope." The Temple was still just a "Outline" on the ground, but it was "Real." They were "Rooting" the future in the physical soil of Zion. The music of David (*Asaph*) was restored to the air of Jerusalem.

3 Wept Aloud (Bokhim): The scrolls record this "Heartbreaking Contrast." The older generation remembered the "Glory of Solomon." This new foundation seemed "Small" and "Poor" in comparison. Their "Tears" were for what was "Lost," while the "Shouts" were for what was "Gained."

4 No one could Distinguish: This is a "Poignant Metaphor" in the scrolls. The "Grief of the Past" and the "Joy of the Future" were "Melted" into one sound. It represents the "Complexity of Restoration." It is never a simple "Reset"; it is a "Deep-Rooted" process of "Grieving well" while "Building forward."

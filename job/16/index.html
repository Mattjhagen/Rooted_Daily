Job 16

Miserable Comforters

“I have heard many things like these; you are miserable comforters, all of you!” 1

The Archers of the Shaddai

“All was well with me, but he shattered me; he seized me by the neck and crushed me.”
“His archers surround me. Without pity, he pierces my kidneys and spills my gall on the ground.” 2

The Advocate on High

“Even now my witness is in heaven; my advocate is on high.” 3
“On behalf of a man he pleads with God as one pleads for a friend.” 4
“I have sewed sackcloth over my skin and buried my brow in the dust.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Miserable Comforters (Menachame Amal): Literally in the scrolls, "Comforters of trouble" or "Toilsome comforters." Job is "Uprooting" their claim to be helpful. Their "Speeches" are just another layer of "Affliction." They are "Pain-givers" in the guise of "Truth-tellers."

2 Pierces my Kidneys (Ye-phallach Kilyothay): The scrolls highlight the "Visceral Violence." The "Archers" represent the "Relentless Attacks" of God. "Kidneys" and "Gall" in the scrolls were seen as the "Seat of Emotion and Vitality." Job feels "Shattered" from the "Inside Out." He is "Rooting" his experience in "Divine Hostility."

3 My Witness is in Heaven (Edi ba-Shamayim): This is a "Stunning Re-rooting" of hope in the scrolls. Even while accusing God of "Violent Injustice," Job "Appeals" to a "Higher Version" or a "Witness" within the Godhead. It is the "Root" of his "Split Perspective"—the God who "Smites" him vs. the God who "Vindicates" him.

4 Pleads with God (Way-Yokhach): Literally "Argues" or "Decides." Job is longing for a "Cosmic Mediator" (*Mokiach*) from Chapter 9. He imagines an "Intercessor" who is a "Friend" (*Verse 20*). It signifies his "Deepest Spiritual Intuition" that the "Gap" between man and God must be bridged by a "Pleader."

5 Sackcloth over my Skin (Saq Thapharti): Literally in the scrolls, "I have stitched sackcloth onto my skin." Job’s "Mourning" is no longer a "Gown" he can take off; it is "Part of his Body." He has "Rooted" his "Grief" into his very "Skin." It marks the "Permanence" of his "Desolation."

Job 35

The Gaze at the Heights

“Look up at the heavens and see; gaze at the clouds so high above you.” 1
“If you are righteous, what do you give to him? What does he receive from your hand?” 2

The Maker of Songs

“But no one says, ‘Where is God my Maker, who gives songs in the night...?’” 3
“Who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth and makes us wiser than the birds in the sky?”

The Empty Plea

“He does not answer when people cry out because of the arrogance of the wicked.” 4
“Indeed, God does not listen to their empty plea; the Almighty pays no attention to it.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Look at the Clouds (Habbet Shechaqim): In the scrolls, Elihu is "Uprooting" human "Self-Importance." He uses the "Altutude" of the clouds to show how "Small" our "Actions" are compared to the "Infinite." He is "Rooting" God’s "Sovereignty" in His "Transcendence."

2 What do you Give to Him (Mah-Thitten Lo): The scrolls highlight the "Non-Obligatory" nature of God. Elihu argues that human "Virtue" is for the "Benefit of Humans" (*Verse 8*), not because God "Needs" it. He is "Uprooting" the "Transactional Theology" of the friends.

3 Songs in the Night (Zemiroth ba-Laylah): This is a "Poetic Root" in the scrolls. It refers to "Joy" in the midst of "Darkness." Elihu argues that the reason many cry out and are not heard is that they are looking for "Relief," not for the "Maker." They want the "Problem Solved" but don't want the "Relationship."

4 Arrogance (Ga’on): Literally in the scrolls, "Pride" or "Majesty." Elihu suggests that "Arrogance" is a "Barrier" that "Blocks the Response" of God. It is a "Direct Rebuke" to Job’s "Prince-like" challenge (*Chapter 31*).

5 Empty Plea (Shaw): Literally in the scrolls, "Vanity" or "Nothingness." Elihu concludes by calling Job’s "Legal Protest" a "Vain Word" (*Verse 16*). He argues that Job is "Talking without Knowledge" because he doesn't understand the "Distance" between himself and the "Almighty." It marks the final "Intellectual Scaffolding" before Elihu begins his "Description of the Storm."

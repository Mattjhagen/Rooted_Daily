Job 2

Skin for Skin

“Skin for skin!” Satan replied. “A man will give all he has for his own life. But strike his flesh and bones, and he will surely curse you.” 1

The Agony of the Ash-heap

“Satan afflicted Job with painful sores from the soles of his feet to the crown of his head.” 2
“Then Job took a potsherd and scraped himself with it as he sat among the ashes.”

The Voice of Despair

“His wife said to him, ‘Are you still maintaining your integrity? Curse God and die!’” 3
“Job replied: ‘Shall we accept good from God, and not trouble?’” 4

The Seven-Day Silence

“They sat on the ground with him for seven days and seven nights. No one said a word to him, because they saw how great his suffering was.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Skin for Skin (Or be-ad Or): In the scrolls, this is a "Mercenary Proverb." Satan argues that a man can endure "External Loss" (wealth/family) as long as his "Personal Shell" is safe. He is attacking the "Physical Root" of Job’s existence. He claims that "Pain" is the "Ultimate Solvent" of faith.

2 Painful Sores (Shechin Ra): Literally in the scrolls, "Evil Inflammations." This was likely a "Systemic Dermatological Torture." Job sitting in the "Ashes" (*Verse 8*) was the "Place of the Outcast." He was being "Uprooted" from "Human Society" as well as "Physical Comfort."

3 Curse God and Die: The scrolls highlight the "Collapse of the Partner." Job’s wife was not being "Evil," but she was "Broken." She saw his "Integrity" as "Useless" in the face of such agony. Her words were a "Temptation to Resign" from the "Root of the Covenant."

4 Accept Good... and not Trouble (Gam eth-ha-Tob Neqabbel): This is the "Radical Acceptance" of the scrolls. Job refused to "Compartmentalize" God. He realized that if God is "Sovereign," He is sovereign over the "Storm" as well as the "Sun." He was "Rooting" his faith in the "Character of God," not the "Circumstance of Man."

5 Seven Days of Silence: The scrolls conclude with this "Holy Hesitation." The friends (Eliphaz, Bildad, Zophar) began well. They didn't "Explain" the suffering; they "Entered" it. The "Silence" was the "Weight" of their sympathy. It signifies that "Great Suffering" can only be "Rooted" in "Presence," not "Propositions."

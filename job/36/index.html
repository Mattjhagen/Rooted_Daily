Job 36

The Sovereign Teacher

“God is exalted in his power. Who is a teacher like him?” 1
“God is great—beyond our understanding! The number of his years is past finding out.”

The Distilling Clouds

“He draws up the drops of water, which distill as rain to the streams.” 2
“The clouds pour down their moisture and abundant showers fall on mankind.”

The Pavilion of Thunder

“Can anyone understand how he spreads his clouds, how he thunders from his pavilion?” 3
“He fills his hands with lightning and commands it to strike its mark.” 4
“This is the way he governs the nations and provides food in abundance.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Who is a Teacher like Him (Mi Kamo-hu Moreh): In the scrolls, Elihu is "Reframing" the character of God. God is not just a "Judge" (*the friends*) or an "Adversary" (*Job*), but a "Teacher" (*Moreh*). Suffering is part of the "Divine Curriculum." He is "Rooting" God’s "Authority" in His "Instructional Intent."

2 He draws up the drops of Water (Ye-gara Nith-phe Mayim): This is a "Sophisticated Botanical/Meteorological Observation" in the scrolls. Elihu describes the "Evaporation and Condensation Cycle." He is "Rooting" God’s "Providence" in the "Precision" of the physical laws He created.

3 Thunders from His Pavilion (The-shu-oth Sukkatho): Literally in the scrolls, "The crashing noise of His booth/tabernacle." Elihu is "Visualizing" the storm as "God’s Moving House." He is "Uprooting" the idea of a "Distant God" and replacing it with a "God of the Whirlwind."

4 Fills His hands with Lightning (Al-Kappayim Kissah-Or): Literally "He covers the light/lightning on [His] palms." It is a "Stunning Anthropomorphism." God "Hand-delivers" the lightning. It signifies the "Sovereign Control" of the "Unpredictable."

5 Governs... and Provides (Yadin... Yitten Okhel): The scrolls conclude with this "Dual Function of the Storm." The same "Rain" that "Judges" (*Verse 31*) also "Nourishes." Elihu is "Rooting" God’s "Justice" and "Mercy" in the "Same Action." It marks the transition to the "Climax of the Storm" in *Chapter 37*.

Job 42

The Vision of the Eye

“I know that you can do all things; no purpose of yours can be thwarted.” 1
“My ears had heard of you but now my eyes have seen you.” 2
“Therefore I despise myself and repent in dust and ashes.” 3

The Rebuke of the Comforters

“The Lord said to Eliphaz: ‘I am angry with you and your two friends, because you have not spoken the truth about me, as my servant Job has.’” 4

The Double Portion

“After Job had prayed for his friends, the Lord restored his fortunes and gave him twice as much as he had before.” 5
“Nowhere in all the land were there found women as beautiful as Job’s daughters.”
“And so Job died, old and full of years.”

Translation Notes

1 No Purpose... thwarted (lo-Yib-batzer Mimme-kha Mez-immah): In the scrolls, Job is "Re-rooting" his "Worldview." He acknowledges that "God’s Plan" is "Un-uprootable." He has moved from "Demanding Explanations" to "Trusting Sovereignty."

2 Now my Eyes have Seen You (we-Attah Eni Ra’ath-kha): This is the "Epiphany of the Rooted." Job’s "Ears" (*Tradition*) were not enough; he needed a "Direct Sight" (*Experience*). This "Vision" is what "Uproots" his "Sorrow." The "Theophany" is the "Healing."

3 Repent in Dust and Ashes (Emm-as... we-Nicham-ti): Literally in the scrolls, "I reject/melt-away... and I am comforted/change-my-mind." Job is not necessarily repenting of "Sin," but of his "Limited Perspective." He is "Uprooting" his "Self-Justice" in the presence of "Infinite Holiness."

4 Spoken the Truth... as my Servant Job (lo Dibbar-them Elay Nekhonah ke-Abdi Iyyob): This is the "Stunning Vindication" in the scrolls. God "Roots" Job’s "Honest Protest" as "Truth" (*Nekhonah*), while condemning the friends' "Defensive Orthodoxy" as "Falsehood." It proves that God prefers "Raw Honesty" over "Polite Lies."

5 Restored... Twice as Much (Mish-neh): The scrolls conclude with the "Double-Root." In the "End," Job’s "Fruit" is "Multiplied." He becomes a "Mediator" (*Verse 8*) for the very men who "Crushed" him. His "Restoration" is not just "Material," but "Relational" and "Spiritual." It signifies that while the "Branch" was "Cut Down," the "Root" remained, and in the "Scent of Water" (*God’s Presence*), it "Budded" and "Spread its Branches" once more. The book ends not in "Explanation," but in "Worship" and "Fullness."

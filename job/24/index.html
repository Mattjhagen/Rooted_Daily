Job 24

The Moving of Boundaries

“There are those who move boundary stones; they pasture flocks they have stolen.” 1
“They drive away the orphan’s donkey and take the widow’s ox in pledge.”

The Drenched and Naked

“Lacking clothes, they spend the night naked; they have nothing to cover themselves in the cold.”
“They are drenched by mountain rains and hug the rocks for lack of shelter.” 2
“The groans of the dying rise from the city, and the souls of the wounded cry out for help. But God charges no one with wrongdoing.” 3

The Terror of the Light

“The murderer rises at twilight to kill the poor and needy; in the night he acts like a thief.”
“For all of them, deep darkness is their morning; they make friends with the terrors of darkness.” 4
“He may let them rest in a feeling of security, but his eyes are on their ways.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Move Boundary Stones (Gebuloth Yassigu): In the scrolls, this is the "Uprooting of Property Rights." The "Boundary Stone" was the "Legal Root" of a family’s land. By moving it, the wicked were "Stealing the Root." It signifies the "Institutional Injustice" that Job sees everywhere.

2 Hug the Rocks (Chassu): Literally in the scrolls, "They take refuge in the rock." The "Poor" are "Uprooted" from "Human Society" and forced to seek "Shelter" in the "Hardness of Nature." It is a "Rooted" description of the "Homelessness" caused by greed.

3 God charges No One (Eloah lo-Yashim Thiph-lah): This is Job’s "Blistering Critique" of the friends’ theology in the scrolls. If God is "Actively Judging" as they claim, then why does He "Allow" these groans to go "Unanswered"? It is the "Root" of the "Problem of Evil." Job argues that God is "Silent" exactly when "Justice" is most needed.

4 Make friends with the Terrors (Yakkar Ballahoth): The scrolls highlight the "Moral Inversion" of the wicked. They "Love the Dark" because it "Hides their Root." They are "At Home" in what should "Terrify" them. It signifies the "Spiritual Darkness" that has become their "Comfort Zone."

5 Rest in a feeling of Security (Yitten-lo la-Betach): Literally in the scrolls, "He gives to him for safety." Job admits that God often "Allows" the wicked to feel "Safe." But he concludes with the "Hidden Observation"—God’s "Eyes" are on them. It marks the "Tension" of Job’s faith—he sees the "Apparent Injustice" but maintains a "Rooted Belief" that God is "Watching."

Job 30

The Song of the Fools

“But now they mock me, men younger than I, whose fathers I would have disdained to put with my sheep dogs.” 1
“And now those young men mock me in song; I have become a byword among them.”

The Gnawing Pain

“And now my life ebbs away; days of suffering grip me.”
“In the night my bones are pierced; my gnawing pains never rest.” 2

The Ruthless Turn

“I cry out to you, God, but you do not answer; I stand up, but you merely look at me.” 3
“You turn on me ruthlessly; with the might of your hand you attack me.” 4
“My lyre is tuned to mourning, and my pipe to the sound of wailing.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Sons of Fools (Bne Nabal): In the scrolls, Job describes the "Social Reversal." The people who were "Uprooted" from society (*Verse 5*) and lived in "Caves" are now the ones "Uprooting" his dignity. It signifies the "Total Loss of Status."

2 Gnawing Pains (Oray): Literally in the scrolls, "My gnawers" or "Those who eat me." Job feels that his "Sickness" is a "Predator" that is "Consuming" his "Internal Root." The "Night" (*Verse 17*) offers no "Refuge," only "Piercing."

3 You merely Look at me (Wa-Tith-bonen Bi): The scrolls highlight the "Indifference of God." Job "Stands" in prayer (*the posture of the petitioner*), but God remains a "Spectator." He is "Uprooted" by the "Lack of Response."

4 Turn on me Ruthlessly (Teha-phek le-Akzar): Literally in the scrolls, "You have been transformed into a cruel one to me." This is Job’s "Rawest Theological Agony." He feels that the "Nature of God" has changed from "Protector" (*Chapter 29*) to "Oppressor." It marks the "Extreme Point" of his "Faith-Struggle."

5 My Lyre is Tuned to Mourning (Way-Yehi le-Ebel Kinnori): The scrolls conclude with this "Musical Desolation." The "Songs of Joy" from his "Golden Days" have been "Uprooted" and replaced by "Wailing" (*Beki*). He is no longer the "King of his People," but the "King of his Sorrow." It sets the stage for his "Final Oath" in *Chapter 31*.

Job 7

The Hard Service

“Do not mortals have hard service on earth? Are not their days like those of a hired laborer?” 1
“My body is clothed with worms and scabs, my skin is broken and festering.” 2

The Weaver’s Shuttle

“My days are swifter than a weaver’s shuttle, and they come to an end without hope.” 3

The Watcher of Men

“What is mankind that you make so much of them... that you examine them every morning and test them every moment?” 4
“If I have sinned, what have I done to you, you who watch everyone? Why have you made me your target?” 5
“For I will soon lie down in the dust; you will search for me, but I will be no more.”

Translation Notes

1 Hard Service (Tzaba): Literally in the scrolls, "Military service" or "Conscription." Job feels that "Life" itself is a "Battle" he didn't volunteer for. He is "Rooting" the human experience in "Toil" rather than "Joy."

2 Worms and Scabs (Rimmah we-Gush Aphar): The scrolls highlight the "Graphic Decay." Job’s "Skin" is literally "Uprooting" itself from his body. He is a "Living Corpse." The "Ashes" of Chapter 2 are now the "Festering scabs" of Chapter 7.

3 Weaver’s Shuttle (Areg): This is a "Rooted Metaphor" of "Speed and Futility." Just as a shuttle flies across the loom, Job’s "Life" is vanishing without any "Thread of Hope" to bind it. He is "Emptying" himself of any "Future Perspective."

4 What is Mankind (Mah Enosh): This is a "Brilliant Parody" of Psalm 8 in the scrolls. While the Psalmist marvels that God "Cares" for man, Job complains that God "Tests" man too much. He wants the "Divine Eye" to look away. He is "Uprooted" by the "Relentless Attention" of the Holy.

5 Made me your Target (Miphga): Literally in the scrolls, "A Mark" or "A Thing to strike." Job feels he has been "Singled out" for a "Cosmic Experiment." He argues that even if he "Sinned" (*Verse 20*), he is too "Small" to affect the "Vastness of God." It marks his "Theological Protest" against "Divine Perfection" being used to "Crush" human "Frailty."

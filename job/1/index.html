Job 1

In the land of Uz there lived a man whose name was Job. This man was blameless and upright; he feared God and shunned evil. 1

The Heavenly Challenge

“Does Job fear God for nothing?” Satan replied. “Have you not put a hedge around him and his household...?” 2
“Strike everything he has, and he will surely curse you to your face.”

The Day of Darkness

“While he was still speaking, another messenger came: ‘Your sons and daughters were feasting... when a mighty wind struck the house... and they are dead.’” 3

The Worship of the Wreckage

“Naked I came from my mother’s womb, and naked I will depart. The LORD gave and the LORD has taken away; may the name of the LORD be praised.” 4
“In all this, Job did not sin by charging God with wrongdoing.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Blameless (Tam): In the scrolls, this doesn't mean "Sinless," but "Whole" or "Complete." Job was a "Rooted" man who didn't have a "Fractured Heart." He "Feared God" (*Yere Elohim*) which in the scrolls means he had a "Holy Weight" of God’s presence in his life.

2 Hedge (Suk): Literally in the scrolls, "Have you not fenced him in?" Satan’s argument is that Job is "Rooted in Prosperity," not in "God Himself." He claims that if you "Uproot the Hedge," you will "Uproot the Heart." It is the "Primal Test" of human motive.

3 A Mighty Wind (Ruach Gedolah): The scrolls highlight the "Atmospheric Tragedy." Everything Job built was "Uprooted" in a single day—his wealth, his servants, and his children. It was a "Surgical Strike" on his identity.

4 The LORD Gave (YHWH Nathan): This is the "Anchor of the Soul" in the scrolls. Job recognized that everything was a "Loan" from the Creator. He didn't see himself as an "Owner," but a "Steward." By "Praising" in the "Nakedness," he proved that his "Root" was deeper than his "Possessions."

5 Charging God (Thiflah): Literally in the scrolls, "Nonsense" or "Folly." Job refused to call God "Unjust." He maintained the "Root of Sovereignty" even when he couldn't see the "Reason for the Suffering." It marks the beginning of the most "Profound Dialogue" in history.

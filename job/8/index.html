Job 8

The Blustering Wind

“How long will you say such things? Your words are a blustering wind.” 1
“Does God pervert justice? Does the Almighty pervert what is right?”

The Harsh Conclusion

“When your children sinned against him, he gave them over to the penalty of their sin.” 2

The Fragile Marsh

“Can papyrus grow tall where there is no marsh? Can reeds thrive without water?” 3
“What they trust in is fragile; what they rely on is a spider’s web.” 4
“God does not reject one who is blameless or strengthen the hands of evildoers.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Blustering Wind (Ruach Kabbir): In the scrolls, Bildad is "Dismissing" Job’s agony as mere "Noise." He is "Rooting" his defense in the "Immutability of God." To Bildad, for God to be "God," He must be "Just" in a way that humans can easily calculate. It marks the "Rigidity" of his thinking.

2 Gave them Over (Way-Yeshallechem): Literally in the scrolls, "He sent them away into the hand of their rebellion." This is a "Cruel Application" of the Retribution Principle. Bildad is "Uprooting" Job’s grief over his children by blaming the victims. It proves that "Doctrine without Compassion" is a "Weapon."

3 Can Papyrus grow (ha-Yig’eh Gome): The scrolls highlight the "Ecological Wisdom." Bildad argues that "Prosperity" requires the "Water of Righteousness." If the "Water" (God’s favor) is gone, the "Plant" (Job) must have a "Faulty Root." He is using "Natural Law" to "Indict" Job.

4 Spider’s Web (Beith Akkabish): Literally in the scrolls, "The House of a Spider." Bildad is saying that Job’s previous "Wealth and Family" were a "Flimsy Foundation" because they weren't "Rooted in Truth." He is "Uprooting" Job’s "Security" as a "Delusion."

5 God does not Reject (Hen-El Lo Yim’as Tam): The scrolls conclude with this "Symmetry of Justice." Bildad offers a "Conditional Promise." If Job is "Blameless" (*Tam*), God will "Restore" him. It is a "Rooted" theology of "Performance." He doesn't leave room for "Innocent Suffering."

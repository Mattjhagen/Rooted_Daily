Job 40

The Hand over the Mouth

“I am unworthy—how can I reply to you? I put my hand over my mouth.” 1
“I have spoken once, but I have no answer—twice, but I will say no more.”

The Sovereignty Test

“Would you discredit my justice? Would you condemn me to justify yourself?” 2
“Then I myself will admit to you that your own right hand can save you.” 3

Behemoth

“Look at Behemoth, which I made along with you... It eats grass like an ox.” 4
“Its tail sways like a cedar; the sinews of its thighs are close-knit.” 5
“It ranks first among the works of God.”

Translation Notes

1 Hand over my Mouth (Yadi Samti le-Phi): In the scrolls, this is the "Root of Silence." Job has reached the "Limit of Language." He "Uproots" his own "Arguments." It signifies that the "Experience of God" has made his "Theological Claims" feel "Small."

2 Condemn me to Justify Yourself (Thar-shi-eni le-Ma’an Thitz-daq): The scrolls highlight the "Ultimate Conflict." God asks Job if he is "Uprooting God’s Justice" just to "Root his own Innocence." He is showing Job the "Cost" of his "Protest."

3 Right Hand can Save you (Thoshi-a-kha Yemine-kha): This is a "Sarcastic Challenge" in the scrolls. God is saying that if Job can "Govern the Universe" and "Humiliate the Proud" (*Verse 11-12*), then He will "Bow" to Job. It is a "Rooted" proof of Job’s "Finitude."

4 Behemoth: Literally "The Beast of Beasts" (plural of majesty). In the scrolls, Behemoth is the "King of the Land." It is "Rooted" in "Earthly Strength." God points to it as a "Fellow Creature" (*Made along with you*) to show Job the "Scale of God’s Craftsmanship."

5 Tail sways like a Cedar (Zanabo khe-Erez): The scrolls highlight the "Magnificence of the Primordial." This description does not fit a "Hippopotamus" (the common modern identification); the "Cedar-like tail" suggests something much more "Prehistoric" and "Mighty." God is showing Job a "Root" of creation that Job cannot "Master." It marks the transition to the even more terrifying **Leviathan** in *Chapter 41*.

Job 22

The Invented Iniquity

“Is it not because your wickedness is great? Is there no end to your sins?” 1
“You demanded security from your relatives for no reason; you stripped them of their clothing, leaving them naked.” 2

Exploiting the Weary

“You gave no water to the weary and you withheld food from the hungry.”
“You sent widows away empty-handed and broke the strength of the fatherless.”

The Path to Peace

“Submit to God and be at peace with him; in this way prosperity will come to you.” 3
“Then the Almighty will be your gold, the choicest silver for you.” 4
“What you decide on will be done, and light will shine on your ways.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Wickedness is Great (Ra-ath-kha Rabbah): In the scrolls, Eliphaz is "Inventing History." Since his "Theology" says Job must have sinned, he "Creates" the sins to fit the "Punishment." It is the "Ultimate Uprooting" of the truth. He is no longer "Listening"; he is "Projecting."

2 Security for no Reason (Chabal Chinnom): Literally in the scrolls, "Taking a pledge for nothing." Eliphaz accuses Job of being a "Predatory Lender." It is a "Social Slander" intended to "Uproot" Job’s character. It signifies how "Ideology" can "Blind" a person to the "Reality" of their friend’s life.

3 Submit (Hasken): Literally in the scrolls, "Accustom yourself" or "Be familiar." Eliphaz is offering a "Transactional Peace." He believes that "Prosperity" is the "Fruit" of "Submission." He is "Rooting" God’s "Favor" in "Human Performance."

4 Almighty will be your Gold (we-Hayah Shaddai Betzarekha): This is a "Beautiful but Misapplied Promise" in the scrolls. Eliphaz suggests that if Job "Uproots" his "Trust in Wealth" (*Verse 24*), then "Shaddai" Himself will become his "Treasure." It is a "Rooted" truth that Eliphaz is using as a "Bait" for a "False Confession."

5 Light will Shine (Or Nagah): The scrolls conclude with this "Glistening Promise." Eliphaz is trying to "Lure" Job back to "Orthodoxy." He promises that "Clarity" and "Success" will follow "Repentance." It marks the end of Eliphaz’s role in the book. He ends as he began—convinced that he has the "Key" to Job’s "Restoration," even though he has "Uprooted" his own "Integrity" to get it.

Job 14

The Fading Flower

“Mortals, born of woman, are of few days and full of trouble.” 1
“They spring up like flowers and wither; like fleeting shadows, they do not endure.”

The Hope of the Root

“At least there is hope for a tree: If it is cut down, it will sprout again, and its new shoots will not fail.” 2
“Its roots may grow old in the earth and its stump die in the soil...”
“Yet at the scent of water it will bud and put forth shoots like a plant.” 3

The Great Question

“If a man dies, will he live again?” 4
“You would call and I would answer you; you would long for the creature your hands have made.”

The Destruction of Hope

“My offenses will be sealed up in a bag; you will cover over my sin.” 5
“As water wears away stones... so you destroy a person’s hope.”

Translation Notes

1 Born of Woman (Yelud Ishah): In the scrolls, this is a "Mark of Frailty." Job is "Uprooting" any "False Grandeur" of the human race. He sees "Life" not as a "Journey" but as a "Withering."

2 Hope for a Tree (Yesh la-Etz Tiqwvah): This is the most "Rooted" imagery in the book. Job is "Envious of the Tree." He sees that a "Dormant Root" can be "Revived," but he fears that a "Dead Mortal" has no such "Mechanism." He is "Questioning the Biology of the Soul."

3 Scent of Water (Re-ach Mayim): Literally in the scrolls, "From the smell of water." This is a "Poetic Masterpiece." It suggests that the "Root" is so "Sensitive" that even the "Aroma" of life can trigger a "Resurrection." Job is "Longing" for such a "Scent" in his own "Drought."

4 Will he Live Again? (ha-Yich-ye): The scrolls highlight this "First Spark of Hope." In the midst of his "Despair," Job "Hypothesizes" about a "Life beyond the Grave." He imagines a "Hiding Place" (*Verse 13*) where he can "Wait out the Anger" of God. It is the "Root" of the "Resurrection Theology."

5 Sealed up in a Bag (Tzarur bi-Tzeror): Literally in the scrolls, "Tied up in a bundle." Job feels that God is "Hoarding" his "Mistakes" like "Treasures" to use against him later. It marks his "Theological Agony"—feeling that "Mercy" has been "Sealed away." He concludes with the "Erosion of Hope" (*Verse 19*), acknowledging the "Relentless Pressure" of "Divine Displeasure."

Job 20

The Momentary Joy

“The mirth of the wicked is brief, the joy of the godless lasts but a moment.” 1
“He will perish forever, like his own dung.”

The Cobra’s Venom

“Yet his food will turn sour in his stomach; it will become the venom of cobras within him.” 2
“The wealth he swallowed he will vomit up; God will make his stomach expel it.” 3

The Fire Unfanned

“When he has filled his belly, God will vent his burning anger against him.”
“A fire unfanned will consume him and devour what is left in his tent.” 4
“Such is the fate God allots the wicked, the heritage appointed for them by God.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Mirth... is Brief (mi-Qarob): Literally in the scrolls, "From nearby" or "Short-lived." Zophar is "Uprooting" any "Stability" the wicked might feel. He argues that "Success" without "God" is a "Vapor." He is mapping "Job’s Previous Prosperity" onto this "Momentalism."

2 Venom of Cobras (Mererath Pthanim): The scrolls highlight the "Organic Reversal." What was "Sweet" in the mouth (*Verse 12*) becomes "Poison" in the "Inner Root." Zophar is saying that "Ill-gotten Gain" has a "Surgical Penalty" built into it. It "Uproots" the life from the inside.

3 Vomit up (Way-ya-qien-nu): Literally in the scrolls, "He will spue it out." Zophar uses "Visceral Language" to describe "Economic Loss." He implies that Job’s current "Poverty" is God "Forcing him to Vomit" his previously "Wicked Wealth." It is a "Grotesque Theology" of "Restitution."

4 Fire Unfanned (Esh lo-Nuppach): This is a "Terrifying Mystery" in the scrolls. It refers to a "Supernatural Fire" (like the one that struck Job’s sheep in *Chapter 1*). Zophar is "Interpreting" Job’s "Tragedy" as "Direct Divine Judgment." He is "Rooting" Job’s "Losses" in "Divine Fury."

5 Heritage Appointed (Nachalath Imro): Literally in the scrolls, "The inheritance of his word [from God]." Zophar concludes with this "Dogmatic Seal." He believes that "Wickedness" is an "Estate" that God "Bestows" upon those who deserve it. It marks the end of Zophar’s contribution to the dialogue. He remains "Unmoved" by Job’s "Pain," seeing only a "Legal Settlement."

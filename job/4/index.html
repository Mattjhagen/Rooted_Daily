Job 4

The Physician’s Scrutiny

“Think how you have instructed many, how you have strengthened feeble hands.” 1
“But now trouble comes to you, and you are discouraged; it strikes you, and you are dismayed.”

The Law of the Harvest

“Consider now: Who, being innocent, has ever perished? Where were the upright ever destroyed?” 2
“Those who plow evil and those who sow trouble reap it.”

The Spirit in the Night

“In the troubling visions of the night, when deep sleep falls on mortals...”
“A spirit glided past my face, and the hair on my body stood on end.” 3
“‘Can a mortal be more righteous than God? Can even a pure man be more pure than his Maker?’” 4
“How much more those who live in houses of clay, whose foundations are in the dust...” 5

Translation Notes

1 Think how you have Instructed (Hinne Yissarta): In the scrolls, Eliphaz is pointing out Job’s "Past Consistency." He is implying that Job is a "Good Teacher" who has failed his "Own Test." It is the "Root" of the friend’s suspicion—if Job was truly "Righteous," he wouldn't be "Discouraged."

2 Who, being Innocent, has ever Perished? (Mi-Hu Naqi Abad): This is the "Foundation of Eliphaz’s Theology" in the scrolls. He believes in a "Mechanical Justice." If you are "Destroyed," you must have "Plowed Evil." He is "Uprooting" Job’s claim of innocence by looking at the "Result" (his suffering). It marks the beginning of the "False Comfort."

3 A Spirit Glided (Ruach Al-Panay): The scrolls highlight the "Mystical Authority" of Eliphaz. He isn't just using "Logic"; he is using a "Private Revelation." The "Shuddering" of his hair (*Verse 15*) was meant to prove that his words were "Heaven-Sent."

4 More Righteous than God (Me-Eloah Yitzdaq Enosh): This is the "Crushing Question" of the vision. It suggests that even the "Purest Root" is "Filthy" compared to the Creator. It is a "Theological Weapon" used to "Silence" Job’s protest. If "No one" is righteous, then Job’s suffering is "Justified" simply by his existence.

5 Houses of Clay (Batte Chomer): Literally in the scrolls, "Homes of Mud." It refers to the "Human Body." Eliphaz is "Uprooting" human dignity to protect "Divine Justice." He sees humans as "Moths" (*Verse 19*) that are crushed between "Morning and Evening." It signifies the "Fragility" of the creature in the presence of the "Holy."

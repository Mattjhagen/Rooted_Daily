Job 5

“I myself have seen a fool taking root, but suddenly his house was cursed.” 1

The Unfathomable God

“He performs wonders that cannot be fathomed, miracles that cannot be counted.” 2
“He gives rain on the earth and sends water upon the countryside.”

The Blessed Wound

“Blessed is the one whom God corrects; so do not despise the discipline of the Almighty.” 3
“For he wounds, but he also binds up; he injures, but his hands also heal.” 4
“In six troubles he will deliver you; in seven no harm will touch you.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Seen a Fool taking Root (Mashrish): In the scrolls, Eliphaz is using a "Botanical Warning." He admits that even the "Wicked" can look like they are "Succeeding" for a moment, but their "Root" is "Cursed." He is subtly suggesting that Job’s previous "Rootedness" (*Chapter 1*) was actually the "Root of a Fool."

2 Wonders that cannot be Fathomed (En Cheqer): The scrolls highlight the "Transcendence of God." Eliphaz uses the "Mystery of Nature" (rain, water) to "Uproot" Job’s demand for "Answers." If Job cannot explain "Rain," how can he explain "Suffering"? It is a "Rooted" argument for "Divine Sovereignty."

3 Correction (Yokach): Literally in the scrolls, "To argue" or "To prove." Eliphaz sees Job’s suffering as a "Merciful Rebuke" from God. He is "Reframing the Tragedy" as a "Teaching Moment." It signifies that the "Pain" is meant to "Re-root" Job in humility.

4 He Wounds, but Binds Up (Yakh’ib we-Yehabesh): In the scrolls, this is the "Paradox of the Healer." God is both the "Surgeon" and the "Nurse." Eliphaz is offering a "Conditional Hope"—if Job accepts the "Wound," he will find the "Healing."

5 Six Troubles... and Seven: This is a "Numerical Proverb" in the scrolls signifying "Completion" and "Exhaustive Protection." Eliphaz is promising that "Total Submission" leads to "Total Security." He concludes with the "Bold Confidence" (*Verse 27*) that his "Examination" of the truth is "Final." It marks the "Theological Pressure" being placed on Job.

Job 9

The Legal Impossibility

“How can a mere mortal prove their innocence before God?” 1

The Maker of Stars

“He is the Maker of the Bear and Orion, the Pleiades and the constellations of the south.” 2
“He performs wonders that cannot be fathomed, miracles that cannot be counted.”

The Scourge of the Innocent

“It is all the same; that is why I say, ‘He destroys both the blameless and the wicked.’” 3
“When a land falls into the hands of the wicked, he blindfolds its judges.”

The Cry for an Umpire

“If only there were someone to mediate between us, someone to bring us together, someone to remove God’s rod from me.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Prove their Innocence (Yitzdaq): In the scrolls, this is a "Legal Despair." Job admits that God is not just "Righteous," but "Overwhelming." If a "Mortal" (*Enosh*) tries to argue with God, he will be "Drowned" by the "Infinite Magnitude" of the Creator. It is an "Uprooting" of the friends’ idea of a "Fair Trial."

2 Maker of the Bear and Orion (Ash, Kesil, Kimah): The scrolls highlight the "Cosmic Distance." Job points to the "Stars" to show that God’s "Scale" is "Non-Human." If God "Moves Mountains" (*Verse 5*), what chance does a "Clod of Dust" have in court? He is "Rooting" God’s "Power" in the "Unfathomable."

3 Destroys both the Blameless and the Wicked: This is Job’s "Radical Counter-Thesis" in the scrolls. He is "Uprooting" the "Performance-Based Universe." He argues that "Suffering" is "Arbitrary" or at least "Beyond Human Logic." He claims that God "Mocks the despair of the innocent" (*Verse 23*), which is his "Rawest Accusation" yet.

4 Mediate between us (Mokiach): Literally in the scrolls, "An Umpire" or "Daysman." Job realizes that the "Distance" between a "Corruptible Mortal" and an "Incorruptible God" is too great for "Dialogue." He longs for a "Middle-man" who can "Place a Hand on both of us." It is a "Prophetic Root" longing for the "Incarnation"—someone who can "Remove the Rod" and bridge the "Infinite Gap."

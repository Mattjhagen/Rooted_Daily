Job 6

The Heavy Heart

“If only my grief could be weighed and all my misery be placed on the scales! It would outweigh the sand of the seas.” 1

The Poison of the Shaddai

“The arrows of the Almighty are in me; my spirit drinks in their poison.” 2
“God’s terrors are marshaled against me.”

The Vanishing Stream

“My brothers have been as undependable as a seasonal stream, like the streams that overflow when darkened by melting ice.” 3
“Now you too have proved to be of no help; you see something terrible and are afraid.” 4
“Teach me, and I will be quiet; show me where I have been wrong.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Outweigh the Sand (Mi-Chol Yammim Yikhbad): In the scrolls, Job is "Uprooting" Eliphaz’s logic of "Retribution" by pointing to the "Infinite Scale" of his pain. He argues that his "Grief" is not a "Minor Correction" (*Chapter 5*) but an "Overwhelming Sea."

2 Arrows of the Almighty (Chitzzei Shaddai): The scrolls highlight the "Personal Nature" of the attack. Job doesn't blame "Satan" (whom he doesn't know about), he blames "Shaddai" (the All-Sufficient God). The "Poison" (*Verse 4*) is the "Internalization" of the trauma. His "Spirit" is being "Rooted" in "Toxicity."

3 Seasonal Stream (Nachal): Literally in the scrolls, a "Wadi" that is full in winter but "Dry" in summer when you actually need water. Job is comparing his friends to "Deceptive Resources." They looked "Full of Wisdom" when he was prosperous, but they "Evaporated" when his "Drought" began.

4 You see something Terrible (Thir’u Chathath): The scrolls record Job’s "Socio-Spiritual Insight." He realized that his friends weren't "Correcting" him out of "Love," but out of "Fear." They were "Afraid" that if a "Righteous Man" could suffer like this, then they weren't "Safe" either. They were "Protecting their own Theology" at his expense.

5 Show me where I have been Wrong: This is Job’s "Rooted Integrity." He isn't claiming to be "Sinless," but he is "Refusing" to confess to "Fictive Sins" just to satisfy a "Mercenary Doctrine." He demands "Specific Evidence" rather than "General Platitudes." It marks his "Intellectual Defiance" against "Simplistic Explanations."

Job 3

The Curse of Being

“May the day of my birth perish, and the night that said, ‘A boy is conceived!’” 1
“That day—may it turn to darkness; may God above not care about it.”

The Peace of the Equalizer

“There the wicked cease from turmoil, and there the weary are at rest.” 2
“The small and the great are there, and the slave is free from his master.”

The Question of Light

“Why is light given to those in misery, and life to the bitter of soul?” 3
“Why is life given to a man whose way is hidden, whom God has hedged in?” 4
“What I feared has come upon me; what I dreaded has happened to me.” 5

Translation Notes

1 May that Day Perish (Yobad Yom): In the scrolls, this is the "Un-creation" of the self. Job is using "Genesean Language" in reverse. He is not "Uprooting" God, but he is "Uprooting" his own existence. He wants the "Light" that God called "Good" to be "Withdrawn" from his timeline.

2 The Weary are at Rest (Yanuachu Yege’e Koach): Literally in the scrolls, "The exhausted of strength find peace." Job sees the "Grave" as the "Ultimate Sanctuary" from the "Turmoil" of a "Hedged Life." In his agony, the "End of the Root" seems better than the "Pain of the Branch."

3 Bitter of Soul (Mare Naphesh): The scrolls highlight the "Organic Agony." "Naphesh" (Soul/Breath) is "Bitter" because the "Root" has been "Poisoned" by suffering. Job is asking why "Consciousness" exists if it only serves to "Register Pain."

4 Hedged In (Way-Yasek): This is the "Tragic Irony" of the scrolls. In Chapter 1, Satan used the "Hedge" to mean "Protection." Here, Job uses the "Hedge" to mean "Confinement" or "Imprisonment." The same "Boundary" that was a "Blessing" has become a "Wall." It signifies that "Perspective" changes when the "Light" is gone.

5 What I Feared (Pachad Pachadti): Literally in the scrolls, "The dread I dreaded." Job reveals that even in his "Prosperity" (*Chapter 1*), he was "Rooted in Anxiety." He knew the "Fragility of the Hedge." The "Collapse" was the "Actualization of his deepest Fear." It marks the beginning of his "Wrestle with the Infinite."

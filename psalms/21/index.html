Psalm 21

For the director of music. A psalm of David.

The King’s Rejoicing

“The king rejoices in your strength, Lord. How great is his joy in the victories you give!” 1

The Crown of Pure Gold

“You have granted him his heart’s desire and have not withheld the request of his lips.” Selah
“You came to greet him with rich blessings and placed a crown of pure gold on his head.” 2

Joy in the Presence

“Surely you have granted him unending blessings and made him glad with the joy of your presence.” 3

The Fiery Furnace

“Your hand will lay hold on all your enemies; your right hand will seize your foes.” 4
“At the time of your appearing you will make them like a fiery furnace.” 5

Translation Notes

1 The King Rejoices (ha-Melekh Yischak): In the scrolls, this is the "Victory Answer" to the prayer of *Psalm 20*. David is "Rooting" his "Joy" (*Yischak*) in "God’s Strength" (*Oz-kha*) rather than his own "Chariots." It is the "Fruit of Victory."

2 Crown of Pure Gold (Atzereth Paz): The scrolls highlight the "Restoration of Honor." God "Roots" the king in "Authority." The "Pure Gold" (*Paz*) signifies the "Divine Quality" of the rule. It is a "Greeting of Blessing" (*Berakhoth Tob*).

3 Joy of your Presence (Sim-cha eth-Phanekha): Literally "Gladness with Your face." The scrolls highlight the "Source of the King’s Strength." It isn't just the "Kingdom," but the "Presence." He is "Rooted" in the "Face of God."

4 Lay hold on all your Enemies (Tim-tza Yad-kha): Literally "Your hand will find [them]." The scrolls highlight the "Inevitability of Judgment." There is no "Root" that can "Hide" from the "Right Hand" of the Lord.

5 Fiery Furnace (Than-nur Esh): This is a "Vivid Image of Destruction" in the scrolls. Those who "Uproot" justice are "Uprooted" by "Fire." It marks the "Final Warning" to the rebellious nations. David concludes with a "Praise of Might" (*Verse 13*). It marks the transition from "Royal Celebration" to the "Messianic Suffering" of the next Psalm. It is the "Rooted Daily" reminder that "Victory" belongs to the "Lord."

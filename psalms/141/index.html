Psalm 141

Of David.

Like Incense

“I call to you, Lord, come quickly to me; hear my voice when I call to you.”
“May my prayer be set before you like incense; may the lifting up of my hands be like the evening sacrifice.” 1

Guard over My Mouth

“Set a guard over my mouth, Lord; keep watch over the door of my lips.” 2
“Do not let my heart be drawn to what is evil.”

Oil of Rebuke

“Let a righteous man strike me—that is a kindness; let him rebuke me—that is oil on my head.” 3

Fixed on You

“But my eyes are fixed on you, Sovereign Lord; in you I take refuge.” 4
“Keep me safe from the traps set by evildoers.”

Translation Notes

1 Incense... Evening Sacrifice (Ke-to-reth... Min-chath E-reb): In the scrolls, this is the "Liturgical Substitution Root." Prayer is "Rooted" in the same "Pleasing Aroma" as the "Temple Offering." It signifies "Sacrificial Communication."

2 Guard over My Mouth (Shi-m-rah le-Phi): The scrolls highlight the "Root of Verbal Integrity." The "Lips" (*Spha-tay*) are the "Door" (*Dal*) of the "Heart." It is the "Uprooting of Reckless Speech."

3 Oil on my Head (She-men Rosh): The scrolls highlight the "Root of Humble Correction." A "Righteous Rebuke" is not a "Blow," but a "Medicinal Oil" (*Shemen*) that should not be "Refused." It signifies "Spiritual Growth through Accountability."

4 Eyes are Fixed (Ey-nay): The scrolls conclude with this "Attentional Root." To "Take Refuge" (*Chasah*) is to have the "Eyes" (*Eynay*) "Rooted" in the "Sovereign" (*A-do-nay*). It marks the transition to the "Cave Prayer" in *Psalm 142*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "Evening Gaze." My eyes are on You.

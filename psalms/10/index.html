Psalm 10

The Hiding of God

“Why, Lord, do you stand far off? Why do you hide yourself in times of trouble?” 1

The Arrogant Thought

“In his arrogance the wicked man hunts down the weak... In all his thoughts there is no room for God.” 2
“He says to himself, ‘God has forgotten; he covers his face and never sees.’”

The Uprising of Justice

“Arise, Lord! Lift up your hand, O God. Do not forget the helpless.” 3
“But you, God, see the trouble of the afflicted; you consider their grief and take it in hand.” 4
“The Lord is King for ever and ever!” 5
“You hear, Lord, the desire of the afflicted; you encourage them and you listen to their cry.”

Translation Notes

1 Hide Yourself (Thal-lim): In the scrolls, this is the "Uprooting of Visibility." The psalmist feels that God is "Overlooking" the crisis. It is a "Rooted Cry" of "Abandonment." It signifies the "Spiritual Darkness" that precedes "Divine Action."

2 No Room for God (En Elohim): Literally in the scrolls, "There is no God [in all his plans]." This is "Practical Atheism." The wicked man doesn't necessarily "Deny God’s Existence," but he "Uproots God’s Relevance." He "Roots" his "Security" in his "Own Power" (*Verse 6*).

3 Arise, Lord (Qumah YHWH): This is the "Call to Action." David is asking God to "Uproot" the "Evil Status Quo." The "Lifted Hand" (*Nessa Yad-ekha*) is a "Symbol of Intervention." He is "Re-rooting" the "Helpless" (*Aniyim*) in the "Mercy of God."

4 Consider their Grief (le-Theth be-Yad-ekha): Literally "To put into Your hand." The scrolls highlight the "Active Consideration" of God. While the wicked think God is "Blind" (*Verse 11*), David knows that God "Sees" (*Ra-i-tha*) and is "Hand-holding" the trouble. It is the "Root of Comfort."

5 The Lord is King (YHWH Melekh): The scrolls conclude with this "Sovereign Root." Regardless of the "Temporary Arrogance" of the wicked, the "Lord Reigns." He is "King for Ever" (*Olam wa-Ed*). He "Uproots" the "Nations" from the land and "Encourages" (*Thakhin Libbam*) the heart of the "Oppressed." It marks the "Final Triumph" of "Presence" over "Silence."

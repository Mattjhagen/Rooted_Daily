Psalm 41

For the director of music. A psalm of David.

The Regard for the Weak

“Blessed is the one who has regard for the weak; the Lord delivers them in times of trouble.” 1
“The Lord sustains them on their sickbed and restores them from their bed of illness.”

The Close Friend

“Even my close friend, someone I trusted, one who shared my bread, has turned against me.” 2
“My enemies say of me in malice, ‘When will he die and his name perish?’”

Uheld in Integrity

“Because of my integrity you uphold me and set me in your presence forever.” 3

Everlasting to Everlasting

“Praise be to the Lord, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting. Amen and Amen.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Regard for the Weak (Mas-kil el-Dal): In the scrolls, "Regard" (*Mas-kil*) implies "Intelligent Care." To "Root" one’s "Attention" in the "Weak" (*Dal*) is to "Root" one’s "Security" in "God’s Deliverance." It is the "Uprooting of Selfishness."

2 Shared my Bread, has Turned against me (Okhel Lakh-mi Hig-dil Alay Aqeb): Literally in the scrolls, "Eating my bread has magnified the heel against me." This is a "Brutal Image of Betrayal." To "Magnify the Heel" is to "Stamp upon" someone who is down. In the New Testament, Jesus quotes this regarding Judas (John 13:18). It is the "Ultimate Social Uprooting."

3 Because of my Integrity (wa-Ani be-Thummi): The scrolls highlight the "Un-shakable Core." Despite the "Betrayal" (*Verse 9*) and the "Slander" (*Verse 5*), David is "Rooted" in "Integrity" (*Thum*). God "Upholds" (*Tha-makh-ta*) him. It marks the transition to "Eternal Presence" (*Verse 12*).

4 From Everlasting to Everlasting (me-ha-Olam we-Ad ha-Olam): The scrolls conclude this first book of Psalms with this "Temporal Root." God is "Exalted" beyond "Time." The "Amen and Amen" (*Amen we-Amen*) is the "Final Seal" of the "Rooted Heart." It marks the end of Book I. It is the "Rooted Daily" doxology. We move now to the "Thirst" of Book II.

Psalm 120

A song of ascents.

Distress and Answer

“I call on the Lord in my distress, and he answers me.” 1
“Deliver me, Lord, from lying lips and from deceitful tongues.”

Burning Coals

“What will he do to you, you deceitful tongue?”
“He will punish you with a warrior’s sharp arrows, with burning coals of the broom bush.” 2

Tents of Kedar

“Woe to me that I dwell in Meshek, that I live among the tents of Kedar!” 3
“Too long have I lived among those who hate peace.”

A Man of Peace

“I am on the side of peace; but when I speak, they are for war.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Song of Ascents (Shi-r ham-Ma-a-loth): In the scrolls, this is the "Root of the Pilgrimage." 15 psalms (*120-134*) were sung while "Ascending" (*Ma-a-loth*) to Jerusalem. The journey starts with a "Cry" (*Qa-ra-thi*) from the "Narrow place" (*Distress/Tzarah*). It is the "Root of Departure."

2 Coals of the Broom Bush (Gach-le Re-tha-mim): The scrolls highlight the "Retributive Root." The "Tongue" (*Lashon*) that "Uproots" others will be "Melted" by "Coals" (*Gach-le*) that burn long and hot. It signify "Persistent Judgment."

3 Tents of Kedar (O-ho-le Qe-dar): The scrolls highlight the "Root of Alienation." Meshek and Kedar were "Remote/Wild" regions. To be "Rooted" among "Peace-haters" is the "Sufferer’s Exile." It is the "Uprooting of Belonging."

4 They are for War (hem-mah lam-Mil-cha-mah): The scrolls conclude the first ascent with this "Hostile Finality." The pilgrim is "Shalom," but the environment is "War" (*Mil-cha-mah*). It marks the transition to the "Help on the Road" in *Psalm 121*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "Leaving the World." I am for peace.

Psalm 5

For the director of music. For flutes. A psalm of David.

The Morning Expectancy

“In the morning, Lord, you hear my voice; in the morning I lay my requests before you and wait expectantly.” 1

The Straight Way

“Lead me, Lord, in your righteousness because of my enemies—make your way straight before me.” 2

The Open Grave

“Not a word from their mouth can be trusted; their heart is filled with malice.”
“Their throat is an open grave; with their tongues they tell lies.” 3

The Shield of Favor

“But let all who take refuge in you be glad; let them ever sing for joy.” 4
“Surely, Lord, you bless the righteous; you surround them with your favor as with a shield.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Wait Expectantly (Atzap-pheh): Literally in the scrolls, "I will keep watch." It is the "Root of Vigilance." David doesn't just "Say a Prayer," he "Mounts a Guard" to see the "Response." It is the "Morning Version" of "Rooted Faith."

2 Make your Way Straight (Hish-shar le-Phanay Darkekha): The scrolls highlight the "Request for Clarity." Because David is surrounded by "Foes," he needs a "Path" that is "Uprooted" from "Obstacles." He wants his "Life-Direction" to be "Rooted" in "Divine Character."

3 Throat is an Open Grave (Qeber Pathuach): This is a "Terrifying Metaphor" in the scrolls. The speech of the wicked "Devours" like a "Tomb." It is a "Root of Decay." While David seeks "Life," they offer "Death" through their "Tongues."

4 Sing for Joy (Ye-ran-ne-nu): The scrolls highlight the "Audible Gladness" of the "Refuge-Takers." To "Refuge" (*Chose*) in God is to have a "Rooted Joy" that "Vocalizes" itself. It is the "Opposite" of the "Grave-Throat" (*Verse 9*).

5 Shield of Favor (Tzin-nah): Literally in the scrolls, a "Large Shield" that protects the "Entire Body." God’s "Favor" (*Rat-zon*) is the "Atmosphere" that "Surrounds" (*Tha-te-ren-nu*) the righteous man. It is a "360-degree Root of Protection." It marks the transition from the "Morning Prayer" to the "Daily Battle."

Psalm 106

We Have Sinned

“Praise the Lord. Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good; his love endures forever.” 1
“We have sinned, even as our ancestors did; we have done wrong and acted wickedly.”

The Bull that Eats Grass

“At Horeb they made a calf and worshiped an idol cast from metal.” 2
“They exchanged their glorious God for an image of a bull, which eats grass.” 3

The Stand of Phinehas

“But Phinehas stood up and intervened, and the plague was checked.” 4

Persistent Mercy

“He took note of their distress when he heard their cry.” 5
“Praise be to the Lord, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting.” 6

Translation Notes

1 Praise the Lord (Hal-le-lu-Yah): In the scrolls, this is the "Imperative Root of Gratitude." The "Love" (*Chesed*) of God is the "Anchor" (*le-Olam*) even when the people "Rebel" (*Verse 7*). It is the "Root of Book IV’s Doxology."

2 Made a Calf (Ya-a-su E-gel): The scrolls highlight the "Root of Infidelity." The "Golden Calf" (*Egel*) represents the "Uprooting of the Living Presence" for a "Metal Idol" (*Mas-se-khah*). It signifies "Spiritual Regress."

3 Image of a Bull (be-Thab-nith Shor): Literally "The likeness/pattern of an ox." The scrolls highlight the "Absurdity Root." They traded the "Invisibile Glory" (*Khabod*) for an animal that "Eats Grass" (*O-khel Eseb*). It is the "Uprooting of Majesty."

4 Intervened (way-ya-phal-lel): Literally "And he executed judgment/prayed." The scrolls highlight the "Individual Root of Responsibility." Phinehas "Rooted" the "Nation" back in "Righteousness" (*Verse 31*) through his "Zeal." It signify "Check on the Plague."

5 Distress... Cry (be-Tzar la-hem be-Sha-me-o eth-Rin-na-tham): The scrolls highlight the "Responsive Root of Grace." Despite the "Pollution of the Land" (*Verse 38*), God "Remembers His Covenant" (*Verse 45*). It is the "Root of Divine Remorse."

6 Everlasting to Everlasting (min-ha-Olam we-ad ha-Olam): The scrolls conclude Book IV with this "Double Doxology." It marks the transition to the "Redeemed Gathering" in *Psalm 107*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "Confessional Hope." Let all the people say, "Amen!"

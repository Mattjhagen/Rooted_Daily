Psalm 3

A psalm of David. When he fled from his son Absalom.

The Rising Foes

“Lord, how many are my foes! How many rise up against me!” 1
“Many are saying of me, ‘God will not deliver him.’” Selah

The Lifter of my Head

“But you, Lord, are a shield around me, my glory, the One who lifts my head high.” 2
“I call out to the Lord, and he answers me from his holy mountain.” Selah

The Sleep of the Sustained

“I lie down and sleep; I wake again, because the Lord sustains me.” 3
“I will not fear though tens of thousands assail me on every side.” 4
“From the Lord comes deliverance. May your blessing be on your people.” 5 Selah

Translation Notes

1 How many are my Foes (Mah-Rabbu Tzaray): In the scrolls, this is the "Uprooting of Security." David is fleeing his own son (*Absalom*). His "Legacy" is "Uprooting" his "Life." The "Many" (*Rabbu*) refers to the "Swell of Popular Opinion" against him.

2 Lifts my Head High (Merim Roshi): The scrolls highlight the "Restoration of Dignity." A "Fleeing Man" walks with a "Bowed Head." God is the "Root" who "Lifts" (*Merim*) him back to his "Stature." He is a "Shield" (*Magen*) not just in "Front," but "Around" (*ba-adi*). It signifies "Total Protection."

3 I lie down and Sleep (Ani Shakhabti wa-Ishana): This is the "Evidence of the Root." In the middle of a "Civil War," David is able to "Sleep." It proves that his "Peace" is "Rooted" in the "Sustainer" (*Yishmekheni*) rather than the "Circumstance." Sleep is his "Protest" against fear.

4 Tens of Thousands (Rib-both Am): The scrolls highlight the "Mathematical Disparity." David is "Outnumbered," but he is "Rooted" in the "Infinite." One "Almighty" is more than "Ten Thousand" people. It signifies the "Logic of Faith."

5 From the Lord comes Deliverance (la-YHWH ha-Yeshu’ah): The scrolls conclude with this "Axiom of Salvation." *Yeshu’ah* is the "Root" of the name *Jesus*. David is "Rooting" his "Victory" in "Divine Character" rather than "Human Strategy." The "Blessing" (*Berakha*) is the "Fruit" of this "Deliverance." It marks the transition from "Individual Crisis" to "Corporate Hope."

Psalm 137

By the Rivers

“By the rivers of Babylon we sat and wept when we remembered Zion.” 1
“There on the poplars we hung our harps.”

A Song in a Foreign Land

“For there our captors asked us for songs, our tormentors demanded songs of joy.”
“How can we sing the songs of the Lord while in a foreign land?” 2

If I Forget You

“If I forget you, Jerusalem, may my right hand forget its skill.” 3
“May my tongue cling to the roof of my mouth if I do not remember you.”

The Cup of Bitterness

“Daughter Babylon, doomed to destruction, happy is the one who repays you for what you have done to us.” 4

Translation Notes

1 By the Rivers (al-Na-ha-roth): In the scrolls, "Babylon" (*Ba-bel*) is the place of "Displacement Root." The "Rivers" signify the "Flow of Exile." "Wept" (*Ba-khi-nu*) is the "Emotional Root" of the "Uprooted Heart." It is the "Root of Collective Grief."

2 Foreign Land (Ad-math Ne-khar): Literally "Alien soil." The scrolls highlight the "Spiritual Context Root." The "Song of YHWH" (*Shir YHWH*) cannot be "Rooted" in "Enemy Territory." It signify "Liturgical Integrity."

3 Forget its Skill (Tish-kakh Ye-mi-ni): Literally "May my right hand forget." The scrolls highlight the "Identity Root." If the "Heart" (*Jerusalem*) is "Uprooted," the "Ability" (*Hand/Skill*) is "Paralyzed." It is the "Vow of Eternal Memory."

4 Daughter Babylon (Bath Ba-bel): The scrolls conclude with the "Imprecatory Root of Justice." This is the raw language of "Trauma." The desire for "Repayment" (*Ge-mul*) is the "Uprooting of Evil" in the same "Brutal Way" it arrived. It marks the transition to the "Praise of the Heart" in *Psalm 138*. It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of the "Unforgettable Zion." By the rivers we sat.

Psalm 6

For the director of music. With stringed instruments. According to sheminith. A psalm of David.

The Anguish of the Soul

“Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am faint; heal me, Lord, for my bones are in agony.” 1
“My soul is in deep anguish. How long, Lord, how long?”

The Drenched Couch

“I am worn out from my groaning. All night long I flood my bed with weeping and drench my couch with tears.” 2
“My eyes grow weak with sorrow; they fail because of all my foes.”

The Answer of Mercy

“Away from me, all you who do evil, for the Lord has heard my weeping.” 3
“The Lord has heard my cry for mercy; the Lord accepts my prayer.” 4
“All my enemies will be overwhelmed with shame and anguish; they will turn back and suddenly be put to shame.” 5

Translation Notes

1 My Bones are in Agony (Nibhalu Atzamay): Literally in the scrolls, "My bones are terrified/troubled." This is a "Somatic Root" of suffering. David feels the "Anguish" in his "Deepest Framework." It is the "Uprooting of Physical Peace."

2 Drench my Couch (Am-seh): Literally in the scrolls, "I cause to melt." David’s "Weeping" is so "Intense" that it "Melts" his environment. It is the "Root of Desolation." He has "Uprooted" his "Rest" to "Fertilize" his "Sorrow."

3 Heard my Weeping (Shama Qol Bikyi): Literally "Heard the voice of my weeping." The scrolls highlight that "Tears" have a "Voice." God is "Rooted" in "Compassion." He "Responds" to the "Sound of the Broken Heart." It marks the "Sudden Shift" from "Lament" to "Confidence."

4 Accepts my Prayer (Tphillathi Yiqqach): Literally "He will take/receive my prayer." David moves from "Doubt" (*How long?*) to "Certainty." He is "Rooted" in the "Divine Acceptance." It proves that the "Night" was a "Sowing" and the "Morning" is the "Reaping."

5 Suddenly be put to Shame (Yeboshu Rega): The scrolls conclude with the "Rapidity of Vindication." Just as David was "Suddenly" assured, his enemies will be "Suddenly" (*Rega*) exposed. It marks the end of the "Sufferer’s Silence." He has found his "Voice" in the "Presence of Mercy."

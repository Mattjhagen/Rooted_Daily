Psalm 42

For the director of music. A maskil of the Sons of Korah.

The Panting Soul

“As the deer pants for streams of water, so my soul pants for you, my God.” 1
“My soul thirsts for God, for the living God. When can I go and meet with God?”
“My tears have been my food day and night, while people say to me all day long, ‘Where is your God?’”

Deep Calls to Deep

“Deep calls to deep in the roar of your waterfalls; all your waves and breakers have swept over me.” 2
“By day the Lord directs his love, at night his song is with me—a prayer to the God of my life.”

The Downcast Soul

“Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise him, my Savior and my God.” 3

Translation Notes

1 As the Deer Pants (ke-Ay-yal Tha-arog): In the scrolls, "Panting" (*Arog*) describes the "Primal Root of Desire." The deer is "Uprooted" from the "Water Source" and is in "Extreme Distress." The "Soul" (*Naphesh*) is "Thirsting" (*Tza-me-ah*) for the "Living God" (*El Chay*). It marks the transition to Book II.

2 Deep calls to Deep (Tehom-el-Tehom Qore): This is one of the most "Profound Roots" in the Psalter. The "Internal Abyss" (*Tehom*) of the human soul calls out to the "External Abyss" of "God’s Power." The "Waterfalls" (*Tzin-no-rekha*) represent the "Overwhelming Nature" of life. It is the "Uprooting of the Self" in the "Presence of the Infinite."

3 Why... are you Downcast (Mah-Thish-tho-cha-chi): Literally "Why do you melt/bow down?" This is the "Acrostic-like Refrain" of *Psalms 42 and 43*. It is the "Dialogue with the Soul." David is "Rooting" his "Will" in "Hope" (*Ho-chi-li*) while his "Emotions" are "Uprooted" by "Agony." He identifies God as his "Savior" (*Ye-shu-oth*) and his "God" (*Elo-hay*). It marks the "Confidence" required to face the "Taunts" (*Verse 10*). It is the "Rooted Daily" anthem of "Resilience."

1 Chronicles 13

“Let us bring the ark of our God back to us, for we did not inquire of it during the reign of Saul.” 1

The New Cart

They moved the ark of God from Abinadab’s house on a new cart, with Uzzah and Ahio guiding it. 2
“David and all the Israelites were celebrating with all their might before God, with songs and with harps, lyres, timbrels, cymbals and trumpets.”

The Breach of Uzzah

When they came to the threshing floor of Kidon, Uzzah reached out his hand to steady the ark, because the oxen stumbled.
“The LORD’s anger burned against Uzzah, and he struck him down because he had put his hand on the ark. So he died there before God.” 3
“David was afraid of God that day and asked, ‘How can I ever bring the ark of God to me?’” 4
“The ark of God remained with the family of Obed-Edom in his house for three months, and the LORD blessed his household and everything he had.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Did not Inquire (Lo Darashnu): In the scrolls, this is the "Chronicler’s Critique" of Saul. Saul had a "Form of Religion" but he didn't seek the "Presence." David’s first priority as king was to move from "Neglect" to "Inquiry." He wanted the Ark (the footstool of God) to be the "Center" of the national life.

2 New Cart (Agalah Chadashah): This was the "Fatal Innovation" in the scrolls. They were following the example of the Philistines (1 Samuel 6) instead of the "Command of the Law" (Numbers 4), which required the Ark to be carried on the shoulders of the Levites. They were trying to serve a "Holy God" with "Human Efficiency." It shows that "Enthusiasm" is not a substitute for "Obedience."

3 He struck him Down: In the scrolls, this is a "Severe Mercy." Uzzah’s attempt to "Steady the Ark" was an act of "Presumption"—assuming that his hands were cleaner than the "Soil" the Ark might touch. God was protecting the "Boundary of Holiness." The name "Perez Uzzah" (Breach of Uzzah) became a "Permanent Marker" of this boundary.

4 David was Afraid: This "Holy Terror" in the scrolls was a "Necessary Education" for David. He realized that the Ark was not a "Mascot" or a "Lucky Charm." It was the "Seat of the King of Kings." His fear was the "Beginning of Wisdom" regarding how to handle the "Presence of God."

5 Obed-Edom: The scrolls record this "Sovereign Surprise." While David was "Afraid," Obed-Edom (a Gittite, possibly of Philistine origin but a Levite) "Welcomed" the Ark. The result was "Explosive Blessing." It proved that the Ark was not "Dangerous" to those who approached it in "Faith and Order." The "Blessing" on his household became the "Incentive" for David to try again (Chapter 15).

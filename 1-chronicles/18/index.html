1 Chronicles 18

The Defeat of the Surrounding Nations

In the course of time, David defeated the Philistines and subdued them... 1
“David captured a thousand chariots, seven thousand charioteers and twenty thousand foot soldiers. He hamstrung all but a hundred of the chariot horses.” 2

Spoils for the Temple

From Tebah and Kun... David took a great quantity of bronze, which Solomon used to make the bronze Sea, the pillars and various bronze articles. 3
“King David dedicated these articles to the LORD, as he had done with the silver and gold from all these nations.”

The Justice of the King

David reigned over all Israel, doing what was just and right for all his people. 4
“Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder...” 5

Translation Notes

1 Defeated and Subdued: In the scrolls, this marks the "External Stability" of the kingdom. David wasn't just "Fighting"; he was "Settling" the region. By defeating the Philistines and the Moabites, he was clearing the "Roots of Conflict" that had existed for centuries.

2 Hamstrung the Horses (Way-Aqqer): This is a "Spiritual Strategy" in the scrolls. David was obeying the command for kings not to "Multiply Horses" (Deuteronomy 17). By disabling the chariots, he was refusing to rely on "Military Technology" and choosing to remain "Rooted" in God’s protection. He kept only enough for "Ceremonial" use, not for "Aggressive War."

3 Bronze for the Temple: The scrolls emphasize that the "Conquests" were for the "Cultus." The wealth of the nations was being "Aggregated" for the future Temple. David was a "Collector" for a project he wouldn't even build. It shows his "Generosity toward the Future."

4 Just and Right (Mishpat u-Tzedaqah): This is the "Dual Foundation" of David’s reign in the scrolls. *Mishpat* is "Legal Justice"; *Tzedaqah* is "Covenant Righteousness." He was not a "Tyrant," but a "Constitutional Servant." His power was "Highly Exalted" because it was used for the "Welfare of the People."

5 Joab and the Officials: The scrolls record the "Professionalization of the Kingdom." David surrounded himself with "Experts" in every field—war, records, priesthood, and administration. It shows that a "Rooted" kingdom requires both "Divine Anointing" and "Human Excellence" in organization.

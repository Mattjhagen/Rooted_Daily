1 Chronicles 10

Now the Philistines fought against Israel; the Israelites fled before them, and many fell dead on Mount Gilboa. 1
“Saul took his own sword and fell on it.”
They put his armor in the temple of their gods and hung up his head in the temple of Dagon. 2

The Verdict of Heaven

Saul died because he was unfaithful to the LORD; he did not keep the word of the LORD and even consulted a medium for guidance. 3
“Therefore the LORD put him to death and turned the kingdom over to David son of Jesse.” 4
“When all the inhabitants of Jabesh Gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul...”
“They took the bodies... and buried their bones under the great tree in Jabesh.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Mount Gilboa: In the scrolls, this mountain is the "Cemetery of the First Kingdom." It represents the "High Place" that became a "Low Point." The death of Saul’s sons (including Jonathan) signified the "End of the Dynasty" before it had even truly begun.

2 Temple of Dagon: The scrolls record this "Total Humiliation." The Philistines treated Saul’s death as a "Victory of their Gods" over the God of Israel. The "Anointed King" was reduced to a "War Trophy." It shows the "National Disgrace" that occurs when a leader is not "Rooted" in the Covenant.

3 Unfaithful (Ma'al): Literally in the scrolls, "Breach of Trust." The Chronicler explicitly links Saul’s "Physical Death" to his "Spiritual Rebellion." He consulted a "Medium" (the Witch of Endor) because he had lost the "Voice of God." It is a warning that when we seek "Guidance" outside of God, we find "Destruction."

4 Turned the Kingdom Over (Way-Yasob): This is the "Pivot of the Book" in the scrolls. Saul’s failure was the "Gateway" for David’s rise. The Chronicler skips Saul’s entire 40-year reign and goes straight to his death, showing that for the purposes of the "Covenant Story," Saul was a "Parenthesis." David is the "True Beginning."

5 Jabesh Gilead: The scrolls conclude with this "Act of Loyalty." The men of Jabesh remembered how Saul had saved them at the beginning of his reign (1 Samuel 11). Their "Courage" to rescue the bodies and their "Mourning" under the "Great Tree" provided a "Dignified End" to a tragic life. It shows that even in "Judgment," there is room for "Human Kindness."

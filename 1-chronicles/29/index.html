1 Chronicles 29

The people rejoiced at the willing response of their leaders, for they had given freely and wholeheartedly to the LORD. 1

David’s Final Prayer

“Yours, LORD, is the greatness and the power and the glory and the majesty and the splendor, for everything in heaven and earth is yours.” 2
“Wealth and honor come from you; you are the ruler of all things.”
“But who am I... everything comes from you, and we have given you only what comes from your hand.” 3

The Coronation of Solomon

“They acknowledged Solomon son of David as king a second time... and the LORD highly exalted Solomon.” 4
“He died at a ripe old age, having enjoyed long life, wealth and honor.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Wholeheartedly (Be-Lev Shalem): In the scrolls, this is the "Rooted Response." The "Unity of Giving" was a sign of the "Unity of the Spirit." David’s own generosity (3,000 talents of gold) acted as the "Seed" that produced a "Harvest" of national participation. They were not "Taxed" into giving; they were "Inspired" into it.

2 Greatness and Power: This prayer in the scrolls is a "Summary of the Divine Nature." David is stripping away his own "Royal Status" and attributing every victory, every talent of gold, and every moment of peace to the "Sovereignty of God." It is the ultimate act of "Rooting" the kingdom in the character of its true King.

3 What comes from Your Hand: In the scrolls, this is the "Theology of Stewardship." David realized that "Ownership" is an illusion. We are only "Giving back" what God has "Loaned" us. This perspective removes "Pride" and replaces it with "Gratitude." It is a "Rooted" understanding of the human condition.

4 King a Second Time: The scrolls record this "Re-coronation." The first (1 Kings 1) was in response to Adonijah’s rebellion; the second was the "National Celebration" where all the people and the leaders officially pledged their loyalty. It was the "Formal Transition" from the "Era of War" to the "Era of Wisdom."

5 Ripe Old Age (Saba): The scrolls conclude David’s life with this "Word of Fulfillment." David began as a "Forgotten Shepherd Boy" and ended as the "Most Exalted King" in the history of the nation. He was "Full" of days, wealth, and honor. His "Death" was not a tragedy, but the "Peaceful Falling" of a mighty tree that had left behind a "Root System" that would sustain the nation for centuries.

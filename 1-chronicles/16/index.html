1 Chronicles 16

They brought the ark of God and set it inside the tent that David had pitched for it... 1

David’s Psalm of Thanks

“Give thanks to the LORD, call on his name; make known among the nations what he has done.” 2
“Sing to the LORD, all the earth; proclaim his salvation day after day.”
“Ascribe to the LORD the glory due his name... Worship the LORD in the splendor of his holiness.” 3
“Let the heavens rejoice, let the earth be glad; let them say among the nations, ‘The LORD reigns!’”
“Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good; his love endures forever.” 4

The Perpetual Ministry

David left Asaph and his associates before the ark of the covenant of the LORD to minister there regularly, according to each day’s requirements. 5
“David left Zadok the priest... at the high place in Gibeon... to offer sacrifices... regularly, morning and evening.”
“Then all the people left, each for their own home, and David returned home to bless his family.”

Translation Notes

1 Set it inside the Tent (Ohel): In the scrolls, this is the "Temporary Restoration." While the Tabernacle of Moses was still at Gibeon, David created a "New Space" in Jerusalem for the Ark. It shows that God’s "Presence" (the Ark) was now being re-connected with the "King" (David). Jerusalem became a "City of Two Tents"—the palace and the sanctuary.

2 Make known among the Nations: This is a "Global Mandate" in the scrolls. Worship was not just for the "Inner Circle" of Israel; it was a "Public Testimony" to the world. The miracles of God were to be "Proclaimed" (*Hodi’u*) so that all nations would recognize His sovereignty. It is a "Rooted" missionary theme in the middle of a national celebration.

3 Splendor of His Holiness (Hadrat Qodesh): The scrolls emphasize the "Beauty of the Divine." Worship is not just a "Duty," but an "Aesthetic Experience." It is coming into the presence of "Infinite Beauty." To worship in "Splendor" means to bring the "Best" of our creativity and reverence to the One who is the source of all holiness.

4 His Love Endures Forever (Ki le-olam Chasdo): This is the "Great Refrain" of the scrolls. *Chesed* (steadfast love/mercy) is the "Root" of the Covenant. The fact that it "Endures Forever" was the "Anchor" for the post-exilic community. No matter the failure (exile) or the distance, God’s *Chesed* remains the "Constant."

5 Regularly (Tamid): In the scrolls, this signifies the "Institutionalization of Praise." David didn't want a "One-time Event," but a "Perpetual Atmosphere." He left the musicians there "according to each day’s requirements." It shows that worship is the "Rhythm of Life," not just a "Special Occasion." It was the "Spiritual Infrastructure" of the kingdom.

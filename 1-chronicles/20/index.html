1 Chronicles 20

In the spring... Joab led out the armed forces. He devastated the land of the Ammonites and went to Rabbah and besieged it. 1
“David took the crown from the head of their king... it was set with precious stones, and it was placed on David’s head.” 2

The Fall of the Giants

“Sibbekai the Hushathite killed Sippai, one of the descendants of the Rephaim.”
“Elhanan killed Lahmi the brother of Goliath... who had a spear with a shaft like a weaver’s rod.” 3
“There was a huge man with six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot—twenty-four in all. He also was descended from Rapha.” 4
“These were descendants of Rapha in Gath, and they fell at the hands of David and his men.” 5

Translation Notes

1 In the Spring: The scrolls note this as "The Time when Kings go off to War." While the parallel account in 2 Samuel focuses on David’s failure with Bathsheba during this time, the Chronicler focuses on the "National Victory." He is building the "Theology of the Kingdom," showing how the "Forces of the King" were successfully subduing the enemies of the Covenant.

2 Crown from the Head (Atarah): In the scrolls, this gold crown was incredibly heavy (a talent—about 75 lbs). It was likely a "Ceremonial Crown" or one that hung above the king. By placing it on his head, David was "Assuming the Sovereignty" of Ammon. It represents the "Total Subjugation" of a persistent enemy.

3 Brother of Goliath: The scrolls preserve the "Martial Heritage" of David’s men. David started as the "Giant-Slayer," and his influence created a "Culture of Giant-Slayers." Elhanan’s victory over Lahmi shows that the "Fear of Giants" had been broken in the army of Israel. They were "Rooted" in the victory of their leader.

4 Six Fingers and Six Toes: This "Biological Detail" in the scrolls emphasizes the "Anomalous Strength" of the Rephaim. They were "Genetic Echoes" of the ancient nephilim. Their defeat by David’s men (specifically his nephew Jonathan) shows that "Divine Anointing" overcomes "Physical Superiority."

5 Fell at the Hands of David and his Men: The scrolls conclude this section by "Distributing the Honor." It wasn't just David; it was the "Brotherhood" he had built. The "Re-rooted" nation was one where every man was capable of "Facing the Giants" that once terrified the previous generation.

1 Chronicles 3

The Sons of David in Hebron

These were the sons of David born to him in Hebron: The firstborn was Amnon... the second, Daniel...
“He reigned in Hebron seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty-three years.” 1

The Sons of the Throne

These were the sons born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan and Solomon. These four were by Bathsheba daughter of Ammiel. 2

The Royal Succession

Solomon, Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa, Jehoshaphat,
Joram, Ahaziah, Joash, Amaziah,
Azariah, Jotham, Ahaz, Hezekiah, Manasseh,
Amon, Josiah... 3

The Branch in Babylon

The descendants of Jehoiachin the captive: Shealtiel his son,
“And the sons of Pedaiah: Zerubbabel and Shimei.” 4
“The descendants of Zerubbabel... 5

Translation Notes

1 Hebron and Jerusalem: In the scrolls, this "Dual Reign" marks the transition from a "Tribal Leader" to a "National King." The Chronicler emphasizes the "Longevity" and "Stability" of David’s rule as the foundation for the entire book.

2 Sons of Bathsheba (Bath-shua): The scrolls list four sons, including Nathan and Solomon. While Solomon inherited the "Throne," it was through Nathan’s line (according to Luke 3) that the "Messianic Link" was preserved. Both the "Political" and "Biological" roots of the future King came from this family.

3 The Royal Succession: This rapid list in the scrolls is a "Covenant Catalog." It shows the "Unbroken Line" of Davidic kings through 400 years of history. Even when kings were wicked, the "Line" survived because of God’s "Promise to David" (2 Samuel 7).

4 Zerubbabel: In the scrolls, this is the "Hope of the Return." Zerubbabel was the grandson of the captive king Jehoiachin. He became the "Leader of the Remnant" that returned to rebuild the Temple. He was the "Visible Proof" that the "Root of Jesse" had not been destroyed by the Babylonian winter.

5 Descendants of Zerubbabel: The scrolls extend the genealogy several generations beyond the return from exile. This was to provide "Legal Legitimacy" for the leaders of the Chronicler’s own time, ensuring the community knew who the "Heirs of the Promise" were in a land without a king.

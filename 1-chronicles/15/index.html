1 Chronicles 15

Then David said, “No one but the Levites may carry the ark of God, because the LORD chose them to carry the ark of the LORD and to minister before him forever.” 1
“We did not inquire of him about how to do it in the prescribed way.”

The Prescribed Way

The Levites carried the ark of God with the poles on their shoulders, as Moses had commanded in accordance with the word of the LORD. 2
“Kenaniah the head Levite was in charge of the singing; that was his responsibility because he was an expert at it.” 3

The King’s Joy

David... went to bring up the ark of the covenant of the LORD from the house of Obed-Edom with rejoicing.
“Now David was clothed in a robe of fine linen... he also wore a linen ephod.” 4
As the ark... was entering the City of David, Michal daughter of Saul watched from a window. And when she saw King David dancing and celebrating, she despised him in her heart. 5

Translation Notes

1 No one but the Levites: In the scrolls, this is the "Recovery of Order." David learned that "Good Intentions" are not enough; you must follow the "Prescribed Way" (*Mishpat*). The shoulders of the Levites were the "Living Platform" God required, rather than the mechanical ease of a "New Cart." It shows that God wants "Human Involvement" in carrying His presence.

2 On their Shoulders (Bi-Kthefam): This is a "Physical Alignment" with the Law. In the scrolls, it symbolizes the "Weight of Responsibility." Carrying the Ark was a "Priestly Burden" that brought "Joy" when done correctly. It is the "Rooted" principle of "Doing God’s Work in God’s Way."

3 Kenaniah the Expert (Bin): The scrolls highlight that "Excellence" matters in worship. Kenaniah wasn't just a leader; he was an "Expert" (*Bin*—discernment/skill). He understood the "Atmosphere" and the "Art" of praise. It shows that God values both the "Heart" and the "Skill" of His servants.

4 Linen Ephod: In the scrolls, this is a "Symbol of Humility." David laid aside his "Royal Robes" and dressed like a simple priest or servant. He was "Diminishing" his own glory so that the "Glory of God" could be the focus. His "Dance" was an expression of "Total Abandonment" to the presence of his King.

5 Despised him in her Heart: The scrolls conclude the chapter with this "Contrast of Cultures." Michal represented the "Dignity of Saul’s House," which valued "Appearance" over "Presence." She saw David’s "Vulnerability" as "Foolishness." Her "Despising" was the "Final Breach" between the old order of Saul and the new order of David. It reminds us that "True Worship" will always be misunderstood by those who are "Watching from the Window" instead of "Dancing in the Street."

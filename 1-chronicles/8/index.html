1 Chronicles 8

The Sons of Benjamin

Benjamin was the father of Bela his firstborn, Ashbel the second... 1
Beriah and Shema were the heads of families living in Aijalon, and they drove out the inhabitants of Gath. 2

The Line of Saul

Ner was the father of Kish, Kish the father of Saul, and Saul the father of Jonathan, Malki-Shua, Abinadab and Esh-Baal. 3
“The son of Jonathan: Merib-Baal, who was the father of Micah.” 4
“They were brave warriors who could handle the bow... All these were descendants of Benjamin.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Benjamin: In the scrolls, this is the "Second Benjaminite Genealogy." While Judah is the focus of the book, Benjamin is crucial because of their proximity to Jerusalem. The Chronicler is rebuilding the "Map of the South" for the returning exiles.

2 Drove out the Inhabitants of Gath: The scrolls record these "Local Victories" that aren't mentioned in Samuel or Kings. It shows that the "Conquest" was often a "Clan-Level" activity. Beriah and Shema were "Pioneers" who secured the western borders of the tribe.

3 Saul (Sha'ul): In the scrolls, this is the genealogy of the "First House." Although the Chronicler focuses on David, he acknowledges the "Legitimacy" and "History" of Saul’s family. It was important for the post-exilic community to know that the "Line of Saul" still existed and was part of the nation.

4 Merib-Baal: This is the name given in the scrolls for Mephibosheth. The name "Merib-Baal" (opponent of Baal) was likely the original, later changed to "Mephibosheth" (shameful mouth) to avoid saying the name of the idol. The Chronicler restores the "Original Names," reflecting a time when the "Stigma" of the name had faded but the "History" remained.

5 Brave Warriors (Gibborim): The scrolls conclude with the "Military Character" of Benjamin. They were known as "Archers" and "Slingers" who could fight with either hand. Their "Resilience" was the reason they survived the horrific events of Judges 20 and remained a "Pillar of the Kingdom."

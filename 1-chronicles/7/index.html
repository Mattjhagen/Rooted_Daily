1 Chronicles 7

Issachar: The Fighting Men

The sons of Issachar: Tola, Puah, Jashub and Shimron—four in all.
“The total number of fighting men listed in their genealogies... was 87,000.” 1

The Mourning of Ephraim

Ezer and Elead were killed by the native-born men of Gath... because they went down to steal their livestock.
Their father Ephraim mourned for them many days, and his relatives came to comfort him. 2
“He named his next son Beriah, because there had been misfortune in his family.” 3
“Non his son, and Joshua his son.” 4

Asher: The Brave Warriors

All these were descendants of Asher—heads of families, outstanding men, brave warriors and prominent leaders. 5

Translation Notes

1 Issachar: In the scrolls, Issachar is portrayed as a tribe of "Quiet Strength." They are frequently associated with "Numerical Strength" and military readiness. This record shows a community that was "Rooted" in its capacity to defend the land.

2 Ephraim Mourned: This is a "Rare Human Detail" in the middle of a genealogy in the scrolls. It records a "Pre-Exodus" tragedy where Ephraim’s sons were killed in a raid on Gath. It shows that even the "Greatest Tribes" experienced "Grief and Loss." The Chronicler included this to show the "Humanity" of the ancestors.

3 Beriah (Be-ra'ah): Literally in the scrolls, "In Evil" or "In Trouble." Ephraim was acknowledging the "Pain" of his family history in the name of his son. However, Beriah’s line became the source of future "Restoration." It teaches that God can bring "Life" out of a "Legacy of Sorrow."

4 Joshua his Son: This is the "Point of the Genealogy" in the scrolls. It traces the line from the "Mourning Ephraim" down to "Joshua," the conqueror of the land. It shows that the "Tragedy of the Past" was not the "End of the Story." Joshua was the "Fruit" of a tree that had been pruned by grief.

5 Asher: The scrolls conclude with the "Excellence of Asher." While some tribes struggled, Asher is noted for "Outstanding Men" and "Leaders." They were a tribe that "Thrived" in the northern territory, representing the "Productivity and Nobility" of the nation when it is "Rooted" in its identity.

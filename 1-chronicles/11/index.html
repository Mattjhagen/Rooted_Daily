1 Chronicles 11

All Israel came together to David at Hebron and said, “We are your own flesh and blood.” 1
“They anointed David king over Israel, according to the word of the LORD through Samuel.”

The Capture of Jebus

The people of Jebus said to David, “You will never get in here.” Nevertheless, David captured the fortress of Zion—which is the City of David. 2
“David became more and more powerful, because the LORD Almighty was with him.”

The Three and the Water

David longed for water and said, “Oh, that someone would get me a drink of water from the well near the gate of Bethlehem!”
So the Three broke through the Philistine lines, drew water... and brought it to David. But David refused to drink it; instead, he poured it out to the LORD. 3
“Shall I drink the blood of these men who risked their lives?” 4

The Exploits of Benaiah

“Benaiah son of Jehoiada... killed a lion in a pit on a snowy day.”
“He also killed an Egyptian who was seven and a half feet tall... Benaiah went against him with a staff, snatched the spear from the Egyptian’s hand and killed him with his own spear.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Flesh and Blood (Atzm’kha u-Besarkha): Literally in the scrolls, "Your bone and your flesh." This is a "Covenant Admission." Israel was acknowledging that their unity was not political, but "Rooted" in a shared ancestry and a shared divine appointment. It took them years to admit it, but David’s patient waiting (7 years in Hebron) eventually led to this "Organic Recognition."

2 Fortress of Zion (Metzudat Tziyon): In the scrolls, Jerusalem (Jebus) was the "Impossible City." It had never been conquered since the time of Joshua. David’s capture of it made it the "Neutral Capital"—not belonging to any one tribe, but to the "King" and the "LORD." It became the "Center of the World" for the people of the promise.

3 Poured it out to the LORD (Way-Yissekh): This was a "Sacramental Act" in the scrolls. David recognized that the "Value" of the water was the "Life" of the men who risked everything for it. By pouring it out, he was "Consecrating" their devotion. He wouldn't consume for his "Pleasure" what they had gained through "Sacrifice."

4 Blood of these Men: In the scrolls, this is a "Leadership Insight." David understood the "Weight of Loyalty." He was "Rooted" in a brotherhood of warriors where "Love" was the primary motivation. This "Spiritual Bond" was the reason his kingdom was so "Mighty."

5 Killed him with his own Spear: This exploit of Benaiah in the scrolls is a "Metaphor for Resourcefulness." He was out-matched (a staff against a giant’s spear), but he used the "Enemy’s Strength" against him. It is a "Rooted" principle of the Kingdom: God uses the "Weak Things" to shame the strong.

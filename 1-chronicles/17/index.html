1 Chronicles 17

David said to Nathan the prophet, “Here I am, living in a house of cedar, while the ark of the covenant of the LORD is under a tent.” 1

The Eternal Covenant

“When your days are over... I will raise up your offspring to succeed you... and I will establish his kingdom.”
“I will be his father, and he will be my son. I will never take my love away from him...” 2
“I will set him over my house and my kingdom forever; his throne will be established forever.” 3

David’s Prayer of Gratitude

“Who am I, LORD God, and what is my family, that you have brought me this far?” 4
“Do as you promised, so that your name will be established and magnified forever.” 5

Translation Notes

1 House of Cedar (Beit Erazim): In the scrolls, this is a "Spiritual Discomfort." David felt the "Incongruity" of his own luxury compared to the "Simplicity" of God’s dwelling. He wanted to "Box" God in a building, but God wanted to "Expanse" David in a dynasty. The cedar represented "Permanence," while the tent represented "Pilgrimage."

2 Father and Son: This is a "Relational Revolution" in the scrolls. This is the first time God uses "Father-Son" language for the King of Israel. It elevates the monarchy from a "Political Office" to a "Family Covenant." This promise is the "Root" of the Messianic expectation—the "Son of David" who is also the "Son of God."

3 Established Forever (Ad-Olam): The scrolls use this "Absolute Timeframe." While other dynasties would fall, David’s "Throne" would be the "Eternal Anchor" of the nation. For the post-exilic readers, this was a "Sustaining Hope"—even without a visible king, the "Promise" was still active in the unseen realm.

4 Brought me this far (Ad-Halom): Literally in the scrolls, "Until hither." David is reflecting on his journey from the "Pasture" to the "Palace." He recognizes that his "Roots" were humble and his "Rise" was entirely due to "Divine Grace." His "Sitting" before the LORD is an act of "Surrender."

5 Magnified Forever (Ad-Olam): In the scrolls, David’s request is that his own "Success" would ultimately lead to the "Exaltation of God." He wanted his family to be the "Stage" upon which God’s "Faithfulness" was displayed to the nations. It is the "Perfect Prayer"—asking God to do what He already promised to do, for His own glory.

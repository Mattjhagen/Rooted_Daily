1 Chronicles 2

These were the sons of Israel: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun,
Dan, Joseph, Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad and Asher. 1

The Royal Line of Judah

The sons of Judah: Er, Onan and Shelah. These three were born to him by a Canaanite woman... Er was wicked in the LORD’s sight; so the LORD put him to death. 2
“Judah’s daughter-in-law Tamar bore him Perez and Zerah. Judah had five sons in all.”

The Family of Jesse

Amminadab was the father of Nahshon, the leader of the people of Judah.
Nahshon was the father of Salmon, Salmon the father of Boaz,
Boaz the father of Obed and Obed the father of Jesse.
“And the seventh was David.” 3
“Their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail. The three sons of Zeruiah were Abishai, Joab and Asahel.” 4
“Caleb son of Hezron had children by his wife Azubah...” 5

Translation Notes

1 Sons of Israel: In the scrolls, this list begins the "National Architecture." By listing the twelve sons, the Chronicler is reconstituting the "Unity of the People" after the scattering of the exile. It is a reminder that they are "One Family" under the Covenant.

2 Er was Wicked: This honest recording in the scrolls shows that the "Royal Tribe" was not without "Stain." The "Root of David" came through the line of Perez (the son of Tamar), highlighting that God’s "Sovereign Choice" often works through "Human Complication" and "Brokenness." It is a genealogy of "Grace" rather than "Perfection."

3 The Seventh was David: In the scrolls, "Seven" is the number of "Completeness." David is the "Cap-stone" of this branch of the genealogy. The Chronicler is tracing the "Path to the Throne," showing that the "King" was not an accident but a "Destiny" rooted in the leaders of Judah.

4 Sons of Zeruiah: This detail in the scrolls provides the "Family Background" for the military leaders of David’s reign. Joab, Abishai, and Asahel were David’s "Nephews." It explains the "Relational Intensity" and the "Conflict of Loyalty" that defined David’s relationship with his commanders.

5 Caleb son of Hezron: This is a "Branching Genealogies" in the scrolls. Caleb represents the "Strong and Fruitful" part of the tribe that settled the southern territory. His name (literally "Dog" or "Wholehearted") signifies "Loyalty." The Chronicler spends significant space on these "Minor Branches" to show that every "Household in Judah" had a place in the story.

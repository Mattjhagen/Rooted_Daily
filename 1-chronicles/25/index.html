1 Chronicles 25

David and the army commanders set apart some of the sons of Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun for the ministry of prophesying with harps, lyres and cymbals. 1

The Prophetic Song

“Jeduthun, who prophesied, using the harp in thanking and praising the LORD.” 2
“Heman, the king’s seer... God gave Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.”

The Order of the Singers

“Young and old alike, teacher as well as student, cast lots for their duties.” 3
“Altogether, those trained and skilled in music for the LORD numbered 288.” 4
The first lot... fell to Joseph, of the family of Asaph. The second to Gedaliah... 5

Translation Notes

1 Ministry of Prophesying (Ha-Nibb’im): In the scrolls, music was not just "Entertainment"; it was "Revelation." The musicians were "Prophesying" with their instruments. This means their "Art" was a vehicle for the "Voice of God." It connects the "Emotional Power" of music with the "Truth" of the Word.

2 Prophesied with the Harp: The scrolls show that the "Instrument" itself became a "Voice." By "Thanking and Praising," the musician was clearing the spiritual atmosphere for God to speak. Jeduthun’s ministry was a "Rooted" example of how "Praise" and "Prophecy" are inseparable.

3 Teacher as well as Student (Mevin im-Talmid): This is a "Democratization of Excellence" in the scrolls. In the service of God, "Position" (teacher) did not exempt one from the "Common Order" (the lot). The "Gifted Master" and the "Humble Student" were paired together. It ensured that the "Legacy of Praise" was passed down from generation to generation.

4 Two Hundred and Eighty-Eight: This number (24 groups of 12) in the scrolls represents "Perfect Organization." The musicians mirrored the 24 priestly orders. It ensured that at any given moment, there was a "Professional and Prophetic" sound rising from the Temple.

5 The First Lot: The scrolls record that these lots were cast in the presence of the King and the High Priests. It shows that "Artistic Service" was treated with the same "Formal Weight" as "Priestly Sacrifice." In David’s kingdom, the "Singer" was as vital as the "Priest."

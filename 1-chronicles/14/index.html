1 Chronicles 14

David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel and that his kingdom had been highly exalted for the sake of his people Israel. 1

Baal Perazim: The Breaking Out

David inquired of God: “Shall I go and attack the Philistines? Will you deliver them into my hands?” The LORD answered him, “Go, I will deliver them into your hands.”
“As waters break out, God has broken out against my enemies by my hand.” 2

The Sound in the Tops

Once again David inquired of God... “Do not go straight up, circle around them and attack them in front of the balsam trees.”
“As soon as you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, move out to battle, for that will mean God has gone out in front of you.” 3
“So David’s fame spread throughout every land, and the LORD made all the nations fear him.” 4

Translation Notes

1 For the Sake of His People: In the scrolls, this is David’s "Perspective of Power." He realized that his exaltation was not for "Personal Pride" but for "National Purpose." He was a "Shepherd King" whose success was "Rooted" in the welfare of the flock. The building of the palace was a "Sign" to the nations that Israel was now a "Permanent Power."

2 God has Broken Out (Paratz): This is the same word used in the "Breach of Uzzah" (Chapter 13). David is using the "Language of Tragedy" to describe a "Victory." Just as God "Broke Out" in judgment against a disobedient priest, He was now "Breaking Out" in salvation against a pagan enemy. God is the "Lord of the Breakthrough" (*Baal Perazim*).

3 Sound of Marching (Qol Tze'adah): In the scrolls, this is a "Supernatural Signal." David was being taught to wait for the "Divine Timing." The sound in the trees was the "Marching of the Heavenly Host" going out before the earthly army. It shows that David’s "Strategy" was to follow the "Movement of God." He didn't move until God moved.

4 David’s Fame Spread: The scrolls conclude the chapter with the "Global Impact" of these victories. By defeating the Philistines twice, David secured the "Security of the Border." His reputation was "Rooted" in the fact that he was the "First King" to truly break the power of Israel’s ancient oppressors. It set the stage for the "Restoration of the Ark" (Chapter 15).

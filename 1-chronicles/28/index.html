1 Chronicles 28

David summoned all the officials of Israel to assemble at Jerusalem... 1
“The LORD... has chosen my son Solomon to sit on the throne of the kingdom of the LORD over Israel.” 2

The Charge to the Son

“And you, my son Solomon, acknowledge the God of your father, and serve him with wholehearted devotion and with a willing mind.” 3
“If you seek him, he will be found by you; but if you forsake him, he will reject you forever.”

The Divine Architectural Plan

“He gave him the plans of all that the Spirit had put in his mind for the courts of the temple of the LORD...” 4
“All this, said David, I have in writing as a result of the LORD’s hand on me.”
“Be strong and courageous, and do the work. Do not be afraid or discouraged, for the LORD God, my God, is with you.” 5

Translation Notes

1 David Summoned: In the scrolls, this is a "Constitutional Assembly." David is ensuring that the "Transfer of Power" and the "Temple Mandate" are "Rooted" in the public consensus. He is making sure the entire nation is "Witness" to the change of season.

2 Throne of the Kingdom of the LORD: This is a "Theological Correction" in the scrolls. David is reminding the leaders that the throne doesn't belong to the "House of David," but to "the LORD." The king is merely a "Steward" on a "Vassal Throne." It represents the "Total Submission" of the earthly power to the divine.

3 Wholehearted Devotion (Lev Shalem): Literally in the scrolls, "A Complete Heart." David’s primary concern was not Solomon’s "Construction Skill," but his "Spiritual Integrity." If the "Heart" was divided, the "House" would be hollow. It is the "Rooted" principle that "Being" must precede "Doing."

4 Plans... in his Mind (Ba-Ruach): Literally in the scrolls, "By the Spirit." This indicates that the architecture of the Temple was a "Prophetic Revelation." David didn't just "Dream it up"; he "Received it." Like the Tabernacle of Moses, the Temple was to be a "Shadow of the Heavenly Reality." David’s role was to "Translate" the spirit into "Blueprints."

5 Do the Work (Aseh): The scrolls conclude with this "Call to Action." Encouragement is not for "Comfort," but for "Execution." David’s God (the God who had brought him through the pasture and the cave) would be Solomon’s God. The "Work" was guaranteed by the "Presence."

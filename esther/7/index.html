Esther 7

The Plea for Life

“Queen Esther answered, ‘If I have found favor... let my life be given me—this is my petition. And let my people be given me—this is my request.’” 1
“For I and my people have been sold to be destroyed, killed and annihilated.”

The Adversary Exposed

“Esther said, ‘An adversary and enemy! This vile Haman!’” 2

The Gallow’s Shadow

“Harbona, one of the eunuchs, said: ‘A pole seventy-five feet high stands by Haman’s house. He had it set up for Mordecai...’”
“The King said: ‘Impale him on it!’” 3
“So they impaled Haman on the pole he had set up for Mordecai. Then the king’s fury subsided.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Let my Life be Given Me (Tinnathen-li Napshi): In the scrolls, this is the "Moment of Maximum Risk." Esther finally "Uprooted" her mask of secrecy (*Chapter 2*) and "Rooted" her survival in the fate of her people. She presented herself not just as "Queen," but as a "Target."

2 This Vile Haman (Haman ha-Ra): Literally in the scrolls, "Haman the Evil." This is the "Public Indictment" of the Amalekite. Esther didn't "Sugarcoat" the accusation. She "Named the Enemy." The "Presence" of the Queen was now the "Judgment" of the official.

3 Impale Him on It (Tolu-hu Alav): The scrolls highlight the "Poetic Justice." Haman was "Uprooted" by the very instrument he had built to "Uproot" Mordecai. His "Pride" (*The 75-foot pole*) became the "Platform" of his "Shame." It signifies that the "Weapons of the Enemy" often become the "Tools of their own Destruction."

4 Fury Subsided (Shakhakhah): The scrolls conclude with this "Restoration of Order." The "Tumult" of the plot was "Calmed" by the execution of the plotter. But while the "Enemy" was gone, the "Decree" (*Chapter 3*) still remained. The "Wall" of the decree had not yet been "Breached." It marks the end of the "Haman Era" and the beginning of the "Mordecai Era."

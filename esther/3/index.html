Esther 3

The Unbowed Spirit

King Xerxes honored Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite... all the royal officials bowed down to him. 1
“But Mordecai would not bow down or show him honor.” 2

The Casting of the Pur

“They cast the Pur (that is, the lot) in the presence of Haman to select a day and month... and the lot fell on the twelfth month.” 3

The Decree of Annihilation

“There is a certain people dispersed among the peoples... whose customs are different and who do not obey the king’s laws.” 4
“Dispatches were sent to destroy, kill and annihilate all the Jews—young and old, women and children—on a single day.”
“The king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was bewildered.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Agagite (ha-Agagi): In the scrolls, this is the "Root of Ancient Hatred." Agag was the king of the Amalekites whom Saul failed to destroy (1 Samuel 15). Mordecai was a Benjamite (descendant of Kish/Saul). This is the "Saul vs. Amalek" battle erupting in the Persian capital. Haman was the "Fruit of the Amalekite Root."

2 Would not Bow (Lo Yikhra): Mordecai’s refusal was not just "Civil Disobedience"; it was "Theological Defiance." He had told them he was a "Jew" (*Verse 4*), implying that his "Root" allowed him to bow only to the God of Israel. He refused to give "Divine Honor" to an Amalekite enemy of God.

3 Pur (Lot): The scrolls highlight the "Pagan Superstition." Haman thought he was using "Chance" or "Fate" to destroy the Jews. He didn't realize that even the "Lot" is under God’s sovereignty (Proverbs 16:33). The fact that the date was set nearly a year away gave the Jews "Time to Root" their defense.

4 Customs are Different: This is the "Accusation of Otherness" in the scrolls. Haman used the "Distinctiveness" of the Jewish people as a "Weapon" against them. He portrayed "Identity" as "Insurrection." It is a "Rooted" warning that a people who are "Different" for God will always be a target for the world.

5 City was Bewildered (Nabokha): The scrolls conclude with this "Social Discord." While the "Elites" (Xerxes and Haman) were "Numb" to the tragedy they had authorized, the "Common People" were "Shocked." The "Injustice" was so "Irrational" that it "Uprooted" the peace of the capital. It marks the beginning of the "God-led Reversal."

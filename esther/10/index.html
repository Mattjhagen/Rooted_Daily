Esther 10

The Exaltation of Mordecai

“All the acts of his power and might, together with the full account of the greatness of Mordecai... are recorded in the book of the annals.” 1
“Mordecai the Jew was second in rank to King Xerxes, preeminent among the Jews.” 2

The Heart of a Leader

“He worked for the good of his people and spoke up for the welfare of all the Jews.” 3

Translation Notes

1 Book of the Annals (Sepher Dibre ha-Yamim): In the scrolls, this is the "Confirmation of Power." Mordecai’s rise from the "King’s Gate" (*Chapter 2*) to the "King’s Throne" (*Chapter 10*) was "Duly Recorded" in the archives of Media and Persia. It signifies that "God’s Plan" has a "Historical Footprint" that even the nations must acknowledge.

2 Second in Rank (Mishneh): Literally in the scrolls, "The Double" or "The Second." Like Joseph in Egypt, Mordecai became the "Administrative Center" of the empire. He was the "Protector" of the Jewish "Root" within the "Persian Soil." He proved that "Identity" can be maintained at the highest levels of "Power."

3 Worked for the Good (Doresh Tob): Literally in the scrolls, "Seeking the good." This is the "Climax of the Character." Mordecai didn't use his power for "Self-Aggrandizement" or "Personal Revenge," but for the "Peace" (*Shalom*) of his people. He "Spoke up for the Welfare" (*Dober Shalom*) of all. It is the "Rooted" definition of "True Leadership"—power used for the "Protection of the Vulnerable." The book ends with a "Remnant" that is not just "Surviving," but "Prospering" in the heart of the empire.

Esther 4

“When Mordecai learned of all that had been done, he tore his clothes, put on sackcloth and ashes, and went out into the city, wailing loudly and bitterly.” 1

The Clarion Call

“Do not think that because you are in the king’s house you alone of all the Jews will escape.”
“And who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?” 2

The Queen’s Resolve

“I will go to the king, even though it is against the law.”
“And if I perish, I perish.” 3
“Go, gather together all the Jews who are in Susa, and fast for me.” 4

Translation Notes

1 Wailing Loudly (Za’aqah Gedolah): In the scrolls, this is the "Outer Agony" of a "Death Sentence." Mordecai’s grief was not private; it was a "Public Testimony" to the injustice of the decree. He was "Rooting" his protest in the streets of the capital.

2 Such a Time as This (La-Eth Ka-Zoth): The scrolls highlight the "Providential Timing." Mordecai is suggesting that Esther’s "Royal Position" was not a "Lucky Break," but a "Divine Assignment." It signifies that every "Opportunity" is a "Responsibility." If she remained silent, God would use "Another Place" (*Verse 14*), but she would lose her "Purpose."

3 If I Perish, I Perish (Ka-Asher Abadti, Abadti): Literally in the scrolls, "As I have perished, I have perished." This is the "Radical Acceptance" of death. Esther moved from "Fear of the Scepter" to "Freedom in Sacrifice." She was "Rooting" her life in a cause greater than her "Survival."

4 Fast for Me: The scrolls conclude with this "Spiritual Mobilization." Esther knew that "Political Bravery" requires "Spiritual Power." By asking for a "Corporate Fast," she was "Gathering the Remnant" to storm the gates of heaven before she stormed the gates of the palace. It is the "Rooted" foundation of her victory.

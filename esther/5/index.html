Esther 5

The Golden Scepter

“When he saw Queen Esther standing in the court, he was pleased with her and held out to her the gold scepter that was in his hand.” 1
“What is it, Queen Esther? Even up to half the kingdom, it will be given you.”

The First Invitation

“If it pleases the king, let the king, together with Haman, come today to a banquet I have prepared.” 2

The Intoxication of Haman

“Haman went out that day happy and in high spirits. But when he saw Mordecai at the king’s gate... he was filled with rage.” 3
“But all this gives me no satisfaction as long as I see that Jew Mordecai sitting at the king’s gate.” 4

The Seventy-Five Foot Pole

“Have a pole set up, seventy-five feet high... to have Mordecai impaled on it.” 5

Translation Notes

1 Gold Scepter (Sherbit ha-Zahab): In the scrolls, this is the "Instrument of Mercy." Esther’s life was "Hanging on a Whim." The extension of the scepter was the "Providential Signal" that her "Intercession" had begun. It signifies that the "Access to the King" is a "Gift of Grace."

2 Banquet (Mishteh): Literally in the scrolls, "A Drinking Feast." Esther’s strategy was to "Isolate the King and the Enemy" in a social setting. She was "Rooting" her request in "Atmosphere" and "Patience." She didn't "Rush the Reveal."

3 Filled with Rage (Chamah): The scrolls highlight the "Volatility of Pride." Haman’s happiness was "Uprooted" by a single man who wouldn't "Bow." It proves that "Worldly Honor" is a "Fragile Root"—it can be destroyed by the smallest "Resistance."

4 No Satisfaction (En-ennu shoveh li): Literally in the scrolls, "It is worth nothing to me." Haman’s "Wealth" and "Status" were "Vitiated" by his "Hatred." His "Identity" was so "Tangled" with Mordecai’s destruction that he couldn't enjoy his own "Glory."

5 Seventy-five Feet (Chamishim Ammah): Literally "Fifty Cubits." This was an "Absurdly High" gallows. Haman wanted Mordecai’s execution to be "Visible to the Whole City." He was "Uprooting" his enemy for "Public Spectacle." Little did he know he was "Building his own Monument of Judgment."
